The Deep End of the Ocean

Michael Elliott

March 12, 1999

1 hr: 46 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Distraction; loss; the Word; spiritual adversary

Author-verified transcription

It is every mother's worst nightmare. It devastates a family. The Deep End of the Ocean, based on Jacquelyn Mitchard's best-selling novel of the same name, deals with a situation unimaginably horrifying for most parents.

Pat and Beth Cappadora have your basic upper middle class family. Nice home... three delightful children... two rising careers... everything indicating if not an idyllic existence, at least a very happy one. And then, in the blink of an eye, it all goes terribly wrong.

Traveling to Chicago (with her three children in tow) to attend a high school reunion, she leaves her 7 year-old and 3 year-old sons in the crowded hotel lobby with their bags as she goes to the front desk to check in. A few minutes later, she returns to find the 3 year-old missing.

The movie follows the subsequent effects that young Ben's disappearance has on the family, notably upon Beth whose only defense against the guilt, shame and pain of the loss is to shut down functionally. She becomes a shell of what she once was, leaving her husband and remaining children to fend for themselves.

The movie then abruptly shifts nine years into the future. The framework of the family is still intact, but its vitality is gone. We see the results of Beth's inability to function reflected mostly in the aloof surliness of the now 17 year-old Vincent.

Suddenly, appearing at their door is 10 year-old Sam Karras. Beth immediately recognizes him as her missing Ben. But the recognition turns out to be the easiest aspect. Sam is a stranger to them, as they are to him. Incorporating him back into the family may not be the easiest or best thing for all concerned.

Michelle Pfeiffer (A Thousand Acres) avoids turning her character into the melodramatic stereotype that usually accompanies a story line such as this. It is a thoughtful, intelligent portrayal of a mother suffering a loss so great, she almost allows everything else to be stolen from her as well.

Treat Williams (Deep Rising) as Pat provides a strong counterpoint to Beth's weakness. Whoopi Goldberg (How Stella Got Her Groove Back) is a wizened police detective with a stripper's name (Candy Bliss) who, for no apparent reason other than to be politically correct, the filmmakers have made a lesbian. Jonathan Jackson (Camp Nowhere) as the teen aged Vincent gives the most realistic portrayal of a modern-day teenager that I've seen in quite a while. He is able to project a I-don't-need-you, I-don't-need-anybody attitude while still letting us see the underlying desperate need for his mother's love and brother's forgiveness.

Director Ulu Grosbard (Falling In Love) obviously chose not to turn The Deep End of the Ocean into an overly long emotional crying jag (Stepmother comes to mind) even though the subject matter does have a TV movie-of-the-week feel to it. There's a certain detachment to the film which engages our brain but does not embrace our heart.

The most wrenching scene takes place in the hotel when, after searching for Ben for over five hours, the police ask Beth for a more current photo. Upon looking at a picture from her purse, the pain of losing Ben proves to be too much for Beth and she lets out a cry from the depth of her soul, resisting any and all attempts to console her. It is a powerful moment. This movie reminds us that all it takes is a moment. A small distraction that draws our focus and "poof!" something dear to us has been stolen. For Beth, it was the distraction of a busy hotel and the loss of her son. Spiritually, we know our adversary is a thief. He uses the pressures and pleasures of this world to distract us. Primarily what he wants to steal is the Word of God from our hearts.

And these are they by the way side, where the word is sown; but when they have heard, Satan cometh immediately, and taketh away the word that was sown in their hearts. Mark 4:15 [KJV]

He knows that the only thing that can defeat his purposes is the Word of God living in the heart of a believing man or woman. That is why God entrusted it to us. It is why the devil wants to steal it from us. Don't let him.
