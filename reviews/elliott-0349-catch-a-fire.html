Author-verified transcription

Apartheid, the former South African policy of racial segregation which ended in 1994, is no less odious when seen with the perspective of time. Catch a Fire tells the story of one man's struggle against an unjust and oppressive system.

As it happens, Patrick Chamusso (Derek Luke) did not set out to be an activist or "freedom fighter." He was living a relatively happy, non-political life with his wife, Precious (Bonnie Henna), and children in the politically charged atmosphere of 1980s South Africa. But when the Secunda Oil Refinery, where he works as a foreman, is the target of a terrorist attack he falls under the scrutiny of Nic Vos (Tim Robbins), the white police colonel investigating the crime.

Chamusso is arrested and tortured for information he doesn't have. When he fails to satisfy his interrogators, his wife is given the same treatment. When finally released, Chamusso is a changed man. He leaves his family to join the ANC (African National Congress) and their fight against apartheid.

Director Philip Noyce is no stranger to political thrillers set against the backdrop of cultural change, having helmed the production of The Quiet American set in Vietnam and Rabbit- Proof Fence set in Australia. In Catch a Fire, he expertly captures a sense of the inevitability of change in a racially divided South Africa.

The film finds its energy from the performances of its leads. Derek Luke is electric as Patrick Chamusso, a man willing to work within the system until the system mistreated him and his family. His outraged response led to a sacrifice that changed the course of his life and helped to change a nation.

Tim Robbins gives a controlled, restrained performance as the Afrikaner cop who sees the futility in trying to rule a people who vastly outnumber their rulers. It is a quiet and nuanced portrayal that adds depth to this villainous role.

The film is greatly enhanced and lifted by the infectious rhythm of African music. It plays an integral part of the story as the musical chants of the ANC helped to unite a people and give them cause to hope for the freedom they desired.

Catch a Fire is further enriched by the knowledge that it is a true story. The film's final moments includes footage of the real Patrick Chamusso and what he has accomplished following the events that are depicted in the film. History is rife with examples of rulers or governments that have tried to stay in power by oppressing their people. It is a practice that is never lasting for it breeds discontent. Discontent will lead to rebellion and rebellion leads to change.

Patrick Chamusso would never have set out to strike a blow against the government had that government wisely adopted fair and equitable treatment for its people. His actions were born from oppression.

It is a wise ruler who recognizes that the key to power is wielding it with a just and righteous hand. Righteousness leads to peace and peace breeds contentment. A people who are content with their lives do not rebel against the authority who has allowed it to exist.

The king that faithfully judgeth the poor, his throne shall be established for ever. Proverbs 29:14 (KJV)
