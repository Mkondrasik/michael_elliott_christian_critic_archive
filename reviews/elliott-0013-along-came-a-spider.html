Along Came A Spider

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 6, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Timothy 1:6 1 Thessalonians 5:19 Ephesians 2:8

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual gifts; calling; courage; grace

Author-verified transcription

Police detective, psychologist, and author Dr. Alex Cross makes a welcome return to the screen in the film adaptation of James Patterson's best-selling novel, Along Came A Spider. While the film takes a number of missteps along the way, the commanding and reassuring presence of Morgan Freeman (Kiss The Girls), as Alex Cross, manages to hold the picture together.

The film opens with a harrowing sequence depicting an undercover operation which ends disastrously, causing Cross to take a guilt-ridden, self-imposed early retirement from the police work for which he is renowned. Some eight months later, after a senator's daughter (Mika Boorem, The Patriot) is kidnapped from the exclusive private school where she takes her classes, Cross is personally invited to join the investigation by the kidnapper himself.

Calling himself Soneji (Michael Wincott, Before Night Falls), this kidnapper doesn't fit the standard psychological profile of those who engage in this particular crime. His motivations are different and much more complex. It will be left to Cross to unlock the keys to this individual's identity before it is too late to save the girl.

Helping Cross is Secret Service agent Jezzie Flannigan (Monica Potter, Patch Adams) who was the agent in command at the school where the kidnapping took place. In order to redeem herself, she joins Cross's efforts to track down Soneji and find the senator's daughter.

Because of the lead character's expertise in psychology and criminal "profiling," Along Came A Spider should probably be classified more as a cerebral thriller. Unfortunately, the script isn't intelligent enough for that claim to be made. The characters are forced to make incredible leaps of logic which defy understanding. The plot twists, which are surprising at first, become rather ludicrous by the end of the film. A spider's web, to the human eye, is something that is precisely and intricately crafted. Along Came A Spider still has much to learn from the arachnids.

Nevertheless, accolades must be given to Morgan Freeman. His seemingly effortless portrayal of Alex Cross is a great study in control. He is proof that an intelligent actor playing an intelligent man can be a pleasurable thing to watch.

It is a bit of a stretch to accept Monica Potter as an FBI agent, much less one which would be in charge of a security operation involving the children of the world's leaders, but her performance is in earnest and so we're willing to temporarily suspend our disbelief. Mika Boorem does a fine job as the scared but resourceful little kidnap victim.

Director Lee Tamahori (The Edge) and first time screenwriter Marc Moss have crafted a slightly above average potboiler which is disappointing only because the ingredients were present for the film to be so much more.

When consoling Flannigan who is berating herself over her perceived failure and considering her resignation, Cross tries to encourage her by saying, "You do what you are." Elaborating further, he states that every person is born with a gift or else develops themselves to distinction in a particular area. To deny that gift or ability, Cross explains, is to deny who you are.

Spiritually speaking, this is something that many do on a regular basis. It is why the apostle Paul repeatedly gave this exhortation to Timothy:

Neglect not the gift that is in thee, which was given thee by prophecy. 1 Timothy 4:14a (KJV)

A gift becomes the property of the one who receives it. It can be put to use or it can be pushed aside and forgotten. God's gift to man is too often placed in that latter category.

For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Ephesians 2:8 (KJV)

It is God who has called us; it is He who has empowered us; and it is He who directs our steps. But it is still we who must have the courage and conviction to act according to His will. To do otherwise is to deny or neglect the gift which He gave each one of us.
