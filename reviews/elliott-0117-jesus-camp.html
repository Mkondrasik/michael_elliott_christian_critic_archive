Jesus Camp

Author-verified transcription

Evangelical Christianity is examined, or at least observed, in the new documentary, Jesus Camp, by Heidi Ewing and Rachel Grady. It focuses on the work of Pentecostal preacher Becky Fisher and her vision to train the next generation to carry the message of Jesus Christ to the world – whether that world wants to hear it or not.

The film focuses on three of the children who will be attending Fisher’s summer camp in North Dakota where children as young as six will be further trained in the disciplines needed to be “soldiers” for Christ.

Levi, a 12-year-old preacher who was born again at the age of 5; Rachel, a 9-year-old who dreams of being a manicurist because she believes her clients will be relaxed enough to accept the message she’d be delivering; and Tory, a 10-year-old who struggles against the desire to dance for the flesh instead of dancing for the glory of God, are the central figures of the film and the children we most follow.

Because the beliefs of these Christians are so strong and inarguable, the filmmakers decided to include footage of talk radio host Mike Papantonio, a moderate Christian openly critical of the political aspirations of the evangelical movement, in order to provide the semblance of conflict.

They needn’t have bothered. There are enough disturbing elements to the methods and practices of Fisher to raise questions if not an eyebrow or two. Less like training and more like indoctrination, Fisher leads her young charges through an emotionally wrenching self-exploration regarding such topics as sin, abortion, temptation, and their responsibilities toward God.

Ewing and Grady (The Boys of Baraka) do a commendable job in letting Fisher and her students speak for themselves. This documentary is fair and even-handed in its treatment of its subjects. It allows us to draw our own conclusions as to what it is that we’re seeing. I’m sure there will be no shortage of polarizing opinions.

Scripture does tell us to “Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.” (Proverbs 22:6). Such instruction or discipline is needed because “foolishness is bound in the heart of a child.” (Proverbs 22:15a). It is important for us to teach our children so when they are of an age to make their own decisions, they will make them based upon godly principles and truths.

And yet it is uncomfortable watching Fisher drive these kids into a religious fervor. It feels like the kids are being forced into a mindset rather than being given the tools they need to one day make their own God-given free-will choice. It will be interesting to see if these children maintain their faith through their teenage years and into their early adult years.

There’s no doubt that Fisher is well-intentioned and believes with all her heart that she is doing what God has called her to do. Her sincerity is evident to see. But sincerity is no guarantee for truth.

Neither is fervor synonymous with belief. The strongest believers I know possess a quiet strength that is born of an overwhelming peace. They speak - not boisterously but assuredly and with authority. They need not whip their audience into an emotional frenzy but rather appeal to our minds by reminding us of the promises of God and the truths He would have us know and apply to our lives.

We need not adopt the methodology of the enemies of the Christian faith in order to prevail. We simply need to walk in the power of God’s love.
