Author-verified transcription

The only problem with borrowing story lines from classic films is that the new film will inevitably be compared with its source material. In the case of Juwanna Mann, a black version of the classic 1982 comedy, Tootsie, the differences are immediately noticeable. Tootsie was timely. Tootsie was funny. Tootsie was good. Juwanna Mann is none of these.

The movie tells the story of Jamal Jeffries (Miguel A. Núñez Jr., Life), a flashily arrogant and obnoxious NBA star whose on court hijinks cause the league to exercise the moral clause of his contract thus placing him on permanent suspension. With his fat NBA paycheck coming to a stop, Jeffries must find a new source of income and fast. Of course, all he knows is basketball.

Unfortunately for him, none of the other teams or leagues want to touch him due to his reputation for being a ball-hogging hothead. If he is to have any hope to play pro ball again, he's going to have to change.

And change he does... into a woman. With the NBA turning their back on him, he's got no choice but to turn his padded bra to the WUBA. With his acerbic agent (Kevin Pollack, Deterrence) reluctantly in on the truth, Jamal Jeffries dons a wig and becomes Juwanna Mann. Not only is she/he accepted into the league, he/she becomes a major star.

The deception is not without consequences. Jamal starts falling for the team captain Michelle (Vivica A. Fox, Kingdom Come), but she of course only sees him as a woman. For that matter, so does gold-toothed rapper Puff Smokey Smoke (Tommy Davidson, Bamboozled) who has set his sights on Juwanna as his next conquest. Meanwhile, Michelle's current beau, Romeo (singer Ginuwine in his film debut) is cheating on her, a fact that Jamal finds increasingly disturbing.

Anyone who has seen Tootsie knows how the story ends. It just plays out so much better in the original film. Try as he might, Miguel A. Núñez Jr. is no Dustin Hoffman. Nor is Vivica Fox a Jessica Lange. Kevin Pollak has some nice moments in his Sidney Pollack role. And though the actress who played Aunt Ruby, Jamal's friend and confidante, did a fine job, we can't help but miss Bill Murray.

Núñez is fine as the fast talking huckster but he can't make us believe his emotional "growth" which comes from his experiences in a dress. Vivica Fox is certainly attractive enough in her role as Marie, but a pro basketball star? I don't think so.

Tootsie worked because Michael Dorsey, dressed as Dorothy Michaels, gained insight, understanding, and sensitivity to issues he never even considered before. The point of the movie's "gender crossing" was to examine, in a unique and entertaining way, the complex dynamics of male/female relationships.

Juwanna Mann doesn't work because it ignores those very things that makes Tootsie so enjoyable. Instead, it settles for lame, overworked sexual innuendoes and the unfunny, crude mannerisms of a guy pretending he's a woman.

The underlying moral of the story is that Jamal Jeffries must learn how to function as part of a team. That we never believe his conversion does not weaken the principle being illustrated. A group of people working together as a unit will always be stronger and more versatile than an individual operating alone.

A team functions much like the human body. When all parts work together, the body can perform to its highest potential. If any single part of the body is not doing its job, the whole body suffers as a result.

That there should be no schism in the body; but that the members should have the same care one for another. And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it. 1 Corinthians 12:25-26 (KJV)

Speaking of suffering, the filmmakers might want to consider changing their title to match the question that may be on the minds of their audience as they watch this film: "Juwanna Leave?"
