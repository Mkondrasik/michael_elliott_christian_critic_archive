Author-verified transcription

Whatever we might think of people who knock on our doors for the sole purpose of spreading the Word of God, we must consider that those people are human and have the same desires and feelings that we have. This is illustrated in the latest entry of an LDS (Latter Day Saints) sponsored film.

The Two Best Years tells the story of four American Mormon missionaries stationed in Haarlem, Netherlands. At the start of the film we are introduced to three of the missionaries. They await the arrival of the new "companion" who will complete the team. What the neophyte does not realize is that those who have been on the field for a while have kind of soured on the experience.

Elder Rogers (K.C. Clyde) has long been distracted from the purpose that sent him to Holland. Once a devout and enthusiastic Mormon evangelist, he has allowed himself to become discouraged due to a personal situation back home. Since then he has corrupted and then discarded many partners (or companions).

His new companion, Elder Calhoun (Kirby Heyborne), will not be so easily dissuaded. His zeal and unbridled enthusiasm for being on the mission field cannot be deterred. In fact, it is a bit infectious. Soon, Rogers begins to feel the stirrings of the evangelical fervor which once coursed through his veins.

Written and directed by Scott S. Anderson, who based his screenplay upon his experiences in Holland as an LDS missionary, the film has the feel of authenticity backed by sincerity. The Mormon doctrine is not so much on display in this film as is the human condition - evidenced by the four very different missionaries who are sharing this experience.

K.C. Clyde plays the discontented Rogers who has let a sour personal experience affect his attitude and effectiveness. He's got a nice natural manner about him which draws us to his character even as his character pushes people away.

David Nibley is Elder Johnson, the official "leader" of the house. He too is affable and warm without being cloying. Cameron Hopkin plays Elder Van Pelt as a self-centered opportunist. It is a broad characterization that could have been pulled back a bit but people will recognize and appreciate the stereotype being portrayed.

Kirby Heyborne, as Elder Calhoun, starts off being too much of a dweeb. His character's lack of interpersonal skills are too exaggerated to be entirely believable and therefore his growth or transformation, when it occurs, loses impact.

Still, the film does a good job of pulling us into the lives of these four young men. It also paints an evenhanded picture of the emotional roller coaster ride that is modern missionary work. Committing two years of one's life in the service of one's belief is commendable. I can't say that I agree with the Mormon doctrine but I do appreciate the level of commitment and discipline that they demonstrate. They also make some pretty decent films. The Two Best Years is one of the best that they've produced thus far.

How do we keep the zeal or fervor for God through all the muck and mire that we must wade through in this world? It is a question that all of us face. God has given us the answer.

He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength. Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall: But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. Isaiah 40:29-31 (KJV)

Waiting on the Lord simply means to live our lives with the hope of his return fueling our drive and motivation. Our zeal is not based upon our outward circumstances. It is based upon the love we have in our hearts.
