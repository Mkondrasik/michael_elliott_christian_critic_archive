Crazy/Beautiful

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 29, 2001
Rating
2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 35 min

Original Author Copy

Translation: NIV / KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Influence; friendship; restoration; discipline

Author-verified transcription

Crazy/Beautiful is an apt description for the film's leading character. The film traces a romance involving a hardworking, ethically-minded Mexican student and an attractive party girl bent on self-destruction.

Carlos Nunez (Jay Hernandez, Living the Life) wakens at 5:00 am each day so he can take a two hour bus ride in order to go to the more affluent Pacific Palisades High. His goal is to make it into the US Naval Academy and become a pilot. Everything he does is designed to get him closer to accomplishing that goal. Until, that is, he meets fellow student Nicole (Kirsten Dunst, Drop Dead Gorgeous). The manner in which they meet should have served as a warning for what was to follow: she was out doing community service as part of her sentence for a DUI.

Nicole is the troubled daughter of Congressman Tom Oakley (Bruce Davison, X-Men) and to say that the girl has issues is to say that Carter has liver pills. Nicole is a mess. Feeling unloved and unwanted at home, she acts the part of a "good-time" girl. Her extreme behavior includes alcohol abuse and promiscuous sex as well as a genuine disregard for authority figures. Quite the opposite of straight arrow Carlos.

When a flirtation becomes something more serious, Carlos risks losing all he has worked for by following Nicole down her path of immediate self-gratification. Not only might he throw away his future, but also all the sacrifices that others have made on his behalf.

Kirsten Dunst leaves her apple-cheeked, cheerleader persona behind in this role of the bad girl from a broken home. In a wise move, she chooses not to reveal all in this film and yet still manages to convey a sexually provocative character. Perhaps this might serve as a lesson to other young actresses (and producers of any age) that nudity is not necessary to tell a story or portray a character.

That said, the film is heavily charged with sexuality and does depict irresponsible behavior by teen aged characters.

Perhaps that is why it is so refreshing to see a character like Carlos in the film. a young man of color who has a dream and is not afraid to work hard to attain it. Jay Hernandez keeps it real and understated. This adds a quiet strength to his characterization.

Taryn Manning (TV's Get Real) adds some spark as Nicole's party partner, Maddie. Bruce Davison (X-Men) is a typical Hollywood (i.e. clueless) father who has no idea of how to control his daughter.

Director John Stockwell (Cheaters) paints his picture with a melodramatic flair and many scenes suffer from a double coating of overemotional teenage angst. It doesn't stop the positive messages from shining through however: there are consequences to our actions; anything worth having is worth working for; friends are worth saving; children (even teen aged ones) need both love and discipline.

The blunt warnings that Carlos receives regarding his relationship with Nicole are valid ones, supported by scripture:

Do not be misled: "Bad company corrupts good character." 1 Corinthians 15:33 (NIV)

By associating with Nicole, Carlos risks accompanying her on the downward spiral that is her life. But then again, if we love someone, we can't just let that person self-destruct without a valiant effort to get him or her off the wrong path before it is too late.

Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted. Galatians 6:1 (KJV)

Here's the thing: restoration is dependent upon two factors. We can certainly help to lead someone back from the brink of self destruction, but unless there is a willingness to change evident in the person we are trying to help, then we must be ever mindful of our own walk. Bad habits can be picked up easily. Once learned, they can be very difficult to drop.
