Author-verified transcription

With so many films being released on today’s market that are obviously targeted to a younger audience, there’s something refreshing about a romantic comedy that honestly holds a mirror to the difficulties of dating after, shall we say, the bloom has left the rose. Enough Said tells the story of two divorced, single parents on the cusp of becoming empty-nesters who manage to find each other but then need to find the courage to invest in a new relationship at this stage of their lives.

Julia Louis Dreyfus stars as Eva, who makes her living as a masseuse by lugging her massage table from client to client, working out the knots and relieving the stress for people she barely knows, let alone likes. At a party where she meets her newest client and potentially new best friend Marianne (Catherine Keener), she is also introduced to Albert (James Gandolfini), a burly but gentle man whose winning personality eventually overcomes the physical shortcomings that Eva first notices.

After the first date goes well, Eva begins to consider that Albert may be someone with whom she could form a deeper emotional bond. But when she discovers he was once married to Marianne, who cannot stop talking negatively about all his faults and idiosyncrasies, she begins to question her judgment and doubt the future of their relationship.

The film is a lovely showpiece for both Dreyfus and Gandolfini who give sweetly honest and brave performances, poking fun at the mild ravages of age that begin to be noticed at the middle-aged years. The tenderness and vulnerability that both these actors manage to infuse into their characters provides the warmth to the production that welcomes us into their story and makes us care about the outcome.

As Eva hears more from Marianne about Albert’s ways, she appears to almost try to cause her relationship with him to self-destruct by prodding or provoking him to demonstrate the behaviors his ex-wife couldn’t tolerate. When asked why, she responds weakly that maybe she was trying to protect herself. Albert’s legitimate rejoinder was “What about protecting us?”

Relationships can be hard to maintain, especially in this fast-paced world that celebrates individualism and self-satisfaction. God expresses His heart on the matter in the exhortation He gives to married couples:

“However, let each man of you [without exception] love his wife as [being in a sense] his very own self; and let the wife see that she respects and reverences her husband [[a]that she notices him, regards him, honors him, prefers him, venerates, and esteems him; and [b]that she defers to him, praises him, and loves and admires him exceedingly]. Ephesians 5:33 (KJV)

This agrees with God’s analogy that a husband and wife become “one flesh.” To make a relationship work, both parties must be willing to view their partner as a part of themselves – not something or someone separate. We must acknowledge that no person is perfect, excepting our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, but a union can be perfect if we are willing to let go of what we want from a relationship and focus instead on what we can bring to it.
