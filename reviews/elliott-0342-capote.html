Author-verified transcription

Who is Truman Capote? After watching the biopic Capote, we may not be any closer to fully answering that question, but with a brilliantly conceived and executed performance by Philip Seymour Hoffman, we do get a sense of the man.

In 1959, when a family of four is inexplicably murdered in the sleepy town of Holcomb, Kansas, the story intrigues New Yorker Magazine writer Truman Capote. Tired of writing fiction, Capote decides to visit Holcomb to write about the town in the aftermath of such violence. Capote, a flamboyant raconteur, is far more at home holding court at celebrity filled parties than in the dust bowl of America but his reputation precedes him and he slowly integrates himself into the lives of those affected by this tragedy.

When the killers are taken into custody, Capote seizes the opportunity and befriends the more sensitive of the two, Perry Smith (Clifton Collins Jr.). He sees Smith as a gold mine, the entrance to a literary work that will surpass anything he's done before. "When I think of how good this book can be, I can hardly breathe," he says. He decides to title his book In Cold Blood.

Smith, on the other hand, sees Capote as his celebrity ticket to winning an appeal. Each tries to use the other to get what he wants. But as the judicial process grinds on over the next four years, it is clear that the whole experience is having a deleterious effect on Capote's mind and emotional state.

Hoffman breathes life into Capote, thoroughly climbing into the skin of his character and making it his own. It is a riveting performance that will place him at the top of Hollywood's A list.

Catherine Keener adds considerable support as Capote's friend and fellow writer, Nell Harper Lee (author of To Kill a Mockingbird), who accompanies him to Kansas as his research assistant.

Clifton Collins Jr. portrays a sympathetic view of a cold blooded killer. It is a small performance that resonates with truth. Chris Cooper contributes another layer as the Kansas lawman who assists Capote's research even though he is distrustful of his motives.

Director Bennett Miller confidently walks us through Capote's story, concentrating on the man and the emotional deterioration he suffers as his ties to the killer becomes more and more complex. In Cold Blood became Capote's seminal work, making him a household name. It also took a major toll. He was never to finish another novel. He died of complications from alcoholism ten years later. The film ends with Capote's own words: "More tears are shed over answered prayers than unanswered ones."

What we choose to do and how we choose to do it will impact us greatly. God continually warns us in His Word to walk circumspectly (diligently) taking care not to become contaminated with the world around us.

Capote, by feigning (?) friendship to garner the information he needed to complete his novel, invited an unhealthy element into his soul. One that would cause him considerable pain and sorrow for the rest of his life.

Therefore what fruit did you have then in the things over which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death. Romans 6:21 (KJV)
