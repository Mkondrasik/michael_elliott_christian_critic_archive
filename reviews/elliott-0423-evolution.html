Author-verified transcription

In 1984, Ivan Reitman directed a blockbuster summer hit called Ghostbusters. Now in the year 2001, he has released his newest directorial effort, Evolution. Watching the latter film reminds us that no matter how much things may "evolve," they stay pretty much the same. Evolution is merely Ghostbusters remade with aliens taking the place of ghosts. There is even an alien version of the giant Sta-Puf Marshmallow Man which appears in the closing reel.

At least Reitman realized that when one is going to copy a formula, it helps to copy one that works. Evolution, though hardly original, is funny. Sometimes extremely so.

David Duchovny (Return To Me) and Orlando Jones (Double Take) are professors in the science department of a small Arizona community college. When a meteor strikes just outside of town, they go to investigate and discover one-celled organisms oozing from the rock.

Figuring they are bound for the Nobel Prize, they keep their discovery a secret... at least for a day. That's how long it takes for these alien life forms to evolve into more complex CGI creatures.

It quickly becomes evident that if left unchecked, these rapidly growing and changing life forms will overrun the earth, relegating human beings to a prime spot on the endangered species list. By the time our heroes comprehend the danger, the more arrogant and incompetent members of the US government and military have become involved which pretty much dooms everyone on the planet unless our community college scientists and their dimwitted pals can somehow manage to save the world.

Duchovny underplays his character nicely, with a wry humor and knowing smile. Jones is allowed to play more broadly and as such serves as an effective counterpoint. They combine to make a decent movie buddy combination... loose and comfortable with an affable give and take between them.

Seann William Scott (Road Trip) becomes the third "alienbuster" and gives another one of his Dude, Where's My Car? doofus characterizations as a fireman-in-training who first discovers the meteor. Julianne Moore (Hannibal) is the attractive love interest and only sane government official. Aside from some scripted pratfalls, she plays it relatively straight.

The key to this film's success is that it never takes itself too seriously. The director is having fun. The cast appears to be having fun. The audience is expected to just sit back and enjoy the ride. On that level, the film delivers what it promises.

Evolution does make use of some crude references, sexual innuendoes and off-color humor which has given it a PG-13 rating from the MPAA.

The filmmakers also appear to be of the school that believes if flatulence is funny, then giant alien flatulence will be hysterical.

Looking past the comedy, there is a spiritual flaw built into the premise of the film. In building their story, Reitman and company simply assume that the theory of evolution will be accepted as fact. Of course, the reason it is called a theory is because no factual or scientific evidence exists to support it.

"What it took us two billion years to accomplish, they did in a day," Duchovny's character marvels. The accomplishment of which he speaks is the process of moving from oozing slime to primate. It is a process which defies logic, especially when seen speeded up by 2 billion times. There has never been any scientific proof of macro evolution (that is where one class of life evolves to form a different class of life.) The Bible is very clear on the question of how life came to be.

For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him. Colossians 1:16 (KJV)

If only scientists would spend less time trying to disprove scripture and more time using it to help them in their pursuit of knowledge, God only knows how much closer we would be to the answers we seek.
