Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 27, 2004
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Psalms 119:16Ephesians 4:1-3Ephesians 4:22-23

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Memory; relationships; forgiveness; patience; renewing the mind

Author-verified transcription

Memories. They can be both a blessing and a curse. But could we live without them? Such is the question of Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind, a surreal new film written by Charlie Kaufman (Adaptation) and directed by Michel Gondry (Human Nature).

Joel Barish (Jim Carrey) is a nice guy - a bit introverted and ill at ease around strangers perhaps - but generally nice. Clementine Kruczynski (Kate Winslet) is an extrovert whose moods change as often as her unnatural hair color.

When they meet, they are drawn to one another and form a romantic relationship that, over time, comes to have more bad times than good. We discover that Clementine, tired of how this relationship is making her feel, has hired a company with the technology to erase all memory of Joel from her mind. When Joel learns of this, he decides to do the same.

But in the middle of the procedure, Joel’s reminded of the good times they shared and subconsciously resists the procedure.

The story, much of which is set in Joel’s mind, becomes a race against time as Joel’s mind, his body lying in a dormant state, must try to figure out a way to retain a memory of Clementine before she is erased forever.

It is a plot that may at first sound odd until we realize that it came from the same writer who penned Being John Malkovich, about people discovering a “portal” that allows them into the mind of the quirky actor, and Adaptation, which explored the duality of the creative process and gave us insight as to the workings of the mind of Kaufman himself.

Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind, the title of which is borrowed from a poem written by Alexander Pope, continues Kaufman’s exploration into the intricacies of the human mind and how it functions (or malfunctions).

Jim Carrey sets aside his over-the-top comic persona to deliver a controlled and conservative dramatic performance. I’ve always thought of him as being capable of demonstrating more emotional depth as an actor than he’s shown in past roles. We’ve seen glimpses of it in such films as The Truman Show, The Majestic, and The Man on the Moon but this may be his finest dramatic performance to date.

Kate Winslet’s character is a bit of an enigma and, as played, it is difficult to get to the heart of why she is as she is. But she is genuine and believable as a lost and emotionally confused woman.

The film boasts a strong supporting cast including Mark Ruffalo, Elijah Wood, and Kirsten Dunst as employees of the memory erasing company headed by Tom Wilkenson.

The film works on multiple levels. First is the broad picture of how memory works. Whether the technology of memory erasure will ever exist is unimportant. We have the God-given capability to control our minds and focus them to think about those things God exhorts us to remember.

I will delight myself in thy statutes: I will not forget thy word. Psalms 119:16 (KJV)

On a different level is the subject of making a loving relationship work. Two people coming together as one is a strange and almost miraculous occurrence. It takes a tremendous effort on both parties. Communication, forgiveness, tolerance and patience are part and parcel of any loving relationship. Clementine’s impulsive decision to erase her memory because of an argument with her boyfriend is a sign of weakness and immaturity.

Our personal relationships deserve all that we have to offer. Working through difficulties or disagreements is simply part of the process. We will be stronger for the effort that we make.

I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called, with all lowliness and meekness, with long-suffering, forbearing one another in love; endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. Ephesians 4:1-3 (KJV)
