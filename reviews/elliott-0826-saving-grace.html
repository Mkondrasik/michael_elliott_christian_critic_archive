It is ironic that in a summer filled with foul and offensive films, a breath of fresh air can be found in a movie which reeks of marijuana smoke. SAVING GRACE is a surprisingly light and amusing British romp peopled with whimsical and quirky characters akin in nature to last year's Waking Ned Devine.

Amateur horticulturist Grace Trevethyn (Brenda Blethyn, Little Voice) is coping with the sudden death of her husband who failed to strap on his parachute before doing the 10,000 foot plunge. She is keeping her well-appointed house in order and tending to her lush garden. However, when the creditors come calling she quickly learns that her late husband's many failed business plans have amassed hefty debts and wiped out their savings, leaving her with, among other things, an unpaid mortgage. To save her home, she must somehow find three hundred thousand pounds.

Her gardener, Matthew (Craig Ferguson, The Big Tease) has got problems of his own. Trying to keep his small patch of homegrown marijuana plants a secret by keeping them deeply covered, he also can't get them healthy enough to produce any buds. He turns to Grace's green thumb for help and Grace seizes upon an idea to solve her money problem. Soon, Grace and Matthew are producing a bumper crop of cannabis.

Living in a small town has its advantages and disadvantages. It isn't long before this little project of theirs spills over and touches the other residents of this sleepy little village, many of whom develop an insatiable case of the "munchies."

Growing the plants comes easily to Grace. Selling them is another story and much humor is found in her efforts of wandering the streets of London ineptly searching for a buyer for her 20 kilos.

Ms. Blethyn's Grace is so decidedly British, using phrases such as "Righty-ho," and much of the humor comes from seeing this proper English lady delve into a line of business not characteristically connected with the upper crust of society.

Mr. Ferguson, who also co-wrote the script, adds nicely to the easygoing and gentle feel to the film with his likable, non-threatening characterization of Martin. Valerie Edmond (One More Kiss) plays Matthew's girlfriend. Tcheky Karyo (Jacques Chevalier, The Patriot) adds a level of sophisticated mystery in his role of a smooth high-level drug dealer.

Director Nigel Cole, a name familiar to English theater and TV audiences, keeps a light hand on this, his first feature film. With a quaint setting, overly simple script, and strong performances, he crafts a film which will leave audiences smiling contentedly.

None of this is meant to legitimize marijuana use (although one of the characters does make a brief pro-legalization statement) and had the film ended differently, perhaps my enjoyment would have been lessened.

A poignant statement made by a supporting character who was counseling Grace was this, "If you have to commit a crime to get what you want, perhaps you were not meant to have it."

God promises in His Word that He will meet all our needs and that he will always provide a way to escape from that which troubles us. But we must apply wisdom to discern whether the solutions being presented to us are godly means to escape or devilish tricks to drive us further into trouble.

God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it. 1 Corinthians 10:13b

A key to remember is that God's way to escape will never include something that contradicts or is contrary to His Word. Getting out of debt is not only a worthy goal, it is God's heart for our lives. But He doesn't want us to achieve that financial freedom by illegal means. Another way to escape the bondage of debt is available which is something that Grace must discover.
