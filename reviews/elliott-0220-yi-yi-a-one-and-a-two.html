Yi Yi: A One and a Two

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 17, 2007
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Matthew 6:34Psalms 68:19Lamentations 3:22-23

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Daily living; the present; fear; renewal; trusting God

Author-verified transcription

It is said that "life is in the details." This can be evidenced in the acclaimed Asian film, Yi Yi, which is currently making its way across the US. This three hour family tale is proof that no matter how foreign one culture might be to the way another culture lives, there is still much that we, as human beings, have in common.

Writer/director Edward Yang (Duli shidai) when asked what his film is about, answers with the all-encompassing response, "It is about life." Using the family dynamic to provide a structure to address issues which we face at different stages, Yang has built a film that speaks to us all.

NJ (Nien-Jen Wu) is a middle aged partner of a successful computer company in Taiwan which, due to complacency, finds itself falling behind in the competitive marketplace. His wife, Min-Min (Elaine Jin), after suffering through a bit of a breakdown, goes off on a spiritual retreat. His brother, Ah-Di (Hsi-Sheng Chen), is getting married although the girl he jilted is none too happy about it.

His 13-year-old daughter, Ting-Ting (Kelly Lee) is embarking on her first romantic relationship with Fatty (Pang Chang Yu) even though he's the beau of Li Li (Adrian Lin), the girl who lives down the hall of their apartment complex. His eight-year old son Yang-Yang (delightfully played by Jonathan Chang) is forever being teased by girls. He begins taking photographs of the backs of heads so he can show people what they can't see for themselves.

On top of these rather mundane events come two momentous occurrences: his mother-in-law suffers a stroke and lies in a coma from which she is not expected to recover; and the woman he left behind some thirty years ago suddenly appears, sparking questions of whether or not he made the right choices in life.

Yang handles each of these stories confidently, blending them together with considerable expertise. As each character takes his or her turn speaking to the comatose matriarch, the protective facades that people build around themselves are torn down and we see into the very simple and real heart of the person speaking.

The film runs a bit long at 172 minutes but there are many moments which more than make up for the exceptional length.

Yi Yi, which is more literally translated as "one one" (meaning "individually"), has a number of layers within it which invite discussion. For me, the film is best summed up by the character of Mr. Ota (Issey Ogata), the Japanese software designer whom NJ must convince to come onboard with his company even though his company isn't sold on his high dollar price tag.

A true innovator, Ota bemoans the industry's pattern of copying that which has been done before. "We never live the same day twice. Every day is a new day," he observes. "So why is it that we are so afraid to do something new?"

As a theme, there is nothing that is earthshaking or revolutionary about this observation. And yet, it is one that touches each one of us individually. Fear of the unknown will often cause us to stagnate within the comfort of what is familiar to us. We must develop the courage to break free of our own "comfort boxes" and experience life as God intended.

As Ota stated, each morning begins with the freshness of a new day. We should embrace the potential that each day brings. Regrets and "what could have been" thinking are defeatist in nature. God encourages us to renew our minds daily. He recognizes that each new day will bring its own set of challenges and obstacles to be overcome.

Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own. Matthew 6:34 (NIV)

We need not be afraid of the future nor do we need to condemn ourselves for the past. God would have us live in the present for it is there that we can be most effective in serving Him and proving His Word to be true as we walk out upon it believing all of His promises, claiming the victories He has given us as we go.
