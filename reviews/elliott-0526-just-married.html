Due to time constraints, I was unable to see this film so I asked my good friend Carter Matthews to share his thoughts. The following is his review. - ME -

If a married couple is supposed to reflect the embodiment of bliss, this film could be viewed as showing the exception to the rule. It's a comedic look at how two young people handle the speed bumps along the road of a newlywed honeymoon.

Tom Leezak (Ashton Kutcher, That 70s Show), a third-shift radio traffic reporter, meets Sarah McNerney (Brittany Murphy, 8 Mile), the daughter of a rich, well-to-do sports owner. They hit it off. Within nine months, the couple is married despite everyone telling them they're too young. Everything looks wonderful as the couple promises to never lie to each other. Unfortunately, both have already withheld truth from each other in one particular area so there isn't much hope for that vow.

Their honeymoon is off to a rocky start as everything that could go wrong does. To make matters worse, the ex-love interest of Sarah shows up to throw sand into an engine already nearly kaput. The rest is predictable.

Just Married is a film for the free of mind. What I mean is that, to enjoy it, you must be free from the use of your mind. This film is very much what it seems to be: a comedic romp of no value. It is intended to be strictly entertainment. And some would question even that. However, in the eyes of this critic, the piece presents what it is meant to present: comedy. Amid the tastelessness of some scenes, the obvious holes in the plot, and the weak, overstated moral - there is an outside probability that you will laugh. At least once.

Shawn Levy (Big Fat Liar) does a decent job of directing this film. There aren't any wonderful shots, no incredible innovations; but nothing in the film sticks out as being horrible, either. He presents the predictable script rather predictably.

Brittany Murphy brings an easy, light quality to her role, almost as though she realizes the simplicity and levity, so to speak, of the piece. Ashton Kutcher does not show us anything new, playing the same character that we've seen him do before. Together, the two lack on screen chemistry. Their dynamic is weak and they almost seem like strangers at times.

The couple vows, even before the wedding, to never lie to one another, trying to build their relationship on trust and honesty. This is a great truth we see in the Word of God.

Lying lips are abomination to the Lord: but they that deal truly are his delight. Proverbs 12:22 (KJV)

We are all members in particular, part of one body (I Corinthians 12.27). To this one body, we are to be especially good. God tells us, in His Word, to not lie to this elect of God.

Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another. Ephesians 4:25 (KJV)

Towards the end of the film, Sarah and Tom realize the importance of perseverance. Within God's household, we share this same need of faithfulness as does a marriage.

And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also. 2 Timothy 2:2 (KJV)
