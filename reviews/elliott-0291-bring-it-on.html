Bring It On

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 27, 2000
Rating
1 star
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Timothy 2:51 Corinthians 9:24-27Ephesians 6:12-13

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Competition; integrity; playing by the rules; spiritual discipline

Author-verified transcription

Universal Studios' newest film, Bring It On, contains any number of perennially perky, peppy, pom-pom prancing preppies pulchritudinously portrayed as pouty post-pubescent privileged princesses. In other words... Cheerleaders.

Not just any cheerleaders. The Toro cheerleading squad of Rancho Carne High School (To understand the level of humor to which we will be subjected, we should note that the high school's name can be translated as "Meat Ranch") are the five time national champions who are about to elect a new captain. This would be the crowning achievement of which Torrence Shipman (Kirsten Dunst, Dick) has long dreamed. That is, whenever she's not having that nasty nightmare of cheering naked in front of the entire school.

After Torrence becomes captain, her fragile cheerleading world starts to crumble. Her sometimes faithful boyfriend leaves for college, she sends a squad member to the hospital with a broken leg, and she discovers that her ex-captain stole every trophy winning cheer from a rival squad. The Clovers come from a less affluent part of town and are now looking for revenge and redemption from the uppity rich kids who gained accolades using their cheers. If that wasn't enough, Torrence starts falling in love with a transfer student who is the antithesis of all her Valley Girl ideals.

Does Torrence have the "right stuff" to be captain of the cheerleading squad? Can the Toros overcome the challenge of the urban squad to gain their sixth championship trophy? With so many airheads on film at one time, does the projector need to be bolted to the floor? All good questions, but good questions do not make a good film.

Ordinarily, any movie centering around a competitive situation will have enough built-in tension to hold interest, but this film is so dependent upon cliché-riddled plot twists and character non-development that interest wanes fairly early on.

This cipher of a script is to competitive cheerleading what Flashdance was to steelworking. It may have something to do with the story, but it isn't represented with any degree of reality.

Coupling a relative newcomer to the director's chair (Peyton Reed) with a first time screenwriter (Jessica Bendinger) does not always add up to freshness. This film is a case in point. It is not helped by the inclusion of an inappropriate amount of sexuality, gross-out humor and forced LA expressions (Examples: one of the straight male cheerleaders lets his fingers wander when lifting female squad members; a nervous cheerleader vomits on her coach; an unpopular choice for a replacement cheerleader is accepted with the phrase, "She's the poo, take a whiff.")

Kirsten Dunst has the fresh faced bubble gum goodness one would expect from a California high school head cheerleader. Standout squad members include Clare Kramer (in her film debut) and Nicole Bilderback (Can't Hardly Wait). The captain of the rival Clovers is played by Gabrielle Union (Love and Basketball). Her squad includes the members of the musical group Blaque.

The film opens painfully with an extended cheer routine which introduces the cast of characters and sets the stage for the long 95 minutes to follow. It ends appropriately enough with the actors performing to Toni Basil's song "Mickey."

Buried in all this "sis-boom-blah" silliness is a shred of truth. When Torrence finds out that all her cheerleading victories were based on a lie, she decides to help the Clovers make it to the nationals so she can compete against them. She explains by saying, "My idea of winning is beating the best." Her definition is what healthy competition should be. It is not about winning at any cost but rather about pitting your abilities against a worthy opponent in a fair contest.

Similarly, if anyone competes as an athlete, he does not receive the victor's crown unless he competes according to the rules. 2 Timothy 2:5 (NIV)

In the spiritual arena, we can prevail when we stay faithful to the rules established by God.
