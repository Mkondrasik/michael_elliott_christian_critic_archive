Twilight

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 6, 1998
Rating
1 1/2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 34 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 8:44Titus 1:2Hebrews 5:14

Sermon / spiritual topic: Truth; Lies; Discernment

Author-verified transcription

There are certain axioms in the business of movie making that we should always try to remember. One important one is this: Actors can only be as good as the script allows them to be. Case in point: The dream cast of Twilight. Every single major role is played by an award winning actor. If we took all the Oscars, Tonys, and Emmys ever won by this cast and melted them down, we would have enough metal to build a replica of the Titanic. Not an altogether inappropriate analogy because even as acclaimed as this cast may be, by signing on with Twilight, they have all set sail aboard a sinking vessel.

Granted, there is still a modicum of enjoyment derived from watching these fine artists ply their craft, but as the old saying goes, "You can't make a silk purse out of a sow's ear." (You can, of course, make some kind of purse but it certainly won't be of the silken variety. No matter how well crafted it might be, it will forever carry that telltale porcine scent.)

There is no doubt that the artists involved in Twilight have indeed made a movie. It just ain't silk. Not that there is anything really wrong with the story line. Paul Newman (The Verdict) plays Harry Ross, a burned out ex-cop/ex-private detective/ex-drunk/ex-husband (talk about the Ex-Files!!) who gets involved in a 20 year old mystery which has resurfaced and now threatens the peaceful and affluent lifestyle of his close friends, the movie stars Jack and Catherine Ames, played by Gene Hackman (Unforgiven) and Susan Sarandon (Dead Man Walking).

It seems Catherine's first husband, Jeff, disappeared under mysterious circumstances. Those now looking into that disappearance are showing up dead. The bad news for Harry is that they usually wait until he's in their presence before they enter into that "dead" state. The good news for Harry is that he still has some friends on the force, most importantly, Stockard Channing (Six Degrees of Seperation) and James Gardner (Maverick) who keep Harry from being dragged into police custody even though those around him have a habit of winding up in the morgue.

Another actress who more than holds her own on the screen with these venerable heavyweights is Margo Martindale (Nobody's Fool), who plays Gloria Lamar, the blackmailer who dredged up this decades old mystery. The sarcastic, quirky style she brings to her character adds a breath of freshness to an otherwise stale script.

One would think that with a workable premise and an extremely talented cast, the end product would be far superior than what we see in Twilight. But films are a collaborative effort. As with any recipe, add the wrong ingredient or an inferior ingredient and the entire soufflé will fall. There are two major ingredients in the making of Twilight that are responsible for its "flatness."

The script, as penned by director Robert Benton (Kramer vs. Kramer) and Richard Russo (Nobody's Fool), is, at best, mediocre. It's not that the actors come off looking bad (they're far too experienced in front of the camera to allow that.), but the script certainly doesn't do them any favors.

The music score by Elmer Bernstein (The Rainmaker) is heavy-handed and melodramatic - more befitting a Mannix TV episode of the 1970's. I suppose it's okay, if you like that kind of thing. I don't.

Nevertheless, even with all the flaws that are present, one may draw spiritual lessons from the film. As with most mysteries, nothing is as it seems. At one point, later in the film, Harry complains, "I'm tired of being lied to." In Harry's world, everyone has something to hide. Everyone lies - including Harry.

The thing to remember is that no lie is of God. In fact, God is incapable of lying (Titus 1:2). It is truth that sets us free. Lies simply weave the proverbial web that will eventually ensnare us. Truth breaks that web - it releases us from the snare. It is the pursuit of the truth that keeps Harry going. The same should be held true for us. Truth is the standard by which we all should be measured. And while it is oftentimes hard to discern truth from error - it is not impossible. As Harry knows, it simply requires perseverance and a willingness to look at every situation with unclouded eyes.

The title of the film obviously refers to the age of the characters and the fact that they are moving into the latter part of their years. Hopefully, there's a lot more twilight left for Mr. Newman and company. They certainly deserve a far more glorious sunset than this movie can muster.
