Author-verified transcription

Love is a simple concept. It’s people that make it complicated. So it seems to 14-year-old Ellis whose romanticized notions are given a harsh dose of reality in the coming of age drama, Mud.

Ellis (Tye Sheridan) and his best friend Neckbone (Jacob Lofland) are the children of “river rats” who manage to scratch out their existence on the shores of the Mississippi. One day they take their skiff out to a small island where they encounter a charismatic drifter (Matthew McConaughey) who goes by the name of Mud.

Mud is in hiding and trying to escape both the law and a vengeful family of bounty hunters. After hearing his tale of true love and a woman wronged, the boys decide to help Ellis with his plan to reunite with his lady love (Reese Witherspoon) and slip through the tightening noose that threatens to close in on him.

The performances are first rate and consistent throughout. McConaughey shines as Mud, providing a multi-layered and honest portrayal. Ray McCinnon and Sarah Paulson give solid support as Ellis’ parents whose own love story may be drawing to a close. But the star of the film is young Tye Sheridan who straddles that awkward time of adolescence with considerable grace and charm.

Love is the theme that is woven through this character study. Ellis is experiencing his first taste of what it feels like as he pursues an older high school girl; Mud has gotten himself into a world of trouble because of his love of a woman who has made some bad choices for herself; Ellis’ parents may be ending their marriage because the love they share is not strong enough to bridge the gulf they have allowed to grow between them.

What Ellis comes to learn is that love cannot be expressed or defined by words alone. Nor is it some ethereal, intangible concept. Love is best defined or understood by the actions we take. Ellis’ romanticized view of love is not wrong. It is just that people often do it poorly.

“Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It does not dishonor others, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres. Love never fails.” 1 Corinthians 13:4-8 (NIV)

It is when our actions reflect the truths expressed above that we are truly able to say that we are acting out of love. We see a number of examples in this film. They occur when the characters stop thinking about what they want and instead take action to address someone else’s need.
