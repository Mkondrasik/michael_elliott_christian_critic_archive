Author-verified transcription

Has there ever been a girl who, at some point in her life, has not dreamed of becoming a princess? The Princess Diaries reminds us that there is another side to the Cinderella story that is not often considered.

The frizzy-haired, bespeckled Mia Thermopolis (Anne Hathaway, Get Real) is an awkward, geeky 15-year-old student whose main goal in life is to remain invisible and make it through high school with as little embarrassment as possible. So far, she has not been all that successful in avoiding embarrassment.

Her life is thrown upside down when her grandmother Clarisse (Julie Andrews, Sound of Music) pays an unexpected visit bearing even more unexpected news. It turns out that Mia is the natural heir to the throne of Genovia, a small pear-producing European country. Mia's first reaction? "Shut... up!"

Of course, being a princess is not all glass slippers and tiaras. There's real work, responsibilities, and for Mia, the horror of being a public figure. Even though she cringes at the thought, she agrees to a royal makeover so she can be presented at Genovia's annual ball, at which time she will announce whether she is going to accept or decline the crown.

Mia goes from geek to gorgeous, and once the news of her regal lineage is leaked to the press, she is invisible no longer. Her new found popularity proves to be just as problematic. Her old friends feel themselves being slighted as they watch Mia being courted by the "in crowd" and the "pretty people."

Anne Hathaway is a delightful find. As beautiful as she is fearless to play against her beauty, her physical brand of comedy brings to mind the clownish antics of a Lucille Ball. We hope to see more from this young lady. Julie Andrews is the picture of grace and elegance. She is quite believable as queen.

Hector Elizondo, who has appeared in every film that Garry Marshall has directed, once again is given a role suited especially for him. Here, he is the trusted Genovian chief of security who takes young Mia under his wing.

I sincerely wish I could be more enthused about the end result of this film. A G-rated live action comedy directed by Garry Marshall (Pretty Women) and featuring some major talent is something of a rarity. The preview trailers showed great promise, building our anticipation for the film's release even more. As it turns out, the promise is unfulfilled as the film plays flat and uninspired. Using caricatures instead of characters and pratfalls instead of plot twists, The Princess Diaries never rises above the mundane quality of its script by Gina Wendkos (Coyote Ugly).

It is a really a pity because this family friendly picture does offer a number of "life lessons" that would be valuable to the pre-teenagers to whom this film is clearly targeted. Here are a few poignant quotes I jotted down from the film:

"No one can make you feel inferior without your consent."

"'Wow' is the power to effect change."

"Courage is not the absence of fear but the knowledge that something is more important than fear."

That last line proves to be a turning point for young Mia, as it would be for any of us if we choose to believe it. There is no sin in fear. We've all experienced it. The sin is in letting fear control our thoughts and actions.

Fear is the number one weapon in our enemy's arsenal and he uses it without mercy. God, through His Word, reminds us that we do not have to settle for a life of fear. We can triumph over fear. One of the most oft repeated exhortations in the Bible, if not the most, is "Fear not" or "Be not afraid." Because it is written in the declarative mode, it can be considered a command, yet even then, it remains our choice whether we will obey or not.

Mia must decide between responsibility towards a country she doesn't even know and her own desires for the life she envisioned for herself. Either choice would be a valid one... if she does the choosing. If she lets fear make the choice for her, it doesn't matter which choice is made, it would still be wrong.
