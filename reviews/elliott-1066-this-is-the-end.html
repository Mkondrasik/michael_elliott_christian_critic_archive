This Is The End

Author-verified transcription

The Apocalypse is no joke. That is, unless, you’re looking at it through the eyes of today’s most popular comedic actors. It may be the end of the world as we know it, but that doesn’t mean that we have to leave it without a smile on our face. Or so says This Is The End.

Showing that they can have a sense of humor about their own personas, Hollywood actors Seth Rogen, James Franco, Jonah Hill, Jay Baruchel, Danny McBride, Craig Robinson, and others play themselves (at least distorted versions of themselves) in a story that begins with the end of the world. A house party at James Franco’s new dwelling becomes the last refuge for those not taken by the rapture. To no one’s surprise, none of the Hollywood A-listers ascended at the trumpet’s call.

Once havoc sets in and the L.A. landscape becomes a devilish inferno, the celebrity “bro-pack” decide to hunker down in Franco’s million-dollar pad to wait out Armageddon. At first, only Jay Baruchel, an L.A. “outsider,” seems to have a handle on the biblical events unfolding before their drug and alcohol afflicted eyes. But as the terrors increase, and the demons walk among them, there is no denying what has transpired. The end times have arrived and America’s People Choice winners have been “Left Behind.”

This is not a call to action film. There’s little to no overt sermonizing. And even fewer examples of Christian doctrine or belief. The picture simply allows Rogen and company a scenario that they can mine for shock comedy. Being who they are, much of the humor is crass or vulgar, relying heavily on the basest elements of humanity – sex, death and fecal matter – to generate the laughs.

All humor aside, the world will, at some point, cease to be. This is a truth that is foretold in the scriptures.

“But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up.” 2 Peter 3:10 (KJV)

Where the film errs greatly is in the depiction of how to escape the wrath of the coming Apocalypse. The movie gives the impression that good works and sacrifice will earn people a place in heaven. This is a belief that cannot be supported by what is recorded in the Bible.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.” Ephesians 2:8-9 (KJV)

There’s only one way to heaven and that is through the accomplishments of Jesus Christ. He died for the sins of man, paying the price for our transgressions, and it is through him and only him that we are given the gift of eternal life.

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.” Romans 10:9 (KJV)

The act of accepting Christ as one’s lord and savior is all that is needed to gain entrance into heaven. No other “works” are required. But… once we accept Christ… we suddenly find that we want to do works that please God because we finally understand that anything else fails to ultimately satisfy our souls.
