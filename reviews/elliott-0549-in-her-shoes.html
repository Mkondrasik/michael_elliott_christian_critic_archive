Author-verified transcription

Is there a bond as strong as the one that exists between sisters? In Her Shoes, a new film by 8 Mile director Curtis Hanson, seems to think not.

Based on the best selling novel by Jennifer Weiner (adapted for the screen by Erin Brockovich scribe Susanna Grant), the film follows the life, death, and rebirth of the relationship between two siblings - Maggie and Rose Feller.

Maggie (Cameron Diaz), a hard drinking party girl whose existence is defined by her good looks and poor morals, is forever mooching upon her friends and relatives. Thrown out of her parents' house after coming home drunk, she turns to her older sister, Rose (Toni Collette) for the support she has come to expect.

Rose, a workaholic lawyer struggling with her weight, is Maggie's polar opposite. Yet despite their differences and the fact that they tend to drive each other crazy, they do consider themselves to be best friends. But there's a limit to the abuses that even best friend sisters can take. When Maggie crosses that line, Rose forces her out of her house to finally fend for herself.

Luckily for Maggie, she has just discovered a grandmother (Shirley MacLaine) she never knew existed. Moving in with Ella is the best thing that Maggie will have ever done. She finally begins to mature and, better yet, find her own personal value. In Maggie's absence, Rose is making some discoveries herself as she begins a romantic relationship with a young lawyer. But even as both sisters grow, they recognize a hole in their heart exists that only a sister can fill.

There's nothing wrong with this film or story that a good cry won't fix... and that's exactly what audiences should expect - especially with a trio of fine actresses doing some of their best work.

Cameron Diaz is very effective as a woman with self-image problems who turns to the only sure-fire way she knows to get attention/approval - sex. She is especially good after she moves to Florida and begins to learn that there is more to life than too much makeup and too few clothes.

Toni Collette also shines as the "ugly duckling" sister with a closet full of shoes and just as much insecurity. Nice work from a reliable actress.

Shirley MacLaine is wonderful as the crotchety grandmother who reenters the lives of her grandchildren after a long forced absence. She brings a lot of layers to what could have been a one-dimensional character.

The directing is assured; the script crackles with wit and humor and there is at least one moment that will have most of the audiences wiping their eyes or reaching for the tissue.

The falling out that occurs between Maggie and Rose is not uncommon among close friends or relatives. Live long enough with someone emotionally close to you and both parties inevitably will do something that hurts or offends the other. The key is getting past the pain in order to be reconciled. That requires practicing the art of forgiveness that Jesus taught his followers:

Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven. Matthew 18:21-22 (KJV)

Easier said than done but the act of forgiveness pays many dividends and the cost of an unforgiving heart is high.
