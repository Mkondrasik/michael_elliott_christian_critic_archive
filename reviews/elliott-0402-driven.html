Driven

Michael Elliott

April 25, 2001

Sermon / spiritual topic: Faith; doubt; focus; being fully persuaded

Author-verified transcription

The theatrical release of films are sometimes benefited by the accidental similarities to news events which might occur in or around the same time. The best example might be what the incident at Three Mile Island did for the box office receipts of The China Syndrome. In contrast, Driven will not benefit from today's headlines.

With so much attention focused upon "safety in racing" spurred by the proliferation of NASCAR deaths, a racing film which seems to relish in the spectacular crashes it can manufacture via its CGI technology seems almost macabre... certainly ill-timed. True, Driven is not about NASCAR, but it is centered around the FedEx Championship Series sanctioned by CART (Championship Auto Racing Teams Inc.). Watching this testosterone-filled crash fest just feels wrong... or at least, hypocritical. It doesn't help that the film has some significant flaws within it.

Sylvester Stallone (Rocky) stars as Joe Tanto, a semi-retired CART racer whose career fizzled after a near fatal accident. He is called back into action by friend and wheelchair-bound car owner Carl Henry (Burt Reynolds, Boogie Nights) who needs a seasoned driver to help keep his rising star Jimmy Bly (Kip Pardue, Remember The Titans) from cracking under the intense pressure associated with the competitive sport.

A rookie, Bly has risen through the ranks to challenge the defending champion, Beau Brandenburg (Til Schweiger, Judas Kiss), but lacks the mental discipline to take his racing game up to the next level. Enter Tanto.

With all due respect, whoever penned the media production notes for this film should have scripted or directed the movie. I enjoyed the notes. There's apparently a lot more substantive material that could have been seen then what the film itself provided. Too much emphasis on disintegrating cars, fiery crashes, and spectacular stunts was made at the expense of developing interesting characters and their relationships.

Even the male/female pairings which pepper the screenplay ring superficial and false. Estella Warren (Tangled) plays Sophia who, after being dumped by Beau because he considers her a "distraction," hooks up with Jimmy. Cathy (Gina Gershon, Bound) is Tanto's shrewish ex-wife, now married to one of his teammates. Luc (Stacy Edwards, In The Company of Men) steps in as Tanto's new love interest, a journalist covering the racing circuit. Unfortunately, the only "sparks" being generated on screen are probably hidden under the hoods of the turbocharged machines.

The relationship which showed the most potential was the one existing between Jimmy and his overbearing brother/promoter, DeMille (Robert Sean Leonard, The Dead Poets Society) but it too eventually jumped onboard the oft ridden cliché train to become hackneyed and dull.

Stallone, Reynolds, and director Renny Harlin (Cliffhanger) all could use a hit to help boost their career marketability. I'm sorry to say that Driven won't be the vehicle to take them there.

The one thing we can agree upon is the degree of concentration and focus needed to drive one of these machines around a track at speeds reaching 240 miles per hour. There is no room for anything less than total commitment to the task at hand. A momentary mental lapse could quickly lead to disaster.

In the race of life, being fully persuaded in one's pursuit of a goal is a fundamental element of success. Entertaining doubts or succumbing to fear will block us from accomplishing that which we most desire. To be fully persuaded requires us to drive from our minds any thoughts of failure or inadequacy.

But when he asks, he must believe and not doubt, because he who doubts is like a wave of the sea, blown and tossed by the wind. James 1:6 (NIV)

Being tossed about when traveling 240 mph would certainly lead to physical calamity. Spiritually, the same logic holds true. A fully persuaded mindset is needed to bring forth the best results.
