Author-verified transcription

It is rewarding to see a forgotten soldier finally get his due - cinematically speaking of course. The Guardian revisits familiar ground - the rigorous training of a new class of military recruits - but with a new twist. Instead of the Navy, Marine, Army or Air Force installations which are so familiar to us from so many films, we get a glimpse of the training required to be a rescue swimmer for the often sadly overlooked branch of the armed forces - the U.S. Coast Guard.

Kevin Costner stars as Ben Randall, a veteran Coast Guard rescue swimmer who has become almost legendary among the ranks of those who aspire to risk their lives to save people in distress. When a mission claims the lives of the rest of his crew, Ben reluctantly accepts an offer to stay out of the water for a while. He agrees to teach the latest crop of recruits at a Coast Guard "A" (or specialty) school.

One of those recruits is Jake Fischer (Ashton Kutcher), a phenomenal swimmer with a past that haunts him - one which may keep him from being all that he can be. It will be up to Randall to exorcise the demons of his past so he might help Fischer to do the same.

Director Andrew Davis and screenwriter Ron L . Brinkerhoff never stray far from the formulaic "boot camp" films that are so familiar to us. A tough as nails "drill sergeant," green recruits, troubled kid with a chip on his shoulder, local girl to serve as a romantic distraction (Melissa Sagemiller)... We've seen it al before, but that doesn't necessarily mean we mind seeing it again.

Costner, though probably too old for the role, is believable as an aging rescue swimmer who doesn't want to hang up his fins. His Midwest, Iowa-corn persona makes him vulnerable and likable even though there are elements of his character that are less than desirable. For example, Randall's wife (Sela Ward) is feeling neglected and unloved due to his unwavering devotion to his job. Divorce quickly becomes another storm brewing on his horizon.

Kutcher, who is also a likable personality, finally finds a film role that might help launch him into the upper echelon of Hollywood stars. He's everything a lead should be - able to command the screen but generous enough to share it with his co-stars and give them their moments in the spotlight as well. This movie was a smart choice for the young actor and one that may lead to more prominent opportunities.

Melissa Sagemiller is attractive and sexy in the Debra Winger/Kelly McGinnis Officer and a Gentleman/Top Gun role. Neal McDonough plays the by-the-book Coastie trainer who must bow to Randall's unconventional ways.

The rescue scenes are harrowing with high seas, panicking victims, and life-or-death situations. This is what the Coast Guard training is all about. As one failing recruit is told, "you have to find a way to stay calm in the midst of chaos."

It is advice that would benefit us all - even those of us who don't have to deal with 20 foot waves and 40 mile an hour winds. The world in which we live is not a peaceful place. Chaos and calamity continually surround us and threaten to envelop us. Pressures that seem insurmountable threaten to crush us. But it is possible to find a way to stay calm.
