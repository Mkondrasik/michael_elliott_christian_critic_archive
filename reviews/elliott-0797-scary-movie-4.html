Scary Movie 4 was not screened for critics (at least not this critic) prior to its release. Was that the reason for its unexpected $40 million dollar opening weekend box office? Who knows? It certainly wasn't due to any kind of outstanding quality that might have appeared on the screen.

I admit to being befuddled at the success of the Scary Movie franchise. No less than 4 films have been made (and after this past weekend, numbers 5, 6 and 7 are certainly foregone conclusions) and none of them remotely resemble the work of intelligent wit or talented filmmaking.

In this fourth version, even its title is misleading. The Scary Movie franchise has moved away from spoofing the horror franchise exclusively to instead take on all of Hollywood. In addition to The Grudge and Saw (or was it Saw II?) which are the legitimate genre spoofs, Scary Movie 4 also lampoons War of the Worlds (sci-fi), The Village (suspense), Brokeback Mountain (homosexual western), Million Dollar Baby (sports drama), and Oprah (daytime talk show). Perhaps, next time, SM 5 will take on animated children's films and National Geographic documentaries. Stay tuned for Death March of the Penguins.

The humor used closely follows the formula established by its predecessors. Lots of sexual innuendos, bathroom references and comic violence carry the film. Why audiences think this is uproarious is beyond me. But they must. Somebody paid enough ticket prices to add up to a $40 million dollar pay day for The Weinstein Company who produced it.

Anna Faris, as the central character Cindy Campbell, continues to run with her female Buster Keaton impression: unflappable, partly clueless but determined to last until the last frame of the picture. She does generate some laughs but not enough to carry the picture. Unfortunately the work of director David Zucker and screenwriters Jim Abrahams and Craig Mazin sink anything that Faris might have been able to keep afloat.

Craig Bierko, in the Tom Cruise War of the Worlds role, skates by but, in the end, is as unmemorable the guy who does the Michael Jackson impression that has been seen in the last two films.

Of course, Scary Movie 4 is heavily populated with star cameos: Charlie Sheen as a troubled man overdosed on Viagra; Carmen Electra as the blind girl in The Village; Dr. Phil and Shaquille O'Neal as the shackled victims in Saw; Cloris Leachman as the mute victim in The Grudge; Bill Pullman as the leader of The Village; and Leslie Nielsen as the President of the US. I hope these well known actors were compensated well for their appearances because I seriously doubt this film will do much for their reputations.

All this leads us to a reflection on humor and laughter. Is "anything for a laugh" a godly attitude? Should there be a higher standard to which art should be held up against?

It is better to hear the rebuke of the wise, than for a man to hear the song of fools. For as the crackling of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of the fool: this also is vanity. Ecclesiastes 7:5-6 (KJV)

Scary Movie 4 does nothing to edify the spirit. Even the laughter that it may wrestle from us is without mirth or joy.
