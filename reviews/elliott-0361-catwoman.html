Catwoman

1½ stars

1 hr: 44 min

Author-verified transcription

Somehow I don't think Carl Sandburg was envisioning this movie when he penned, "The fog comes, on little cat feet." Because if he was, the fog he wrote about must have blown in like a tornado.

The highly stylized, fast-paced Catwoman makes no apologies for being ridiculously laughable. It blithely steams ahead believing that it will get by on a combination of Spider-man-like CGI effects and one killer costume.

Patience Philips (Halle Berry) is a mousey art school graduate working in the advertising department of a cosmetics company.

When she accidentally uncovers a secret that, if exposed, could ruin the firm, she is flushed down a gigantic sewage pipe and killed.

On the scene is a centuries old Egyptian cat named Midnight who brings Patience back to life and in so doing, instills within her a number of feline powers. Where Patience was once timid and slightly uncoordinated, as Catwoman she is bold and brazen with razor sharp reflexes.

Fiercely independent, Catwoman prowls the night and, like Spider-man, is perceived to be up to no good. Detective Tom Lone (Benjamin Bratt) is on the case to track her down but finds his attention being divided as he starts to become smitten with Patience.

After a while she begins to remember that someone killed her and she should probably try to find out who it was. Her "investigation" leads her to the CEO of the cosmetic firm (Lambert Wilson) and his aging model wife (Sharon Stone).

It is impossible to take this movie seriously. Directed by the French visual artist Pitof, the film has a consistent and identifiable artistic style but layered on top of the background visuals are performances that can only be described as unintentionally funny.

Give credit to Halle Berry for taking the risk. She gamely plays a woman who uncontrollably begins to exhibit the mannerisms and characteristics of a cat. She struts on top of her furniture, downs tuna fish by the case, and loses control in the presence of catnip. A more subtle approach might have served the project and her career better but it would have also lessened the "campiness" factor that just might be the film's saving grace.

Benjamin Bratt doesn't co-star as much as he graces the film with an easy-going manner that is long on charm and short on characterization. Sharon Stone could have gotten more mileage out of her role as the aging model with a rock hard face and a heart to match. Alex Borstein as Patience's loud and obtrusive friend was fine in the role but the role was not needed in the film.

I found the CGI effects to be a bit too much. Using the same technology as that web- slinging movie, it is fascinating to see how well they could match (in skin tone and texture) the CGI Catwoman with the live actress. But the speed and agility which the CGI character demonstrated was hardly realistic. To cattily paraphrase from the Superman tagline - you won't believe a woman can purr!

The conflicting duality within Patience (cat/woman) continues until she learns to embrace both. She is told that "only by accepting all of who you are can you be free."

We too are multi-dimensional creations. Man was formed, made, and created by God as a three-fold individual: body, soul, and spirit. As we learn how to function with all three aspects of our lives operating in concert, we will grow to enjoy a freedom and deliverance that we can now scarcely imagine.
