Author-verified transcription

When Trey Parker and Matt Stone released their South Park movie, as much as I disliked the film on spiritual grounds, I could and did recognize the cleverness behind it. Their newest film, Team America: World Police, has all of the offensive qualities of the South Park film with a fraction of the cleverness.

Parker and Stone go from the overly simplistic animation of Cartman and his chubby friends to the difficult and detailed art of puppetry. Using the Thunderbird-like 2 foot tall marionettes, they concoct an outrageous and politically incorrect story of a band of Thunderbird-like American do-gooders who patrol the world looking for terrorists to kill.

The film opens in France where Team America is doing battle with a small band of Middle Eastern terrorists who possess weapons of mass destruction. We know they're Middle Eastern terrorists because their dialogue consists primarily of words like "dirka, dirka" and "jihad." Team America is victorious of course, although much of Paris now lies in ashes thanks to the forcefulness of their first strike capability.

That seems to be a running gag in the film. Whenever Team America comes to the rescue, they end up destroying much of what they came to save.

Other targets that Parker/Stone have zeroed in on include loudmouth liberal actors who cluelessly get involved in political issues (Alec Baldwin, Sean Penn, Tim Robbins, Susan Sarandon, Matt Damon all get satirized); patriotic country western songs are exaggerated to cloying and annoying levels, and the entire structure of the film is designed to be a spoof of the Jerry Bruckheimer style of big-budget, high explosive, adventure films.

Parker and Stone have publicly stated that their film is apolitical and I believe them. I may have had a bit more respect for them and their film if they had a political point to make. They don't. Their sole point seems to be, as it was in the South Park movie, to rub their so-called cleverness in the face of the MPAA ratings board.

Rub it they do. These anatomically incorrect puppets engage in non-stop profanity, simulated sexual activity, and graphic violence. The question which remains is not whether the film is funny or good... (an answer which will be dependent upon one's morality and good taste) but rather is it appropriate for this time? It is difficult to imagine the insensitivity involved in a project that has a terrorist-themed movie where characters, puppets though they be, are bloodily beheaded.

Parker and Stone, after so cavalierly thumbing their noses at those who find profanity unbecoming and unworthy of a sophisticated society, prove that they haven't matured much in five years. What's more, their irresponsibility in the way they utilize the free speech they have the privilege to claim is shameful.

Buried within the juvenile humor are a number of valid observations worthy of consideration. Among them is the idea of Team America unilaterally entering foreign countries with guns blazing in response to a terrorist threat with absolutely no concern of the collateral damage being done to the region they supposedly are there to liberate.

For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? Mark 8:36 (KJV)

What makes America great is not our military strength or weapons superiority. It is the values which we hold dear; the values upon which our country has been built. Those values are missing from this film. We must not allow them to go missing from our land.
