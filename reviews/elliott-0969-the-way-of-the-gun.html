Author-verified transcription

Given two roads to take, it is pretty much a certainty that someone will choose the wrong one. The Way of the Gun follows two anti-heroes on their chosen path which they hope will lead to riches and glory. Needless to say, the odds are against it.

The anti-heroes go by the names of Longbaugh (Benicio Del Toro, Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas) and Parker (Ryan Phillippe, Cruel Intentions.) Stepping out of a perceived "natural order," which they figure held only two options for them, petty crime or minimum wage, they chose instead to blaze their own trail through the wilderness called life. This equates to their selling blood and other bodily fluids until a big score comes along.

That score finally arrives in the form of a pregnant surrogate mother. A young woman named Robin (Juliette Lewis, The Other Sister) is expecting to deliver a million dollar baby on behalf of an older man and his trophy wife. The million dollars is what they have promised her for carrying their fetus to term. Parker and Longbaugh figure if a couple would pay one million to bring a baby into the world, they'd pay even more to get it back. So they kidnap the surrogate mother along with the fetus she's carrying.

What follows is a series of shoot-outs which rack up a considerable body count, using enough ammunition to arm a small country, until we reach the climatic Mexican standoff with the bags of money lying in the center of the square, gunmen positioned all around, and our two anti-heroes drinking whiskey at an adjoining bar while contemplating the irony of life.

Mr. Phillippe and Mr. Del Toro are well cast as the self-proclaimed outcasts of society. The philosophy their characters spout may be sheer foolishness, but they spout it well.

Ms. Lewis plays the suffering surrogate with an uncomfortable reality, waddling around in pre-labor distress, bearing more than the child-to-be. Dylan Kussman (Wild Hearts Can't Be Broken) plays a doctor with a clouded past who is thrust into the middle of this deadly fracas.

James Caan (Mickey Blue Eyes) ably provides a supporting role as Sarno, a battle scarred "bag man" who tries to keep the situation under control. Taye Diggs (The Best Man) and Nicky Katt (Boiler Room) also give believable performances as a pair of professional bodyguards who let their charge be taken from them by a pair of amateur thugs.

Screenwriter Christopher McQuarrie (The Usual Suspects) directs his own script which is filled with as many implausible plot twists and turns to qualify it as a ride in a surreal theme park. It is also abundantly filled with vulgarities and bloody images of violence which more than justify its "R" rating.

The action sequences are tense and nicely paced and there are many scenes which show a distinct cleverness. The car chase following the abduction of Robin is a most unique and unexpected sequence, a genuine surprise in an oft overworked scenario.

For Parker and Longbaugh, the way they have chosen is a way of death. Ultimately this is the choice we all are called to make... The way of life, or the way of death.

Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it. Matthew 7:13-14 (KJV)

Though few may truly find it, the "stait gate" and "narrow way" do exist. Finding and choosing that way will place us upon the spiritual road leading to eternal life, peace, and joy. With all of its noise and fireworks, "the way of the gun" remains a poor substitute for the way that is Jesus Christ.

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. John 4:16 (KJV)
