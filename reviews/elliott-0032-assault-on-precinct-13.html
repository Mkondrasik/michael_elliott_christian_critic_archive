Assault on Precinct 13

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 19, 2005
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ezekiel 34:8 1 Timothy 5:17 Isaiah 11:6

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Leadership; service; compassion; responsibility; shepherding

Author-verified transcription

“The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the kid...” and the cop and the crook shall come together as one. This addition to the scripture comes courtesy of Assault on Precinct 13, the new cop action/drama now playing in theaters.

Jake Roenick (Ethan Hawke), a troubled sergeant, is dealing with the emotional aftermath of having members of his last command killed during a bust gone bad. Armed with booze and pills, he plans on spending a quiet New Year’s Eve relatively alone in an all but abandoned police station.

A snowstorm changes those plans in a hurry when a police van is forced to deposit its prisoners at his facility until the weather clears. One of those prisoners is the nefarious Marion Bishop (Laurence Fishburne), a cop-killer and criminal mastermind. His presence at the stationhouse attracts a squad of heavily armed men to descend upon the understaffed precinct which forces the few cops that are there to release their newly arrived prisoners so they might help defend the “fort” against the invaders.

If the plot sounds familiar, it’s because it is. This film is a remake of John Carpenter’s 1976 film which was, in turn, an updated version of Howard Hawk’s 1959 Rio Bravo. Not that it matters. The story is of only passing concern in this shoot-’em-up tale. Like a video game, this kind of film is all about the body count as the bullets start flying and the victims start piling up.

Peopled by stereotypes instead of fully drawn characters, the cast includes Maria Bello as a sexy psychiatrist who’s been treating the sergeant, Brian Dennehy as the retiring veteran, Drea de Matteo as a sexy secretary attracted to the “bad boys” who sit on the other side of the bars, Ja Rule as an insolent street hood, John Leguizamo as a mouthy junkie, and Gabriel Byrne as the leader of the assault team.

The cast is actually better than the material and as a result the movie reaches the level of effective B-level entertainment. Fishburne’s quiet authoritative manner contrasts well against Hawke’s performance as the psychologically damaged cop.

As should be expected, the level of violence and profanity is quite high and audiences should expect to see and hear plenty of both.

At the same time, we can also contemplate on what the scriptures say about leadership and its responsibilities. Because of the fatal consequences of his last command decision, the last thing Jake wants is the responsibility of having people look to him for guidance and leadership in a life or death situation. And yet, that’s the role which the group so desperately needed filled. Without a leader to direct their actions, they are as good as dead.

As I live, saith the Lord GOD, surely because my flock became a prey, and my flock became meat to every beast of the field, because there was no shepherd, neither did my shepherds search for my flock, but the shepherds fed themselves, and fed not my flock; Ezekiel 34:8 (KJV)

True leadership is not about ego or prestige or power. It is about service. It is about compassion. It is about recognizing a need in others and expending an effort to meet that need. Jake understandably did not want the burden of command but had he not assumed it, everyone within the precinct house would have been slaughtered.

The scriptures tell us that a leader who selflessly serves others is worthy of double honor. We would be blessed to follow such a leader... or better yet... aspire to become one.
