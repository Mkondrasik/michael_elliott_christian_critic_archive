Nacho Libre

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 16, 2006
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Philippians 2:41 Corinthians 10:241 Corinthians 10:33

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Motivation

Author-verified transcription

Heroes come in all shape and sizes... some of them pudgier than others. In Nacho Libre, Jack Black stars as the dough-boy framed savior of a Mexican orphanage.

A lowly monk/cook with no budget to buy decent supplies or provisions, Brother Ignacio (Black) dreams of one day receiving the kind of respect offered to the Mexican luchadores. They are the masked and costumed professional wrestlers who are treated like rock stars by fans of the sport. With that kind of respect he can afford to buy the supplies needed to make the orphans a decent meal.

After having yet another donated bag of nacho chips stolen by a wiry mugger (Hector Jimenez), Ignacio has the vision to team up with his assailant to form the greatest wrestling tag team that Mexico has ever known. The partnership of Nacho Libre (which I suppose means "free nachos") and Esquelito (which I know means "the skeleton") was born.

Can Ignacio keep his secret passion and alter ego a secret from his judgmental brother monks? Can Nacho Libre and Esquelito really win against more seasoned and better conditioned athletes? Will Ignacio win the heart of the beautiful nun (Ana de la Reguera)? (And what exactly does he expect to do with it once he has?) Most importantly how does he ever expect to fit into the stretchy-pants of the luchadore?

Director Jared Hess (Napoleon Dynamite) answers some of these questions and more in this silly and admittedly superficial film.

Most of the humor comes from Jack Black who carries the movie upon his broad but somewhat fleshy shoulders. His total commitment to his character - poor Mexican accent and all — is what wrests the laughs from us... but it is an uphill struggle. The material just isn't rich enough for Black to mine with consistent success.

Jimenez is the only other actor given the opportunity to generate laughs and he does so through deadpan reactions to his circumstances. The rest of the cast, including Ana de la Reguera, as the forbidden object of Ignacio's desire, are there only to react to Black's antics.

The script, co-written by Napoleon Dynamite's Jared and Jerusha Hess and School of Rock's Mike White, miss more than one opportunity to develop their story into something more. As shown, it appears to fall into the category of an extended skit than it is a fully realized film. Had they taken the opportunity to make more of the available social commentary and mix it in with the rudimentary humor, Nacho Libre could have been something rather special - like Napoleon Dynamite was (though even it had its share of detractors). Instead, with Nacho Libre, we are given 100 minutes of forgettable slapstick humor without a consistent heartbeat.

But the filmmakers do provide a lesson in the meaning of success. Ignacio began his wrestling quest in order to help others. Along the way, his ego took center stage. It was only until he was able to take himself out of the equation by focusing on his needs rather than his wants, that success truly began to come his way.

It is a spiritual reality that continues to hold sway in our day: Whenever we reach out to others without regard to our own selfish wants, we position ourselves to receive help from a source of power greater than anything that may stand against us.

Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others. Philippians 2:4 (KJV)
