Rock Star

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 7, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 John 2:16-17Galatians 5:16-23Matthew 6:19-21

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Worldliness; temptation; dreams; priorities; eternal values

Author-verified transcription

Sex. Drugs. Rock 'n Roll. Yep, all present and accounted for in Warner Brothers' modern morality tale Rock Star starring Mark Wahlberg (Planet of the Apes). The moral of the story? Having a fantasy is nowhere near the same as "living out" that fantasy.

Chris Coles (Wahlberg) is a copy machine repairman by day, rock star wannabe by night. Lead singer of Blood Pollution, a tribute band which pays homage to the rock 'n roll superband, Steel Dragon, he has copied every move and vocal intonation of his famous counterpart, Bobby Beers (Jason Flemyng, The Red Violin). In fact, he is so insistent upon copying Steel Dragon's style that he is driving the rest of his band up the wall.

Ironically, Bobby is doing the same thing to his band. Even as Chris is being thrown out of the garage band he helped start, Bobby is being summarily tossed out of the musical money machine known to the world as Steel Dragon. And guess who they turn to for a replacement?

With girlfriend/manager Emily (Jennifer Aniston, Friends) at his side, Chris is rechristened Izzy and made the front man of the band he once worshipped. Izzy soon learns that there is more to the life of a rock star than the music (see the opening sentence of this review).

The temptations come fast and furious and threaten everything that Chris once held dear... including his love for the music of Steel Dragon.

The screenplay by John Stockwell, who directed this year's teen melodrama Crazy/Beautiful, is a bit too pat or cliché-ridden to warrant high marks, but it does provide a framework in which a parable of sorts can be told.

Inspired by the story of salesman Tim "Ripper" Owens, the salesman turned lead singer of Judas Priest, the film features Mark Wahlberg as a working man whose dreams come true... much to his ultimate dismay. Wahlberg is fine in this role and at times even delightful. When asked to adopt a raunchy rock 'n roll attitude in his first photo shoot as a Steel Dragon, he can't manage to suppress a silly grin because he is so intoxicated with what is happening. Likewise, when he learns the truth about his "god" Bobby Beers, he expressively shows the impact of every surprising discovery.

Jennifer Aniston is fine in the thankless role of the girlfriend who gets lost in the shuffle. She shows no great depth, but manages to convey the proper emotions and conflicts which her character faces.

Timothy Spall (Chicken Run) does some fine work as Max, the overworked road manager who has seen it all and lived to tell about it.

Stephen Herek (Mr. Holland's Opus) directs Rock Star, which is deservedly rated R for its scenes of rock star debauchery. As depicted in the film, there is no wonder that Chris grows disillusioned with the scene. It's a hollow, self-destructive existence based on the perverted philosophy of living fast/dying young.

The musical score and concert scenes may be the best things about the film, capturing as it does the 1980's heavy metal sound and surroundings with great accuracy. Anyone who has played air guitar at some point in his/her life may find something in this film that will pluck a heartstring or two.

And therein lies the lesson for us all. We all have fantasies. We all have dreams and aspirations. What we need to consider is the question: Upon what are those fantasies, dreams, and aspirations based?

Chris/Izzy learns that there is some truth to the cliché of being "careful for what we wish because we just might get it." God encourages us to have dreams, aspirations, and goals. However, they are to be rewarding, not only for the moment, but for all eternity.

For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world. And the world passeth away, and the lust thereof: but he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever. John 2:16-17 (KJV)
