Author-verified transcription

Ten films. Ten hours. Ten commandments. The Decalogue is more than a movie. It is a cinematic treat and a rarity among films. It's a treatise on the condition of man and an exploration into his soul and psyche made by an acclaimed filmmaker.

Written and directed by Krzysztof Kieslowski (Red, White & Blue trilogy), The Decalogue takes the ten commandments which were given to Moses from God and builds a story around each one. Some are obviously related, others less so, but each story is compelling, moralistic, and wonderfully rendered.

Decalogue One: "I am the Lord thy God, thou shalt have no other gods before me":

A university professor trusts implicitly in his almighty computer and won't let his young son try out his new ice skates until the thickness of the ice has been calculated by his infallible machine.

Decalogue Two: "Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain."

A pregnant woman approaches her doctor with an unusual request. Her husband lies dying in the hospital. She is pregnant by another man. Her request presents the doctor with a moral dilemma.

Decalogue Three: "Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy."

A married man is coerced by an ex-lover to leave his family on Christmas Eve to help search for the woman's new boyfriend.

Decalogue Four: "Honor thy father and mother."

A young woman discovers that the man who raised her is, in fact, not her biological father.

Decalogue Five: "Thou shalt not kill."

After a troubled youth murders a cab driver, a young lawyer fights to keep him from receiving the death penalty.

Decalogue Six: "Thou shalt not commit adultery."

A young postal clerk obsesses over a sexually active woman who lives across from him. Actually meeting her will change both their lives.

Decalogue Seven: "Thou shalt not steal."

A young woman allows her mother to raise her daughter as her own. Now that the child is 6 years old, she wants to experience the maternal love that should be hers. She chooses to take the child and run away from home.

Decalogue Eight: "Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbor."

An ethics professor faces the decisions of her past when a Jewish woman visits her class and reveals herself to be the girl who the professor once refused to hide from the Nazis during the war.

Decalogue Nine: "Thou shalt not covet thy neighbor's wife."

An impotent man gives his beautiful wife permission to seek other sexual partners and then has difficulty living with the possibility that she may have done so.

Decalogue Ten: "Thou shalt not covet thy neighbor's goods."

A pair of brothers inherit their deceased father's valuable stamp collection and then go through a comedy of errors trying to complete a missing series.

I would not suggest trying to watch all ten films at one sitting for that would defeat one of the best byproducts of this series. Each film is sure to prompt discussion and debate among those who view it as they consider the ethical and moral implications of each story in light of the corresponding commandment. The after film discussions that will surely take place is and should be a part of every Decalogue experience.

While each event depicted is monumental in the life of the central characters of the story, the characters themselves are nothing extraordinary. Set around and within the same non-descript apartment complex, each window suggesting that another story dwells just behind it, Kieslowski and his fellow screenwriter Krzysztof Piesiewicz capture the average man (and woman) struggling to make sense out of life as they agonize over decisions that will shape the course of their own.

Selecting a favorite among the ten is a moot exercise. All ten are the work of a master filmmaker, each one being made with the collaboration of a different cinematographer.

Seeing the difficulty these characters have in navigating their way around one commandment, let alone ten, perhaps we should express our thanks that Jesus managed to condense the ten laws into two:

And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment. And the second is like, namely this, Thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself. There is none other commandment greater than these. Mark 12:30-31 (KJV)

In the past, it has been difficult to see The Decalogue. Originally shown on Polish TV, its length makes theatrical screenings uneconomical. A video box set was released in 2001 after which it fell into a distribution limbo which made obtaining a copy near impossible.

Facets Multi-Media has picked up the gauntlet and is releasing a special edition DVD of all ten films which will include an introduction by film critic Roger Ebert, an interview with Kieslowski, and commentary by the late director's collaborators and colleagues. It will also include a booklet packed with information and interviews concerning the director and his work.

The release date for the DVD is August 19, 2003. For more information or to purchase a copy of the 3-disc DVD set or the 5-tape VHS set, visit the official Facets website.
