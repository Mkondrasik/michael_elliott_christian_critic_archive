Author-verified transcription

If A Guy Thing really is a "guy" thing, then it may be time to start shopping around for a new gender. Nothing this stupid can survive for long.

Jason Lee (Stealing Harvard) stars as Paul Morse, a nice guy engaged to be married to the attractive, levelheaded Karen (Selma Blair, The Sweetest Thing). Following his raucous bachelor party, he wakes up with a hangover and a blonde in his bed k (Julia Stiles, The Business of Strangers). "It happens," he is told by those in whom he confides. "Don't worry about it. It's a 'guy thing'." He decides to forget the incident and the blonde. Fat chance. She seems to be everywhere. To his horror, he discovers that the blonde, Becky, is the cousin of his bride-to-be. So naturally, he fakes a four hour bout of diarrhea.. Oh, wait. I'm getting ahead of myself. We all know how this movie will end. Our enjoyment should come from watching Paul's antics as he tries to keep his little secret from reaching his fiancee. But those antics are so moronic (case in point, Paul's gastronomically-based deception), so poorly conceived and lamely executed that our laughter is more incredulous than amused.

Is it that the "dumbing down" of America has been so successful that these filmmakers believe they have to cater to an intelligence that no longer registers on any measurable scale? Whatever the case, if you can read this review, you're more than likely too smart for this film.

The story comes from Greg Glienna, the same man who brought us Meet the Parents. The slapstick style humor found in that film is recycled here but to a far lesser effect. Perhaps part of the reason is that director Chris Koch (Snow Day) never gets a handle on the material. Another reason may be the four screenwriters (all guys) who are credited with the most spurious attack on guy-hood ever to reach the screen. It would serve them right to be thrown out of the guy union on grounds of treason.

Jason Lee is a rather bland leading man and fails to really connect with either of his two female costars. There is so little chemistry to be seen that it is hard to believe that there's a potential couple among the three of them.

By continuing to accept roles like these, Selma Blair runs the risk of being forever typecast as the "smart girl who doesn't get the guy." I would like to think she's capable of more. As for Julia Stiles, I'm willing to consider this film an aberration caused by a momentary lapse in judgment. She's already proven herself far too intelligent an actress to be wasting her time with mindless drivel such as this.

Even the offhanded moral of the story seems to come from left field. Paul decides that it is better to go through life feeling scared. That settling for a relationship that is safe and comfortable is somehow not as desirable as the adrenaline rush of fear. I don't know what Bible the screenwriters read but I know what mine says:

There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love. 1 John 4:18 (KJV)

Whatever choice we make, whether it is choosing a mate, choosing a profession, or choosing a direction in life, to choose something because it scares us makes as much sense as... A Guy Thing.
