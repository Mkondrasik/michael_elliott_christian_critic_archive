The Wedding Singer

Sermon / spiritual topic: Marriage; Faithfulness; Selflessness

Author-verified transcription

Some movies have a funny way of surprising you. More often than not, that surprise comes in the form of a disappointment. You enter the theater with high expectations, only to find that the movie doesn’t measure up to them. Rarely does it ever happen in reverse. Movies that you expect to be bad are generally worse than you ever imagined.

I did not expect The Wedding Singer to be an exception to this rule. Not being a fan of either Adam Sandler (Happy Gilmore) or Drew Barrymore (Scream), it was with a great deal of skepticism that I approached a movie where they share star billing. However, at the end of the film, I walked away with one of those warm fuzzy feelings that tend to linger long after experiencing a particularly sweet moment. The Wedding Singer, I have to admit, is a particularly sweet moment.

Mr. Sandler plays Robbie Hart, a man who makes his meager living by singing at bar mitzvahs, anniversaries and other such red-lettered events. But it is at weddings where he really shines. After he is left standing at the altar, jilted by his own fiancee, his heart isn’t in it anymore. Try as he might, he just can’t sing for the happiness of others while he is so miserable. And as we soon learn, there are few things worse than a wedding singer who hates weddings. Leave it to Julia Sullivan (Miss Barrymore) to break him out of his doldrums. The problem: She’s engaged to be married to someone else... a someone who happens to be a first class jerk.

Mr. Sandler underplays the title character (there’s the first pleasant surprise), allowing us to actually like the guy. We want him to get the girl... especially this girl. Miss Barrymore couldn’t be more adorable as Julia (that was the second pleasant surprise), the waitress that captures Robbie’s heart and brings him back from the brink of despair. Her smile would light up a dozen discos.

Director Frank Coraci (The Waterboy) demonstrates that he has a firm grasp on the genre as well as the time period (1985) in which the movie takes place. Perhaps much of the humor comes from our recognition of the things that go on at the wedding receptions where we see Robbie practicing his trade.

We’ve lived through these events. We’ve all seen the hideous bridesmaids’ gowns and the ridiculous pastel tuxedos. Some of us may still have photos hidden around the house that can prove our close familiarity with these garments. We can remember the best men who, after too many champagnes, start to make inappropriate comments during the toast. We’re even related (distantly, of course) to the guy who lays claim to the buffet table, making it his personal domain and ignoring everything else around him. Oh yes, Mr. Coraci is on familiar territory.

He also wisely allows the romantic interest between Robbie and Julia to build gradually. Love doesn’t happen overnight... or at first glance. Here, it is more like watching a pot of water on a low fire. Regardless of what the cliché says, we all know it is going to boil... eventually. We just have to wait a while for things to warm up. In the meantime, we get to know the characters and appreciate them for the good-hearted souls that they are.

Robbie and Julia agree, it’s the little things that matter. Things like Robbie helping a young lad who sneaked too much champagne; Julia dancing with an overweight boy who was being shunned by his peers; giving up the window seat on an airplane even if you would rather not. All of these are small things, but they tell a much bigger story about the person who performs them. The scriptures say:

“He that is faithful in that which is least, is faithful also in much.” [Luke 16:10 KJV]

As we see very quickly, and as Julia and Robbie come to realize later, the ones to whom they are engaged at the beginning of the film do not have the right mindset to make a lasting relationship work. As I tell my own wife, “If I focus on your needs, and you focus on mine, neither of us are selfish and both of us get blessed.” When you are married to the right person, it is a wonderful thing. It is nice to be reminded of that.
