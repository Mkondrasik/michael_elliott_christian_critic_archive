Author-verified transcription

Miramax has been sharply criticized by cinephiles for delaying the release and American distribution of Shaolin Soccer, a popular Chinese martial arts comedy. The original Chinese version of the film has been on DVD for a while now. Miramax, having reedited the movie for American audiences (eliminating some 20 minutes of footage) is finally ready to bring it to US theaters.

The story is simple and silly. As soon as the old, crippled, washed-up soccer star Golden Leg (Man Tat Ng) sees a young kung fu master named Sing (Stephen Chow) kick a can so that it embeds itself into a brick wall, he gets an idea that will revolutionize the sporting world. He will build and coach a soccer team of kung-fu fighters. Lucky for him that Sing, also known as "Mighty Steel Leg," has five brothers - all of whom trained under the same master.

Unfortunately, time has not been kind to the Shaolin-trained fighters. As Sing begins to try and recruit his brothers for the new soccer team, he finds them disheartened, disillusioned, and stripped of their kung-fu powers. The redemption to be found in fielding a winning soccer team provides them with a glimmer of hope but first they must find a way to regain the Shaolin powers that they have lost. It is the only way they are going to be able to defeat the current champions, Team Evil, which is coached by Golden Leg's nemesis, Hung (Yin Tse).

Hong Kong star Stephen Chow stars and directs the script that he has also co-written. Although the version that is being released by Miramax has been cut and considerably reworked, enough of the original flavor of the 2001 Chinese hit remains to make Shaolin Soccer a goofy and enjoyable treat.

Chow takes the art of martial arts and exaggerates it for comedic effect. The first time they practice, Sing makes the mistake of using his Mighty Steel Leg to kick the ball straight up into the air, forcing everyone to wait a few hours until its return to earth. Gravity, physics, and the natural laws of the universe don't apply in these films and so we are treated to a number of unusual special effects. The force of the ball when kicked with kung fu might cause a wind shear that can plow a soccer pitch and rip the clothes off the back of any player standing in its path.

Stephen Chow, already a big name in Hong Kong cinema, looks to be on the verge of following Jackie Chan into becoming the next big Hollywood box office draw. He has the triple threat attributes of having leading man looks, athletic ability, and a sense of humor.

Some of the film's stylized humor doesn't work such as the breakout "Celebration" musical number or the fantasy battlefield bit but these are quickly over as the film never strays far from its core appeal - the Shaolin Soccer team.

According to Sing, kung fu is not just about fighting. It is a way of life. Therefore, he sees a potential kung-fu application everywhere he looks: while parking cars; while trimming hedges; and, most especially, when playing soccer. His approach to his chosen discipline is admirable and should be emulated by us.
