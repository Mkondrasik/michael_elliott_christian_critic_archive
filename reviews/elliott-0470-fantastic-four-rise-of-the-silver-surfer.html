Author-verified transcription

If it is summer, you know there's just got to be a comic book movie adaptation playing somewhere. This year, you don't have to look far to find Fantastic Four: Rise of the Silver Surfer playing at a theater near you. This sequel, once again based upon the popular Marvel franchise, finds the team of super-heroes facing a destructive force of global proportions. Oddly enough, that's the least of their concerns. First on the list is the pending marriage of Mr. Fantastic aka Reed Richards (Ioan Gruffudd) and The Invisible Woman aka Sue Storm (Jessica Alba).

It seems their wedding plans keep getting interrupted by one disaster after another. This latest one is a doozy. A metallic silver man on a metallic flying surfboard (hereinafter named the Silver Surfer) swoops down from the stratosphere and wreaks havoc upon the earth. When Richards discovers that every planet the surfer has visited has been destroyed eight days later, things get a bit tense.

It doesn't help that the team is showing signs of fraying. Sue wants to live a "normal" life and Reed wants to make her happy. Johnny Storm aka The Human Torch (Chris Evans) and Ben Grimm aka The Thing (Michael Chiklis) get the picture. The Fantastic Four may be going the way of the Beatles... if the world survives long enough.

The original film didn't garner widespread critical approval and it is doubtful that the sequel will fare much better. The story, for all its end-of-the-world bravado, is rather shallow. For that matter so are the characters. A lot of money has been poured into the special effects and they are the best thing about the movie... but this franchise will never become fantastic until the special effects begin serving the story instead of just taking our minds off it.

Though his parts may be rubbery, Ioan Gruffudd's Reed Richards remains a bit wooden. But he's an acting dynamo compared to Jessica Alba. Chris Evans pretty much showed us what he could do in the first film and has nothing new to offer here. Michael Chiklis is simply wasted.

This leaves all the attention on the silver dude (voice by Laurence Fishburne). Trouble is that he's more special effects than character in a film in desperate need of a character with whom the audience can connect.

Something we can connect with is the conflict battling inside of Sue. She wants a normal life. She wants to raise a family with the man she loves. She doesn't want to be at beck and call 24/7 whenever the world decides it needs a savior. We can empathize with that. So can Jesus Christ.

And Jesus saith unto him, The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head. Matthew 8:20 (KJV)

When we say that Jesus Christ gave his life for us, we mean much more than the fact that he died on the cross. His very life - all he said and all he did - was given to us in service. Jesus Christ remains the greatest example we have of a man who lived such a life. His selflessness and compassion are woven through every account in the gospels.

Few (if any) are capable of doing the same. Yet, that is exactly the life to which we are called. May God bless and give strength to those who answer that call.
