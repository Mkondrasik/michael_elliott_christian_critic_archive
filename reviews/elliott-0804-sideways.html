Sideways

Author-verified transcription

There's something about Alexander Payne's films that leave me slightly cold. The writing is always good and the direction is competent. But I just don't like his characters. It was true in Election and also in About Schmidt. It is again true regarding his newest film, Sideways, although, to be fair, of the three this may be the most accessible and least polarizing.

Paul Giamatti plays Miles Raymond, a pathetic little man who is an aspiring writer and wine connoisseur. With his best friend Jack (Thomas Haden Church) set to get married in a week, Miles has prepared a special week-long tour of California's wine country to celebrate the end of Jack's bachelorhood. Jack, as it turns out, has other ideas.

After a quick stop at Mom's house to steal some money to finance their little trip, Miles and Jack head off to the vineyards of California where Miles teaches a "could-care-less" Jack about the finer points of wine tasting. All Jack is interested in is seeing how many more female conquests he can score before he ties the knot.

Stephanie (Sandra Oh), a wine pourer at a local vineyard, becomes the next notch on his ever loosening belt and she brings along a friend to occupy Miles. Maya (Virginia Madsen) is an attractive waitress at his favorite restaurant who caught his eye some time ago though he was far too shy and insecure to do anything about it.

With Jack's unwanted help, Miles finds himself paired with Maya and up to his neck in lies and deceptions. It's not the healthiest way to form a relationship.

Payne takes great pains to establish wine as a metaphor for life and living. Both Miles and Maya have wonderful monologues as they explain to the other their passion for wine. They could very easily be talking about themselves. That one scene is by far the most effective moment in the movie.

Giamatti plays Miles as a "glass is half empty" kind of guy. Even when things look like they may be picking up for him, he manages to find the dark cloud lurking on the other side of the silver lining. Church presents Jack as an immature and somewhat likable cad whose lack of a moral compass leads him to many poor decisions.

Sideways does contain some witty lines and poignant observations about life and relationships but once again Payne utilizes flawed and weak characters that are difficult to embrace. The litany of negative examples for personal conduct contained in the film is indeed long. Each character demonstrates some kind of addictive or obsessive behavior that is unhealthy and unprofitable for them.

Despite the poor moral choices of the characters, we can use an attribute seen in Miles as an illustration for a positive lesson. He has built and refined his palate to such an extent that he is able to discern slight nuances in the aroma and taste of different wines and vintages.

Our spiritual senses should be just as developed. We accomplish this in the same manner Miles developed his palate... by exercising them on a regular basis.

But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil. Hebrews 5:14 (KJV)

The "strong meat" refers to the depth of understanding one has regarding spiritual truths. As we continue to utilize our spiritual abilities, we are able to grow in our knowledge and understanding. It is available to become a connoisseur of truth.
