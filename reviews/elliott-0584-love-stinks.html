Author-verified transcription

There may be a benefit to having no (or even negative) expectations when seeing a film. I was prepared to hate Love Stinks with a passion generally reserved for The Jerry Springer Show. The minimal advance publicity I had received convinced me that this film would not be one that I would under normal circumstances have any desire to see. However, after viewing the film, I have to say with some surprise... Love Stinks doesn’t stink all that badly. In fact, one or two of the characters are quite amusing and likable.

French Stewart (TV’s 3rd Rock From The Sun) plays Seth Winnick, a successful TV sitcom writer with a problem. Her name is Chelsea Turner (Bridgette Wilson, I Know what You Did Last Summer) and despite her beauty and obvious sex appeal, this woman spells trouble.

After worming her way into his heart, she proceeds to worm her way into every other aspect of his life. A manipulative schemer, she does not plan to rest until a ring is firmly planted on her finger... and through his nose.

The first part of the film shows the meeting and rapid courtship of the two love birds. In the second part of the film the love birds, having been transformed to carrion eaters, look for ways to destroy the other.

Never having seen Mr. Stewart’s work on his popular TV series, I was not expecting the unassuming charm he exhibits as Seth. Hopelessly infatuated in the beginning, he allows Chelsea to influence every iota of his life, from his home to his workplace, to his choice of pets. Mr. Stewart plays the befuddled, enamored paramour very well.

Miss Wilson, a relative newcomer to the screen, seems to be a cut above the vacuous starlets who are often tapped to play this kind of role. This girl can do a bit of acting. At least she makes you feel glad that you’re not engaged to her. Her character is attractive and knows it yet the actress is not afraid to be seen in an unattractive light.

Bill Bellamy (How To Be A Player) and Tyra Banks (Higher Learning) are their best friends and as such, have very little to do other than serve as window dressing. They do it adequately.

Jason Bateman (TV’s Chicago Sons) and Tiffani-Amber Thiessen (TV’s Beverly Hills 90210) are cast as the leads in Seth’s sitcom. Considering their TV background, one would think this to be an inspired choice. And it may have been if their roles had been more fully realized. As it were, their scenes play flat and uninspired.

This is a bit of a disappointment, seeing as writer/director Jeff Franklin is predominantly known as a TV producer. He has the background experience to offer a more biting satirical look at the industry of which he is so familiar. It would have certainly kept with the overall edginess apparent in the rest of the film.

Instead, Mr. Franklin chose to keep the focus entirely upon the “relationship from hell,” centering the film around his male star. It quickly becomes clear that whenever Mr. Stewart is not on the screen, nothing much of interest is taking place. What keeps the film from attaining the same odorous descriptive as its title is the fact that his character is the one featured in most scenes.

Had Mr. Stewart’s character been able to utilize just a little scriptural knowledge, he may have been spared much of the pain and anxiety he lived through. There are a number of scriptures that might have helped Seth come to a realization that this was not a relationship to be desired:

It is better to dwell in the wilderness, than with a contentious and an angry woman. Proverbs 21:19 [KJV]

A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike. Proverbs 27:15 [KJV]

Chelsea’s idea of marriage is a far cry from the union that God designed. A virtuous woman is one who supports, helps, and lends strength to her mate. Chelsea’s selfish, materialistic, one-sided view of a relationship does indeed “stink.” It just isn’t what one would call love.
