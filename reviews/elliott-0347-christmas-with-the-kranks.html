Christmas with the Kranks

Author-verified transcription

It should be obvious to all that there are two "Christmases" that are celebrated in this world. One is the religious and spiritual acknowledgement of the birth of Jesus Christ - an event so important that it is the demarcation of time itself. The other is a secular, commercialized event that celebrates family and brotherly love without much spiritual significance. Christmas with the Kranks is all about the latter.

Christmas cheer is in short supply in the Krank household. Their only child Blair (Julie Gonzalo) is heading for Peru and a Peace Corps assignment and will miss the family's traditional event for the first time in 18 years. Parents Luther (Tim Allen) and Nora (Jamie Lee Curtis) are going through the motions of their holiday preparations but it their hearts aren't in it.

Until Luther puts pen to paper and figures out that if they simply skip all things Christmassy, not only can they afford a luxury Caribbean cruise, they can actually save money.

A crazy scheme is born. No decorations; no Christmas cards or gifts; no tree; and no annual Christmas Eve party. The Kranks become the Scrooge-like pariahs of the neighborhood.

Luther is beside himself with anticipation of his dream vacation - indulging himself in self- tanning, botox injections, and Hawaiian shirts. Nora, on the other hand, is a nervous wreck - trying to avoid the judgmental stares of her friends and neighbors who don't understand their lack of community spirit.

Then, out of the blue, Blair announces that she's coming home with her new Peruvian fiancé to show him a traditional American Christmas.

The Kranks must crank up their holiday making machine and do six weeks of work in less than a day. Based upon the novel "Skipping Christmas" by John Grisham and a screenplay by Chris Columbus, Kranks is an inoffensive family comedy that is large on slapstick and situational humor. Tim Allen plays a derivation of his Home Improvement character - a good man who loses himself in his own selfishness temporarily. Jamie Lee Curtis is excellent as an uber-mom who is not altogether comfortable unless she's doing for others. The expressive joy and love she manifests whenever dealing with her daughter is engaging and very real.

Dan Akroyd plays the unofficial community leader who pressures Luther to bend to the neighborhood's will. M. Emmett Walsh is one of the neighbors and husband to a cancer patient which adds a storyline with a bit of pathos.

The characters in the film, like many people in today's world, find themselves ensnared in the trappings of this secularized holiday season. The traditions and rituals which take place can be enjoyed on a superficial level but are largely without substance and, when we examine them closely, are often a bit ridiculous.

The "Christmas spirit" that so many try to rekindle in their hearts every year actually knows no date or time. The feeling that giving is better than receiving; that family ties are important and should be strengthened; that a like-minded community is a healthy and supportive environment in which to live.. these are truths that need no holiday permission. We should strive to keep them alive in our hearts and minds throughout the year.

And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not. As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all [men], especially unto them who are of the household of faith. Galatians 6:9-10 (KJV)
