The Big Bounce

The irony is not lost on us. The Big Bounce lands with a resounding thud and simply lays there for its mercifully short running time. You can’t blame the actors for being in it. I suppose even Hollywood stars don’t mind receiving a free Hawaiian vacation, which is where the film was shot. Apart from the location, I can’t see what else would have attracted them to this script.

Owen Wilson stars as Jack Ryan, a petty thief who has been running from trouble for so long that he’s finally ended up in Hawaii, dubbed “the end of the line” by Judge Walter Crews (Morgan Freeman). After breaking his construction foreman’s jaw with a baseball bat, he is encouraged to leave the island. But that was before he caught a glimpse of a barely clad blonde named Nancy (Sara Foster).

Nancy is the mistress of construction tycoon Ray Ritchie (Gary Sinise) and she knows where he is stashing $200,000 in payoff money to the mob. With a wink and a bikini sashay along the beach, she gets Jack to agree to just about anything she wants him to do and what she wants is the 200 grand.

From there the film meanders aimlessly for about an hour until we arrive at the final 10 minutes or so where the plot (what is left of one by then) is wrapped up.

Owen Wilson’s laconic drawl and goofy persona sets the tone for this laid back crime/comedy/thriller/tourism advertisement. The other actors pretty much follow his lead. The motto seems to be “Nice and easy, no one gets hurt.” If Morgan Freeman were any more relaxed in this film, he’d have to deliver his lines from a Barca-lounger. He’s natural, real, and trapped in a film that goes nowhere.

Sara Foster, supermodel turned actress in her first feature film, actually does a decent job. Of course, her role in the film consists merely of exciting every male on and off the screen. It is something she does very well. Her costuming (or lack thereof) helps her considerably.

As for the rest: Charlie Sheen, Harry Dean Stanton, Willie Nelson, Gary Sinise, Vinnie Jones... They do enough to get by but not enough to salvage the movie. I hope the surfing was better.

The Big Bounce is directed by George Armitage (Grosse Pointe Blank) and written by Sebastian Gutierrez (Gothika).

In a film where every major character is a criminal of one kind or another, you wouldn’t expect there to be any moral or spiritual lessons from which we might learn. You’d be right to think so. In fact, a statement is said (twice!) that is so erroneous, I feel the need to address it.

At some point Walter Crews says to Ryan for no good reason, “God is an imaginary friend for grownups.” Ryan later repeats it - again, for no good reason. I suppose the screenwriter thought it was a pithy remark. The screenwriter (along with his characters) was wrong.

The existence of God, a Creator of all we see and know, is unmistakably all around us. To believe in Him is far less foolish than to think that the universe is a product of a series of random occurrences that just so happens to perfectly support and maintain life on this planet.

God deals with the question succinctly and precisely.

“The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good.” Psalms 14:1 (KJV)

Far from being imaginary, God is very much real and alive and He’s even willing to forgive fools.
