Battlefield Earth

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 12, 2000
Rating
1 star
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Knowledge; truth; study; spiritual awareness; avoiding ignorance

Author-verified transcription

Labors of love, much like the emotion itself, sometimes go unrequited. John Travolta, celebrated Scientologist and actor, has for many years desired to bring the sci-fi novel Battlefield Earth, written by Church of Scientology founder L. Ron Hubbard, to the big screen. Now that he has, it is difficult to imagine anyone else sharing his enthusiasm for the project. The film is a disjointed mumble of special effects, pretentious acting, a cacophonous soundtrack and dialogue unworthy of even the cheesiest B-movie of yesteryear.

Battlefield Earth is set in the year 3000, some 1000 years after the alien invasion which decimated the planet Earth and killed most humans, driving the survivors into hiding or slavery. Those in hiding have regressed to become cave dwelling hunters whose sole purpose in life is one of survival.

With dwindling food supplies, one of these survivors, Jonnie Goodboy Tyler (Barry Pepper, The Green Mile), sets out in search of a better existence. His journey will eventually set him at odds with Terl (John Travolta, The General's Daughter) the alien chief of security who views the "man-animals" with disdain and his assigned responsibility on Earth as being beneath his self-perceived status as one destined to conquer galaxies.

Jonnie grows from being a mild and amusing irritant into the leader of a human uprising which threatens to break the oppressive hold which the aliens (called Psychlos) have imposed upon Earthlings for the past millennium.

One would think that the cosmic battle for control of a planet and the survival of a species would be enough fodder to generate a modicum of excitement or interest. However director Roger Christian and his production team are simply lost in the vast scope of this story. Filling the screen with furious activity is not a substitute for a logical and cogent plot or believable and empathetic characters.

The metallic grays of the set and costumes, the dim lighting and the curious camera angles combine to produce a most frustrating and less than satisfying viewing experience. The musical score by Elia Cmiral is intrusive to the point of annoyance. Instead of underscoring the action, it overwhelms it.

BATTLEFIELD EARTH borrows freely from films which have preceded it. Indeed, there are scenes and styles which appear to have been lifted directly from other movies. For example, Jonnie runs in slo-mo glory down a hallway as the entire alien race fires at him, causing the walls to explode on either side of him ala The Matrix.

Imitation may be the sincerest form of flattery, but this imitation pales in comparison to the original. Whereas one used its style and technical prowess to further the story, this film is so enthralled by its own gimmicks that the story takes a back seat to the visual effects. This proves to be a fatal judgmental error.

The acting is more caricature than character-driven. John Travolta (nose plugs by Breathe Right) is so over-the-top that he is more buffoon-like than threatening. Forest Whitaker (Ghost Dog: The Way of the Samurai) is badly miscast as Ker, who comes off looking like a Psychlo wimp - the offspring of Chewbacca and Yogi the Bear. Barry Pepper as the hero, Jonnie, is athletic enough to be believable as a man of action. But the motivation for his actions must have been left on the cutting room floor.

A blessing of writing Movie Parables is that spiritually, it matters little if the film is good or bad. Lessons can be drawn from either. In BATTLESHIP EARTH, the "key" to human victory was found in the knowledge that Jonnie obtained by strapping himself into a "learning machine" a la Star Trek: Episode #61. God tells us repeatedly not to be ignorant. The more knowledge we have - the more truth we retain - the more power we can bring forth into manifestation.

Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices. 2 Corinthians 2:11 (KJV)

There is only one way to guard against ignorance. That way is by following the exhortation of 2 Timothy 2:15 --- Study to show thyself approved unto God. After all, it is the reason He wrote the book.
