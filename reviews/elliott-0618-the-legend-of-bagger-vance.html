Author-verified transcription

Life’s lessons are teed up and metaphorically driven down the fairway in Robert Redford’s The Legend of Bagger Vance. This golf story may come a bit short of scoring a hole-in-one but like the game itself, it sure is a pleasant way to spend a few hours.

Matt Damon (The Talented Mr. Ripley) stars as Rannulph Junuh, Savannah’s golfing golden child of 1928. Hero to the local boys and a heartthrob to the ladies, Junuh can do no wrong on the golf course. However, when war is declared, Junuh enlists and is forever changed by his experiences. Home from the war and now seeking only to be left alone, he manages to lose his girl, Adele Invergordon (Charlize Theron, Reindeer Games), his friends and his own zeal for living. In this allegorical tale, all of the above is of course figuratively represented by the fact that he has lost his golf swing.

When the depression hits Savannah, Junuh has long settled into his role as town recluse, drinking his way into the anonymity he desires. Meanwhile, faced with her own problems, Adele has hit upon an idea that may help save Savannah (and the newly built golf resort that her father built) from the poverty that lies lurking around the corner. She plans to host an exhibition match between the two greatest golfers of the day: Bobby Jones (Joel Gretsch, Kate’s Addiction) and Walter Hagen (Bruce McGill, The Insider). The town leaders approve of the idea on one condition: A local Savannah golfer be allowed to play in the match as well.

And thus, the stage is set for the redemption of our fallen hero. To guide him along the way, the mystical caddie Bagger Vance (Will Smith, Wild Wild West) appears out of nowhere with a Zen-like philosophy which he doles out with great frequency. “A man’s grip on his club is like a man’s grip on his world,” he muses to the bewilderment of Junuh.

With Bagger’s almost spiritual guidance, Junuh sets out to rediscover his “authentic swing” and in so doing, recapture some of the idealism, purity and the vitality of life which he allowed the horrors of war to steal from him.

Matt Damon turns in a fine, understated performance as a man who was on top of the world, cast down to the bottom, and offered a second opportunity to rise to the top.

It is Will Smith, however, who sets the tone and ethereal pace to the film. As Bagger, Smith is a calm and subdued presence whose whispered pearls of wisdom force Junuh out of his self-styled pity party and back into the rhythm of life.

Newcomer J. Michael Moncrief shines as the 12 year old hero worshipper who idolizes Junuh and also learns some hard lessons about sacrifice and dignity which come about as a result of the economic depression in which he lives. Charlize Theron captures the honey-dewed sweetness coupled with the steely resolution of a blue-blooded Georgian socialite.

The Legend of Bagger Vance may be old-fashioned hokum but it is such a breath of fresh air that we can’t help but be swept away with its apple-cheeked optimism regarding the resiliency of the human spirit.

The lesson of Bagger Vance is one which we could all stand to learn. Within the course of our lives, adversity will visit us on more than one occasion. When it does, we have the choice to take one of two roads: Lie down and quit, or meet the adversity head-on and fight through it.

Whether we find ourselves in a sand trap, the woods or a water hazard, all we can do is select a club and take the best shot we can. The only other option is not to finish the game. There are rewards which will come to those who endure and persevere. In the game of life, those rewards are eternal.

I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith: Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing. 2 Timothy 4:7-8 (KJV)
