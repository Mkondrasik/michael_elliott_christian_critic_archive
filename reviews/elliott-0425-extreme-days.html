Author-verified transcription

Last year, audiences were subjected to Road Trip, an over-sexed, overly crude endurance test of a movie. This year, audiences are being treated to a kinder, gentler journey. Extreme Days tells the story of four friends who, having graduated from college, decide to take time for the road trip they've talked about having since they were kids.

Corey, Bryan, Will, and Matt are decent fellows... underachievers all except when it comes to extreme sports activities. Only then do they become masters of discipline, relentless in their pursuit of perfecting a move, whether it be on a surfboard, skateboard, snowboard or BMX racer.

And so off they go, loading their sports equipment into their Joyota, a homemade contraption combining parts of a Jeep, Toyota, and Volkswagen. Starting in Mexico, for no other reason than it is there, they surf the radical waves off the Baja Peninsula. When Corey gets word that his grandfather died, they adjust their plans and set off for Yacima, Washington for the reading of his will.

They pick up another passenger as they come across Jessie, a beautiful damsel in distress who, they come to learn, can take care of herself. Traveling up the coast of California, the five of them stop whenever the extreme sports mood or some other juvenile impulse strikes them.

Director and co-writer Eric Hannah came up with the concept of Extreme Days after making a pair of sports videos called Moment of Truth. His idea called for a fictional narrative about a group of friends sharing an adventure the telling of which is often performed by accomplished extreme sportsmen.

What makes Extreme Days somewhat unique is the fact that it is being distributed by Providence Films, a group specializing in entertainment for the Christian market. There's no "end times" story here. No apocalyptic struggle between the forces of good and evil. There's not even any major proselytizing going on as the characters' faith in God is only directly mentioned a few times.

Ryan Browning (Smokers) plays Bryan, the ladies' man of the group who develops a crush on Jessie (Cassidy Rae, Favorite Deadly Sins). Derek Hamilton (Silver Man) is Matt, an odd goofball sort that reminds us of a tamer Crispin Glover. A.J. Buckley (Disturbing Behavior) as Bryan's little brother and Dante Basco (Hook) as Corey complete the quintet.

It is an uneven production at best. There are scenes that work very well such as the diner "spoons" challenge between Jessie and Bryan. And then there are scenes that don't work very well such as the kung-fu spoof between two groups vying for the same campsite. Showing that bad taste knows no religious boundaries, director Hannah includes an extraneous scene wherein the boys demonstrate their prowess in igniting their own flatulence.

Nonetheless, the cast manages to build a nice sense of camaraderie and while the script could have benefited from some tightening here and there, Extreme Days is a somewhat innocuous film offering some pretty cool sports footage.

The "message" of the film is that "when God throws you a curve ball, don't duck.. you might miss something." While I would disagree that God, who is good always, is to blame when life takes unexpected or unwelcome turns, the principle behind the message is still sound. The discipline exhibited by the extreme sportsmen makes the point. Meet each challenge head on. Don't shrink away. Success lies on the other side of each challenge and can only be achieved by going through it.

But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved. Matthew 24:13 (KJV)

Job, Joseph, Daniel, Paul, and countless other men of God whose acts are recorded in the Bible faced obstacles and difficulties in their lives of which they were able to overcome. They did so not by giving up or trying to avoid the hard times but by keeping their faith intact as they worked through the hard times. The point is that we will never be victors if we quit playing the game that includes the "game of life"
