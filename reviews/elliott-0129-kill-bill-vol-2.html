Kill Bill Vol. 2

Michael Elliott

April 16, 2004

Author-verified transcription

It’s been a four month break from the bloody violence of Kill Bill: Vol. 1 but the time has finally come to finish watching the rest of Quentin Tarantino’s fourth film - an homage to the grindhouse cinema of the past.

You might recall that due to its length, the movie was truncated into two volumes. The first introduced us to a character known simply as “The Bride” who, along with every member of her wedding party, was massacred by her ex-boss Bill and her fellow members of the Deadly Viper Assassination Squad which he ran.

Miraculously, The Bride (Uma Thurman) survived the deadly assault and upon waking up from her four year coma, has vowed to take her revenge upon those responsible. In the first film, she eliminates two of the five on her kill list: Vernita Green (Vivica A. Fox) and O-Ren Ishii (Lucy Liu). Vol. 2 has The Bride picking up where she left off - hunting down the remaining three names: Budd (Michael Madsen), Elle Driver (Daryl Hannah), and of course, Bill himself (David Carradine).

This second volume has a considerably different feel to it than the first. The first film was a visual assault to the eyes with extensive violence and gallons of blood splashing across the screen. Director Quentin Tarantino uses the extreme violence that preceded volume 2 to set up a surprisingly strong emotional tension. There’s not nearly the amount of violence or bloodshed as there is in the first film but the audience can’t help but recognize that the potential for that level of violence lies very close to the surface of every scene.

Kill Bill Vol. 2 is much more character driven. There are fewer fight scenes (by a considerable amount) but there are more scenes that reveal personalities, relationships and the drama connecting the characters. That’s not to say that the film is devoid of extreme violence and gore. There is one segment in particular between The Bride and Elle Driver that was quite uncomfortable to watch.

Uma Thurman again makes the most of what must be considered to be the role of her career. She presents a fully realized, complete character with flaws, hopes, emotions, and a sense of purpose. She is magnificent on the screen.

This time, she has an actor who meets her on her level. We didn’t see much of Bill in the first half of the film, but we see quite a bit of him in Vol. 2. David Carradine is brilliant. He’s able to present a passive, engaging facade while letting us know that he carries within him real and tangible danger.

I have to admit, I liked Vol. 2 much more than its predecessor simply because of the focus on the characters instead of on the ways they killed each other. While vengeance remains the central theme of the film, we pretty much covered the biblical aspects of that in the review of Vol. 1.

This time, I was drawn to a scene where The Bride is learning her martial arts technique from an old master named Pai Mei (Gordon Liu). He was trying to teach her how to punch a hole through solid wood. After many attempts, she was still unsuccessful in mastering the technique. Pai Mei identified the problem and succinctly explained it to her: “You cannot do it if you acquiesce to defeat before you do it.”

Believing is the most important key there is to achieving success. Rarely do we see success if we don’t believe that it is possible. God continues to exhort us to believe in Him and in the Word that He has given us because He knows that as we do, we will see the signs, miracles, and wonders that He is ready and willing to send our way. If we don’t believe, then we are not tapping into His power.

For this cause also thank we God without ceasing, because, when ye received the word of God which ye heard of us, ye received it not as the word of men, but as it is in truth, the word of God, which effectually worketh also in you that believe. 1 Thessalonians 2:13 (KJV)
