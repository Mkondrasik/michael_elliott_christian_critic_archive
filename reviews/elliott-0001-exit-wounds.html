EXIT WOUNDS Movie Parables Bringing you the Word on movies! By: Michael Elliott (OFCS) March 19, 2001

Rating: **

Directed by: Andrzej Bartkowiak

Starring: Steven Seagal DMX Tom Arnold Isaiah Washington Anthony Anderson Jill Hennessy

Written by: Ed Horowitz Richard D'Ovidio

Rated: R for strong violence, language and some sexuality/nudity.

Scripture References: Matthew 7:15-18 Galatians 5:22-23 1 John 4:1

Dirty cops, illegal drugs, car chases, car crashes, high body count, high bullet count, explosions, martial arts, and a cool, collected maverick who can kick butt with the best of them... What isn't in the new Steven Seagal film, Exit Wounds? OK, a little logic would have been nice, but not really expected.

In Exit Wounds, Seagal (Under Siege) plays super-cop Orin Boyd who, after saving the Vice President's life by single-handedly expending the entire Detroit police department's ammunition budget, finds himself inexplicably transferred to the pits of hell itself, Precinct 15, where police brutality is something practiced on fellow police officers instead of the criminals they arrest.

It isn't long before Boyd catches wind of something corrupt taking place in the halls of the police department and he won't rest until he kills all the suspects who might be able to tell him something about the case.

His new, attractive female commander, (Jill Hennessy, Autumn in New York), aware of his clouded history, forces him to partake of a ridiculous anger management therapy group where he meets the ridiculous talk show host Henry Wayne (Tom Arnold, McHale's Navy) who becomes a great fan of Boyd's macho kick-butt style.

Meanwhile, Latrell Walker (DMX, Romeo Must Die) appears to be a major player around town and is on the verge of scoring big as a shipment of heroin has disappeared from the police evidence vault and he positions himself to take it off the hands of the bad cops. But all isn't what it seems and Boyd must walk a fine line to determine the good guys from the bad. Either that, or he just has to beat up everyone who crosses his path. It should be no surprise that he chooses the latter course of action.

Seagal is very successful in keeping his character's personality a secret. Naturally somnambulistic, he underplays his dialogue to the point that one gets the impression that he would prefer not having any.

The rest of the cast all perform serviceable jobs, even if somewhat stereotypical. Notably, Anthony Anderson (Big Momma's House) provides the expected comic relief as TK, Latrell Walker's right hand man.

The true star of the picture however belongs to the impressive stunts and special effects which are generously spread throughout the film. Cars smash, flip, skid and burn with little provocation. Both the adrenaline and testosterone flow freely. It's just the semblance of any brain wave activity which appears to have come to a screeching halt.

Nevertheless, Director Andrzej Barkowiak (Romeo Must Die) pulls out all the stops in this highly violent and energetically styled action film.

Boyd's dilemma, after recognizing that corruption runs rampant in the department, lies in knowing whom he can trust. Neither the good guys nor the bad are what they appear to be. It is a dilemma which we encounter every day in our spiritual walk. Oftentimes, outward appearances are deceiving.

Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Matthew 7:15 (KJV)

The benefit of having God's Word available to us is that it gives us a standard of truth by which we may discern good from evil; right from wrong. In the case of the above verse, God tells us how to avoid being deceived by these false prophets:

Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit. A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit. Matthew 7:16-18 (KJV)

The spiritual fruit which is manifested by a good and godly man is listed for us in Galatians 5:22-23 and is a telltale sign of the overall quality of a person's life and walk.
