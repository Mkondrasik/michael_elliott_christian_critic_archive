The Best Man

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 22, 1999
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Proverbs 26:20Proverbs 11:13Proverbs 18:18

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Words; gossip; forgiveness; marriage

Author-verified transcription

Sometimes, The Best Man... isn't. At least not in the case of Universal Films' newest release starring Taye Diggs (The Wood) in the title role. From the standpoint of character integrity as well as acting ability, the title of "best" goes to another.

Mr. Diggs plays Harper, a soon-to-be-famous novelist whose first effort has been selected by Oprah Winfrey herself to be the next book of the month pick. However, before the big announcement is made, Harper must fulfill an obligation...serving as best man for friend and NFL running back star Lance (Morris Chestnut, GI Jane) as he marries his virginal college sweetheart Mia (Monica Calhoun, The Players Club).

Unfortunately for Harper, an advance copy of his book preceded him. As his friends circulate the steamy novel amongst themselves, they are able to see through the thinly-veiled characters, recognizing their own lives and personality quirks.

Jordan, (Nia Long, Stigmata) "the best girlfriend Harper never had," reads enough regret in the book to try and rekindle the smoldering flame of passion that might have existed between them. This turn of events isn't received well by Robin (Sanaa Lathan, Blade), Harper's current girlfriend, who is suffering through his inability to commit to a long term relationship.

Meanwhile, Lance, being initially amused by the "fictional" recounting of his collegiate sexual conquests, is not as amused to learn that his bride-to-be might not be the vestal virgin he had been led to believe.

Malcolm D. Lee (cousin of director Spike Lee), in his feature film debut as writer and director, presents an overtly sexual but moderately amusing tale. Presenting personalities rather than characters and an ethnically cliché-riddled script instead of dialogue that rings true, Mr. Lee also manages to squander a unique opportunity to make a genuine crossover hit.

The actors appearing in The Best Man remain to be the single redeeming feature in the movie. While Mr. Diggs has not yet dug any deeper than his marquee "centerfold" persona, the supporting cast more than compensates for any deficiencies of the lead.

Morris Chestnut is outstanding as the larger than life Lance, whose faith is stronger than the flesh which has continually given into temptation. He sees marriage (with its "miraculous" properties) as being the way out of his promiscuous ways. He obviously has some discoveries to make before the end of the film.

Terrence Dashon Howard (The Players Club) plays the sarcastic but brutally honest Quentin for all he is able. The script doesn't allow him to go anywhere with the characterization, but he sure is interesting to watch. Harold Perrineau Jr. (Macbeth in Manhattan) rounds out the group of friends as the dreadlock wearing Murch, saddled with a shrew of a girlfriend and who would rather volunteer his time helping disadvantaged children than accept a six figure job.

The women of the cast are largely forgettable, although Mr. Lee does treat them with a bit more kindness and respect than his more famous cousin has done throughout his film history. Sanaa Lathan, as Harper's girl Robin, stands out as being a "sister" with strength.

If you look to the filmmaker's intent, there is a lot that can be found to comment on spiritually. Lance's profession of faith, though poorly realized, does have an impact and is strongly felt. The institution of marriage is presented as something real and permanent. Many of the characters are called upon to forgive the trespasses of their friends in order to move on with their lives.

Harper also should have learned a lesson which the scriptures teach us:

The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly. Proverbs 18:18 [KJV]

Our words do have impact. Whether they appear in print or are used verbally, we should take time to choose our words carefully so we may be assured that they have the impact we desire of them.
