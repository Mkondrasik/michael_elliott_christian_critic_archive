Author-verified transcription

They say that an average man thinks about sex once every 4 minutes. Judging from their film, the makers of 40 Days and 40 Nights are operating at such an accelerated pace they make the average man look like a cloistered monk.

Matt Sullivan (Josh Hartnett, Black Hawk Down) is caught in a downward spiral. Months after being dumped by his "hot" girlfriend Nichole (Vinessa Shaw, Corky Romano), he still can't get her out of his mind. Bouncing from one meaningless sexual encounter to the next, he's rapidly realizing the emptiness of his actions. Fortunately for him, his brother (Adam Trese, Polish Wedding) is a priest-in-training who uses Matt as a kind of confessional practice dummy. During one of his confessional visits, Matt gets an epiphany... he'll give up sex for Lent. Not just the sex act itself but all manner of sexual behavior, including the solitary ones.

Thus the ridiculous premise is launched. Instead of being supportive, his friends and coworkers start an Internet betting pool, letting people place wagers on the day Matt will finally give into temptation. The movie lumbers through its many variations on a one joke theme as Mr. Celibate shakily fends off sexual opportunity after sexual opportunity.

Of course, during his vow of abstinence, he meets Erica, the girl of his dreams (Shannyn Sossamon, A Knight's Tale). The attraction is both immediate and reciprocated. All Matt has to do is figure out how not to have sex with her (or take any of the preliminary steps leading to sex) until his 40 day period of self-denial is over.

Josh Hartnett plays Matt as a kind of cute goofball whose sexual energy manifests itself in a series of twitches, facial tics, and grimaces. It's worth a laugh or two at selected moments but there's not enough there to keep our interest for the length of the film.

Paulo Costanzo (Road Trip) has some good sardonic moments as Matt's incredulous roommate and Shannyn Sossamon is appealing as Matt's love interest.

The problem is not with the performers who are for the most part adequate in their roles. It is just that the script by first timer Rob Perez is so painfully lame. 40 Days and 40 Nights has as much insight on sex and relationships as a bunch of drunken frat guys at a sorority kegger. It is about as amusing as well. It will generate a few laughs despite our better judgment but over time reveals itself to be blatantly moronic.

Parents should not be misled to think that 40 Days and 40 Nights is some kind of poster film for celibacy. This movie has no such moralistic message to deliver. It merely panders to the salacious desires of its targeted teenage audience using Matt's vow of abstinence as a "hook" to justify the titillating sexual temptations they parade before him and us.

The title of course is a biblical reference alluding to the period of time Jesus was tempted by the devil. Scriptures only record two people ever to have been directly tempted by the devil himself. Jesus was the second. Eve was the first. The reason Jesus was successful when Eve was not was due to his knowledge and application of the Word of God. After every devilish temptation, Jesus would respond by accurately quoting scripture. By adopting an "It is written..." mindset, Jesus was able to repel the devil's attacks and receive his eventual deliverance. We are able to do the same.

It is the light of the Word of God that we hold in our minds that is able to repel the spiritual darkness that exists in this world. Our bodies are subject to our mind's control... not the other way around as this film would have us led to believe. God has designed our minds with the ability to withstand the temptations of the flesh.

There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it. 1 Corinthians 10:13 (KJV)
