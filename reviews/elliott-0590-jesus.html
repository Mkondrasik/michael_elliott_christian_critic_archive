“Jesus is coming.”

No, that is not the announcement heralding the end of the world. Rather it is the promise of four of the most spiritually oriented hours we might ever hope to see on network television.

To be aired in two segments starting May 14th on CBS, JESUS takes a fresh look at the life and earthly ministry of Jesus Christ as recorded in the gospels. High production values, an eye for historical accuracy, an accomplished international cast, and an obvious respect for the biblical source of the material, combine to make this a must-see event for believers and nonbelievers alike.

Executive Producer Lorenzo Minoli, who was responsible for the series of Bible related films which appeared on TNT: Joseph, Abraham, Moses, David, Samson and Delilah, and Solomon, now tackles what for many remains “the greatest story ever told.”

In this new production, Mr. Minoli and director Roger Young (who also directed the three of the previously mentioned films, including the Emmy award winning Joseph) emphasized two key elements. Says Minoli, “Our JESUS refers to history and tries to explain the time in which Jesus lived. We attempt to show what really happened when the Jewish community was under Roman control.”

The second element was in how Jesus’ personality was to be presented. “I wanted to show Jesus as a real guy,” says Mr. Young, “What’s always missing from the films of Jesus, it seems to me, is that he was a human being. If you go to the New Testament, you can see that he liked to dance, liked to drink, liked to eat. He made jokes, he called people by their nicknames -- he made up their nicknames. He got angry, he blew up at people. All of that’s in the Bible... And that’s the Jesus we’re trying to portray.”

Jeremy Sisto (Suicide Kings) was cast in the difficult title role and he approached it with more than a little trepidation. “There are so many reasons to feel daunted by playing this role,” admits Sisto. “So many people have a personal and specific idea of who Jesus is -- and he’s someone who has inspired millions of people. So, I had to decide from the beginning to let that go and try to relate to the story and person and interpretation that we were telling. I tried to see him not as an icon, but as someone who was more human and relatable to me.”

Joining Mr. Sisto in the cast is Jacqueline Bisset (Dangerous Beauty) as Mary, Gary Oldman (Lost in Space) as Pontius Pilate, Debra Messing (Will & Grace) as Mary Magdalene, Armin Mueller-Stahl (The Thirteenth Floor) as Joseph, David O’Hara (Braveheart) as John the Baptist, and Jeroen Krabbe (An Ideal Husband) as Satan.

The scenes with Mr. Krabbe are among the few that depart from scriptural documentation, utilizing the dramatic license of the filmmakers by including contemporary images. Executive Producer Judd Parkin explains, “We took a pretty radical approach to the section where Jesus is tempted in the desert by the devil - by going outside of Jesus’ time. But (the consulting scholars) were very supportive of the idea.

The filmmakers also felt justified in writing a second scene for Satan to tempt Jesus. This invented visit takes place in the Garden of Gethsemane as Jesus awaited the time of his arrest. Satan tries to inject doubt in the mind of Jesus by showing him flashes of the future indicating that his sacrifice will be in vain. Although a powerful moment, this brief scene is the most ill-advised from a scriptural point of view. The scene contradicts Satan’s true intent and accredits him with more power and foreknowledge than he has. (2 Corinthians 2:8)

A screenwriter’s invention that is more successful is the character of Livio (G.W. Bailey, Police Academy), a Roman citizen who helps to provide the political insight to the behind the scenes action regarding Jesus’ arrest and crucifixion.

Depicting those political machinations is what first interested Gary Oldham, who plays Pontius Pilate. “I was looking forward to playing Pilate the way it (the script) was written,” says Gary Oldman. “There’s a little bit of Nero in him and just a tiny bit of Caligula. It’s kind of delicious. For him (in this mini-series) it’s a deliberate mission to get rid of (Jesus) because politically he’s a problem.”

Adds Minoli, “No movie has ever said that Pilate intentionally sent Jesus to death. We are saying it, and it’s a very important element. We also explain why the high priest (Caiaphas) would want to get rid of Jesus. He was a threat to their survival under the Romans -- and we show why.”

The Italian ratings (of the mini-series which aired there in December) were astonishing. It was the highest rated show for the year. Thirty-seven percent of people watching television (approximately 10 million viewers) tuned in to night one and forty percent (more than 12 million viewers) watched night two.
