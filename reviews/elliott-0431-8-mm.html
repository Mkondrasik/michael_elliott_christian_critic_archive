Author-verified transcription

If you don't believe that we live in a sick and twisted world, fifteen minutes of 8 MM should give you more than enough reasons to change your mind. The world it depicts is as vile and unseemly as any you'll ever see.

The story is simple enough. A rich man dies. His widow opens his private safe and finds a grainy 8 millimeter film that apparently captures the image of a young girl being brutally murdered. She hires a private detective to find out if this snuff film is the real thing or just a realistic illusion.

Tom Welles (Nicolas Cage, Snake Eyes) is that private detective. Happily married, new father of a baby girl, highly ethical... there is little in his life that could have prepared him for the depravity into which he is about to delve.

Helping him on his foray into the underbelly of society is a perceptively sharp adult bookstore clerk named Max California (Joaquin Phoenix, Return To Paradise). Despite Max's warnings, ("If you dance with the devil, the devil don't change... The devil changes you.") they begin their descent into the dark and perverted world of sadistically oriented hard-core pornography.

Director Joel Schumacher, recovering from the universally panned Batman and Robin, crafts a suspenseful enough mystery/thriller, but if seeing an actual "snuff" film is revolting, running a close second would be seeing a film about those who allegedly make them. Mr. Cage does a very credible job as the detective for whom this case takes on a dual purpose:

1) To find out if the film is real by finding either the girl or those responsible for killing her and; 2) To find out why people would watch this kind of thing in the first place.

As he gets closer and closer to unveiling the truth, we see Max's warning take effect. This case is having a profound effect on Welles... None of it good. It causes him to put at risk his own family, his own life, and his own sanity. His soul becomes one that is touched by the devil's hand and Mr. Cage plays the part very convincingly.

Mr. Phoenix also turns in a fine performance as the young would-be musician who gets sucked into this world of darkness. James Gandolfini (Fallen) and Peter Stormare (Armageddon) are nauseatingly believable as the slime responsible for making the film.

And Catherine Keener (Your Friends and Neighbors) as the working class mother of the girl in the horrifying film footage is movingly effective.

The screen play is penned by Andrew Kevin Walker who, not too long ago, scored at the box office with Seven, another dark and disturbing film. His work clearly indicates a fascination with devilish subject matters. It is a fascination that I don't share (nor do I care to).

The warning Max gives is glaringly accurate and it is one of which we should all take note. God in His Word puts it this way:

Evil companionships (communion, associations) corrupt and deprave good manners and morals and character. 1 Corinthians 15:33b [Amplified]

If we subject ourselves continually to the devil's realm, our godly or moral resolve and commitment will be weakened. It is like having a small piece of meat stuck between your teeth. If allowed to remain, it will start eating away at the enamel which protects your teeth. The longer it remains, the more damage it will do.

So it is spiritually. We can't play the devil's games and expect to stay on God's team.

And I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils. Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of devils: ye cannot be partakers of the Lord's table, and of the table of devils. 1 Corinthians 10:20b-21 [KJV]

Mr. Schumacher and Mr. Walker have combined to make an artistically competent film, but because of its subject matter, my question is the same one that Tom Welles asks: "Why would anyone want to watch it?"
