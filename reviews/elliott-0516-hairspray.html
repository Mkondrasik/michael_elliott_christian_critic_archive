Author-verified transcription

The Unsinkable Molly Brown should move over and make room for the indefatigable Tracy Turnblatt! The heroine of Hairspray, the new movie musical based upon the cult John Waters' film later remade into a Broadway hit by the same name, is the very picture of high hopes and undeterred spirit.

Tracy (Nikki Blonsky) is a short and wide high-schooler who dreams of one day dancing on the Corny Collins' American Bandstand-style TV show circa 1960. Tracy's dreams are as big as her bloomers. White-bread TV audiences just weren't ready to welcome someone like Tracy into their living rooms... or so corporate America thinks.

But thanks to Tracy's indomitable spirit and her ability to "shake her tail feathers" like no one else on the lily-white show, she finally gets her chance - much to the chagrin of Amber von Tussle (Brittany Snow), 3 time winner of the Miss Hairspray title.

But Tracy's unexpected popularity is just the tip of the cultural iceberg. Tracy's friendship with and admiration of the black dancers who move and groove during Corny's once a month "Negro Day" leads her to think that integrating the show is not only a good idea, but the right thing to do.

Director and choreographer Adam Shankman keeps the film bouncy and full of energy. There are plenty of dance and musical numbers - although over time they do tend to start feeling a bit repetitive.

The film's biggest accomplishment was the casting of Nikki Blonsky as Tracy. She captures her character's positive outlook in such an infectious way that we can't help but smile along with her. She's also got a big voice and the moves that are needed.

The movie is blessed to be populated with some big name supporting actors who bring their A-game to the table. Most notably, John Travolta, in a cross gender performance, plays Tracy's overweight mother with verve. He is able to communicate through all the make up and prosthetics to reach both our hearts and our funny bones.

Michelle Pfeiffer is deliciously catty as Amber's manipulative mother; Christopher Walken is dotty as Tracy's joke store owning dad; James Marsden is spot on perfect as Corny Collins; and Queen Latifah delivers her goods as Motormouth Maybelle, host of Negro Day.

The film has an effervescent quality about it throughout most of its running time. It does run out of steam at certain times but seems to have no trouble recovering the energy and rousing itself back to life. It may not aspire to be of ground shaking proportions but it sure is an enjoyable summer diversion.

Tracy's tenacity to realize her dream is only surpassed by her willingness to forgo that dream for a higher cause. It is a laudable attitude. Many times things that we think we want can seem within our grasp if we but act in opposition to our values. The problem with doing that is that after we get what we think we wanted, it doesn't seem all that important any more. Tracy's dream never died. She was simply unwilling to compromise her values to get it. That is a mark of a big person any way you look at it. Jesus Christ chose to submit his will (his dreams and desires) in order to do the will of his Father which was for the greater good.

For I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me. John 6:38 (KJV)
