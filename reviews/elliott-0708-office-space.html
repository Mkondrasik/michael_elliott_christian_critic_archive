Author-verified transcription

In the cubicle world of Office Space, Peter Gibbons hates his life. Ever since he began working at a mind-numbing desk job for Initech, each day seems to him to be worse than the one before. This means that every time we see him, we're seeing him on what is, in effect, the worse day of his life.

Writer/Director Mike Judge, the mind behind Beavis and Butthead as well as King of the Hill, takes aim at the corporate management weenies that make life miserable for the lowly white collar workers. When Mr. Judge hits the target, it is with blistering accuracy. Sadly, in this movie, his aim is not all that consistent.

Office Space is based on Mr. Judge's comic strip character, Milton, which was parodying office life back when Dilbert was still trying to break out of the mailroom. In the movie, Initech has hired some efficiency experts to help them through the process of downsizing.

Peter just happened to be involved in a freak hypnotic accident which made him (guess what?) indifferent to his job. The experts read his indifference as management quality and suggest his promotion, even as they recommend firing his programming buddies, Samir and Michael Bolton (no relation, ha, ha), played by Ajay Naidu (Pi) and David Herman (Mad TV). To retaliate, the three friends plot their diabolical scheme to take revenge on the company.

Mr. Judge had all the pieces in place. He had introduced all the recognizable types: the oily zephyr of a boss; the middle manager who can't really justify his own existence; the droning receptionist who answers the phone in the same way with the same intonation time after time after time; the dissatisfied employee who is normal except for the fact that he can't stand his job; the computer nerds whose sex lives, by necessity, are conducted via computer mouse; and of course Milton. He's the mumbling, overweight, stapler stealing employee with bad complexion. Think Rainman with a rolodex.

Had Mr. Judge stayed within these bounds, keeping his focus on the workplace and its denizens, showcasing the ironies and illogical practices that is so familiar to much of corporate America, Office Space could have been one of the most scathingly sarcastic inside looks of the business world ever filmed.

Instead, he introduces a gratuitous love interest (Jennifer Aniston, The Object of my Affection), a moronic plot to steal money from the company, and an unsatisfactory cop-out of an ending that leaves the audience feeling cheated.

Ron Livingston (Swingers) is competent as Peter Gibbons. Frankly, we was more interesting as the put upon employee. As the hypnotized "don't give a darn" employee, his characterization flattened out.

Jennifer Aniston is just wasted in her role as a waitress being constantly reprimanded for not having enough "flair" (buttons and chotchkies) to adorn her uniform.

Gary Cole (A Simple Plan) plays a talentless (and clueless) manager superbly. John C. McGinley (Truth or Consequence, NM) and Paul Willson (Circuitry Man), as the so-called efficiency experts are a riot.

Spiritually, of course, the whole attitude toward work as depicted in the film, was wrong. There is profit or value to all work, even the most menial tasks. God commands us:

And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men; Colossians 3:23 [KJV]

We should always remember that we are His ambassadors in this world. He is our boss. If we merely approach our secular jobs (whatever those jobs may be) with the attitude that we work for Him, every day gives us the opportunity to shine as His lights. As we faithfully maintain that mindset, each day becomes better than the one that came before. That means that each time people see us, they are seeing us on the BEST day of our lives!
