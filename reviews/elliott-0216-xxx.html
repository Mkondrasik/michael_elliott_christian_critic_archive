Author-verified transcription

Whether we believe it to be an exceptionally exhilarating experience or an excruciating example of excess, one thing is certain: xXx explodes on the screen with extreme exuberance and exaggerated excitement. It is James Bond on steroids. If only steroids were also known to build intelligence, these filmmakers might have had something.

Xander "xXx" Cage (Vin Diesel, The Fast and the Furious) is a extreme sports daredevil who not only performs outrageous death-defying stunts, but also films them to sell on an underground Internet site. With little regard for the law and even less for his self-preservation, Cage is somehow viewed as the best candidate to serve his country as an undercover espionage agent during a crisis of global consequences. The man holding that opinion is NSA agent Augustus Gibbons (Samuel L. Jackson, Changing Lanes).

No sooner that you can say "Cowabunga, dude," Cage is whisked off to Prague in the Czech Republic to infiltrate an evil organization named Anarchy 99. It seems that their leader, an ex-Russian military commander named Yorgi (Marton Csokas, Lord of the Rings: Fellowship of the Rings), has no love for governments or the laws they enact. He plans to do away with them so that anarchy may reign supreme. Only then, he surmises, will man experience true freedom.

As Cage gains access into Yorgi's inner circle of lingerie models and body builders, he meets the lovely and enigmatic Yelena (Asia Argento, La Sirene Rouge) and uncovers the details of Yorgi's twisted plan. In order to get that information into the hands of the good guys, he must ride motorcycles through explosions, snow board in front of an onrushing avalanche, throw himself out of a plane, and piggyback a ride on a hydrofoil equipped with poison gases. All in a day's work for America's newest and most overly tattooed super spy.

As was director Rob Cohen's previous film, The Fast and the Furious, xXx has lots of action, lots of explosions, and lots of speed, but very little story. No matter. The target audience for this film isn't looking for logic, plot twists or in depth characterizations. xXx is all about the attitude.

This makes Vin Diesel the perfect choice as its star. This guy has attitude coming out of his ears. His acting talent may be debatable, but his machismo certainly is not.

Much of the dialogue is absurd, to the point of being laughable. Not helping matters much are the thick accents adopted by some of the supporting cast. Samuel L. Jackson adds a bit of unneeded credibility as the scarred NSA agent who enlists Cage.

Yorgi, a terrorist with a Hugh Heffner lifestyle, says that he wants to bring down all of the world's governments along with their laws in order to find the freedom he seeks. What he doesn't realize is that true freedom is found within the law, not outside of it.

The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul: the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple. The statutes of the LORD are right, rejoicing the heart: the commandment of the LORD is pure, enlightening the eyes. The fear (reverence) of the LORD is clean, enduring for ever: the judgments of the LORD are true and righteous altogether. More to be desired are they than gold, yea, than much fine gold: sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb. Moreover by them is thy servant warned: and in keeping of them there is great reward. Psalms 19:7-11 (KJV)

Without laws, which serve as a framework within which men may live peaceably with one another, chaos would be the rule of the world. In chaos, there is no peace. Without peace, one is not truly free.
