Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 15, 2002
Rating
4 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Peter 1:13Ephesians 6:13-18Hebrews 6:18-19

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Hope; perseverance; spiritual warfare; courage; endurance

Author-verified transcription

It is impossible to adequately review an epic film the likes of Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers in a mere 800 words. Suffice it to say that this second installment of J.R.R. Tolkien's trilogy is every bit as good as the first and, in many ways, could even be considered an improvement. It is a perfect compliment to The Fellowship of the Ring, a fact which only serves to whet our appetite for the third and final film which will complete the story.

By the end of the first movie, the fellowship has been separated into three parties and they remain divided for the duration of the second film. Thus director Peter Jackson must split the film's narrative three ways and it is remarkable how seamlessly he and his screenwriters have managed to weave the stories together and still keep the narrative flowing.

Frodo (Elijah Wood) and Samwise Gamgee (Sean Astin) have slipped away to finish their journey to Mordor. The task they have been given is to destroy the one ring that has the power to control all. The ring, which Frodo carries, is beginning to have more and more of a negative influence upon him. Along the way they come across a previous ring bearer, the strange CGI-created Gollum who agrees to lead them to the black gates of Mordor.

Meanwhile, hobbits Merry (Dominick Monighan) and Pippin (Billy Boyd) have been taken prisoner by the dreaded Orcs but manage to escape by slipping away into the forest where they meet the Ents, a race of beings as old and strong as the trees they shepherd. The hobbits try to convince the Ents to join the battle against the evil powers of Sauron and Saruman.

The third grouping consists of the man Aragorn (Viggo Mortenson), the elf Legolas Greenleaf (Orlando Bloom), and the dwarf Gimli (John Rhys-Davies). When tracking the Orcs who took Merry and Pippin, they encounter a far greater danger. Saruman's devilish army is planning to strike against Rohan, one of the last great cities of man in Middle Earth. Aragorn, Legolas, and Gimli agree to stay and add their strength to the vastly outnumbered Rohan forces which have fallen back to their stronghold called Helm's Deep.

While the human actors all perform exceedingly well, the stars of this film may very well be the computer artists which have outdone themselves. In addition to the grand scale battle scenes which are awesomely conceived and executed, the CGI team produces a major character: Gollum. So successful is this animation that there is a strong push to nominate the character for an acting award. It's hard to argue against it. Seriously demented by overexposure to the ring, Gollum (or Smeagol as he was once known) is the most interesting character on screen. He commands our attention.

The Two Towers is darker and more ominous than the original film. Battles are bloodier and the creatures are more frightening. The PG-13 rating is well deserved.

There is also little hope on display through the nearly three hour running time. Survival seems unlikely and help doesn't not appear to be in sight. Even after the battle is fought, after being in so much darkness and danger, a character wonders how the world could ever go back to the way it was (or at least the way the Shire was). And yet no one thinks of giving up or giving in. Sam explains it best when he says that we have to believe that there's some good in this world... and that it's worth fighting for. Amen to that, young and noble hobbit.

It is sometimes too easy to think that God has turned His back upon this world what with all the death and despair that is reported every day. The truth is that God is as near to us as our own breath. When things appear to be hopeless, that is the time we need to believe in hope the most.

Wherefore gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and hope to the end for the grace that is to be brought unto you at the revelation of Jesus Christ. 1 Peter 1:13 (KJV)
