Dirty Work

Author-verified transcription

With movies like Dirty Work, Night at the Roxbury, Here's Pat, among far too many others, I'm surprised there's not more of a movement to ban any future Saturday Night Live alumni from making films.

The alum starring in Dirty Work is Norm MacDonald (Dr. Doolittle). He plays Mitch, a loser whose only "skill" is in exacting revenge. To raise money for his father's heart transplant (rich comedy fodder there!) he starts a revenge for hire business with his half-brother Sam (Artie Lange, Mad TV).

Jack Warden (Mighty Aphrodite) plays the sex-obsessed father. His portrayal makes a case for the abolition of Viagra.

Chevy Chase, Adam Sandler, and the late Chris Farley take on supporting roles, thereby renewing the suspicion that somewhere in the Saturday Night Live contract there is a clause that requires cast members to appear in each other's films.

Bob Saget (America's Funniest Home Videos) directs this lame and largely unfunny comedy with all the flair of a dyslexic paint-by-the-numbers artist. Nothing works in this movie. It is painful to watch a formula movie when the formula doesn't mix well. We see the jokes being set up. We hear the punchline being delivered. But we never get the payoff. The joke (for lack of a more accurate word) falls flat.

While part of the problem is obviously Frank Sebastiano's and Mr. MacDonald's script, which is a few levels beneath mediocre, a fresh and inventive cast should have been able to provide a few laughs from the premise. With this cast... nada. Although he has apparently developed an effective TV persona, Mr. MacDonald does not yet have the ability to carry a full length motion picture. Once his character is introduced, the movie goes nowhere.

Of course, the subject matter is ridiculous and contemptible. Revenge is an exercise practiced by weak and immature minds. I would venture to say that this movie will only appeal to the same.

God's Word tells us what we must do if and when we are wronged. We are certainly not to indulge ourselves in an emotional retaliation performed for perverse self-gratification. Let God handle the revenge. According to Romans 12:19, it's His job anyway. Life is too short to spend it bearing grudges. Our time can be more valuably spent thinking of ways to serve God and bless His people.

Matthew 18 describes the godly progression of handling disputes and grievances within the household of God. Revenge is never mentioned. We are to confront the one who has transgressed against us. If they hear our words and respond properly, our next move should be one of forgiveness.

Therefore, let me be the first to say that I forgive Mr. Saget and Mr. MacDonald for foisting this terrible film on an unsuspecting audience. There will be no revenge sought, no request for reimbursement of wasted time or money. Just try and do better next time.
