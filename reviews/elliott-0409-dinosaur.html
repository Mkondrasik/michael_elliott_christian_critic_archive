Dinosaur

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 19, 2000
Rating
3 stars
Running time
1 hr: 22 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Romans 15:1Galatians 6:1-21 Thessalonians 5:14

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Helping the weak; compassion; teamwork; strength

Author-verified transcription

Set amidst the backdrop of a pre-human existence, Walt Disney Pictures presents the best that human technology has to offer. DINOSAUR is a remarkable blend of computer animation and digitally enhanced photography which tells a compelling story.

The opening sequences shows a dinosaur egg becoming separated from the nest and herd of its mother. In so doing it also sets up the level of animation we are to expect. Following a pteranodon as it soars over the beautifully lush landscape we are given a "birds-eye" view of thousands of dinosaurs as they graze. Over thirty different species of these fascinating creatures are depicted in the film and thanks to new breakthroughs in the technology used, the dinosaurs in this film make the CG animation used in the making of Jurassic Park look positively... well, prehistoric.

The aforementioned egg ends up on a remote island populated by family of lemurs. The dinosaur, an iguanodon named Aladar (voice by D.B. Sweeney, The Cutting Edge) grows up in complete isolation from his own species, raised instead by his adopted monkey-like family led by Yar (voice by Ossie Davis, Doctor Dolittle) and Plio (voice by Alfre Woodard, Mumford). He Lears from the playful and supportive lemurs the qualities of compassion, familial love, and tribal unity. A far cry from the "survival of the fittest" mentality which is being promoted amongst his own kind back on the mainland.

After a beautiful meteor shower turns deadly when a massive asteroid hits the earth, Aladar and his "family" escape the devastation, returning to the mainland where they attach themselves to a traveling herd of dinosaurs who are being led through the now desolate landscape to the promised land of their idyllic nesting ground.

This band of dinosaurs is relentlessly led by Kron (voice by Samuel E. Wright, The Little Mermaid) who sets the pace based on the strength of the young and fittest, knowing that the old and weak will straggle behind and fall prey to pursuing predators. This is, in his mind, the dinosaur way and perfectly acceptable.

It is not acceptable to Aladar who, by helping those in the rear of the pack, puts himself at risk and challenges Kron's authority and leadership. Caught in the middle is Neera (voice by Julianna Margulies, ER), Kron's beguiling sister who finds herself drawn to the goodness in Aladar's manner.

As stated before, the animation is fantastic. The elasticity of the skin which rippled or shivered at appropriate times helped to make these characters appear realistic and alive.

The dialogue is disconcerting at first. The extended opening sequence is shot naturalistically with only a musical background score by James Newton Howard (The Sixth Sense.) When the lemurs and dinosaurs begin to speak, the film breaks the convention it previously set, requiring us, as an audience, to mentally shift gears.

Being a dinosaur movie, there is a certain amount of violence or threatened violence to be expected. The pursuing carnosaurs are big, vicious, and unmerciful. There is also a charming sequence on the island which is built around the lemurs' mating ritual (which is depicted as being more like a courtship.) In any case, this is a PG rated film and parents with very young children should exercise appropriate caution.

Aladar's compassionate reaction to Kron's dismissal of those too weak to keep up with the stronger members of the herd is one which is mirrored in the scriptures. The concept of the strong helping the weaker regain strength can be seen throughout the Word of God.

We then that are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves. Romans 15:1 (KJV)

What Aladar teaches us is that "survival of the fittest" can be expanded to refer to the entire herd, not just each individual. The herd that works together, supporting and encouraging each other will be the fittest herd and the one most likely to survive.
