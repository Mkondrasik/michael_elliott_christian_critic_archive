Author-verified transcription

One must remember that director Garry Marshall has made The Princess Diaries 2: Royal Engagement for an age group not known for their cultural refinement. When judged on that level, we can safely say that Marshall succeeds in making a film that an 8 year old will enjoy. The rest of us can only smile benignly when our child or grandchild turns to us to say "Wasn't that the best film ever?" Unfortunately, we know better.

This G-rated film picks up the Cinderella story as Mia Thermopolis (Anne Hathaway) graduates from college and is immediately whisked away to the kingdom of Genovia. As next in line for the throne (a revelation that was handled in the first film) Mia will learn her regal duties from her grandmother, Queen Clarisse Renaldi (Julie Andrews).

A little known Genovian law has thrown a wrinkle into the royal plans. Custom demands that upon turning 21, a princess must take a husband or relinquish the crown. Should she remain single, the throne would go to Nicholas Devereaux (Chris Pine). It is something that his opportunistic uncle Viscount Mabrey (John Rhys-Davies) tries to assist by plotting against Mia.

Overly simplistic and treacly to a fault, The Princess Diaries 2 is a sugary sweet Disney confection that designed to delight young girls who dream of Prince Charming and the joys of living in castles with every convenience at their beck and call.

Family friendly in every way, PD2 offers the type of entertainment normally reserved for the Disney channel. In fact, one of the Channel's rising stars, Raven, is given a meaningless part in the film for no other reason than to give her a duet with Julie Andrews. Ms. Andrews is the picture of class and grace which gives the film whatever regal bearing it has. She even manages to retain her dignity while mattress surfing at Princess Mia's slumber party.

Anne Hathaway's debut performance in the original film was such a delightful revelation that it led me to compare her to a young Lucille Ball. That is high praise and, in retrospect, perhaps it was a bit prematurely given. There's no doubt as to her talent but she really needs to test her limits more by choosing projects that will challenge her. Only then will we see if she has what it takes to be favorably compared to a legend.

Generally speaking, the film takes a safe route, finding laughs through exaggerated characters and slapstick actions which come from nowhere. There's little consistency in the characterization and little attempt made to give Genovia a character or heritage of its own. This will be of little concern to the princesses-in-waiting in the audience. Mia's Cinderella story and her attempts to find her true love without sacrificing her royal duty will easily capture their imagination. For the rest of us, perhaps we should just be thankful that they have a film they can watch and enjoy.

Mia wrestles with a conflict that has beset man from the beginning of time - the internal battle between self and service. Does she follow her heart or does a sense of duty override her personal desires? It is a hard question that does not get easier with time. And, as Christians who are exhorted by God to walk in the steps of Christ, it is a question that we must answer many times each day.

A man's heart deviseth his way: but the LORD directeth his steps. Proverbs 16:9 (KJV)

God knows our hearts - as we trust in him and follow his direction as we honestly walk through this world, he will give us the desires of our hearts... just as Mia received her happily ever after.
