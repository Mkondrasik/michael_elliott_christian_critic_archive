Author-verified transcription

It sure is nice to see that Mel Gibson (Braveheart), Danny Glover (Switchback), and the rest of the team are not “getting too old for this $#&!” (Please excuse the language, the movie is obviously R rated.)

Of course, we’re referring to the fourth installment of the popular Lethal Weapon series. Since the advancing age of Mr. Glover’s character, Roger Murtaugh, was a running gag in the original movie some twelve years ago, you can well imagine the jokes that will flow now that Mr. Gibson’s character, Martin Riggs, is also starting to feel the ravages of time.

Director Richard Donner (Conspiracy Theory) has assembled the old cast (no pun intended) together for another adventuresome romp. Judging from the explosion that caps the opening scene, better hold on tight to the popcorn, we’ve got quite a ride ahead of us.

For the purists in the audience, there is a plot of sorts. But in all honesty, who really cares? The enjoyment of the Lethal Weapon films have always been in following the various subplots and rich character relations. In Lethal Weapon IV, these subplots actually take center stage. Riggs is struggling with the fact that he’s getting older and perhaps should settle down. It’s a decision that would please Lorna (Rene Russo, Tin Cup) seeing as she is nine months pregnant with Riggs’ child. But she’s not alone in this motherhood thing. Rianne (Traci Wolfe), Murtaugh’s daughter, is also pregnant, which forces Murtaugh to deal with all the emotions that goes with the prospect of becoming a grandfather. On top of that, he’s being investigated by Internal Affairs.

While Joe Pesci (My Cousin Vinny) isn’t pregnant, his character, Leo Getz, still talks enough for an entire family of five. But perhaps he’s met his match in the newest member of the troupe - Chris Rock (Saturday Night Live) is Lee Butters, a fellow cop with some interesting connections to Roger Murtaugh. The rapid fire dialogue in the scenes between Leo and Butters is hysterical, made more so by the reactions of Riggs and Murtaugh who look on with increasing incredulity.

The best thing this sequel has going for it (besides the wonderful chemistry between its principle actors) is a first class villain. Jet Li (Hitman), an amazing master of martial arts, plays the part of Wah Sing Ku with a stoic silence born of supreme confidence. He almost makes you believe that Riggs and Murtaugh are not going to prevail in the end. We had to keep reminding ourselves that the producers would be financially insane not to leave a door open for having a Lethal Weapon V.

Still, Wah Sing Ku is an intimidating foe. Certainly more than either Riggs or Murtaugh can handle alone. But they aren’t alone, are they? They have each other. Together, they may be strong enough to stand against the bad guy.

Similarly we, as Christians, have a common enemy who is stronger than any individual man. But he is not stronger than the God Who empowers us and Who, in His wisdom, has called us to stand together as a household. As the scriptures say:

Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices. [2 Corinthians 2:11 KJV]

There’s a reason that verse refers to the plural. Together, we are strong enough and wise enough to stand against the wiles of the devil.

And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken [Ecclesiastes 4:12 KJV]

Each of us must individually believe God on a daily basis, but we do so within the context of a collective stand. As we strengthen ourselves individually, we add to the strength of the household with whom we stand. Riggs has Murtaugh; Murtaugh has Riggs. We have God and each other. Together, we can prevail.
