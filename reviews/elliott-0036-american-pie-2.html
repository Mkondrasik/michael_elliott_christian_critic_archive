American Pie 2

Michael Elliott

Author-verified transcription

Those wacky horn dogs of American Pie are back. Jim, Jim’s Dad, Oz, Fitch, Stifler... even the pie makes a brief cameo appearance. As familiar as the sequel is, there is also something a little different about American Pie 2. Could it be that some of these characters are actually showing signs of maturing?

Perhaps... but what is certain is that the filmmakers aren’t. American Pie 2 is as sexually obsessed as its predecessor. There are some characters who will undoubtedly always stay in a state of repressed adolescence, but other central characters do seem to be on the verge of a unique discovery... that relationships can and should consist of something deeper than sex.

The sequel reunites the cast from the original film, picking them up as they complete their freshman year in college. Oz (Chris Klein) has managed to stay in a monogamous relationship with Heather (Mena Suvari); Kevin has remained celibate, pining after his ex-girlfriend Vicky (Tara Reid); Fitch (Eddie Kaye Thomas) has discovered Tantric meditation as a way to channel his sexual energy, although he still has fantasies about Stifler’s mom (Jennifer Coolidge).

Then there’s Jim (Jason Biggs) who continues to find new and unusual ways to be embarrassed during his clumsy sexual exploits. Of course, Stifler (Seann William Scott) needs no outside help... he is simply an embarrassment all by himself.

These pals, expecting a memorable summer, decide to rent a house on the lake and host a big blowout party at summer’s end. During the summer, each must deal with sexually oriented issues. Oz is facing his first physical separation from Heather; Kevin is struggling with his new “friends only” relationship with Vicky; Fitch prepares for a visit from Stifler’s mom; Jim prepares for a visit from Nadia (Shannon Elizabeth) by receiving sex tips from band camp geek Michelle (Alyson Hannigan); and Stifler just wants to know if his two sexy neighbors are lesbians.

Director James B. Rogers and writer Adam Herz have deliberately set out to make more of the American Pie magic that entered theaters in the summer of ’99. By their standards, I’d have to say they succeeded. Anyone who is a fan of the first film will probably enjoy this one just as much. Those who didn’t care for the original will find little in the sequel to appreciate. I find myself grouped with the latter category.

Much of the humor was forced... deliberately crude to match or exceed the expectations set by the first film. Many of the returning actors - Natasha Lyonne, Mena Suvari, Tara Reid - are given relatively no reason for reappearing. They are as gratuitous as many of the sexual jokes and references.

The relationship which showed the most promise was the one developing between Jim and Michelle. As she helps him improve upon his obviously lousy sexual technique, we can see his growing awareness that the “girl of his dreams” might not necessarily be the one with the largest breasts and lowest IQ.

It is a lesson that more young men could stand to learn. The media, operating with the notion that “sex sells,” promotes a certain female image as being “ideal.” Therefore, the qualities which young men are being “trained” to appreciate and admire in women are temporal or temporary... not the ones that will necessarily lead to a permanent and deeply satisfying relationship. Physical beauty might be the icing on the cake, but the true worth of any individual lies beneath the surface.

God, who is so much wiser than any media hack, gives this exhortation to women:

Your beauty should not come from outward adornment, such as braided hair and the wearing of gold jewelry and fine clothes. Instead, it should be that of your inner self, the unfading beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is of great worth in God’s sight. 1 Peter 3:3-4 (KJV)

Men, looking for a list of qualities to truly desire in a woman, would do well to read Proverbs 31.
