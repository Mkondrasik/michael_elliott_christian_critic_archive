Spartan

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 12, 2004
Rating
3 stars
Running time
1 hr: 46 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Acts 4:19Acts 5:29Matthew 22:21

Sermon / spiritual topic: Obedience; Higher Authority; Integrity

Author-verified transcription

Robert Scott is one of the best of the best. A character in Spartan, he's a highly efficient government agent in an elite and secretive military/political agency. From the start, it is clear that he will do whatever is necessary in order to make things right. His next job will test his limits.

The US president's daughter (Kristen Bell) has been abducted and Scott (Val Kilmer) has been called in to be a member of the team to retrieve her before the press becomes aware of her disappearance. It is, after all, an election year.

Scott and his new partner Curtis (Derek Luke) try to find the daughter but run into various difficulties. It appears that the abductors do not know who they have. It also appears that they plan to funnel the girl into an overseas slave/prostitution ring that is being run in an Arab state.

Scott has no problem tracking the girl to the ends of the earth if need be. His problem begins when the US government calls the rescue off. Although the president's advisors are telling the commander-in-chief that his daughter is already dead, Scott and his partner Curtis know otherwise. The code that they have sworn to uphold demands that they attempt to bring the girl back home, even if it means that they have to do it alone.

Writer/director David Mamet is a master of minimalist dialogue. He scripts this film with "agency" shorthand and acronyms but he doesn't waste any time explaining them. He expects us to bring our intelligence to the theater with us. We, the audience, must learn to pick up things as we go. Because we won't be going back. There's no time. Mamet's script continues inexorably forward. The final act is a bit too contrived and preposterous but overall the film is a taut and thrilling adventure of political intrigue.

Val Kilmer - though many believe that his career may be waning - shows a new potential in Spartan. He's a bit older, seems more mature, and is totally in control and believable in his role. William H. Macy, as a high operative in the president's think tank is not given a plumb role and even seems a bit miscast. A less recognizable face may have been a better choice.

Derek Luke and Tia Texada as two of Scott's fellow operatives do what they are required to do but offer little more than a pair of stereotypical performances. Kristen Bell, as the kidnapped victim, is properly confused and frightened. Ed O'Neill plays the president with authority but has few scenes to develop his character.

The film does contain scenes of violence and frequent uses of profanity. It is properly rated R.

Scott has a moral and ethical question to answer midway through the movie. All of his superiors have called off any rescue attempt. Scott becomes convinced that rescue is not only a viable option, it is the only course of action to take. In order to take it he must disobey his official orders and act on his own volition.

The question the film asks is at what point do we stop following the orders of men in order to answer to a higher authority? The answer must be "always." We are to give respect and honor to our superiors, bosses, managers, political leaders, etc... But we should never bend our principles or our integrity when it comes to doing God's will.

Jesus Christ always did God's will. He also never violated man's law except where man's law contradicted his Father's.

But answering them Peter and John said, Whether it is right before God to listen to you rather than God, you judge. Acts 4:19 (LITV)

Ultimately, we will all be judged by the words we speak and the actions we take while on this earth. We will be judged fairly but we will be judged. The standard by which we will be judged is the same standard that Jesus Christ upheld. Did we do what God wanted us to do, or did we follow the false doctrines of deceived men? For our sake, let us try to do the former.
