Author-verified transcription

Superheroes continue their march off the comic book pages onto the silver screen. The latest spandex-wearing do-gooders to make the transition are the dysfunctional "family" known as the Fantastic Four.

A scientific experiment gone awry leaves five individuals with scrambled DNA. Reed Richards (Ioan Gruffudd). described by one character as the dumbest smart guy in the world, has a brilliant mind, a milquetoast demeanor, and a newfound ability to stretch like rubber. His ex- girlfriend, Susan Storm (Jessica Alba), is attractive, wears tight costumes, and can turn invisible which requires the removal of said costume.

Her brother Johnny (Chris Evans), aka The Torch, is a bit of a hothead — figuratively and literally. Reed’s bodyguard Ben Grimm (Michael Chiklis) is made of stone and impervious to pain... except the kind that goes beyond the physical.

The fifth individual, Victor Von Doom (Julian McMahon). is a multi-billionaire who fronted the money for Reed's failed experiment. Blaming Reed for his company's failure, he seeks revenge and redemption through their demise. Because the accident has turned him into titanium, he may be just powerful enough to do it.

The film is all about the sudden acquisition of superpowers and how each individual responds to them. Reed and Sue view it as a disease to be cured. Johnny sees it as an opportunity to be embraced. For Ben it is a curse and for Doom it is a validation of his superiority. For Hollywood, it is yet another excuse to make a larger than life CGI-filled action adventure picture.

Tim Story (Barbershop) gives us most of the requisites that we demand of a superhero flick: interesting but conflicted heroes; a dastardly villain; and amazing visuals. But he doesn’t tie them all together sufficiently to transport us to the Marvel-ous world of comic book fantasy.

Some of the problem stems with Welsh actor Ioan Gruffudd (doing a passable American accent). His character. as leader of the group. is at the center of the action but he offers no weight to anchor the film. Alba does what little is required of her but because her character is so tightly associated with his, she might as well be invisible.

The two remaining team members really do live up to the name. Chris Evans as Johnny and Michael Chiklis as Ben are indeed fantastic. Evans is a riot as the freewheeling. opportunistic “Torch.” Energetic and full of wisecracks, he’s a perfect counterpoint for his favorite target - Ben Grimm. Chiklis. under what seems like a ton of prosthetic makeup. manages to act through the mask in order to communicate the heart and soul of this broken man. These two are easily the best part of the movie and worth the accolades they will receive.

It takes a while for our reluctant heroes to recognize that their new superpowers could be a gift that they give to the world. God has granted us “powers” as well. But he warns us not to get carried away with the power itself. Our focus must remain on what the power signifies.

Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means hurt you. Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven. Luke 10:19-20 (KJV)

The only time the Fantastic Four are worthy of their name is when they look beyond themselves and start using their "gifts” for good.
