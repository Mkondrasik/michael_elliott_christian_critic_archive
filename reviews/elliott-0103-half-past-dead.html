Half Past Dead

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 15, 2002
Rating
2 stars

Original Author Copy

Author-verified transcription

If it is Half Past Dead, then it must be time to blow something up. Steven Seagal’s (Under Siege) explosive new action thriller expends a considerable amount of ammunition but, not surprisingly, it doesn’t really hit anything. Stretching credibility beyond the breaking point, it actually transcends any semblance of logic thus eliminating the need to try and follow the story. But for laughs, here goes:

Seagal’s character, Sascha Petrosevitch, is a Russian and an FBI agent who tragically lost his wife (and apparently all trace of a Russian accent). He’s gone undercover to expose a criminal car theft ring and in so doing has befriended Nick Frazier (Ja Rule, Turn It Up) a smooth and dapper thief who has taken Sascha under his wing.

When an FBI bust goes sour, (did they not get the memo about the undercover guy?) Sascha saves Nick from a barrage of bullets and almost dies in the process, hence the title. Nick is subsequently sent to prison and Sascha, still undercover but having recovered from his near fatal wounds, follows him there. Why? Who knows? It’s of little importance because the film’s plot doesn’t really start until after they get there.

They are sentenced to Alcatraz, refitted to be the ultimate facility for prisoner confinement. It is currently home to Lester (Bruce Weitz, Hill Street Blues), a death row inmate who knows where 200 million dollars worth of gold bricks is buried but plans to take that knowledge to his grave.

Just before his execution, a team of leather clad commandoes calling themselves 49ers break into the high security prison to stop the execution and force Lester into telling them the location of the gold. In order to stop them, Sascha must form a ragtag team of Alcatraz inmates into a fearsome fighting force. The fact that there is no reason for the Alcatraz convicts to fight doesn’t seem to bother the filmmakers... After all, there’s stuff to blow up!

Seagal, who may have slowed a step or three, is greatly assisted by some stylish direction by first timer Don Michael Paul as well as by some snappy, MTV style editing. Morris Chestnut (The Best Man) plays the chief 49er, a mental case and political attaché (whatever happened to governmental background checks?) Nia Peeples (Fame) looks great in her blue eye shadow and her midriff-baring, zippered, leather commando outfit. It’s what all the crazed psychopaths will be wearing this season.

Surprisingly, the most entertaining performances are actually turned in by a pair of non-actors: Rap artist Ja Rule, as the smooth car thief Nick, manages to build a nice rapport with Seagal. Fellow rapper Kurupt (Dark Blue) has a number of amusing moments as a comedic inmate named Twitch. The scene between him and his girlfriend during a prison visitation which plays over the end credits is perhaps the funniest in the film.

What might be a surprise is that there is plenty of talk about God going on in Half Past Dead. It primarily involves Lester who is facing his pending execution. Although he is resigned, even content, about being punished for his misdeeds, Lester wants assurance that God has forgiven him. He need look no further than the pages of his Bible for that assurance.

The apostle Paul, one of the greatest believers in history, had much for which to be forgiven. In his own words:

“I verily thought with myself, that I ought to do many things contrary to the name of Jesus of Nazareth. Which thing I also did in Jerusalem: and many of the saints did I shut up in prison, having received authority from the chief priests; and when they were put to death, I gave my voice against them. And I punished them oft in every synagogue, and compelled them to blaspheme; and being exceedingly mad against them, I persecuted them even unto strange cities.” Acts 26:9-11 (KJV)

Even though he worked vehemently against those who believed in God’s only begotten son, persecuting them unto death, forgiveness was not withheld from him. There is nothing in our past so heinous that would keep God from reaching out to us in love, wiping away our sins from his remembrance.
