Clockstoppers

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 29, 2002
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 5:15-16Colossians 4:5Psalms 90:12

Translation: Amplified
Sermon / spiritual topic: Time; stewardship; wisdom; using life well

Author-verified transcription

Even though time is one of life’s few constants (there is always 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, and 24 hours in a day), it can often feel like the passage of time is relative to whatever it is we are experiencing. Ever notice how time “drags” when we’re in a state of heightened anticipation and “flies by” when we’re having fun? Clockstoppers takes this common experience and magnifies it to form a family friendly sci-fi adventure.

Zak (Jesse Bradford, Bring It On) is a high school student who is “earning” car money by finding things that he can sell for a profit on e-bay. When he finds a watch among his father’s experiments, e-bay is the last place he goes. This watch is able to catapult him and Francesca (Paula Garces, Harvest), a cute foreign exchange student, into “hypertime,” a state in which they move so fast, everyone else appears to be standing still. Conversely, people in hypertime are invisible to the “slow movers” of this world.

Naturally, Zak and Francesca use this newfound power to pull practical jokes on people too slow to stop them. But not all is fun and games. There are some governmental bad guys looking to recover the watch and they can operate in hypertime as well. Zak’s father is eventually taken hostage and it will be up to Zak to stage a rescue, elude the bad guys and keep the watch safe from those who would abuse its power.

OK. So the plot is a more than a little bit ludicrous. It also isn’t all that original. The fact that it is directed by Jonathan Frakes of Star Trek: The Next Generation fame seems oddly appropriate. Doesn’t all this sound rather like that old Star Trek episode where Kirk’s body chemistry accelerated to the point that he was invisible to the rest of the crew?

There is more fiction than science on display in this sci-fi film. Too many inconsistencies and unexplained gaps in its logic might frustrate the more demanding moviegoer. For the casual viewer, Clockstoppers is a bit of innocuous fun despite its many flaws. While it certainly won’t be winning any awards, it does makes for a pleasant diversion and a safe albeit silly option for parents looking to provide movie entertainment for their children.

The cast is uniformly competent without being stellar. Jesse Bradford is an attractive lead who should appeal to the young teenage audience for whom this film was made. French Stewart (3rd Rock From The Sun) elicits a few laughs as the inventor of the hypertime watch who hasn’t quite worked out all the bugs.

I’m sure there are times when we might have desired a watch similar to Zak’s which would allow us to control time at the push of a button. Alas, such a device does not exist. We must therefore control time the old fashioned way... by spending it wisely.

“Look carefully then how you walk! Live purposefully and worthily and accurately, not as the unwise and witless, but as wise (sensible, intelligent people). Making the very most of the time (buying up each opportunity), because the days are evil.” Ephesians 5:15-16 (Amplified)

Life is a gift from God. How that gift is used an individual choice. There are few things sadder than time wasted. It is, after all, our most precious commodity. Once spent, it cannot be reclaimed or reused. May we spend the time we have on this earth wisely and well.
