Author-verified transcription

To hear her describe herself, Mary Cep is a flamingo in a flock of pigeons. In Confessions of a Teenage Drama Queen, Mary is a sixteen year old girl with an unusual wardrobe and a flair for the dramatic. Why be merely upset when you can be ruined for life and devastated beyond all hope of recovery?

The latter description is how Mary (Lindsay Lohan) feels when her mother moves the family out of New York City and transplants them to what might as well be the middle of Siberia - suburban New Jersey. Gamely, she makes the effort to go on living.

At her new school, Mary quickly makes herself known as being different. She adopts a new name - Lola; invents a story about how her father tragically died (he didn't); and makes friends with the shy and reserved Ella (Alison Pill) who shares her undying passion for the rock group Sidarthur (whose frontman, according to Lola, is the greatest poet since Shakespeare)

She also encounters Carla Santini (Megan Fox), the trendiest girl in school, who will become her chief rival for the coveted role of Eliza in the school's version of Pygmalion.

The film follows Lola through teenage crisis after teenage crisis, all exaggerated to ridiculous heights by the drama queen herself.

Seventeen year old Lindsay Lohan, who shone in last year's Freaky Friday, plays Lola with energy to spare. It is a performance that will no doubt appeal to the preteens and "tweenies" in the audience but may grow tiresome for everyone else. Being in the presence of a drama queen may be amusing at first but it gets old very quickly. She does show herself as having a nice singing voice, performing four songs in the film.

Alison Pill does a nice job growing her character from a reserved mousy girl to someone who is no longer afraid to assert herself. Carol Kane gives an amusing if someone stereotyped performance as Ella's strict and uptight teacher and director of the school play.

Clearly, director Sara Sugarcane and Walt Disney Pictures have made and are targeting this film for young females using Lindsay Lohan as the next pop star attraction. It is a very mild film by today's standards and has few objectionable elements to it. Wrong behavior is punished, parents are shown as being concerned and caring for their children, and lessons are learned by those who need to learn them.

Lola perhaps learns the biggest lesson of all as her flair for the dramatic leads her to telling outright lies. After being caught in her lies, when she tells the truth, people still don't believe her. Lies will never accomplish what the truth can bring. Eventually the truth will come to light and our lies will be turned against us.

A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall not escape. Proverbs 19:5 (KJV)

Making up stories and exaggerating truth was Lola's way to set herself apart from the crowd - to make herself appear different and special. What she learns is that she has the ability to do that for real - not by telling lies but simply by being herself. The bottom line is that nobody can be the person we are but us. That automatically makes us unique.
