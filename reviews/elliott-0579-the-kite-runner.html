Author-verified transcription

It is perhaps likely that you will not remember any of the actors appearing in The Kite Runner. But it is a story that you will never forget. Based on the best selling novel by Khaled Hosseini, this engrossing tale of friendship, shame, and redemption revolves around two young boys growing up in the pre-Taliban environs of Kabul, Afghanistan.

Young Amir (Zekiria Ebrahimi) and his best friend Hassan (Ahmad Khan Mahmoodzada) spend most of their days preparing for the city’s esteemed kite-running competition.

Hassan, in particular, is uncanny in his ability to run down kites whose strings have been cut by more skilled flyers. When Amir witnesses the brutal attack and rape of his friend their relationship changes. Amir, filled with shame and guilt over his cowardly inaction, can no longer bear to be in Hassan’s presence.

When the film jumps ahead some twenty years, the story moves to America where Amir (Khalid Abdlla) and his father (Homayon Ershadi) emigrated to escape the invading Russian army. Now married, Amir receives a phone call from his old childhood friend asking him to return to Afghanistan by promising that there is a way “to make things good again.”

Director Marc Forster and screenwriter David Benioff have done a wonderful job adapting Hosseini’s novel for the screen. Forster has found the emotional center of the story and builds his film around it with considerable skill. We are immediately drawn to these characters and are invested in what happens to them.

The child actors who portray Amir and Hassan are perfectly cast and Homayon Ershadi who plays Amir’s intellectual and compassionate father is nothing short of wonderful in the role.

However, in this film the actors know that they are there to serve the story and it is the story which deservedly takes center stage. Not having read the novel (and thus having no preconceptions or expectations), I was immediately taken with the themes and relationships being presented.

It has been said that every human being is a composite of his experiences and that the sins we commit in our youth can often haunt us throughout our lives. One sinful act (or moment of inaction) can poison our souls and cause a ripple effect that affects every other aspect of our lives… if we allow it.

We are truly blessed because we have a choice. We do not need to live with shame nor must we seek out our own redemption. We have been redeemed by a savior who has already paid the price for our sins.

For the scripture saith, Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed. Romans 10:11 (KJV)

Those who carry the burden of the past on their shoulders become stooped with time. God wants us to live our lives to their fullest. He desires for us to be His vibrant, bold, and active witnesses in this world. To do this we must be able to let go of the past and look forward with hope to the future. God has given us a way to do the former and a promise with which to do the latter.
