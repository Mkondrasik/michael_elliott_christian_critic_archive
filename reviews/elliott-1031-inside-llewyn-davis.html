Inside Llewyn Davis

Sermon / spiritual topic: Choices; Sowing and Reaping; Character; Consequences

Author-verified transcription

Life consists of a series of choices, one after another after another. We all must make them continuously and endlessly throughout our lifetimes. Sometimes we choose well. Sometimes we choose poorly. The lead character of Inside Llewyn Davis seems to have the uncanny and unfortunate knack of always making the wrong choices. This film depicts the futility and frustration Davis experiences as he tries to make something out of his life without ever realizing that the greatest obstacle to his success is his own self.

Inside Llewyn Davis is set in the 1960s and takes place primarily in New York’s Greenwich Village, which was, at the time, a magnet for alternative artists. Llewyn Davis (marvelously played by Oscar Isaac) is a folk singer. He is a very good folk singer. But, in the arts, being very good does not necessarily equate to marketability. Davis has no money, no home, and no winter coat. All he has is a cat that he is forced to look after in exchange for being able to crash on someone’s couch… and then he loses the cat.

The Coen Brothers do a fantastic job in recreating this iconic time in U.S. history. And, as executive music producer, T Bone Burnett continues his impressive string of spot on musical scores. Oscar Isaac pulls off the near impossible, somehow making his character appealing while simultaneously displaying a multitude of unappealing characteristics.

There are a number of supporting characters, ably performed by extremely talented actors and singers (John Goodman, Justin Timberlake, Carey Mulligan) but they all revolve around and take back seat to the persona of Llewyn Davis. He is the central figure in the story and the one to whom we are drawn. His is a simple story of a struggling artist trying to land his big break. Unfortunately, the breaks don’t come and Davis’ story ends up being a bittersweet tale about a “never-was.” And perhaps that is all that he was ever able to be.

In his eyes, and in many of the eyes around him, Llewyn Davis is the antithesis of a success. And it is a shame for he is not without talent, ability, or an artist’s soul. But that soul of his, expressed through his music, was largely filled with depressing images and sad tales about death, dying, and loss.

The biblical reference that kept circulating through my mind as I watched Llewyn make one bad choice after another was Galatians 6:7.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.” Galatians 6:7 (KJV)

If we desire to be satisfied or happy in our lives, then the seeds we should be sowing are those that will bear the fruit we desire. Davis struck me as being an insensitive, selfish man with a genuine talent… a tortured soul without a heart. His negative, pessimistic outlook on life became a self-fulfilling prophecy. It was as if he were on an endless treadmill that kept him running but brought him no closer to a finish line.
