Never Been Kissed

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 9, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Acts 10:34Romans 2:11James 2:8-9

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Impartiality; acceptance; judging appearances; diversity; treating others fairly

Author-verified transcription

I imagine that high school can bring back some pretty dreadful memories. Sure, it was supposed to be a time of learning and growing, but for anyone labeled a "loser" by the "in crowd," it was also a time of constant humiliation.

In Never Been Kissed, Josie Geller had it rougher than most. With braces, bad complexion, matted hair, and an awkward manner encasing a romantic soul, the cruel nickname of "Josie Grossie" followed her throughout her high school career. At twenty-five, finally free of her ugly duckling stage, she has become a successful copy editor for a big city newspaper with an opportunity to become an investigative reporter. Her first assignment? To pose as a student at a local high school and "root out" a story about today's young people.

The success of her assignment is dependent upon her acceptance as one of the "cool kids." This feat, which she found impossible the first time around, has not been made easier with age.

Drew Barrymore (The Wedding Singer, Ever After) serves as both star and executive producer for Never Been Kissed, showing her marketing savvy by cementing her credibility as an actress who can play sweet and congenial roles. Her Josie (named after the guitar playing pussycat) is a clumsy charmer, one who will steal your heart even as she trips over her own.

David Arquette (Scream 2) plays her brother Rob, for whom popularity has never been a problem. Growing up... Taking responsibility... That's a whole different matter. He's a very relaxed, easygoing actor whose rakish slacker of a character is enjoyable to watch. It is he that helps Josie "transition" to the status of high school elite.

Michael Vartan (It Had To Be You) plays her teacher who is too good (and too good looking) to be true. John C. Reilly (Nightwatch) and Molly Shannon (Analyze This) play Josie's newspaper coworkers who follow her progress via a hidden camera (a la Edtv). Garry Marshall (Murphy Brown) is also on hand as the quick tempered and quirky publisher who fires at will (and apparently, whim).

Director Raja Gosnell (Home Alone 3) gets a bit unrealistic with all the high school hijinks and he also allows Ms. Shannon's character to be drawn too over-sexed for anyone's good. The scene where she is mistaken for a sex-ed instructor and gets the class to practice safe sex techniques using a common household fruit as a prop was a cheap and gratuitous inclusion.

Nevertheless, that scene is relatively brief and those criticisms are minor compared to the achievement that Miss Barrymore and Mr. Gosnell have made. They have, for the most part, been able to successfully walk that finest of lines in movie making... a romantic comedy that works. So take a date, share a soda, and enjoy the film for what it is. A lighthearted romp with some likable characters.

Likable or not, all high school students, the cool as well as the un-cool, suffer from the same malady... A lack of perspective. This will in time change, but for right now, everything in their small universe takes on a life or death/black or white significance. The prom theme has to be perfect or the prom will be ruined. One has to be accepted by the right crowd or one's life is meaningless.

The message of Never Been Kissed is quite refreshing, in its own way. High school is just four short years out of what should be a very long and experience-filled life. Josie, at 25, knows what her new school "chums" do not... It is a big world out there and there is room for all kinds of diversity. Judging solely by appearance or by how "rufus" one is, is both superficial and wrong. God's heart is clear on this matter:

Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons: Acts 10:34 [KJV]

If we could renew our minds to the level of God's truth in this area, many of the petty problems of man would be ended. Many avenues, by which strife and discord enter, would be shut down and barricaded. Instead of labeling the "losers" that we meet, we could help each other claim the victory that is available.
