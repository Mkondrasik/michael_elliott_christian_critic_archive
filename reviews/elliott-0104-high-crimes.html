High Crimes

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 5, 2002
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Addiction; relapse; temptation; sobriety; spiritual vigilance

Author-verified transcription

High Crimes is a film that rather reminds me of a car I once briefly owned: sleek and fast moving, it was really fun to drive until it got a few miles from its destination at which point it would suddenly break down and leave me by the side of the road, puzzled and more than a little bit miffed. This film does pretty much the same thing except that there’s no AAA to call.

Ashley Judd (Someone Like You) plays Claire Kubik, a happily married, highly competent defense attorney who is thrown for a loop when she discovers that Tom, her loving, mild-mannered husband (Jim Caviezel, The Count of Monte Cristo) has a secret military past. That past intrudes upon their present after he is arrested in an FBI sting operation and accused of the 1988 mass murder of nine El Salvadorian citizens.

Convinced of her husband’s innocence and refusing to allow him to be thrown to the wolves by an unjust military court, Claire signs on as his attorney and begins to unravel a military cover-up that reaches high up the tarnished chain of command.

Joining Claire in her David vs. Goliath struggle is an ex-JAG, ex-drunk, motorcycle-riding attorney named Charlie Grimes (Morgan Freeman, Along Came A Spider) who helps her navigate the strange and politically charged waters of the military court system. “Military justice is to justice what military music is to music. Wake up and smell the napalm,” Charlie wisely advises.

Also on the defense team is a still wet behind the ears first lieutenant (Adam Scott, Seven and a Match) and Claire’s loose and fun-loving sister, Jackie (Amanda Peet, Whipped).

Director Carl Franklin (One True Thing) keeps the action taut and suspenseful through the various twists and turns of the plot. He is ultimately done in though by a couple of wrong turns taken in the final reel by screenwriters Yuri Zeltser (Playmaker) and Cary Bickley (The Gun in Betty Lou’s Handbag). These missteps take what was otherwise a fascinating, riveting story and send it down the path of the mundane. Such a pity.

The film’s saving grace is the seemingly effortless work of Ashley Judd and Morgan Freeman in their second pairing since Kiss the Girls. Judd is able to play both the vulnerability of a woman in love as well as the toughness of a high powered trial attorney with equal effectiveness and it pays off for her in this role.

Freeman is, quite simply, a joy to watch. The traits and nuances that he layers upon his character give a richness and fullness to Grimes that make us feel like we’ve been watching him for years. Like an old friend, Grimes is a character with whom we look forward to spending some time.

Perhaps Freeman’s ability to make his character so accessible to us is what heightens our concern when we see Grimes risk his hard-earned sobriety in order to help Claire. Watching him in the beginning stages of the downward spiral of an alcoholic is painful but also spiritually poignant. The lesson to us is both clear and meaningful: If we reopen a door that we once ourselves closed, that which we had shut out will come back with a vengeance.

When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest; and finding none, he saith, I will return unto my house whence I came out. And when he cometh, he findeth it swept and garnished. Then goeth he, and taketh to him seven other spirits more wicked than himself; and they enter in, and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first. Luke 11:24-26 (KJV)

Grimes may have had the best of intentions when he took that first drink after 450 plus days of sobriety. But regardless of his intentions, the outcome was the same. Alcohol abuse has been the downfall of many potentially great men. Those who were strong enough to escape the snare of alcoholism once should never contemplate opening that door again. It just isn’t worth it.
