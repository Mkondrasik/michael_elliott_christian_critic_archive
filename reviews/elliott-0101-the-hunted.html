The Hunted

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 14, 2003
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Consequences; violence; sowing and reaping; responsibility

Author-verified transcription

The Hunted should have spent a bit more time hunting for a decent screenplay. As it is, it wastes the talents of three Oscar winners (Tommy Lee Jones, Benicio Del Toro, and director William Friedkin).

Benicio Del Toro (Traffic) plays Aaron Hallam, a governmentally trained military assassin, whose experiences in Kosovo apparently sent him over the edge. He’s back in the States now and people are dying in horrible, mutilating ways.

Brought in to hunt him is L.T. Bonham (Tommy Lee Jones, The Fugitive), the civilian contractor who trained Hallam and others like him. Bonham, now retired, is reluctant to return to this way of life but is driven because of his own feelings of guilt and culpability.

Leading the FBI investigation is the no-nonsense Abby Durrell (Connie Nielsen, One Hour Photo), but this film takes little note of the secondary characters. Its focus is on the man against man, teacher vs. student, hunter vs. hunted formula. Everyone else there exists mainly to spill blood.

And spill it they do... in large quantities. This is an extremely violent and bloody film. Hallam’s preferred weapon of choice is a knife, which he wields with incredible skill and deadly accuracy.

We follow Bonham as he tracks Hallam through woods of the Northwest Pacific as well as the streets of Portland, Oregon. While watching, one can’t help thinking that for two such accomplished trackers, they certainly don’t do a great job in covering their own tracks.

That is just one of the many inconsistencies or incredulities that mar the film. Others include the impossible lightning fast speed Hallam would have to possess in order to do the things he’s shown doing. The film also could have benefited from a bit more exploration of the characters’ relationship and history.

For instance, is Hallam a dangerous indiscriminate killer who needs to be stopped or is he merely acting in self-defense against a government who has grown embarrassed by him and wants him to “go away?” There is a hint at the latter but it is not clearly expressed nor developed. A pity, as this would have made for a far more compelling and dramatic hook to the story.

The film, while a disappointment, is far from a disaster. There is talent on display. Friedkin (The Exorcist) still knows how to shape scenes to maximize the tension and exploit the natural beauty of his surroundings. Much of The Hunted will remind us of his earlier (and superior) work. Certainly fragments of The Fugitive will come to mind throughout as well as certain scenes from The French Connection.

Tommy Lee Jones is very effective as a weary and guilty man who recognizes the responsibility he shares in the making of a man like Hallam. There apparently is a symbiotic father/son connection between the two characters, which is inferred by a couple of inappropriate references to the story of Abraham and Isaac. It is an angle which is quickly glossed over in favor of the hunt and chase.

Benicio Del Toro is less effective but only because his character is a bit of an enigma. We’re never quite sure what drives him and his performance doesn’t clarify matters. The only thing which is clear is the fact that Hallam is a product of that which was planted and cultivated within him. A man bred for violence will become a violent man.
