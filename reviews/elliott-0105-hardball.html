Hardball

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 14, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Corinthians 11:24-27Romans 8:35-371 John 4:4

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Perseverance; endurance; showing up; overcoming hardship

Author-verified transcription

While I can’t speak for the rest of the nation, here in Central Florida we recently fell in love with the boys of Apopka... the 2001 National Little League Champs. Those who might want the cheering to continue will welcome Hardball, a gritty little film about some inner city kids whose baseball team provides their only escape from the harsh ghetto reality of their lives.

Keanu Reeves (The Matrix) stars as Conor O’Neill, a loser in more than one sense of the word. When he loses, he loses big... to the tune of $12,000 which he can’t pay back to the bookies who took his action. Out of desperation, he accepts money to help coach a baseball team made up of economically disadvantaged kids from the projects of Chicago.

What happens next is as predictable as a seventh inning stretch. Conor gains a newfound sense of direction and responsibility; the boys gain relief from a life of gang wars, drive-by shootings and abject hopelessness. It’s Bad News Bears with a harder edge.

Inspired by the non-fiction account, “Hardball: A Season in the Projects” by Daniel Coyle, Hardball somewhat sanitizes the more unsavory elements of life in the projects, presumably in order to get its PG-13 rating. No matter... what it does show or infer is sufficient to make the point and move the story to its formulaic conclusion.

It should be noted that Coyle’s book was, in part, based upon the experiences of Bob Muzikowski who has unsuccessfully tried to block the release of this film, citing that it misrepresents both him and his league.

As audience members, all we can judge is the film itself. And as a film, Hardball manages to score enough runs to make it a winner. It’s not a shut out by any means but neither is it a no-hitter.

Keanu Reeves plays Conor in his readily familiar style which will surely please his fans and infuriate his detractors. Diane Lane is competent but wasted in a negligible role as an attractive white schoolteacher trying to reach these inner city youths.

The kids play a large part in making the film as effective as it is. Bold, brash and sassy to cover up the fear and insecurity they all feel, they drop their facades to show the pure joy that comes when they begin playing ball. In the midst of their poverty-stricken, crime-ridden lives, baseball is something that remains pure and untarnished... allowing them to be kids while showing them a glimpse of a life where fear isn’t a constant reality.

The film does have its faults. This paint-by-the-numbers inspirational tale manages precious few surprises and we can generally stay two or more steps ahead of the action. The film does take a tragic turn in the final reel which is used as a catalyst to cement the bond forming between the irresponsible Conor and the young black boys who look up to him. As manipulative as this device is... it still works.

The underlying message of the film is the lesson that Conor learns from these underprivileged boys who comprise his team. Win or lose... the most important thing in life is to show up.

There is no guarantee... no promise... indeed, no indication from the Word of God that life is fair. Life is anything but fair. But giving up is no solution. Despite all that “life” throws at us, we have the ability to meet it head-on, work through it and rise above it... if we don’t give up. Look at what Paul endured:

Of the Jews five times received I forty stripes save one. Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned, thrice I suffered shipwreck, a night and a day I have been in the deep; In journeyings often, in perils of waters, in perils of robbers, in perils by mine own countrymen, in perils by the heathen, in perils in the city, in perils in the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among false brethren; In weariness and painfulness, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness. 2 Corinthians 11:24-27 (KJV)

All this, and yet because of his perseverance and willingness to “show up” for God despite the opposition, God was able to work within him to bring forth the greatest revelation known to man today.
