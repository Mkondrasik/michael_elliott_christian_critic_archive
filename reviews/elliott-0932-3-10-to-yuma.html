3:10 to Yuma

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 7, 2007
Rating
3½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 57 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Good vs. Evil

Author-verified transcription

Good genres never die... they simply get remade. The Hollywood western rides again - this time in the guise of a revamped 1957 horse opera called (then and now) 3:10 to Yuma.

In the remake, Dan Evers (Christian Bale) is a wounded Confederate War veteran trying to scrape out a meager living as a rancher in drought-plagued Arizona. On the verge of losing his ranch, his wife, and what little respect his two boys give him, Dan accepts a financially generous offer for what nearly everyone considers a suicide mission.

He's to escort Ben Wade (Russell Crowe), a notorious killer and thief, to a train that will take him to a federal penitentiary. Between them and the tracks wait Wade's cutthroat gang who have no intention of letting that happen.

What follows is a dusty, dirty morality tale that pits a good but flawed man against a man who has given himself over to pure evil. And as old-fashioned as the formula is, it still works. 3:10 to Yuma is as engrossing a movie as it gets.

Director James Mangold stacks his deck well by casting brilliantly. Russell Crowe is one of the smartest actors working today and he brings his undeniable talent to full bear as a charming, Bible quoting sociopath.

Balanced against this character is an equally intelligent performance from Christian Bale. As the one legged war veteran with an unshakable sense of doing what is right, Bale anchors the film and keeps it grounded.

These two actors are so true to their characters which are so resolutely set in their diametrically opposing ways that the film becomes the classic struggle between an irresistible force and an unmovable object. As the film winds to its rousing close, the resolution may confuse or even upset people but it remains true to the characters; to the nature of man; and to the power that both good and evil wield.

In addition to Crowe and Bale, Mangold is blessed to have a number of supporting actors who likewise bring significant quality to the production - most notably Ben Foster, who plays Wade's right hand man. His is a chilling and star-making performance.

So we're left with the $64,000 question: Who wins in a contest between good and evil? We all know what we want the answer to be but life's experiences tell us that evil sometimes wins. Or does it?

As we stand resolute in our spiritual and moral conviction - despite what evil may be done to us - we do win. And our stand has an impact on those who witness it - even upon our enemies.

Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good. Romans 12:21 (KJV)

The only sure way to overcome evil is to not succumb to it. Not that it is easy. It is hard to live by a set of principles when the harsh reality is that those principles may set us against a stronger and more ruthless opponent. But the promise of God is that as we continue to stand upon what is right and just in His eyes, He stands with us and rewards our conviction.

We may lose a battle or two, but our faithfulness to do what is right guarantees us the ultimate victory.
