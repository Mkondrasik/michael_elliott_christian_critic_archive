Author-verified transcription

In the book of Ephesians, God refers to marriage as “a great mystery.” He knows of which he speaks. How to get two people with different backgrounds and separate lives to come together and form the “one flesh” relationship that God designed has baffled more than one couple. Mr. & Mrs. Smith explores the concept of marriage using the unlikeliest example - a pair of assassins who have kept their professional lives hidden from each other during their 5 or 6 years together.

We first meet John & Jane Smith (Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie) in a counseling session where it becomes clear that whatever sparks drew them together in the first place have long since been extinguished. When their competing firms assign them to the same target, they discover the truth about their spouse's occupation. In order to preserve their secret identities, John and Jane know that they must kill each other.

The funny thing is, now that the truth has come to light and all the lies and deceptions have ceased, their once dead relationship shows renewed signs of life. The question remains: “Will they, as so many other couples must do, work through their problems to save their marriage? Or will they opt for the ’till death do us part’ clause in their vows?”

The clever wordplay and banter between Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie is what makes this movie work. Placing their tabloid exploits aside, the two actors do have an on-screen chemistry that works and they use it to great effect in this film. They have to. There's not much else in it.

Vince Vaughn and Kerry Washington have miniscule roles as best friends and co-workers to the Smiths. There's also an army of nameless, faceless bodies to count as the Smiths do their semi-automatic work. But the film belongs to the movie star couple and their director Doug Liman (The Bourne Identity) who keeps the action moving at a decent pace.

The screenplay by Simon Kinberg (xXx: State of the Union) is clever in how it takes topics of discussion familiar to couples and twists them to fit the unusual scenario that serves as the framework of the film. For instance, after John learns of the truth he begins grilling his wife for details about her past. He's not interested in how many men she slept with. He wants to know how many she killed and has a comically tough time handling the fact that she's more experienced than he.

As implausible as the Smith's relationship may be, the film does offer an ounce of truth in how it depicts marriage. While every marriage is different as respective couples make the minute, day-to-day adjustments needed to keep their union intact, there is a fundamental element that must be present in all.

A marriage based upon lies and deceptions is doomed to fail. God designed the relationship so that each partner would be strengthened by the addition of the other. It is far more than merely living together under a single roof. It is achieving a state of like-mindedness that extends to every aspect of life.

The “one flesh” analogy that God uses throughout His Word vividly communicates how deeply we are to reach an accord with our husband or wife. We won’t achieve that goal through the telling of lies or the pretenses that we put on for show. Honesty and truth are the building blocks for an abiding love.

Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband. Ephesians 5:33 (KJV)
