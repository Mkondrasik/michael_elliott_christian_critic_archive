Author-verified transcription

A running gag in Jay and Silent Bob Strike Back is built upon the often stated opinion that only an idiot would try to make a movie starring this pair of hapless losers who between them can barely find a functional brain cell. Leave it to Kevin Smith (Dogma) to not only make such a film, but call himself an idiot in the process.

Jay and Silent Bob Strike Back can be best likened to a Hope/Crosby road picture... if Hope and Crosby were sex-crazed, vocabulary-challenged pot heads. Using the movie within a movie convention, Smith goofs on Hollywood using profanity, sex, drugs, and certain bodily functions to propel his humor. It is said that, following this film, Smith will be retiring these characters who have appeared in every Smith movie since Clerks. As a kind of swan song, he has decided to make this film primarily to reward his ever-faithful fans.

Jay (Jason Mewes) and Silent Bob (Kevin Smith) have admittedly provided some amusing moments in previous films (ones in which they weren't the central attraction). By seeing these guys in a starring role, we discover that a little Jay and Silent Bob goes a long way but a lot of Jay and Silent Bob gets tedious and repetitive.

The film only makes a lame attempt at providing a story to follow. Basically, the titular characters (I'll pause while the "Jay emulators" chortle over the use of that adjective) find out that a Bluntman and Chronic movie is being made. Since these comic book characters were originally based upon their personas, they decide to set out for Hollywood and stop the movie from being made. It seems they don't like being the subject of "personal attacks" by web nerds talking about the film in Internet chat rooms.

On the way to Hollywood, they get sidetracked by a quartet of sexy jewel thieves who need a patsy to frame; chased by a Federal Wildlife Officer in search of an orangutan (which they happened to have ape-napped)... ah, heck. Why bother trying to outline this plot? It is lame, nonsensical and, when all is said and done, irrelevant. Here is the angle that Kevin Smith is playing: The hook is the characters of Jay and Silent Bob who in previous films have proven to be audience favorites. The bait is a vast array of celebrity cameo appearances.

Some of the celebs who must owe Smith a favor are: Shannen Doherty, Ben Affleck, James Van Der Beek, Jason Biggs, Matt Damon, Joey Lauren Adams, Eliza Dushku, Carrie Fisher, Ali Larter, Jason Lee, George Carlin, Will Ferrell, Chris Rock, Judd Nelson, Seann William Scott, Mark Hamill, Jon Stewart, Morris Day, Wes Craven, Gus Van Sant, and Shannon Elizabeth. Also appearing are Brian O'Halloran and Jeff Anderson who reprise their Clerks' roles of Dante and Randal.

The celebrity-studded bait is tempting. And Smith manages to poke fun at the Hollywood scene, even including a few jabs at his own projects. Audiences should be advised that the film is rated R for nonstop crude and sexual humor, pervasive strong language, and drug content. Nonstop is definitely not an exaggeration. The humor is admittedly regressive, juvenile, unashamedly crude, and totally pervasive.

The old saying: "Tis better to be silent and be thought a fool, than to speak and remove all doubt" becomes clearly understood when watching the characters of Jay and Silent Bob do their thing.

Certainly, due to his nonstop stream of profanity-ridden observations, Jay leaves no doubt as to his foolishness. Silent Bob, who chooses to hold his tongue throughout most of the film, comes across as infinitely more intelligent than Jay, although this cannot really be considered as a particularly difficult feat.

Abe Lincoln, who has been attributed with the above quote, may have framed his remark after reading his Bible:

Even a fool, when he holdeth his peace, is counted wise: and he that shutteth his lips is esteemed a man of understanding. Proverbs 17:28 (KJV)
