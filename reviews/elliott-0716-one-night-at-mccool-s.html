Author-verified transcription

Men can be jerks... especially where woman are concerned. One Night at McCool's tells the story of three such jerks and the woman who drove them to their ruin.

Three quarters of the movie is told in a jumpy flashback fashion as each of the men relate their sordid version of the story to their corresponding "confessor." Bartender Randy (Matt Dillon, Wild Things) is telling his tale to the hit man he hired (Michael Douglas, Traffic); Lawyer Carl (Paul Reiser, Mad About You) is pouring his heart out to his therapist (country singer Reba McEntire); and Detective Dehling (John Goodman, Coyote Ugly) is easing his conscience by confessing to his priest (Richard Jenkins, Say It Isn't So.)

The woman in question is the sultry Jewel Valentine (Liv Tyler, Cookie's Fortune) and she is nothing but trouble. She costs Randy his job and his home; she has the married Carl dressing up in leather and chains for a s/m session; she leads Dehling to destroy evidence in a murder investigation. All this because she has the one thing that they each want... sex.

With her voluptuous curves, her baby-doll voice, and her enthusiastic willingness to "get physical," she has these men doing her bidding in an almost Pavlovian response. What they are willing to overlook is that she is also a scheming, manipulative, materialistic murderess who uses them to get what she wants. Did I happen to mention that this is a comedy?

The screenplay by the late Stan Seidel (TV's True Colors) jumps around so much between the three storytellers that the movie isn't able develop a proper rhythm. While there are humorous moments, they never build to become more than unconnected bits of silliness.

Liv Tyler is able to vamp into the sensual nature of her character but she fails to give any deeper insight as to who Jewel is and why she's decided to saddle up with the pathetic losers that these three men appear to be. If she is such a desirable creature, I would think she could do better.

Paul Reiser's character is merely an excuse for a few sight gags revolving around kinky sex. It is highly forgettable stuff which is far beneath his comedic talent. Matt Dillon is more palatable as the simple-minded bartender whose life Jewel turns upside down. John Goodman plays an emotionally vulnerable cop who sees Jewel as the image of his dead wife proving that lust can get someone to see whatever it is he wants to see.

Michael Douglas's character as the contract killer consists merely of an ugly shirt and an uglier hairpiece. Surprisingly, the most effective acting job in the film comes from Andrew Dice Clay (The Adventures of Ford Fairlane) who appears briefly as Jewel's first boyfriend (and victim).

Director Harald Zwart makes his feature film debut with One Night at McCool's and he's left plenty of room for improvement. For a comedy, there is an unusually high amount of violence, some of it bloody, and it should be evident that there are many scenes which contain sexual acts, dialogue, or references.

For every good and beautiful thing that God has created, the devil promotes his own twisted perversion of that truth to deceive and confuse us. God desires that we live a life of love; our spiritual adversary desires that we settle for a life of lust. Love has positive benefits; lust does not.

But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death. James 1:14-15 (KJV)

Every major character in this film operates out of lust. The men lust for Jewel and Jewel lusts for material possessions. As the scripture promises, when lust is in the picture, sin isn't going to be too far behind. Bottom line: There's not much that is "cool" about One Night at McCool's.
