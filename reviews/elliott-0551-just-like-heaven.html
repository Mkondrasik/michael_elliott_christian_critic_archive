Author-verified transcription

Sometimes, people in love are prone to exclaim, “I can’t live without you.” For these folks, Just Like Heaven is a movie sent from heaven.

Dr. Elizabeth Masterson (Reese Witherspoon) is a dedicated medical professional. She is so focused upon earning a spot as a resident in a San Francisco hospital that she thinks nothing of putting in 26 hour long days. Needless to say, her love life is non-existent.

Involved in a horrendous car accident on the way to her sister’s house, Elizabeth appears at her old apartment… despite the fact that she’s not quite all there. Her spirit (which looks remarkably like her) runs into the new tenant of her furnished apartment.

In some ways David Abbott (Mark Ruffalo) is just as disconnected from life as she. He’s in a grieving state due to his wife’s death a few years earlier. He’s withdrawn from human contact and spends most of his time drinking beer and watching television. That is to say, he does until Elizabeth walks through the wall and into his life.

After the ensuing confusion has died down a bit, these two “half souls” finally realize that they are whole only when they are around each other. Of course, there is one small problem: how are they to meet up in the same plane of existence so they might have a chance at forming a more substantial relationship?

Reese Witherspoon is always a joy to watch on the screen. Fresh and immensely likable, we warm up to her instantly. She has a bit of trouble navigating the early stages of her character’s disembodied life but it passes quickly and we immediately forgive the lapse as she moves on to more familiar ground.

Mark Ruffalo has the opposite problem. His character, as written, is, at first, cold, distant, and unsocial. Not a heck of a lot he can do with that and it shows in his performance. But as time goes by and his relationship with Elizabeth deepens, so does our interest in his character. By the end of the film, we are rooting for these two souls to find the peace and contentment they have long been without.

The screenplay by Peter Tolan and Leslie Dixon is a bit wooden at times and doesn’t always flow from scene to scene with the best of grace. But it does build steam as the film reaches its climax and there are one or two plot twists that are enjoyable surprises.

Elizabeth and David fell into the same trap – albeit from different directions. She became so obsessed with her career that she failed to recognize that work was only part of the life experience. He so loved his wife that when she died, he stopped living as well… not physically, but in every other way that matters.

Life is filled with choices. We make them every day. The greatest choice that faces us is what we do with the gift of life that God has given us. We may face obstacles, temptations, disappointments, setbacks, and more but nothing should stand in the way of living every day to its fullest. God would have us do nothing less.

For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith. 1 John 5:4 (KJV)

We have been granted a rare gift. We have the strength, power, and ability to live our lives the way they should be lived – the way God intended for us when he breathed life into our bodies. We can do all things through Christ who strengthens us.
