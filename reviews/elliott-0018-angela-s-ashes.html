Angela's Ashes

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 21, 2000
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Psalms 37:25 1 Timothy 4:15-16 Philippians 4:19

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: God's provision; righteousness; faithfulness; perseverance

Author-verified transcription

Angela's Ashes is an amazing study in contradictions. Beautiful yet dreary, amusing yet heartbreakingly sad, inspiring yet depressing, irreverent yet devoutly religious. It is a delicately crafted film which tells the story of a miserable childhood. Or, as the narrator explains, something which is worse... a miserable Irish childhood. Worse than that, he continues... a miserable Irish Catholic childhood.

Based on the best selling and Pulitzer Prize winning autobiographical novel by Frank McCourt, it is one of the most successful screen adaptations of a major literary work this reviewer has seen in years.

Set in the 1930's and 40's it follows young Frank from the age of 5 until about the age of 16. The film begins in Brooklyn, NY, where life was hard for an immigrant family of seven. The struggling McCourt family decide to leave America after the death of their baby daughter and return to Ireland where, two deaths later, it becomes apparent that life would not be getting any easier.

In Limerick, they are reluctantly given shelter by the family of Frank's mother, Angela, whose marriage to a charming but completely useless man (a Protestant no less) from Northern Ireland has never been fully accepted by the staunch Roman Catholics of the South. Constantly out of work (and in the pubs), his "addiction to the drink" is the key element keeping his family in squalor and abject poverty.

As Frank grows, we follow his many experiences: First communion, first (second and third) confession, first job, first love, first drink. As he comes of age, he develops a growing awareness that if he is to obtain a future worth having, he will need to leave Ireland and his loving but pathetically suffering family behind and make his way back to the promised land of America.

Director Alan Parker (Evita) owes a great deal of the film's success to his casting directors (John Hubbard, Ros Hubbard and Juliet Taylor), all of whom have worked with him previously. The faces and talent which populate the screen are marvelous, fitting with both the time and setting of the film. Each of the three actors playing Frank are superb and fully believable as the same character at different ages.

One of Mr. Parker's concerns was that the audience would be so taken with the performance of one of the "Franks", that they would resent moving on to an older version played by a different actor. The subsequent care he took in selecting his actors and crafting his transitions eliminated all need for concern. The performances of newcomer Joseph Breen as Young Frank, Ciaran Owens (Agnes Browne) as Middle Frank, and Michael Legge (Straydogs) as Older Frank are each enjoyable and winning in their own right.

Emily Watson (Hilary and Jackie), as Angela, gives a generously restrained performance as does Robert Carlyle (Ravenous) who plays her feckless husband. Neither allows their character to hide or, for that matter, trumpet their defects. They provide honest and devastatingly real portraits of tragically flawed individuals.

Filmed in Ireland, the filmmakers received little assistance or support from the local Roman Catholic church leaders, who, it is said, did not take kindly to the depiction they received at the hands of Mr. McCourt. Seen in the film as a religion which uses and excuses guilt, pain, and intolerance to goad their followers into doctrinal obedience, their lack of enthusiasm for this project is understandable.

The religious beliefs of the McCourt family obviously did nothing to rescue them from the hard and abusive life we see unfolding on screen. And yet, God's Word promises us that He will supply all our need.

I have been young, and now am old; yet have I not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed begging bread. Psalms 37:25 (KJV)

The "righteous" are those who have learned to live by God's standards, claiming His deliverance daily by the faithful adherence to His truth. The simple application of biblical principle in one's life will bring more victory and success than an entire lifetime of fear.
