Undercover Brother

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 30, 2002
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 28 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Race; Appearances; Stereotypes

Author-verified transcription

Look out Shaft... Move over Cleopatra Jones... The baddest, hippest, coolest, and yes, most ridiculous black action star is heading for the screen. Undercover Brother takes a comic but not at all unflatteringly look at the blaxploitation films of the 1970s and 80s.

Eddie Griffin (The New Guy) stars as Undercover Brother, a African-American do-gooder who joins a secret organization known as B.R.O.T.H.E.R.H.O.O.D. He must try and stop a fiendish plot concocted by The Man (an actual character who is seen only in shadows). With help from his fellow agents, Sistah Woman (Aunjanue Ellis, Men of Honor), Smart Brother (Gary Anthony Williams, Malcolm in the Middle), Conspiracy Brother (Dave Chappelle, Blue Streak), and Lance the token white intern (Neil Patrick Harris, Doogie Howser MD), Undercover Brother must infiltrate The Man's organization and stop him from turning the entire black community into a culture as white and bland as Wonder Bread and mayonnaise.

It is an assignment not without peril. The Man and his sidekick, Mr. Feather (Chris Kattan, Corky Romano) are well aware of the black man's "kryptonite" and so dispatches the White She Devil (Denise Richards, Drop Dead Gorgeous) to work her wiles on the unsuspecting and totally susceptible agent.

Will Undercover Brother maintain his blackness or will he lose himself in the toasted cheese sandwiches and penny loafers that permeate the land of the Caucasians? Will The Man succeed in turning the black community's hope of a presidential candidate into a successful fried chicken salesman? These are the burning questions of the film and they are ones which are asked with tongue firmly planted in cheek.

The success of Undercover Brother is found in its ability to spoof both black and white stereotypes equally. Whether poking fun at the white man's attempts to incorporate black culture into his own or skewering many of the black man's images of himself, the film has crossover appeal for audiences of all races.

Eddie Griffin is effective as both the black superspy as well as his undercover persona, the yuppiefied and Oreo-like (white on the inside) Anton Jackson complete with a whiny, nasally voice, tennis sweaters, and a complete absence of "soul."

The supporting cast also performs well, especially Chi McBride (The Kid) as the gruff but lovable Chief of B.R.O.T.H.E.R.H.O.O.D, and Dave Chappelle as the cynical and suspicious Conspiracy Brother who sees everything that happens as being proof of the white man's relentless attempts to keep the "brothers" down.

Undercover Brother has an ample supply of sexual innuendoes, equivalent to the films which serve as source material for its spoofs. It also uses, for comic effect, many of the cheesy cinematic techniques of the past such as split screens and slo-mo action, all underscored by a funky bass-heavy musical score.

Within this good-natured but sometimes risqué film lies a considerable amount of honesty in the way it ridicules stereotypes. Too often our perception of an entire culture or race is based on a lack of experiential knowledge. Instead of looking beneath the surface, searching for that which we might have in common, we rush to a conclusion based upon the differences that are readily apparent on the surface. To do so is an injustice to those we unduly judge.

God has called all of mankind to join as one body. He makes no distinction as to race, culture, or creed. Everyone who would respond to His call is welcome to His house. We who reside there must be waiting with open arms to accept all who seek Him, regardless of their past or outward appearance.

For God shows no partiality (undue favor or unfairness; with Him one man is not different from another.) Romans 2:11 (Amplified)
