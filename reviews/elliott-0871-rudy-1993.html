Author-verified transcription

As long as he can remember, Rudy Ruettiger (Sean Astin, The Lord of the Rings) has wanted to play football at the University of Notre Dame. Only a few things stood in his way: He didn't have the size; he didn't have the talent; he didn't have the grades; and he didn't have the money. What he did have was heart... and that made up for all that he lacked.

Rudy is a testament to anyone who not only has a dream but also the willingness to work hard to achieve it. Although it smacks of an inspirational tale concocted by Hollywood writers, Rudy is based on a true story and many of the specific events depicted in the film actually happened.

After dreaming about attending Notre Dame for years, a tragic accident in his home town convinces Rudy that he had better act if that dream were ever to become a reality. Walking onto the Notre Dame campus with a duffle bag, a thousand dollars, and his dream, Rudy begins the long and arduous road of fighting against the odds.

Along the way, he is befriended by many "mentors" who help Rudy to grow and achieve small victories which move him closer to his desired goal. The campus priest (Robert Prosky, Hill Street Blues), recognizing his determination, arranges for him to attend a nearby community college. If he spends a couple of years there and if his grades are good enough he'll be able to submit an application to Notre Dame.

At the small college, he meets D-Bob (Jon Favreau, Swingers), an upperclassman who tutors Rudy and recognizes that he has a slight learning disability. Together, they manage to overcome that obstacle and Rudy's grades begin to improve.

At Notre Dame, Rudy forms a relationship with Fortune (Charles Dutton, Roc), a janitor and groundskeeper for the stadium who recognizes Rudy's dream. He gives Rudy a job, a place to stay, and advice that further strengthens his resolve. And the Notre Dame coaches recognize Rudy's heart and find him a place on the scrub team which helps the first string players prepare for their games.

Sean Astin does a fine, understated job as Rudy, a young man with meager abilities who doesn't let that fact stand in his way. Ned Beatty (Deliverance) plays Rudy's father, a Notre Dame supporter who must work three jobs just to support his family of 14 children.

Director David Anspaugh and screenwriter Angelo Pizzo last teamed up for the 1986 inspirational sports film Hoosiers and this film bears a striking resemblance to it. Both feature underdogs whose discipline, drive and hard work allow them to compete at levels far above their natural abilities.

The principle that Rudy illustrates is rarely promoted to a generation of people so readily obsessed with instant gratification. Nothing about Rudy's story is instant.

As the film depicts, each action he takes is a small, hard-earned step towards his heart's desire. His mentors may have helped to guide and support him with their advice and encouragement, but everything that Rudy accomplished came about as a result of his faithfulness to continually work towards his goal. He simply refused to quit or abandon his dream, despite the obstacles and naysayers who tried to discourage him by saying that it was not attainable.

God knows the desires of our hearts and He wants us to realize them. Throughout His Word, He tells us how we can.

Jesus said unto him, If thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth. Mark 9:23 (KJV)

Believing entails more than sitting at home waiting for God to give us what we want. Believing requires that we take action. Just as Rudy never gave up on his goal to play football for Notre Dame and proved himself willing to apply himself with his whole heart and soul to achieve that goal, so must we fully apply ourselves to accomplish the goals we have set. As we believe and frame our doings according to that belief, all things are indeed possible.
