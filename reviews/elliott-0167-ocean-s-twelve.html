Ocean's Twelve

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 10, 2004
Rating
1 star
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Corinthians 10:12Luke 18:11Philippians 2:3

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Comparison; ego; pride; competition; humility

Author-verified transcription

The charming master thief Danny Ocean (George Clooney) and his gang are back. This time, they're out to steal some box office gold with Ocean's Twelve. If they succeed, it will be by the sheer force of celebrity drawing power because this sequel is an over-padded, sleep-inducing bore.

Someone has tipped off Terry Benedict (Andy Garcia) the whereabouts of the now scattered Ocean's Eleven. He visits each of them personally to deliver a message: "Return to him the money they stole from him with interest or suffer the consequences."

They reconvene to discuss their options which, for some reason, are limited to one. They decide to steal the money to pay off Benedict. After all, it's only $160 million and they have all of two weeks in which to do it.

So off they fly to Europe (figuring they're too "hot" to work in America) in search of a big score. What they find is competition. A talented thief known as the "Night Fox" (Vincent Cassel) has a knack for selecting the same targets as Ocean's crew.

With the deadline fast approaching, the movie boils down to a winner take all gamble involving the theft of a priceless Faberge egg.

Ocean's Twelve fails to connect with the "Rat Pack" coolness of the original film. This time, these rats have attached themselves to a sinking ship. The script by George Nolfi is cumbersome and complicated. The set up takes too long to establish and the payoff is incomplete and unsatisfying.

Of the cast, Matt Damon and Vincent Cassel seem to be the only ones working at portraying a character. The rest (Clooney, Julia Roberts, Catherine Zeta Jones, etc) seem content to ride the celebrity train and collect their high dollar paycheck for simply walking in front of the camera. In fact, the screenplay provides a scripted sequence that incorporates this fact resulting in the film's single moment of light hearted fun.

All in all, there's a certain smugness in the performances that I found to be off-putting. I hear that the cast had a grand old time together while making the film. It is too bad that fun didn't translate to the screen.

A core element to Ocean's Twelve is the competitive relationship between the Night Fox and Danny Ocean. The former seems overly concerned with proving to all that he is the better thief. All his notoriety, incredible (stolen) wealth, and lavish lifestyle mean nothing to him if he isn't recognized at being the best in his field.

This kind of ego is unhealthy and unproductive. The only real competition we have in this life is the one we have with ourselves. Are we being the best that we can be in every situation? If we compare ourselves to others we are establishing a false set of standards that will eventually lead us away from success and the goals that we should be setting for ourselves.

For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise. 2 Corinthians 10:12 (KJV)
