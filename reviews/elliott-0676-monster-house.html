Author-verified transcription

The process of moving through puberty is hard enough without your next door neighbor's house trying to eat you. Monster House, an animated horror film for kids, taps into the nebulous world of the "tweener" to deliver frights for all ages both real and fantasized.

DJ (Mitchel Musso) is a typical kid who is on the road to becoming a "grown-up." Voice cracking; noticing girls; he's even contemplating skipping the annual Halloween 'trick or treat' in his neighborhood on the grounds that he may be too old for such childish traditions.

His best friend, Chowder (Sam Lerner) isn't as developmentally advanced. He's still a boy in every sense of the word and is left speechless at the thought of missing out on all that free candy.

The debate over trick or treating is quickly forgotten when Mr. Nebbercracker, DJ's crotchety old neighbor drops dead while yelling at him for retrieving a ball that had accidentally landed on his yard. Filled with remorse for "killing" the old coot, DJ suspects that Nebbercracker's spirit now inhabits his house and will devour any trick or treaters foolish enough to come to its door.

DJ and Chowder become the neighborhood watch to protect unsuspecting kids from the supernatural harm that awaits them. Their first opportunity as saviors comes when Jenny (Spencer Locke), a girl from a neighboring prep school, gets too close to the hungry 2-story abode. She joins their cause and the frightening adventure begins in earnest.

And frightening it is. For a PG rating, the level of intensity is surprisingly high. Young children may easily be disturbed by the images, music, and thematic content of this film. Adults may even find themselves biting a fingernail or two.

The animation, using the same motion-capture that was used in The Polar Express is amazingly realistic. But what sets this film above the bar is the uncanny depiction of that very difficult age between child and man. The characterizations of the changing DJ, the repressed Chowder, and the already matured Jenny are pitch perfect and is sure to cause audiences to reminisce of their own childhood journeys.

The adult actors cast in supporting roles more than measure up to their underage co-stars. Steve Buscemi straddles the fence between menacing and pathetically sad as the lonely and crabby neighbor. Jon Heder has an amusing cameo as a video game guru who gives DJ much needed advice. Maggie Gyllenhaal adds the right tone as DJ's self-absorbed babysitter.

The life of the house, DJ is told, is contained in its "heart." The same can be said of man. Our lives consist of what is in our hearts. I speak not of the physical organ that pumps blood throughout our body but rather of our figurative heart that represents our core belief.

For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he. Proverbs 23:7a (KJV)

What we believe in the very core of our soul is who we are. This is why so much of God's exhortation to us is to drive His Word so deeply into our minds that it lives within every corner of our heart. Whole heart believing is the most powerful tool in the great tool belt of resources that God has given His people. Most of us have experienced it in one form or another. It is available to master in all areas of life and in every situation we may encounter.
