Beyond Narnia

Michael Elliott

December 9, 2005
Rating

1 hr: 0 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Imagination; problem of evil; suffering; free will; faith and comfort

Author-verified transcription

Fans of C.S. Lewis or just those who appreciate his work and might desire to know more about the life behind the legacy would do well to take in the new docudrama, Beyond Narnia.

Produced by Faith and Values Media for a television broadcast, Beyond Narnia is a no-nonsense, bare bones production that gives the factual details behind the life of one of the more influential Christian writers of the day.

Almost entirely narrated by Anton Rodgers who plays the part of Lewis, the film takes on a dry and almost sluggish pace as we are taken methodically (and chronologically) through a happy childhood interrupted by the death of his mother; a search for intellectual satisfaction; a denial of God as well as the eventual rediscovery of Him; his time spent at Oxford University and his friendship with J.R.R. Tolkien. We see the inspiration behind the birth of his Narnia stories. The film culminates with his surprising discovery of romantic love late in his life as American poet Joy Davidman (Diane Venora) arrives in London with her two boys and eventually inserts herself into his heart.

It is a handsomely mounted production (albeit rather staid). Its one major drawback is an over-reliance on a voice over narration to tell the story. However, for those seeking a simple primer on the life of C.S. Lewis, Bevond Narnia would be a good place to start.

Anton Rodgers does quite well with the role, approaching it as a character looking back with some bemusement on the journey he has just taken. He captures the quirky humor of the man and is very touching while retelling the moments of tragedy that visited his life.

The film could have benefited from a bit more meat. Whole sections of Lewis's life and relationships are skipped over or brushed aside. Most disappointing was the short shrift given to on Lewis's transformation from devout atheist to confirmed Christian. It was a bit too simplistically handled, missing an opportunity to delve into the depths of the man that made him a beloved figure throughout the Christian world.

Viewers might also be surprised to find that The Chronicles of Narnia, though referred to frequently, is not covered in much detail. The mini-film is far more interested in the man than his works and while it provides only a thumbnail sketch of the literary giant, it does so with great sincerity and respect.

During his life, Lewis wrestled with a question that has bewildered many men before him and will continue to do so long after his death: "How could God be good, when all evidence we see is to the contrary?" In other words, if God is good why do bad things happen?

It was this question that caused Lewis to first walk away from his religious upbringing. What he came to realize, with the help of Tolkien and other Christian friends, is that the existence of evil does not preclude the existence of God. There is no contradiction present. God is good and all powerful and yet evil does exist. It has to if we are to have free will to choose between the two.

I form the light, and create darkness: 1 make peace, and create evil: I the LORD do all these things. Isaiah 45:7 (KJV)

We do have a spiritual adversary in this world that manipulates the systems of this world and causes evil to flourish - the same evil that God must permit as an option. But we have the ability to shun it; to choose not to bend to its temptations or its power.

Our faith may remain steadfast even in the face of tragic events or grievous times. We know that God has no hand in such things and as we turn to Him, we find the comfort and the hope that enables us to bear them.
