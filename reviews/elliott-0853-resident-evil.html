Author-verified transcription

Video games seem to have progressed quite a bit since my college days of playing Pong. It is obvious that it has not been all for the better. Resident Evil, a film based upon a best-selling video game series by Capcom, is nonstop carnage featuring flesh eating zombies, a homicidal computer and a horrific looking mutated creature known as The Licker. It's a far cry from Ms. Pac Man.

In Resident Evil, the Umbrella Corporation is a multinational conglomerate which, among its other interests, runs the Hive, an underground facility doing research and experimentation in genetic engineering. When the plant suffers a viral contamination, the massive super computer known as the Red Queen shuts the Hive down, killing the hundreds of staff members who were employed there.

A team of paramilitary types storm the Hive with the intent to take the Red Queen off-line and find out what went wrong. Upon their arrival, they discover that not only are the staff workers not dead... they are "undead". Turned into zombies with limited brain power driven by the simplest of human needs - hunger. The commando team is the fresh meat they've been craving.

The commando team must stay ahead of the zombies, avoid the elaborate defense mechanism of the Red Queen, and reach the surface before the complex is sealed off and traps them inside. They do this by running from room to room, screaming and yelling and firing round after round at attackers that just won't stay dead. Did I mention receiving just one scratch from a zombie is enough to turn you into one? That's an important thing to know.

Milla Jovovich (Zoolander) stars as Alice, a kickboxing amnesiac found at the entrance of the Hive and brought along by the commando unit. Michelle Rodriguez (The Fast and the Furious) is Rain, a machine gun-toting commando with a bad attitude. Rounding out the entree selections on the zombie buffet bar are Eric Mabius (Cruel Intentions), James Purefoy (A Knight's Tale), Martin Crewes (in his film debut), and Colin Salmon (Tomorrow Never Dies).

Writer/director Paul W.S. Anderson, who previously adapted the video game Mortal Kombat for the screen, reportedly took great care to stay true to the story line and game elements of Resident Evil while including enough background information that non-game players would know what's going on.

The production values of the film are high. Sets, special effects, makeup, and music all contribute to the overall effect which is obviously to scare or creep out the audience while keeping them fidgeting on the edge of their seats. Frankly, it wouldn't be my preferred way to spend 100 minutes or $7.00 but fans of the genre will most likely disagree with me. I found its incessant macabre violence to be both repetitive and tiresome.

The depiction of the zombies, whose relentless pursuit is driven by a hunger that will never be satisfied, is reminiscent of our own spiritual adversary with one important exception... the devil isn't brain dead. He's smart and wily, and he wants to devour us with the same level of intensity as the undead of the film.

And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: Luke 22:31 (KJV)

Our protection lies not in man's firepower or super human martial arts abilities. What keeps this world's resident evil at bay is the spiritual power that is resident within us.

Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy: and nothing shall by any means hurt you. Luke 10:19 (KJV)
