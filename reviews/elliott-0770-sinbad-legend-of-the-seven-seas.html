Dreamworks mixes a heavy dose of mythology with a healthy portion of swashbuckling adventure in their newest animated tale, Sinbad: Legend of the Seven Seas.

In the film, Sinbad (voiced by Brad Pitt, The Mexican) is a charming, personable pirate out to steal the Book of Peace, a light-emitting tome which has kept the Twelve Cities protected lo these many years. It is being guarded by his childhood friend, Proteus (voiced by Joseph Fiennes, Shakespeare in Love) as it makes its way to its new home in Syracuse.

Unfortunately for Sinbad, his every movement is being watched by Eris, the ethereal goddess of Chaos (voiced by Michelle Pfeiffer, White Oleander). Looking for some trivial entertainment to pass the eons, she decides that it would be "fun" to mess with Sinbad's life. After thwarting his original high seas attempt at piracy by sending a sea monster, she then steals the Book of Peace herself knowing that Sinbad would be blamed. When Sinbad is sentenced to death, Proteus gallantly gives him the opportunity to seek, find and return the book. Of course, if he fails to return, Proteus will have to die in his place.

To keep Sinbad honest, Marina (voice by Catherine Zeta-Jones, Chicago), who is betrothed to Proteus, chooses to accompany Sinbad on the journey.

And that's just the back story. The story truly hits its stride when Sinbad and his crew sets forth to regain the Book of Peace. Their travels take them through unexplored territories and bring them face to face with dangers beyond their understanding, all thanks to the interference of Eris.

The 2-D hand-drawn animation set against the 3-D computer generated backgrounds are simplistic and more "cartoon-like" than more recent animated features. But the story is strong, the adventures are exciting, the characters are well-defined, and the message (one of trust, honor, and friendship) is worth hearing.

There does seem to be a slight sexual subtext laced within the story. That, in addition to the many life-threatening perils that Sinbad faces, may make the film inappropriate for the extremely young.

The voices behind the characters were cast wisely. Brad Pitt and Joseph Fiennes both are able to convey the machismo needed; Catherine Zeta-Jones emits a strong and independent femininity; and Michelle Pfeiffer seductively purrs her way through the evil Eris' lines.

While much of the film liberally borrows elements from Greek mythology, there is one aspect that could have been taken right off the pages of the Bible. In the film, the Book of Peace gives light, provides protection, and is stolen by an evil and egotistical spiritual being. Sound familiar?

Those by the way side are they that hear; then cometh the devil, and taketh away the word out of their hearts, lest they should believe and be saved. Luke 8:12 (KJV)

God makes His will known via His Word. The knowledge of His Word brings light to all who receive it with meekness. Since light will always dispel darkness, that is why the prince of darkness tries at every opportunity to blind people to the truth of the Word. The applied knowledge of God in the mind of one who believes is the only defense that works against our spiritual enemy.
