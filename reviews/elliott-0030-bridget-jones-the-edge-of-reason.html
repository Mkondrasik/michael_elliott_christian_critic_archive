Bridget Jones: The Edge of Reason

Bridget Jones returns to the screen in The Edge of Reason, a sequel to the smash comedy hit of 2001. Unfortunately, there’s little reason for a return visit – she’s telling the same story she told the first time around.

Still English; still slightly overweight; still subject to embarrassing situations, Bridget (Renee Zellweger) at least has one redeeming element in her life... a man. And not just any man either. She has managed to hold onto the perfect man, Mark Darcy (Colin Firth) who, despite Bridget’s “wobbly bits” and penchant for humiliation, sees something in her that has captured his heart.

If we find that hard to believe, we’re in good company. Bridget can’t seem to believe it either and requires constant reassurance that Darcy isn’t ready to call it quits. Her lack of trust and self-confidence leads her to making a fool of herself on numerous occasions.

It doesn’t help Bridget’s peace of mind that Darcy’s assistant, Rebecca (Jacinda Barrett) is attractive and thin with legs (we’re told) that reach up to her chin. Eventually, Bridget does the unthinkable – she “chucks” Darcy and returns to her unhappy single life.

From that point on, all that she has left in her life is her job as a TV field reporter where her assignments place her in one ridiculous situation after another. In the midst of her post-Darcy depression, the studio executives pair her with none other than Daniel Cheever (Hugh Grant) the charming scoundrel who gave Bridget so much heartache in the first film.

Thus, all the elements from the original are there. The love triangle, the self-depreciating humor, the well-meaning but meddling friends, and, of course, the diary into which Bridget writes her always funny observations.

Renee Zellweger, the American actress whose original casting caused such uproar among the English fans of Helen Fielding’s novels, has already proved herself to be a perfect choice for the British heroine. This time around there’s sure to be more complaints regarding the fact that she’s been given such a weak script rather than the fact that she was given the script at all. She has again plumped up for the role by reportedly gaining thirty pounds to play the non-svelte heroine. In addition to capturing the look, she also has nailed the awkward pluckiness that has endeared the character to millions.

Colin Firth, as the too-good-to-be-true Darcy coasts upon a characterization he has been asked to play many times before – that of a slightly stuffy but always proper English gentleman. Hugh Grant, who has recently announced his semi-retirement from acting, has the more-naughty-than-bad character of Daniel Cheever down pat. It is a seemingly effortless portrayal.

Bridget’s doubts and insecurities about her relationship with Mark Darcy lead her to throw away the very thing she was afraid of losing. Fear has a funny way of becoming a self-fulfilling prophecy. Job learned the reality of this all too well.

For the thing which I greatly feared is come upon me, and that which I was afraid of is come unto me. Job 3:25 (KJV)

If we harbor fear in a category of life and reinforce that fear through negative thoughts, negative believing and negative confessions, we should not be surprised to see what we fear manifest itself in a big way in our lives. God has given us his truth to believe so that we might rid ourselves of fear; cast off all doubts and live a worry-free existence.
