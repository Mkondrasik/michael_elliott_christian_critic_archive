Author-verified transcription

16 years? Has it really been that long since Patrick Dempsey starred in Can’t Buy Me Love? Even so, it must be too soon to be watching a remake of it because everything about Love Don’t Cost a Thing feels familiar and stale.

Nick Cannon slips into the Dempsey role as Alvin Johnson, a high school loser who cleans pools, builds cars, dresses like a nerd and dreams of being popular. He gets his opportunity to make his dreams come true when Paris Morgan (Christina Milian), the hottest cheerleader in school wrecks her mother’s car. Nick offers to fix her car and pay for the parts if she will pretend to be his girlfriend for the next two weeks.

She reluctantly consents and with her as his “ticket,” Alvin gains access to the cool kids. But in order to keep the nerd out of Nerdville, this nerd needs a complete makeover. With Christina’s help the payer is transformed into a “player” and gains acceptance from the snooty upper class of the high school social strata.

It isn’t long before Al is forgetting about his old friends, ignoring Christina, and blowing his future because he’s too wrapped up in his new popularity. He will eventually learn that something gained under false pretenses can be quickly taken away.

The script closely follows the basic structure of the superior Can’t Buy Me Love. Most of the changes seem to come from the “urbanization” that was added to fit the predominantly black cast. There’s also a far greater emphasis on sexual humor and references than was in the comparatively tamer 1987 comedy.

Nick Cannon overplays Alvin at both extremes. As the dorky and insecure Alvin, he’s all twitches and jerky movements. His transformation swings too far in the opposite direction. With a pimp roll that makes him look like he’s hobbled and a vocabulary straight from the ‘hood, his overblown version of cool is just as dorky.

Christina Milian gives the movie its center as Paris Morgan, Alvin’s ideal girl who learns a little something about herself while watching Alvin pretend to be something he’s not.

The rest of the cast play the requisite stereotypes: empty-headed girls/eye candy; muscle-bound jocks; or shy and foolish nerds. Steve Harvey plays Alvin’s dad, a man who is trapped in his own kind of 80s world. His fatherly advice to his confused son consists primarily on the selection and application of condoms.

Peer pressure can be a real pain in the designer labeled adorned posterior. But it does exist and we should learn how to deal with it. The question to ask ourselves is “Who do we want to live for? Who are we trying to please?”

If we are trying to please others, we will always come up short and we will never be consistent in our actions. If we are trying to please ourselves, we will be subject to our own weaknesses and temptations.

The answer is to live for and by a consistent standard that exists outside of ourselves. An standard that encourages us to be the best that is within us. A standard that is both attainable and rewarding. The first century believers understood this - even when the peer pressure was on them to change, they resisted, preferring the way they had chosen.

Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men. Acts 5:29 (KJV)
