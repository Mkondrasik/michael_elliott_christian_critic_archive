Reign Over Me

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 23, 2007
Rating
2 stars
Running time
2 hr: 4 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Revelation 21:4Isaiah 25:8Isaiah 61:3

Sermon / spiritual topic: Grief

Author-verified transcription

A cataclysmic event may bring a tragedy of such dimensions that our emotional systems may not be able to fully process it. In Reign Over Me, the enormity of the loss that occurred on 9/11 is reduced to one man's pain.

Adam Sandler stars as Charlie Fineman, a widower whose wife and children were on board one of the terrorist hijacked planes on that infamous day. He's now a mere shell of a man, choosing to try to forget the past rather than deal with the emotional aftermath of his entire family's murder.

Don Cheadle plays Alan Johnson, Charlie's college roommate. Now a successful dentist with a wife and family of his own, Alan is feeling a bit of ennui regarding his own choices. When he runs into Charlie on the street, he seeks to reinsert himself into his life, perhaps remembering the good times of his carefree college days. His newly acquired friendship becomes a burden and responsibility as Charlie has not let anyone else get close to him. This leaves Alan alone to decide how best to get him the professional care he doesn't want but badly needs.

Writer/director Mike Binder (The Upside of Anger) may have tackled a subject beyond his facilitation skills. He's a bit ham-handed here, taking too long to set up the conflict and not enough time reconciling it. The ending is a disappointment and poorly foreshadowed early on.

Adam Sandler, following in the footsteps of just about every comedian who wishes to be taken seriously as an actor, does fine in the dramatic or emotional scenes. This is not the vehicle in which he'll get the Oscar nod but he didn't embarrass himself and shows a bit more depth than he did in Billy Madison or The Waterboy.

I would have liked to see Don Cheadle's character's story developed a bit more. He has the ability to bring much more to the table that he was allowed in this film. But it is a credible performance and one worth watching.

Other supporting cast members include Jada Pinkett Smith, as Alan's somewhat demanding wife; Liv Tyler as a young and sympathetic therapist; Saffron Burrows as a love-starved dental patient; and Robert Klein as Charlie's father-in-law.

Depicting grief onscreen can be a difficult task... just as experiencing grief can be a difficult time. God understands that our grieving is to be expected because we live in a world where bad things are allowed to happen... but He promises that our grief won't be permanent.

And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. Revelation 21:4 (KJV)

The more we believe God's Word, the more able we are to deal with the pain that this world may bring. Allowing ourselves to live as a victim, as Charlie has, may be understandable in a certain respect but it is not the only option available to us. Tragedies can be withstood. Emotional pain can abate. Despite the negative events of our lives, God says that we can be happy and blessed. We need not live as a victim to our own tragedies. And as we find our way into happiness, we can help others find their way out of their miseries as well.
