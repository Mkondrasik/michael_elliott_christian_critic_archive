Author-verified transcription

There are good films. There are bad films. Then, there are the headscratchers -- films that are so fundamentally bad we cannot but help wondering how or why they ever got made in the first place. It is in this latter category that Superbabies: Baby Geniuses 2 belongs.

Director Bob Clark, who gave the world the critically lambasted 1999 film Baby Geniuses, inexplicably returns with a sequel. It wasn't a good idea then and it isn't a good idea now.

The film tells the fantasy tale of a evil German (Jon Voight) who desires to rule the world by transmitting a mind control program via an insipid television show featuring a giant frog that is at one point described as a big zucchini.

The only one who stands in his way is a 70 year old tyke named Kahuna (Gerry, Leo, and Myles Fitzgerald). A super-intelligent, gadget-wielding crime-fighter who still has his baby teeth, Kahuna is thought to be a fictional character by a quartet of infants whose baby talk masks from adults the fact that they are actually capable of having complex communication.

After the babies see Kahuna in action, they accompany him to his baby Batcave-like Fortress of Solitude where they are transformed into their superhero personas - Brainboy, Cupid Girl, Bouncing Boy, and Capt. Courageous.

Together, these babes in arms are the only ones who can save the world from becoming dominated by an evil genius with a bad hair piece and exaggerated accent.

Jon Voight does his best (or is it his worst) to justify his involvement in this project by acting like an Aryan ham but as Kathleen Turner and Christopher Lloyd discovered in the original film, it is difficult to draw the audience's eye away from the freak show of babies being computer manipulated to make it appear that they are capable of adult speech. And such immature adult speech it is.

Scott Baio and Vanessa Angel play a pair of daycare running parents. They'll be happy to learn that they are immediately forgettable. Skylar Shaye and Justin Chatwin add a teen flavor to the cast but very little else.

What is the target audience for this PG film? Certainly it isn't the children who will have a hard time sitting still for the 90 minute running time. At the screening I attended, a row of four year olds walked out of the movie, demonstrating once again that children may be the best and most honest critics on the planet. They may not be geniuses but they at least showed some common sense.

Manipulating babies into talking, acting, and reasoning like adults is creepy. It violates the natural order of life.

When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things. 1 Corinthians 13:11 (KJV)

Even fantasy films need to be based upon recognizable universal laws to which the audience can relate and accept. Superbabies is conceptually flawed and those flaws are carried through into its execution.

The movie ends with an appeal to youngsters to stop watching mindless junk and go outside to play or read. I'd echo that sentiment. Any other activity would be better than wasting time with this cinematic drool.
