Swordfish

Sermon / spiritual topic: Integrity; Ends and Means; Materialism

Author-verified transcription

At the beginning of Swordfish, John Travolta's character is bemoaning the lack of realism in today's Hollywood films. Obviously, he must have been watching the rushes to this film. Not only unbelievable, Swordfish borders on absurdity.

Travolta (Battlefield Earth) plays mystery man Gabriel Shear who is in need of a skilled computer hacker in order to break into the files of the DEA where 9 billion dollars of confiscated drug money sits, just waiting to be transferred to his accounts. He finds a reluctant Stanley Jobson (Hugh Jackman, X-Men), a convicted felon who, because of his past computer crime (performed in the act of righting an injustice), is barred from seeing his daughter who now lives with his ex-wife and new husband, the porn king of Southern California. He also, by court order, can never touch a computer keyboard again.

After Jobson agrees to meet Gabriel, thanks to the persuasive talents of Ginger (Halle Berry, X-Men), one of Gabriel's shapelier employees, it isn't long before he's got a blonde in his lap, a gun to his head, and 10 million in his bank account... tactics designed to persuade him to crack the code Gabriel needs to carry out his plan. To make matters worse, everybody seems to be interested in his actions: the FBI, the DEA, even a secret governmental agency that is so deeply undercover we never get to know who they are.

It is hard for Stanley to know who to trust although it gets easier as the body count begins to pile up. Still, not everybody is who they appear to be. Not everyone's motives are exactly clear. Some things, quite frankly, don't even make sense. Not that it's really expected in a summer action film. There is a lot of explosions, gunplay, car chases, and a highly glossed look to this Joel Silver (Die Hard) production which, they must figure, is what people come to this kind of film to see.

Director Dominic Sena (Gone In 60 Seconds) delivers the goods in terms of visual style and pacing. The film moves briskly and the stunts are thrilling to watch. Screenwriter Skip Woods (Thursday) does not deliver the goods when it comes to substance. This Swordfish has no meat to it. It is pure cotton candy... sugar spun around a tale of theft, murder, mayhem and sexual titillation.

John Travolta plays a variation of his bad guy persona seen before in Face Off and Broken Arrow. Gabriel is cool under pressure, unhesitant to kill if it suits his purpose, erudite and sophisticated, cold-blooded and unmerciful. The twist is that he sees himself as a patriotic warrior. It is this misguided patriotism that drives him to take the heinous actions that he does.

Hugh Jackman actually makes computer geeks look rather sexy, strutting his stuff by banging away at the keyboard and talking to the monitor. Don Cheadle (Traffic) has a few good scenes as the FBI agent trying to figure out why Stanley has risked his parole by resurfacing in LA. Halle Berry is little more than window dressing as she appears throughout the film in various stages of undress... including a brief, gratuitous topless scene for which she reportedly earned a $500,000 bonus. It is a report which Ms. Berry has since denied.

A thematic question which is raised in the film is: Do the ends justify the means? One of the characters continually justifies his illegal and immoral actions as being necessary to maintain the quality of life we enjoy in this country. In other words, his bad actions will benefit the greater good.

Sorry, but that logic completely escapes me. A key to having true quality of life is integrity. When a man compromises his moral integrity regardless of the reason, his quality of life immediately suffers... along with those who are affected by his actions.

For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul? Matthew 16:26 (KJV)

When phrased like that, the answer is self evident. There is nothing on this earth that is worth the price that is paid when one compromises one's own values and beliefs. Not even nine billion dollars.
