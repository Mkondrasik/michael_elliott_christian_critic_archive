Author-verified transcription

Whoever it was that once said, "You can never go home again," obviously hadn't met Birdee Pruitt, the central character of Hope Floats. Birdee was, in her day, an audacious high school beauty queen who married the star football player and was headed for what was supposed to be a lifetime of bliss and happiness. That is, until she agreed to appear on a "tabloid-type" television show where, instead of the make-over she expected, she was ambushed with the news that her husband and her best friend were having an affair. Her picture perfect world now shattered, she returns to her small hometown of Smithville, Texas to pick up and piece together the remains of her life.

Sandra Bullock (Speed) was an inspired choice to play Birdee. Of all the actresses working today, the title of "America's Sweetheart" is hers and hers alone. She wins our heart instantly and does not relinquish it until long after the movie's end. But we're not complaining. Our heart is in good hands.

Birdee's heart isn't faring as well. After her nationally televised humiliation which she stoically endures, Birdee has trouble opening her heart again. Trying to shut out the pain, she almost succeeds in shutting out everything and everybody else. Thankfully, the people in Birdee's life refuse to let her get away with it.

Harry Connick, Jr. (Independence Day) is Justin Matisse, a house painter/architect, who left Smithville for the lure of California, only to return for what the big city could not provide him. He becomes the solidifying and stabilizing force of the film. His down-home, carefully considered, and totally honest presence gives the movie its center.

Gena Rowlands (Paulie) provides the movie its life force. As Ramona Calvert, Birdee's eccentric, animal-stuffing mother, Ms. Rowlands turns in an award-worthy performance. She is delightful, touching, and completely believable.

Director Forest Whitaker (Waiting To Exhale) keeps a light hand on the camera, letting the actors and the script tell the story. This is a mark of a competent and confident artist.

Hope Floats is the type of film that we had in mind when first developing the idea of The Christian Critic. It is a feel-good, family-oriented, spiritually enriched movie-going experience. Just the title alone is a sermon unto itself. When events have got you down, hope is what you hang onto in order to float back to the surface. It is hope that enables us to endure the various pressures of this life that can appear overwhelming to us at times. Hope, or the expectation of something promised, is the life preserver upon which we ride when we are lost in a sea of trouble. The effectiveness of that life preserver depends entirely upon the faithfulness of the one who has made the promise. As Christians, we have a hope that will never sink. God always keeps His promises.

Spiritual truths that can be seen within the context of the film are in great abundance:

Justin, explaining why he left California, said, "The American Dream: Find something that you love - twist it and torture it and try to find a way to make money at it. You can spend a lifetime doing that and at the end, you can't find a trace of what you started out loving." He left California not because he couldn't find work, but because he refused to elevate the work above everything else. A sure-fire way to spiritual disappointment is to consider your career to be the end-all and be-all of your life.

Birdee, when explaining the decline of her marriage, said, "The more I tried to be what he wanted to see, the less I saw of myself." All of us need to spend less time changing ourselves to please man and spend more time changing ourselves to please God.

Listen closely when Ramona tells a bedtime story to Birdee's daughter, Bernice (Mae Whitman of The Gingerbread Man). The ants in that story could easily be used as an analogy for how God's people are to work together to overcome any obstacle in life.

Seeing the effect that Birdee's separation/divorce has on her daughter is enough to remind us that God's heart has always been for marriages to remain an unbroken commitment between a man and a woman. Divorces stem from a weakness or failure on our part as non-perfect humans who enter into God's perfectly designed marriage relationship. We should remember that it takes as much work and effort to fall out of love as it does to stay in love.

The most heartwarming spiritual example that can be seen is the close knit family bond that exists in the Calvert/Pruitt household. When one hurts, they all hurt. When one rejoices, they all rejoice. They aren't afraid to confront one another when the need arises and they are quick to comfort one another in times of despair. That's what a loving household is supposed to do. The scene where Birdee and the family dress up in costume to perform a lip-synched version of The Temptations' Motown hit "I Can't Get Next To You" in order to cheer up a sad little girl is priceless. Moments like these (and there are many) are what keeps Hope Floats so buoyant.
