Halloween, 1991. For the crew of the swordfishing boat Andrea Gail, it was not a holiday to celebrate. For unbeknownst to these Glousester fisherman, nature was about to play its greatest trick and they were to be its victims. Based on a true story, which was brilliantly novelized in best-selling fashion by Sebastian Junger, THE PERFECT STORM is about a once in a lifetime meteorological condition which had no mercy on the humans caught in its path.

The book focused on three groups of people caught in the midst of the storm: The Andrea Gail and its fishermen trolling off the coast of Massachusetts; a pleasure sailboat somewhere off the coast of Bermuda; and the combined rescue teams of Air Force and Coast Guard personnel who knowingly place themselves in peril in order to try to save the lives of those already there.

The movie, while featuring all three groups, only has the luxury of time to fully engage us in the story of one -- the fishermen of the Andrea Gail. The film sets the stage slowly and with great assurance by introducing us to the five men who unsuspectingly take a 73 foot commercial fishing boat into the heart of the storm of the century, and then gives us just enough information about their lives to make us care deeply about whether they survive that journey.

Captain Billy Tyne (George Clooney, Three Kings) a veteran fisherman, is having a string of bad runs. After bringing in yet another disappointing catch, he gathers his crew together, who agree to sail with him one last time in the hopes to improve their financial status before entering into the off season.

Mr. Clooney's rugged good looks combined with a commanding presence make him a perfect choice to play Capt. Tyne. No saint, but no worse sinner than others, he comes across as a good man in a hard profession who genuinely loves what he does.

Mark Wahlberg (Three Kings) is Bobby Shatford, a young but savvy fisherman whom Tyne takes under his wing. Shatford has a young and pretty girlfriend (Diane Lane, My Dog Skip) who is begging him to quit this dangerous life at sea.

The others on board include John C. Reilly (For Love of the Game), William Fichtner (Drowning Mona), and Allen Payne (A Price Above Rubies). Mary Elizabeth Mastrontonio (Limbo) appears as Linda Greenlaw, another swordfish boat captain having a string of good catches, who shares a friendly jibe or two with Billy.

As well as these actors perform their roles, the movie, like a fishing boat leaving the pier, opens slowly, then methodically continues at a steady pace. But when the weather takes a turn for the worse, and the winds and rains begin to whip up, we'd best batten down the hatches and get ready to hang on. We're in for a wild and woolly ride.

With director Wolfgang Petersen (Das Boot) at the helm, THE PERFECT STORM is completely see-worthy. He has crafted a taut picture with the tension building until the final resolution has been reached. The visual effects used to simulate the massive 10-story waves and 140 mile an hour winds were extremely impressive and added to the overall impact of the story.

The power unleashed by natural forces can be impressive indeed. But as awesome as that power is and can be - we must be reminded that there is a power greater than even the mightiest storm.

But as they sailed he fell asleep: and there came down a storm of wind on the lake; and they were filled with water, and were in jeopardy. And they came to him, and awoke him, saying, Master, master, we perish. Then he arose, and rebuked the wind and the raging of the water: and they ceased, and there was a calm. And he said unto them, Where is your faith? And they being afraid wondered, saying one to another, What manner of man is this! for he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him. Luke 8:23-25 (KJV)

The works that Jesus Christ did were always done via the manifested power of God. Man vs. the unleashed power of nature is always a risky business. But without knowing how to call on God's power, it is a risk where the odds would be stacked increasingly against man.
