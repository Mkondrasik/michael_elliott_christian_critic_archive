The Climb

Author-verified transcription

Worldwide Pictures, the motion picture division of the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association, has produced over 125 films over a period of 50 years. Their latest, The Climb, is scheduled for television broadcast at various times during the month of June. Air dates and times can be found at: http://www.theclimbmovie.com/home.asp

The Climb introduces us to two men who share a passion for rock/mountain climbing but couldn't be more different in every other way. Derrick (Jason Winston George, Clockstoppers) is a showboating risk-taker whose ego is eclipsed only by his fear (masked by anger) which drives him to the top of every physical challenge he faces.

Michael (Ned Vaughn, Courage Under Fire), on the other hand, is a humble, conservative and safety-conscious man who finds spiritual meaning in his climbing.

When these two solo climbers come to the mountain rescue of a millionaire's son, he shows his gratitude by financing their assent of the Andes' Mount Chicanagua, the dream climb of both adventurers. The catch is that they have to work as a team, serving as the poster boys for the community-minded ad campaign of the tycoon's chain of discount stores.

Will their differing styles and values make it impossible for them to work together? Will they be able to learn from each other? The Climb is unabashedly evangelistic in tone and content so the answer to those questions should be obvious. Even though the Christian message of sacrifice, forgiveness, and redemption makes the outcome of the story somewhat predictable, our enjoyment of the film is in no way diminished.

This is largely due in part to a pair of strong performances by the personable and attractive leads. Jason Winston George captures the cockiness of a self-assured and self-made man, making Derrick a likable character without downplaying any of his faults. Likewise, Ned Vaughn avoids turning Michael into a sanctimonious bore by giving the character depth and shadings. Though a practicing Christian, Michael is also a man subject to the same emotional outbursts and errors in judgment that Derrick is. The difference is in how quick they are to recognize their faults and correct their behavior.

The film has a number of back stories which add to the flavor of the production. The millionaire (Dabney Coleman, Recess: School's Out) has been getting a lot of bad press of late due to some unpopular business decisions that his company has made. Derrick, an avowed non-Christian, is linked romantically with Cheryl (Kyli Santiago, The Young and the Restless), a Christian who has compromised her values for his sake. Thus, issues of trust, faithfulness, responsibility, and forgiveness all come into play as the stories merge together toward the inevitable climax. Along the way, as an added attraction, we are treated to some lovely cinematography.

There is certainly no shortage of biblical analogies or lessons to be gleaned from The Climb. It is, after all, overtly Christian, with a distinct and clearly defined evangelical message. Those who would find this to be a failing should really examine why they might feel that way. Stories about people who have chosen to follow the teachings of Christ are as much a part of this life as are the stories about those who don't. Why is it that vampires, adulterers, mobsters, and serial killers are more warmly received at our box offices than characters with Christian values who are bold enough to express them? Perhaps films such as this one may help to change that perception. We can certainly pray that it does.
