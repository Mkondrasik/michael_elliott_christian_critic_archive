What Dreams May Come

Author-verified transcription

Let’s say this right at the beginning: The vision of heaven and hell as presented in the new film, What Dreams May Come, is very imaginative, somewhat original, but completely unsupported by scripture. It is a lie. At times, it is a very pretty lie, but it is a lie nonetheless.

Chris Nielsen (Robin Williams, Mrs. Doubtfire) is a good man. Loving husband, devoted father, compassionate doctor... car accident fatality.

Upon his death, he has to learn how to let go of the earthly plane and live in the heavenly realm. Guiding him through the process is Albert (Cuba Gooding, Jr., Jerry Maguire), a kind of spiritual social worker. Albert explains that everyone has the ability to “create” their own heavenly universe. Chris, who was an art lover while on earth, finds that his heaven is literally painted for him, becoming whatever he wills it to be.

But heaven isn’t a perfect place for Chris because his wife Annie (Annabella Sciorra, Cop Land) isn’t there. The combination of losing her children in an car accident four years earlier and now having Chris die in a second car accident has pushed her over the brink into a suicidal depression. Suicides don’t go to heaven. They go... someplace else.

Unable to accept this, Chris sets off in search of her, using the services of a man known only as The Tracker, played by Max von Sydow. Mr. Von Sydow is no stranger to philosophical film treatises on life and death, having appeared in Ingmar Bergman’s classic, The Seventh Seal, some 41 years ago.

Director Vincent Ward (A Map of the Human Heart) and screenwriter Ronald Bass (My Best Friend’s Wedding), working from the novel by Richard Matheson (Somewhere In Time), have teamed up to present a fairy tale version of heaven and a depiction of hell that can only be the devil’s fantasy.

Robin Williams does an admirable job in navigating through this emotional roller coaster of a movie and Annabella Sciorra will absolutely rip your heart out as the sole survivor of her family who can find no solace.

It is ironic that art plays such an integral part in this film because most people’s preconception of what heaven and hell is like is more firmly rooted in how artists have envisioned it throughout the centuries rather than from any scriptural truth. Indeed, by turning to the scriptures we see that the movie’s very premise is in error.

It is also odd that in this version of heaven, God is mentioned only once and dismissed with a vague, “He’s up there somewhere” reference. Jesus Christ, who is the one who gives us access to our eternal spiritual lives, is nowhere to be seen.

This heaven of What Dreams May Come does not exist. According to the scriptures, the dead are “asleep”, awaiting the return of Jesus Christ. Upon that return, the “dead in Christ will rise first,” thus beginning their new eternal life. But until he returns, they remain “asleep”, unaware of the passage of time.

For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not anything, neither have they any more a reward; for the memory of them is forgotten. Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might; for there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave whither thou goest. Ecclesiastes 9:5 & 10 (KJV)

This movie’s version of hell is borrowed from many different sources - all of them erroneous in nature. In reality, the people who, as a result of their believing (or lack thereof) have not received eternal life, through grace or works, are subject to what Revelation 2:11 calls “the second death.”

There is no reason to believe that the nature of the second death is any different than the nature of the first. It is nothingness. Those who of their free will decision choose not to accept the deliverance of God will one day simply cease to exist.

But everyone does have a choice. We who choose God understand that there is a heaven that we will one day know. It will exceed anyone’s expectations or visions. And best of all, this heaven will be no dream or fantasy world... it will be real.
