Author-verified transcription

In Mean Girls, teenage students are frequently compared to wild jungle animals and the comparison is hardly flattering and rather unfair... to the animals. Touted as this generation's Heathers, Tina Fey's screenplay is blistering in its depiction of the social and political maneuvering that takes place in the "girl world" of high school cliques.

Lindsay Lohan stars as Cady Heron, a home schooled daughter of a pair of zoologists who have been living in Africa most of her life. When her mother lands a professorship at Northwestern University, Cady finds herself attending her first public school and she is ill prepared for what awaits her.

Cady is immediately lost and bewildered at the strange world known as high school and the myriad of rules and regulations that come with the territory. Fortunately, she is "befriended" by The Plastics, a trio of shallow hotties who consider their clique to be the royalty of school society.

Led by Queen Bee Regina George (Rachel McAdams), a cross between a Barbie doll and Cruella de Vil, the Plastics spend their days shopping, gossiping, and standing in front of the mirror pretending to find flaws in their physical perfection. When Cady makes the totally serious gaffe of thinking that Regina's ex-boyfriend (Jonathan Bennett) is cute, the gloves come off and she finds that her new friends are evil incarnate.

Not outwardly, of course... in "girl world" all fighting has to be sneaky. Cady decides that in order to survive in this new world, she's going to have to fight by their rules and so she sets out to surreptitiously destroy The Plastics and their perfect little world.

Lindsay Lohan does a credible job as a wide-eyed innocent who allows herself to become as "plastic" as the girls she is out to get. She has an easy, natural way about her that is warm and engaging. She is ably supported with a large cast including a pair of amusing performances by Saturday Night Live cast members Tina Fey and Tim Meadows.

Ms. Fey wrote the screenplay for Mean Girls based upon the novel Queen Bees and Wannabes by Rosalind Wiseman. The insights and observations of high school life, while exaggerated for comedic effect, will strike a chord with anyone who remembers the days of acne and lunch room protocol.

Mark S. Waters, who last directed the successful remake of Freaky Friday, has another winner on his hands with Mean Girls. While it does have a bit of a hard edge, it still remains accessible. While there is a fair amount of sexual referencing, drinking and examples of poor or inappropriate behavior, there is also a decent message being communicated to us.

The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly. Proverbs 26:22 (KJV)

Spreading rumors, gossip, or even truth with the intent to hurt or belittle another human being bears a cost. It will tear at the fabric of friendships and community. It does nothing to strengthen, encourage or edify. It is human behavior that shows us at our worst.

Cady must learn what she knew before entering into this "girl world." That it is far better to offer words of kindness and support than mean-spirited criticism and insults.

Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out: so where there is no talebearer, the strife ceaseth. Proverbs 26:20 (KJV)
