Finally... an action film that actually explodes off the screen. Far outdistancing its predecessors, Mission Impossible III delivers the goods with more thrills per minute than most amusement parks. Perhaps that is because the man behind the television mega-hits Lost and Alias, J.J. Abrams, is competently at the helm.

M:I III finds Ethan Hunt (Tom Cruise) retired from active field service. As a recruiter/trainer, he is able to contemplate a "normal" life with the love of his life, Julia (Michelle Monaghan), who knows nothing about his top secret past or expertly honed skills. When one of his trainees (Keri Russell) is captured by a very bad man, Hunt agrees to come out of retirement lead an insertion team to rescue her.

One thing leads to another and Hunt gets sucked into the impossible mission business once again, trying to stop black marketer Owen Davian (Philip Seymour Hoffman) from acquiring and selling a doomsday device called the "Rabbit's Foot." His mission - once he chose to accept it - leads him from Germany to the Vatican to Shanghai; and puts his new wife in jeopardy.

Abrams starts off his feature film career with a bang. Right from the beginning, he lets us know we're in for a wild ride by showing us a tense climactic moment then jumping back in time to fill us in on what led to it. By the time we reach the moment again we are spent.

Cruise is in fine form and at 44 retains his boyish looks and athleticism to make the extreme physicality of the role totally believable. It certainly helps to have Academy Award winner Philip Seymour Hoffman cast as the heavy.

The script is logical and, unlike MI: I & II, perfectly understandable and in keeping with the spirit of the original TV series. There's plenty of misdirection, split second timing, masquerades, and complex planning to keep fans of the genre absolutely giddy with joy.

The other members of the impossible mission aren't given much to do: Ving Rhames, Jonathan Rhys Myers, and Maggie Q are supporting Cruise without ever having the spotlight to shine upon them. The only supporting member of the cast who is given scene stealing moments (and boy, does he make the most of them) is Simon Pegg as the wryly funny computer expert Benji Dunn.

Throughout the film Hunt is placed in life or death situations that are made even more serious because his enemy has discovered a weak link in his defenses - he loves Julia... They are able to use that emotion against him by capturing her and using her life as a bargaining chip.

It is no less an issue in the spiritual competition. The apostle Paul makes note of it in his letter to the Corinthians:

I want you to be free from anxieties. The unmarried man is anxious about the affairs of the Lord, how to please the Lord; but the married man is anxious about worldly affairs, how to please his wife, 1 Corinthians 7:32-33 (KJV)

Knowing this, it becomes vitally important that the husband and wife work together - fully sharing all things - so they can be prepared to withstand the attacks of their adversaries as a united front. It was Hunt's attempt to keep his IMF job a secret from his wife that put her in danger. It was only when he began to share his knowledge with her that she had a fighting chance of survival.
