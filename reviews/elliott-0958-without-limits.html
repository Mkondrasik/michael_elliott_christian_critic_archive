Without Limits

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 11, 1998
Rating
2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 57 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 9:24Hebrews 12:12 Timothy 4:7

Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual Race

Author-verified transcription

"Faster, higher, stronger"... Those are the words chosen as the motto of the Olympics. They can also be used to describe the incredible athletic ability of Steve Prefontaine, known to his fans and friends simply as "Pre." Without Limits tells the true story of his rise to fame, his victories and his disappointments, culminating in the tragic car accident that ended his life at the young age of 24.

Born a millworker's son, Pre grew to become one of the fastest human beings in the entire world. His talent was recognized by just about every collegiate institution in the world and they all sought him for their respective programs. All except the one man he wanted as a coach, the legendary Bill Bowerman at the University of Oregon. Bill didn't believe in personally recruiting his runners. Pre wanted a personal invitation and categorically refused to attend Oregon unless he knew that Bowerman personally wanted him there. It would be the first of many battles of will in which these two men would engage. And it ended as so many of their battles ended. Pre prevailed.

Billy Crudup (The Hi-Lo Country) plays Pre, who I'm told bears an uncanny resemblance to the late Steve Prefontaine. His portrayal is adequate even if somewhat pedestrian.

Donald Sutherland (Fallen) plays Bill Bowerman, a track genius whose homemade experiments with running shoes (the soles of which he tooled on his wife's waffle iron) led to the development of the Nike brand.

Monica Potter (Patch Adams) is the virginal Mary, a young coed who captures Pre's attention and heart.

As directed by Robert Towne (Tequila Sunrise), Without Limits excels during each race sequence. The movie flattens out a bit during the scenes in between which are there to establish character and relationships. But the defining moments of the film come while Pre is on the track.

"A race," Pre believes, "is a work of art." If that is the case, Pre was a grand master. The major conflict between Pre and his coach was in their disagreement on how to run a race. Pre favored "front-running" which basically meant running as fast as you could from beginning to end. Bowerman tried to get Pre to pace himself - to run with the pack in a steadily increasing speed so as to conserve his strength for the final laps.

To Pre, holding something back when you had it to give was unacceptable. We should all feel the same way when running our spiritual race:

Know ye not that they which run in a race run all, but one receiveth the prize? So run, that ye may obtain. 1 Corinthians 9:24 [KJV]

Running a long distance race requires stamina, perseverance, and endurance. Most of all it requires a mindset not to quit before expending every last ounce of strength. In a physical race, many factors combine to persuade us to stop running: Physical pain, loss of breath, faster opponents... In the spiritual race, the stumbling blocks are more subtle. God commands us to lay them aside and concentrate on the race.

Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience [endurance] the race that is set before us, Hebrews 12:1 [KJV]

Pre's way of running - all out from beginning to end - meant that he could never lose. He may not have always finished first, but he always ended with the knowledge that he gave it everything he had to give. May we, in our spiritual lives, be as assured. Then perhaps we could claim the Apostle Paul's claim as our own:

I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course [race], I have kept the faith: 2 Timothy 4:7 [KJV]
