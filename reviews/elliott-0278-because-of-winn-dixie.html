Because of Winn-Dixie

Author-verified transcription

For those whose hearts have any cockles in need of warming, Because of Winn-Dixie is just the film to do the job. Based upon the best selling novel by Kate DiCamillo, this family-friendly shaggy dog story may be the best "kid with a pet" film since My Dog Skip.

India Opal (Annasophia Robb) is a 10 year old girl who has moved to a sleepy Florida town with her preacher father (Jeff Daniels). Her mother left them some 7 years ago and that absence has left a chasm-sized hole in her life. With no friends and a father who is not really there for her emotionally, her life is a lonely one until a stray dog "finds" her.

Naming it after the grocery story chain where they first met, Opal and Winn Dixie bond as only a lonely kid and scruffy dog can.

Because of Winn Dixie, Opal meets and befriends a number of the town's resident loners: a guitar playing pet shop owner (Dave Matthews); a blind recluse (Cicely Tyson) thought to be a witch by the unkind Dewberry boys; a spinster librarian who glories in the stories of her past; and the ornery landlord who hates dogs... especially the large smelly one called Winn Dixie.

Director Wayne Wang (The Joy Luck Club) captures the small Southern town feel of the book giving life to the quirky characters and setting a general air of nostalgia over the production. The preacher's church services are conducted in a convenience store. ("Nothing wrong with making church more convenient," he quips to a somber congregation.)

Best of all, Wang and screenwriter Joan Singleton avoid the obvious pitfall of most dog movies - making the dog smarter or wiser than any animal has ever been. Winn Dixie is simply a dog doing the things that dogs do... which is why we love them.

Young Annasophia Robb has the perfect wide eyed look of youth which supports the character and keeps the film consistent despite her inexperience as an actress. The adult supporting cast is far more credible and effective. Cicely Tyson (who also appears this year in Diary of a Mad Black Woman) is especially appealing as a blind woman who sees what others have missed - that this young girl simply needs someone to talk to.

That really is the message at the core of this film. None of us need be (or indeed should be) alone. We can and should reach out to people and welcome them into our lives even as they invite us into theirs. The fellowship between friends and family provides strength and a sense of stability. It allows us avenues by which we may practice graciousness, giving, and the art of loving others.

It is, after all, the first observation that God made regarding the human condition:

And the LORD God said, 'It is not good that the man should be alone.' Genesis 2:18a (KJV)

To rectify this state of man, not only has God called us to be reconciled to Him, he also exhorts us to seek out each other in fellowship and brotherly love. It is the one thing that the residents of Naomi, FL so desperately needed - even if it took a dog to show them the way.
