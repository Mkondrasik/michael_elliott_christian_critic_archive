I ♥ Huckabees

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 22, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Timothy 2:23Proverbs 3:5-71 Corinthians 8:1-3

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Meaning of life; foolish questions; wisdom; seeking God; philosophy

Author-verified transcription

If you think watching something called an existential comedy is hard... try your hand at reviewing one. I ♥ Huckabees is a jumbled mess of a movie which, I suppose is only to be expected seeing as it is trying to make sense out of the jumbled mess that is human existence.

It all starts when Albert (Jason Schwartzman) runs into a tall black man on three separate occasions. Perhaps a simpler man might chalk it up to being a coincidence. Albert is not so simple. He wants someone to investigate the coincidence to see if it is something more meaningful. He hires a pair of existential detectives (Dustin Hoffman and Lily Tomlin) whose sole purpose is to look for “the big picture” amid all the tiny fragments which make up our lives.

He gets more than he bargained for as the “detectives” begin prying into every tiny aspect of his life and work. Leaving no stone unturned, they visit with Open Spaces, an environmental awareness group Albert helped to form. This group, following Albert’s lead, has just entered into an alliance with a corporate retail outlet named Huckabees and its smooth talking PR guy (Jude Law) who seems to have some existential questions of his own. His girlfriend (Naomi Watts) certainly does. As the voice of Huckabees (as well as the sexy image), she grows fearful of allowing her essence to be overwhelmed by the cheesecake image she presents.

Meanwhile Albert is paired with Tommy, his firefighting “other” who is trying to sort out his own philosophical dilemma - whether the world exists as part of a universal connectedness (which is what the existential detectives believe) or if in fact there is no meaning or interconnectivity to anything leaving us in a state of universal nothingness (which is a theory promoted by the detectives’ chief antagonist, a French nihilist named Catarine Vauban (Isabelle Huppert).

Despite the clever philosophical rambling and world-sized questions many of the characters raise, nothingness is what this film amounts to. We simply wait for the circuitous logic to run its course and deposit us at the end of the film as clueless as we were when we started. In other words, we shouldn’t expect this movie to take us anywhere... all we are supposed to do is enjoy the ride.

Perhaps, as some critics surmise, director David O. Russell, who also co-wrote the script with Jeff Baena, meant for all this to be a joke - a spoof on the self-absorbed whose “deep thoughts” prove to be nothing of the kind.

The film does have a moderate amount of entertainment value thanks to a talented cast and Russell’s fast-paced direction but audiences who are accustomed to more traditional kinds of storytelling may find I ♥ Huckabees to be a frustrating viewing experience.

Christians will certainly be frustrated in seeing how they are depicted. Albert’s family is seen as being stereotypically religious and close-minded. They are so sure they have all the answers that they have no desire to even consider the kinds of questions that Albert is asking.

Perhaps some Christians share those traits. Certainly, there are some questions that are unprofitable to spend time contemplating.

But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes. 2 Timothy 2:23 (KJV)

Yet, despite Russell’s broad brush painting, questions relating to the meaning of life and man's existence are hardly foolish. Christians have just as much interest in exploring these issues as anyone else. What may be different is the approach we use. Because we recognize that all we see was purposely created, we have no qualms in going to the Creator to seek the answers from him.
