Author-verified transcription

Young girls around the world dream of gliding on the ice, leaping into the air and spinning around in a dazzling display of athleticism and grace. It is for them that Ice Princess was made.

Rated G for general audiences. Walt Disney’s live action film offers no surprises. It offers no real thrills or laughs. What it does offer is a wholesome, well-made film intended for family audiences.

Casey Carlyle (Michelle Trachtenberg) is a high school physics geek with an eye focused upon a difficult Harvard admission. With the encouragement of her teacher she begins an unusual physics project – finding the aerodynamic formula that explains the various moves and jumps of champion figure skaters.

It is when she personally puts her formula to the test that she rekindles her barely suppressed passion for skating. Though she knows her mother (Joan Cusack) would disapprove, she secretly begins spending all her time and energy at the ice rink where a hard-driving coach Tina (Kim Cattrall) is preparing her skaters for competition.

As Casey’s skills increase, so does her popularity. She befriends other skaters including Tina’s daughter Gen (Hayden Panettiere), and a little dynamo nicknamed “the jumping shrimp” (12 year old skater Kirsten Olson). But she also learns that in the cutthroat world of competitive skating, friendships are ranked low on the priority ladder. Dirty tricks and mind games are tools used as often as toe picks.

Michelle Trachtenberg is the film’s emotional center and her genuineness is responsible for much of the movie’s appeal. She is quite believable as her character makes the transition from an ugly geek duckling to a beautiful skating swan.

The ever reliable Joan Cusack adds a bit of gravitas as Casey’s quasi-feminist mom who can’t share her daughter’s dream because she finds the sparkly outfits and ultra-glam look of professional skaters demeaning and silly.

Kim Cattrall is all business as a demanding coach who refuses to see that her daughter (Hayden Panettiere) does not share the passion for the sport that fuels her drive.

The skating is impressive. Trachtenberg and Panettiere both look comfortable on the ice. Competitive skaters Kirsten Olson, Juliana Cannarozzo, and Jocelyn Lai play supporting characters and provide some of the film’s best skating moments. Olympic medalists Michelle Kwan and Brian Boitano also make cameo appearances.

I was gratified to see that even though Casey’s scientific calculations help her understand the physics of the sport, they weren’t a magic formula that turned her into a champion overnight. The film does depict the hours upon hours of practice needed and the sacrifices that these young athletes must make in order to perform the moves that they do.

Unfortunately, competition often brings out the worst in people. Winning, for some, is so important that they feel they must exploit every advantage and if none exists, manufacture one by any means necessary.

Winning at any cost is the ultimate loss. A gold medal is not worth the price of one’s integrity. Being the best one can be by performing at 100% of one’s ability is the only victory that matters. It is a victory that fills us with the joy of satisfaction regardless of how many medals, ribbons or trophies come our way.

For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? Mark 8:36 (KJV)
