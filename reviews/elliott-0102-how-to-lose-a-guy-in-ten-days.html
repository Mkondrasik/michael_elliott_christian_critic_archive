How To Lose A Guy in Ten Days

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 7, 2003
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 13:34Ephesians 5:2Philippians 2:4-5

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love; relationships; selflessness; giving

Author-verified transcription

We already knew that it takes a lot of effort to make a relationship work. But who knew it could be so hard to ruin one? How To Lose A Guy In Ten Days, takes a comedic look at love and the things we do to drive each other crazy.

Annie Anderson (Kate Hudson, The Four Feathers) is the resident “how to” girl for Composure Magazine, a Cosmo-like forum geared towards women. It addresses various “issues” in which women supposedly have interest. Her newest column will handle the unusual angle of “how not to act” in a loving relationship. Her goal is to make some poor schlub fall in love with her and then, by copying commonly used mistakes, drive him away from her.

What she doesn’t know is that the guy she has “chosen,” Ben (Matthew McConaughey, Reign of Fire), has made his own bet... that he can make any girl fall in love with him in ten days. By doing so, he hopes to prove his worth to his advertising agency’s boss and land a lucrative client.

Thus the immovable object meets the unstoppable force as Annie must try more and more outlandish stunts in order to get rid of the guy who can’t really afford to leave. It’s a “girl meets guy, girl can’t lose the guy no matter how hard she tries” kind of story.

Director Donald Petrie (Miss Congeniality) is on familiar ground here thanks to the screenplay by the formula following team of Kristen Buckley, Brian Regan, and Burr Steers. Because there are no surprises as to where the story is heading, our enjoyment must come from the journey itself. The charismatic stars are just likable enough to overcome most of the script’s flaws. Alas, the final act proves too much (or provides too little) for the cast. The film builds to the point where the two leads discover each other’s deception and then doesn’t quite know where to go from there.

Kate Hudson, the daughter of Goldie Hawn, must have inherited her mother’s comic timing and she uses it to great effect here. Matthew McConaughey’s relaxed country charm provides for a nice, comfortable counterpoint. The two of them work well together, with an ease and affability to which audiences should respond.

The supporting characters are largely underwritten, stereotypical ingredients in this formula comedy. They include: Robert Klein (Two Weeks Notice) as Ben’s boss; Bebe Neuwirth (Tadpole) as Annie’s editor; Adam Goldberg (The Salton Sea) as one of Ben’s pals; and Kathryn Hahn (Crossing Jordan) as Annie’s friend whose hapless love life provided the inspiration to her article.

An effective way to illustrate a point is to show its opposite - to teach “what it is not.” Certainly that’s what observing Annie’s antics will do for us. It is interesting to note that all of the schemes she devises to drive Ben to the point of breaking off their “relationship,” reflect a selfish nature. She recognizes that in order to cause him to lose interest in her, she must show no consideration for his needs, wants or desires.

Such behavior is just the opposite of how God designed a male/female relationship to work. It is the opposite of how any human relationship should work.

A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another. John 13:34 (KJV)

To love as Jesus Christ loved requires that we give of ourselves. The joy of being in a loving relationship does not come from what we receive from the other, but in what we can give.
