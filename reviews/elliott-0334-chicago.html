Chicago

Sermon / spiritual topic: Fame; greed; prosperity of the ungodly; temptation

Author-verified transcription

"Razzle-dazzle 'em." That's the motto (and signature song) of Chicago shyster Billy Flynn. It is also the trademark look of the late choreographer Bob Fosse (All That Jazz), whose indelible style is stamped all over this musical film. Set in the corrupt and criminally jaded Chicago of the 1920's, Chicago is an indictment of America's obsession with fame, celebrities, crime, and juicy tabloid gossip.

Roxie Hart (Renee Zellweger, White Oleander) dreams of being a vaudeville star like Velma Kelly (Catherine Zeta-Jones, Traffic). Of course, that was before Velma was arrested for killing her cheating husband and sister. But irony knows few limitations and a few months later, Roxie joins Velma in prison after shooting her lover to death in a fit of anger. Soon, the two incarcerated divas are competing... for newspaper column space. They know that she who wins the headlines may win her freedom.

They both hire defense attorney Billy Flynn (Richard Gere, Unfaithful) to represent them while the prison matron, "Mama" Morton (Queen Latifah, Brown Sugar) angles to represent their professional interests if and when they are released.

Truth and justice take back seats to fame and titillation as we learn that in 1920's Chicago, murder is a form of entertainment. The sordid story of Chicago is told primarily through a series of fanciful musical numbers imagined by the starry-eyed Roxie. To Roxie, grit turns to glitter and the harsh light of reality becomes the spotlight of fame and public adulation.

Emmy award winning director Rob Marshall (Annie) and screenwriter Bill Condon (Gods and Monsters) present a wonderful adaptation of the stage musical (book by Fred Ebb and music by Hal Kander), by keeping true to the vaudeville framework in which the play was conceived.

This may not be the cast that would immediately jump to one's mind when thinking musical theater but thanks to some judicious editing they are surprisingly effective. Renee Zellweger plays both sides of the manipulative murderess / wide-eyed ingenue with equal aplomb. Catherine Zeta-Jones has a background in musical theater (who knew?) which is showcased to great effect here even though the character of Velma has been significantly reduced in this film version.

Richard Gere as an oily snake of a lawyer gets by due to his charm and admirable chutzpah. Although he'll never be mistaken for a Gene Kelly, his courtroom tap dance segment was quite amusing.

John C. Reilly (Magnolia) is a perfect choice to play Roxie's cuckolded husband.

This is not a musical like My Fair Lady or Singin' in the Rain. It is highly sexual and sensual, featuring elements which speak to the baser instincts of man: greed, lust, backbiting, and of course, fame.

It would be nice to think that we've come a long way since the roaring 20s but a look at the entertainment sections of our country's newspapers will immediately put such notions to rest. Trash journalism has never been more in demand as we, as a nation, clamor to read the juicy details of celebrities who have been caught doing something immoral or illegal. And if, in a slow news week no star has fallen, we'll take a common criminal, turn him into a celebrity just so we can have the "pleasure" of talking about his "fall" over our coffee and bagels.

Honestly, we shouldn't be surprised or amazed when sin seems to be rewarded. This world is still being run by the one who committed history"s first sin and now tempts others to follow in his evil ways.

Behold, these are the ungodly, who prosper in the world; they increase in riches. Psalms 73:12 (KJV)

The above scripture is certainly true and were that the whole story, life wouldn't make much sense. But we know there to be another life after this one. One where righteousness is rewarded justly and the ungodly judged righteously.

Yes, there are temptations in this world to prosper at any and all costs. All I know is that, in the long run, we'll be the richer if we resist those temptations and live according to God's standard of truth.
