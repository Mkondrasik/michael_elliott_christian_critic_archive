Author-verified transcription

Jarhead may be to military recruitment what The Poseidon Adventure was to cruise line marketing. This sober reflection of one marine’s experiences during Desert Shield/Desert Storm paints a grim picture that certainly conflicts with the image our military leaders would like to present.

Jake Gyllenhaal stars as Swofford, a young man who enlists in the Marine Corps because his father served in Vietnam and it seemed like the thing to do. Immediately upon setting foot in boot camp, he realizes that he may have made a bad mistake.

Enduring the stereotypical “in your face” drill sergeant and some not so good-natured hazing from his bunkmates, Swofford slowly becomes indoctrinated to the mindset of a “jarhead” – the self-appointed nickname for a Marine soldier.

Swofford is paired with Troy (Peter Sarsgaard) to be trained for the elite position of scout sniper. At the end of their training, Saddam Hussein gives them the opportunity to apply what they’ve learned… or so they thought.

Upon landing in the desert, these well-trained, eager for action men find that there’s little to do. They train, they drink, they wonder what their wives and girlfriends are doing at home. But most of all they begin to wonder why they are there – ground soldiers in a war that is being fought by planes and smart bombs.

Director Sam Mendes depicts the tedious life of the “grunt” soldier using strong language and surreal images. He isn’t able always to escape the danger of filming bored men and sometimes his film does lag a bit but he is ably assisted with strong performances by his main characters.

Gyllenhaal is quite good as the central figure – a man too intelligent to accept things blindly but a man too lost to find his own direction in life. His path of discovery is the one we follow. The others we meet along the way.

Peter Sarsgaard plays a man who knows exactly why he is there. It’s because he doesn’t belong anyplace else. It is a reality which turns his tale tragic when a secret he has been keeping threatens his continuation in the Corps he loves.

Jamie Foxx is a hard core staff sergeant in command of Swofford and Troy. His performance of a stock character is made more interesting by the authority and intensity with which he delivers it. Chris Cooper also appears in a small scene stealing cameo role.

While the violence is kept to a minimum, the profanity and images of the aftermath of violence are abundant. There is also a strong sexual undertone as these men must continually deal with separation anxiety and the frustrating loneliness of their duty.

Swofford’s training reminds us of the following scripture:

Blessed be the LORD my strength, which teacheth my hands to war, and my fingers to fight: Psalms 144:1 (KJV)

Swofford was drilled incessantly so that he could assemble, disassemble, load, aim, and fire his weapon mechanically. His rifle became part of him – as familiar to him as if it were an extension of his body.

Building this kind of muscle memory is available in all forms of discipline – including our spiritual walk. The more we read, study, and apply the truths of God’s Word, the more we are training ourselves to turn to it for the solutions to the problems or obstacles of our lives.
