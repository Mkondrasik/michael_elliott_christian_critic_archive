The Glass Jar

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 12, 1999
Rating
1 star
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: Not quoted in visible source
Sermon / spiritual topic: Arguments; foolish disputes; truth; meekness

Author-verified transcription

Put two relatively stupid yet opinionated individuals in a room and give them a reason they can’t leave. That’s the key concept behind The Glass Jar, one of the entries in this year’s Florida Film Festival.

Tony (Anthony Crivello) and John (John Kassir) are passing the time channel surfing and arguing about everything that comes to mind.

They argue about how smug Jeopardy’s Alex Trebeck is (“We should have marched up to Canada right after Korea and kicked their asses.”), or about how Christianity ruined the sport of hockey, or the observation that all Indians are merely Mexicans with feathers.

In between arguments, the phone rings and they stop. When it isn’t the call they’re anticipating, they go back to the TV and to their arguments.

The “mystery” of why they are there and what phone call they could be expecting loses its appeal almost as fast as this movie was losing its audience. The film became a tedious exercise in stamina... I’m talking about the audience, of course. I don’t know how the actors fared.

Anthony Crivello, at times, reminded me of a young Christopher Lloyd (around the Taxi era) except he hasn't quite mastered Mr. Lloyd's brilliant off-center timing which made his early work so enjoyable.

Meanwhile, John Kassir could be a stand-in double for Giovanni Ribisi. But I doubt the two more accomplished actors mentioned could have salvaged this weak effort from writer/director Gil Wadsworth. His characters are dimensionally challenged, the relationship between them defies logic, and the dialogue they speak evokes either groans or yawns depending upon the resiliency of each particular audience member.

The only one of the cast who managed to rise above the script was C. Thomas Howell in a brief cameo appearance as a mobster chieftain.

This is not to say that there were not effective moments in the film. After the reason for the characters’ inability to leave the house is made known, there is a 4 to 5 minute scene which is extremely tense and well directed. But even that dissolves and is wasted by the scenes following which stretch credibility to the breaking point and beyond. There is also a decent explosion scene that is cinematographically superior. But those two scenes alone are not going to carry or redeem this picture. It will be judged by how much people want to watch two uninformed people argue about inconsequential matters.

As a critic, I am well familiar with opinions and the fact that not everyone shares the same ones. As a Christian, I am well aware that people hold beliefs without substantiation as firmly as we hold the substantiated truth. To argue with such minds, even with proof in one’s hands, is fruitless.

Scriptures say to avoid foolish questions and strife. As such, we don’t argue with individuals who position themselves against the truth. Who are we to debate the veracity of the Word? The Word of God will always speak for itself. It doesn’t need us to defend it. We hold it forth and then we teach it to people who show a meek response to it and a hunger to know more. Those who don’t, have the free will right to choose incorrectly. No argument from us.

The title, The Glass Jar, comes from a story told by Tony. He used to keep bugs in a glass jar, but because there were no air holes, they would keep dying.

I imagine that we were supposed to see these two characters as bugs in an unventilated glass jar. The analogy doesn’t work for me. The bugs in question were caught and placed in the jar against their will. These two characters chose to be placed in this position. It was hard to stir up any sympathetic emotions for them.

Of course, all this is simply my opinion of the film. I’m sure, if you looked hard enough, you’d find somebody who would argue with me. Find him, turn a camera on us, and we may have a film for next year’s festival.
