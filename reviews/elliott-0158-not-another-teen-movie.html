Not Another Teen Movie

Author-verified transcription

I suppose that any popular film genre will invite spoofs. It is just that Not Another Teen Movie tries to make fun of a genre that hardly anyone took seriously in the first place.

The plot, such as it is, is borrowed from a number of well-known and oft-copied movies. Starting with the premise from She's All That, Jake, the high school jock (Chris Evans, Boston Public) makes a bet that he can turn bookish Janey (Chyler Leigh, Kickboxing Academy) into a prom queen. Taking a page from Cruel Intentions, Jake turns to his seductive and incest-minded sister Catherine (Mia Kirshner, Now and Forever) to help him turn a wallflower into the belle of the ball.

Meanwhile, Janey's nerdy friend Ricky (Eric Jungmann, The Faculty) is not-so-secretly pining away for her just like the guy from Sixteen Candles. During all these shenanigans, friends Mitch (Cody McMains, Tumbleweeds), Bruce (Samm Levine, Freaks and Geeks), and Ox (Sam Huntington, Detroit Rock City) make a pact (again) to lose their virginity a la American Pie.

Obvious references to other teen movies abound: Can't Hardly Wait, Bring It On, Varsity Blues, Pretty in Pink, 10 Things I Hate About You, Clueless, Porky's and The Breakfast Club, are among the titles which will be recognized in part.

Where the filmmakers run astray is the lengths that they go in search of a laugh... any laugh no matter how offensive. While there are a few amusing details: the high school is named after John Hughes (king of the teen movie genre); and a few familiar faces appear in cameo roles (including Paul Gleason as the detention monitor from The Breakfast Club), these moments are few and sandwiched between large sections of tedium and twaddle.

The mostly unknown cast appears to have been selected based on how closely they resemble or imitate familiar teen stars or the stereotypical teen characters. Based on that limited litmus test, the actors perform acceptably. It is very easy to pick out the Freddie Prinze Jr. type, the Jennifer Love Hewitt character, the Chris Klein look-alike, as well as the nerd, the morally loose girl, and the goody-two-shoes characters.

For the most part, Not Another Teen Movie misses opportunities. Using the same philosophy as the Wayen Brothers (Scary Movie), the filmmakers go for shock humor rather than wit or genuine satire. Casting an octogenarian as the "undercover" narc masquerading as a student was mildly amusing. Using the same actress in a send-up of the lesbian kiss scene from Cruel Intentions was not. It is this kind of over the top humor that warranted the R rating which Not Another Teen Movie received. The rating is an indication that the filmmakers did not have the teen audience in mind when they made their film.

It is difficult to extract anything spiritually worthwhile from a spoof. This is mainly because the whole point of this form of humor is to ridicule the subject being spoofed. It is a motivation which runs contrary to the Christian view and mindset.

Do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building others up according to their needs, that it may benefit those who listen. Ephesians 4:29 (NIV)

There is a kind of humor that will edify the viewer or listener; a humor that can heal, bring relief, and refresh the mind and soul. Not Another Teen Movie doesn't employ this kind of comedy. It is neither refreshing nor edifying. To tell the truth, it is hardly even funny.
