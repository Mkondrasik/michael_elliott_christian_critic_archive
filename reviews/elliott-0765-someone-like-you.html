Author-verified transcription

We've all heard it said that men can be "pigs." In Someone Like You, the filmmakers finally move us out of the porcine family. The bad news is that they merely shift us to a different genus. The argument is made that men's behavior can be understood because they act like cows.

Based on Laura Zigman's novel Animal Husbandry, Someone Like You focuses upon the unsuccessful love life of Jane Goodale (Ashley Judd, Double Jeopardy), a talent booking agent for a up and coming TV talk show. Getting dumped for no good reason isn't new for Jane and she is beginning to look for answers to the age-old questions, "What's wrong with men and why do they do the things they do?"

After Ray (Greg Kinnear, Nurse Betty), seemingly the perfect man, inexplicably backs out of their relationship, Jane is left hurting, homeless, and clueless as to why men keep leaving her. With no other place to turn, she moves into an apartment with the office lothario, Eddie (Hugh Jackman, X-Men). Watching him conduct his lifestyle of casual sex and meaningless encounters furthers her opinion that men have serious commitment problems.

She receives an epiphany of sorts when she stumbles upon an article which describes how a bull won't service the same cow twice. Thus is born Jane's "old cow theory," which likens men to the bulls who refuse to sexually perform unless they are presented with new cows. Her contention is that men are incapable of sustaining a prolonged relationship with an "old cow."

Blunt? Sure. Crass? Of course! Accurate? Oh, please. It is merely a fictitious oddball theory born of the hurt and confused mind of a jilted woman.

The acting is well above average. Ashley Judd does a fine job carrying the picture and she obviously is working hard at doing so. Her expressions are priceless. She can be coquettish, angry, flirtatious, and devastated... all within the same scene.

She is ably supported by Greg Kinnear as the bum who dumps her early on in the film. Is there an actor working today who portrays sincere insincerity any better? Hugh Jackman is the epitome of cool in his role and Marisa Tomei (My Cousin Vinnie) is a welcome addition as Jane's friend and confidante, Liz.

Despite the excellent cast, the picture never seems to hit its stride. There are moments which are, at turns, very funny, poignant, or emotional but the moments in between the highlights aren't enough to sustain our interest. Director Tony Goldwyn (A Walk On The Moon), elicits some wonderful work from his cast, but not enough to compensate for some of the weaknesses of the script. There are also a number of "dead" moments which should have been excised from the film during editing as they disrupt the film's rhythm and thus prevent any momentum from building.

In Someone Like You, Jane's "old cow theory" is embraced by a world looking for answers to excuse or rationalize the unfaithful sexual habits practiced by the male characters. While this adds to the "comedy" of the film's premise, it is not based upon scriptural truth. A monogamous relationship between a man and a woman is most certainly available. While God did design man and woman to have a natural physical attraction to one another, He also designed our bodies to be subject to our minds. To be monogamous is an act of the will, not a physical reflex action.

Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth. Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe; let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love. And why wilt thou, my son, be ravished with a strange woman, and embrace the bosom of a stranger? Proverbs 5:18-20 (KJV)

The rhetorical question is included in order to emphasize the logic of the exhortation which preceded it. The spirit of God within man and the free will choice we have to follow the guidance of God, instead of any desire of the flesh, sets us on a higher plane than any other creature God has placed upon this earth. Including, most definitely, cows.
