Author-verified transcription

As far as mixed or erroneous messages go, our culture can’t do much worse than the way it deals with the topics of sex and virginity. To say one thing while doing another is simply “par for the course” in our uptight but sexually obsessed society. The 40-Year-Old Virgin continues building that dichotomy and plays it for laughs.

Andy (Steve Carell) is a stock clerk of an electronics store. He rides a bike, dresses like a nerdy high school student, collects action figures, and has never (ever) had sex. He sounds like a typical teenager. Andy, however, is forty years old and therein is the problem... or so we are told.

When his coworkers guess his hidden secret, he becomes their pet project. Making Andy “a man” by forcing a sexual encounter upon him leads him into one embarrassing situation after another.

Meanwhile, he has managed to meet a woman who seems to like him. Trish (Catherine Keener), mother of three, is oddly attracted to Andy but he is advised not to “make a move” until he becomes more experienced. “You're going to be bad your first time out so you never want to do it with someone you like,” is the kind of convoluted advice coming his way.

So he attempts to find an intimacy with unlikely sources — among them a black transvestite and a free-spirited sex machine (Elizabeth Banks). What he intuitively discovers is the same thing that most of us eventually learn — that sex for sex's sake is not all that appealing.

And there’s the rub. Because if the overall message of the film is that being a 40- year-old virgin is nothing of which to be ashamed, why spend the lion’s share of the film making fun of the title character?

That objection aside, Steve Carell continues to prove himself to be a very funny individual. His deadpan expression and clueless demeanor is fodder for a number of laughs that the film produces. Granted, much of the humor comes along with an abundance of profanity and sexual references. The movie is rated R for pervasive sexual content, language and some drug use.

A lot of the bawdy humor also comes from the many supporting characters who surround Andy. They represent the supposedly “normal” people who have little else to think about other than the sex they have had or the sex they expect to have in the future. Paul Rudd, Romany Malco, and Seth Rogen play the trio of coworkers who won't rest until they know Andy has gone horizontal with a woman, even though their own sexual history makes them ill prepared to offer any constructive advice.

When considering sex from a spiritual perspective, it is important to remember that it is part of God’s design. He made the physical union between a man and a woman to be pleasurable and desirable.

Sex is or can be a beautiful thing between a man and a woman. But it has also led to the downfall of many individuals who have unwisely succumbed to its temptations and used it in a manner God never intended.

God gave us the ability to resist any physical desires that might lead us to make decisions that are unhealthy or spiritually unsound. There is nothing shameful about keeping one’s virginity until sex becomes less of a physical exercise and more an expression of love and commitment. That’s the lesson of the film as well... it’s just given in an insincere and exploitative way.
