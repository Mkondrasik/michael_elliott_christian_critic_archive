Bulletproof Monk

Author-verified transcription

"Stifler" as an action hero? Sounds implausible, but in a kung-fu movie like Bulletproof Monk where laws of physics don't apply, the laws of logic are also often in short supply.

According to the film, there exists a Tibetan scroll which, if ever read by man, would give that man the power to control the world. And so an order of Buddhist monks selects one guardian to protect it from ever falling into the wrong hands. That guardian, granted special powers and protected from aging, will, at the end of 60 years, choose the next keeper of the scroll based upon the fulfillment of three prophecies.

The current guardian (Chow Yun-Fat, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon), for the past 60 years has been on the run from a nefarious Nazi general (Karel Roden, Blade II) whose obsession with the scroll has stretched across the decades.

A chance meeting with a common pickpocket/Asian cinema projectionist named Kar (Seann William Scott, Stifler of American Pie) leads the monk with no name to believe he may have found a most unlikely replacement.

Together they do battle against the aging general, his Aryan daughter (Victoria Smurfit, About a Boy), and their black-suited, neo-Nazi henchmen.

We shouldn't be surprised to learn that this ridiculous premise is based upon a comic book (and an obscure one at that). The inanity of the story line aside, the fight sequences, which are typically the saving grace of these kind of movies, are simply not up to Chow Yun-Fat's standard.

There are flashes that suggest a better movie hidden somewhere within the celluloid. (Kar learns his fighting technique by imitating what he sees in the Asian cinema; there's a short sequence involving Yun-Fat that reminds us of the work he did with director John Woo; and the two male leads do have a certain chemistry that works). But first time director Paul Hunter offers little of the artistry needed to strip away all that is bad or amateurish in the film.

On a positive note, the film does imply that there will be life after American Pie for actor Seann William Scott. Setting his dumb, beer-drinking Stifler persona aside as well as his even dumber "Dude, Where's My Car" character, Scott manages to present himself as a credible, likable leading man type. Who'd of thought?

In the film, three prophecies must be fulfilled which would show the current guardian the individual who has been chosen to be the next "keeper of the scroll." If that sounds familiar, it is. A Messiah had long been promised to God's people. By researching the Old Testament, we will come across a number of prophecies that give clues as to what this Messiah would do once he arrived. By researching the gospels which record the life and ministry of Jesus Christ, we see each one of those prophecies fulfilled.

And he said unto them, These [are] the words which I spake unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and [in] the prophets, and [in] the psalms, concerning me. Luke 24:44 (KJV)

This Easter weekend, which is when this film opens, it might be beneficial to remember that he who could have been truly "bulletproof" chose instead to be killed so that we might live eternally.
