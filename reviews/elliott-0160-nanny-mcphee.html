Nanny McPhee

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 27, 2006
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Obedience; parenting; discipline; consequences; honoring parents

Author-verified transcription

Sugary sweet Mary Poppins' day must be over. Today's nannies have to be made of a different cloth if they hope to survive the difficulties of raising children in today's world.

When Nanny McPhee comes to the aid of the Brown family, her warts, unibrow, bulbous nose and generally gruesome appearance masks the truth - she is exactly what the family needs.

This fits right in with her one condition: If the family needs her but does not want her, she must stay. When they want her but do not need her, she must go. Based on the children's initial reaction to her arrival, she'll be there for quite a long while.

Mr. Brown (Colin Firth) is a widowed mortician who is incapable of managing his seven unruly offspring. Led by Simon (Thomas Sangster), the eldest child, they take pride in having run off 17 previous caretakers in record time. They figure that this new nanny will be no different.

But it is McPhee, her magical ways, and her much needed lessons in obedience that turns this household on its well-cuffed ear. And, oddly enough, as the children begin to respond favorably to McPhee, her appearance grows less hideous to them.

But learning discipline is just part of the Browns' problem. Matronly Aunt Adelaide, who provides financial aid to the weak-willed father, is threatening to pull her money from the family unless he weds by the end of the month. The only prospect in sight is the perfectly dreadful (and outrageously costumed) Mrs. Quickly (Celia Imrie). It would be a disaster for all concerned but with the children now on good behavior, how can they stop their father from making a terrible mistake?

Emma Thompson wrote the screenplay based upon the Nurse Matilda children's books by Christianna Brand. She isn't going for subtle humor or complex messages. Her script is simple, straightforward, and rather predictable. Laced with a great deal of physical humor (pratfalls, food fights, sight gags, and a bright neon color scheme) Nanny McPhee is definitely for the younger crowd. And though we can all see the ending coming all the way from the opening reel, it arrives in a pleasant and appealing sort of way.

Emma Thompson, even from behind all the prosthetic makeup, shines as the titular character. Unflappable, all-knowing, and resolute in her actions, she's the calm in the midst of a storm around which everything revolves.

Firth is appropriately flustered and amusingly ineffective as the clueless dad. Celia Imrie is a hoot as the fashion-challenged Mrs. Quickly. Angela Lansbury has a few scene-stealing moments as the overbearing and intrusive family matriarch. And the kids seem to be having fun playing kids.

All of Nanny McPhee's rules are born out of the same spiritual truth. Obedience is the key to life, love, and happiness.

Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. Ephesians 6:1 (KJV)

For many of us, learning to obey our parents whom we can see and hear is simply the first step towards developing our ability to obey our heavenly Father who we cannot see.

In both of these cases, (obeying God and obeying our parents), our obedience will be rewarded with blessings that we can scarcely imagine at the time just as our lapses in obedience will be met with negative consequences that we often did not first consider.
