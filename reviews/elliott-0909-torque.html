Author-verified transcription

Torque: 1) n. a force that produces rotation or torsion (ex. "Wow dude, what kind of torque does that awesome bike put out?"); 2) v. slang for being seriously frustrated and angry (Oh man, I am so torqued.); 3) n. a film with elements of the first definition that elicits a response from the audience matching the second (ex. Torque).

Who knew that it would be possible to "dumb down" 2 Fast 2 Furious? And yet the makers of Torque not only managed to do it, they went above and beyond the call. Torque is not just dumbed down... it is dumbed up, sideways, forwards, and backwards. Name a direction that this film can go and it will dumb its way there.

Its ludicrous plot deals with Ford (Martin Henderson), a hot shot biker, who has spent the last 6 months in Thailand, hiding from both the law and certain unsavory characters who would kill him given half a chance over a little matter of a million dollars worth of drugs. Ford returns to LA. Naturally.

He comes back for Shane (Monet Mazur), his blonde, curvy girlfriend whom he left without an explanation. At first, she wants nothing more to do with him. But after playing hard to get for a few seconds, she succumbs to his screenwritten charms (which, unfortunately, we never manage to actually see translated to the screen.)

Alas for our love birds, there is no room for romance as the drug bikers, the black bikers, and the incompetent Feds all descend upon Ford to claim their pound of flesh. He chooses not to stick around and heads out to the desert with his biker buddies.

But return he shall - this time for a final showdown: bike vs. bike; mano a mano; stupid is as stupid does.

It is hard to know where to begin a critique for a film like Torque. The acting is cartoonish. The stunts are loud, broad, fast and completely unbelievable. They exist only because they happen to be the only draw this sorry excuse for a movie has.

Don't look to the acting. There's nothing there to see. Please don't look to the screenplay. It is an embarrassment of the first order. It, at times, even tries to be self-aware in a hip sort of way by poking fun at itself. It fails miserably. We can do a much better job of it. Don't look to the direction (by Joseph Kahn in his first and hopefully last attempt). His copycat style fails to provide much torque to this lame project.

If there is one redeeming feature to this film, it is the relatively short length (approximately 81 minutes). Play your cards right and you can catch another film right after to purge yourself of any foul memories that may remain.

Amid all the scantily clad women, illegal activities, foolish and dangerous driving (or is it biking?), and inane banter, there was actually one piece of wisdom offered. When the leader of the drug biker gang tries to pin a murder on Ford to force him to cooperate, Ford responds by saying, "You ever hear that what you put out will come back to you tenfold?"

Even as I have seen, they that plow iniquity, and sow wickedness, reap the same. Job 4:8 (KJV)

Actions do have consequences and bad actions will have bad consequences. The bad guys in the film are certainly punished for the despicable actions they take. I just have to wonder what's in store for the filmmakers who are responsible for this fiasco. Praise God for forgiveness, grace and mercy. They'll need it.
