Red Planet

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 10, 2000
Rating
1½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 46 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Peter 3:13Isaiah 65:17Isaiah 45:18

Sermon / spiritual topic: Stewardship; Earth; Salvation

Author-verified transcription

Dreamers have long looked at the stars and fantasized of interstellar colonization. However, fantasies are rarely proved to be as desirable as first thought. Red Planet paints such a dismal picture that perhaps those same dreamers might be encouraged to begin refocusing their energies on more earthbound projects.

Set in the not-to-distant future, the spacecraft Mars 1 is on a rescue mission. The inhabitants of Earth have so trashed their planet that they have no other option but to look elsewhere in the cosmos for a home. After seeding Mars with algae to begin the oxygenation process, the Earth's scientists are puzzled when the oxygen count begins to drop. So they send a manned mission to the planet to investigate and correct the situation. Failure may mean extinction for the human race.

The Mars 1 mission is commanded by Kate Bowman (Carrie-Anne Moss, The Matrix.) As the only woman on board of a space ship which will take 182 days just to reach Mars orbit, she nonetheless spends her first few moments in the shower where even the janitor can catch a glimpse of her naked body. Since this is a PG-13 movie, he has a better view than we.

Upon reaching orbit, a sudden disaster forces the landing crew to rush their departure and crash on Mars jettisoning their high tech navigational robot (AMEE) in the process. Bowman, who stayed on board the mother ship, must try to guide them to the landing site where oxygen and provisions necessary for their survival await them.

Faced with the desolate terrain, failing equipment, and environmental conditions unsuitable for human life, the death of the landing crew appears to be certain. And that's before they discover that AMEE has gone over to the dark side and is trying to kill them off as well.

Despite the interesting special effects, the quality of the production design of the space craft, and the simplicity of the story itself, there is something essential missing. Red Planet is as emotionally cold as the vacuum of space. We simply never become interested in the characters or their stories and so when disaster begins to pick them off one by one, we shrug and wait for the next victim to bite the Martian dust.

Antony Hoffman, an award winning director of commercials, makes a dubious feature film debut. While the film is visually striking, the narrative is surprisingly dull.

Except for Val Kilmer (At First Sight) who plays the mission's janitor AKA systems engineer, we never really get a clue as to the personalities of the ground crew. Tom Sizemore (Saving Private Ryan) is Burchenal, head of the science team who is puzzled by what they find on Mars. Benjamin Bratt (The Next Best Thing) is Santen, the cocky copilot. Simon Baker (LA Confidential) is Pettengil, a last minute replacement on the mission. Terence Stamp (The Limey) appears briefly as Chantilas, a man of science who has turned to God for the answers science cannot provide. Granted, these descriptions are pitifully insufficient, but it is all the information we are ever really given.

As far as the premise of mankind needing a new world to inhabit, we who trust in the scriptures have the comfort of God's promises and the assurance He gives that this world will endure until the Lord's return. There will be an end to the world in which we live, but it won't be come about by our actions or inactions. The God Who created it will be the One to end it, replacing it with a newer and better version.

Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness. 2 Peter 3:13 (KJV)

That doesn't give us liberty to trash the earth we now have, for we are also called to be stewards, but neither do we need fear that our planet is so fragile that we must move to Mars if our species is to continue.
