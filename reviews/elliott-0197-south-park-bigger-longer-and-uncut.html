MEMORANDUM

TO: Trey Parker, Matt Stone RE: South Park: Bigger, Longer and Uncut

I suppose a begrudging congratulations are in order, gentlemen. Early reports indicate your new movie to be a resounding critical success. I'm sure that comes as a bit of a surprise to you, seeing as you went out of your way to insert as much vulgarity, perversion, and miscellaneous naughtiness as has ever been amassed in a 90 minute feature film.

Of course we'd expect a certain percentage of the population to respond to your film with glee and excitement. The idea of your childish animation depicting those featureless third grade characters with sweet chipmunk-like voices as they spout the filthiest epithets imaginable has a certain shock value which is sure to attract the curious and titillate the young of mind.

But to gain the acceptance of the critics must be considered a real coup. It was a brilliant move on your part to center your film around an obvious "social/political" satirical statement on the importance of "Free Speech." By doing so, you may have helped to deflect some of the deserved negative criticism that could have been directed your way.

The irony of having your characters, Cartman, Stan, Kenny, and Kyle sneaking into an R-rated film and mimicking the language and behavior they see is unmistakable. From my theater seat, I could see a few 11 and 12 year olds rollicking with laughter and singing along with your catchy but unprintable tune, "Shut Your [expletive deleted] Face, Uncle [expletive deleted]." Hearing that emulation has already started must make your hearts swell with pride.

Of course, you make it clear that your movie should not be held responsible in any way for the imitative actions of children who may see it. You cleverly emphasize that point in your film by having the boys' parents lay the blame at the feet of the Canadian stars of your film within a film. Tensions mount until war breaks out between the nations. All over a few inventive dirty words. As one of your sweet cherubic-faced characters says: "What's the big [expletive-deleted] deal?" How wise of you not to provide an intelligent, morally-sound character who could answer that question.

It was also very clever of you to turn your malicious, acerbic wit on so many targets. Where should your detractors start? On the language, the overt sexual references, the character assassinations, the violence, or the spiritual error? It's all so overwhelming.

You must realize, of course, that you have crossed the line spiritually. Depicting Satan as a lonely, sensitive soul while calling God a [expletive deleted] is too offensive for words. Amazingly, you have a God-given right to your spiritual beliefs and a constitutional right to express them. You see, God so loves us that He allows us the choice to love Him back. Within the freedom that God grants us is His desire that we freely come to Him. I find it pitiful that you've chosen to use your spiritual freedom in such a thoughtless and heartless manner.

We shouldn't make the mistake of thinking that freedom of speech is free. It is not even cheap. We have attained it at a great price. Such a precious right should not be handled frivolously. Within the freedom we enjoy comes a responsibility to exercise it with wisdom and a certain amount of common sense. To abuse the freedom and ignore the responsibility one has for one's fellow man is simply reprehensible.

You, my brothers, were called to be free. But do not use your freedom to indulge the sinful nature; rather, serve one another in love. Galatians 5:13 [NIV]

Your indulgent success comes at a dear price so try not to chortle too much as you watch America embrace your obscenity-filled cultural indictment. You've found that it is rather easy to appeal to man's baser nature. Lowering the bar is never difficult. Raising it is where the challenge lies. Your movie will indeed have an impact on our culture. For that, you should be [expletive deleted] ashamed of yourselves.
