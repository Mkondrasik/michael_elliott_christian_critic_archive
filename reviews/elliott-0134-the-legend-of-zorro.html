The Legend of Zorro

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 28, 2005
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Colossians 2:6-7Psalms 1:3Matthew 7:24-27

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Priorities; foundation; family; faith; responsibilities

Author-verified transcription

The original man in black rides again in The Legend of Zorro. Director Martin Campbell and his stars (Antonio Banderas, Catherine Zeta Jones) reunite 7 years after their first foray (The Mask of Zorro) which scored a bull's eye with a global audience. This time, they're a little off their mark.

Don Alejandro de la Vega (Banderas) is now married to the beautiful Elena (Jones) and together they have produced a delightfully playful son Joaquin (Adrian Alonso). But all is not perfect in their paradise circa 1850. As California prepares itself for its entrance into American statehood, de la Vega's secret life as the swashbuckling Zorro is needed more than ever. There are some who do not wish for the event to take place. The territory needs a hero but his heroic activities are making him a stranger to his son and estranged from his wife.

Elena throws Alejandro out of the house and into a tailspin when she later succumbs to the charms of a French nobleman (Rufus Sewall) who has plans that goes far beyond wooing the high spirited beauty. He's a lot less obvious than his hired henchman, Jake McGivens (Nick Chinlund) who has been terrorizing anyone who shows support of the move towards statehood.

Banderas does make for an appealing Zorro - strong and athletic with a touch of whimsy that brings out some nice comedic touches. Jones has never looked lovelier than she does in these Zorro movies. She was breathtaking 7 years ago and the years have been kind. She also manages to adopt a believable Spanish accent.

Despite this, 11 year old Adrian Alonso is able to steal the film from both of his more experienced co-stars. His comedic timing is very good and the scene in his classroom where he emulates his hero Zorro (not knowing that Zorro is his father) is priceless.

Still, the film is not as much fun as its predecessor. There are moments which hint at the fact that all the ingredients are there and yet for some reason, this cinematic cake didn't rise. The chemistry between Banderas and Jones is still evident but their scenes together were few and far between. And the sequel had no Anthony Hopkins who provided so much depth of soul in the original.

Life is not one dimensional and we all wear many "hats" throughout our lifetime: Father, son, husband, employer or employee, coach, mentor, hero... The trick in balancing all areas of life is to know how and where to set our priorities. This becomes easier once we establish a foundation.

The steadier we are, the easier we can juggle responsibilities. De la Vega's problems began when his perceived responsibilities (i.e. being a hero) became his foundation or reason for being. His life as Zorro stole from his time with his family. Yet as he mentioned in the film, "My family is my life." His actions up to that point contradicted his statement. The proper foundation of life is set forth in the Scriptures.

As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him: Rooted and built up in him, and stablished in the faith, as ye have been taught, abounding therein with thanksgiving. Colossians 2:6-7 (KJV)

As we stand faithfully upon this foundation of faith, we can handle any and all responsibilities successfully, manifesting God's grace and goodness.
