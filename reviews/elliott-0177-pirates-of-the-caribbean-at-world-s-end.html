Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 25, 2007
Rating
2 stars

Sermon / spiritual topic: Trust

Author-verified transcription

Proving an axiom to be true by testing its converse is an old trick. Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End plays the trick on us by proving without a doubt that "more is indeed less." This third installment of the popular Disney franchise is overly flush in pirates, swashbuckling, supernatural curses, and mind-blowing, sea-faring adventures... but even with nearly three hours to play out its story, the end result is a muddled, albeit occasionally entertaining, mess.

Johnny Depp returns (in spades) as the newly-dead Captain Jack Sparrow. Imprisoned in Davy Jones' locker (a desert-like place), Sparrow has no means of escape unless his fool-hardy friends and enemies conspire to free him.

Elizabeth Swann (Keira Knightly) and Will Turner (Orlando Bloom) do just that but their motives might not serve Jack's best interests. Captain Barbossa (Geoffrey Rush) is also on board the rescue ship with an agenda of his own.

There are more plot lines going on here than can be categorized or even fully followed: Barbossa has a plan to defeat the English armada run by Lord Cutler Beckett. His plan requires the assistance from the nine pirate lords (a group in which Jack Sparrow happens to be a part) to release a sea goddess trapped in a human's body. There's the romance between Swann and Turner; the obligation that Turner Jr. feels towards his father Bootstrap Bill (Stellan Skarsgaard); the enmity that just about every character feels towards Jack; a back-story concerning Davy Jones and Tia Dalma (Naomie Harris); and a slew of vignettes featuring the ragtag crew of misfit pirates and scallywags.

In the end, none of these stories matter because the narrative presented to us makes little sense. The script is weak. But this is a big budget production that the studio knew would make money and so it spared no expense in its production values. The last 40 minutes or so is non-stop action that will not disappoint. Just try to stay awake until you get to that point.

Depp does the swishy-swashbuckling bit to death and is consistently enjoyable but his character would have been better served with a solid, linear narrative to drive the action.

A common theme to the film seems to be one of trust. Characters constantly turn the tables on one another or keep secrets from each other in order to serve their own interests. Trust is therefore in short supply and even when it appears it is usually abused and/or broken.

Trust can be just as hard to come by in our day and time. Once earned, it is easily lost and once lost, it is nearly impossible to regain. That is why it is such a source of comfort to recognize that we have a God and Father in whom we can always trust without hesitation or reservation. He cannot lie. He cannot be coerced. He will not disappoint those who put their trust in Him.

(Pro 30:5) Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. (KJV)
