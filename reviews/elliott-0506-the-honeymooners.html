Author-verified transcription

In 1951, Jackie Gleason and a brilliant supporting cast introduced to the world a quartet of characters which changed the face of television comedy history. The Honeymooners followed the lives of the lovable loser Ralph Kramden, his long-suffering wife Alice, and their best friends and neighbors Ed and Trixie Norton. So strong was the concept and so powerful were these characterizations that the show would become a classic and the actors would be forever linked to the characters they brought to life.

So it was with a great deal of apprehension that I approached the 2005 movie remake of this popular series. How can any filmmaker or actor hope to measure up to what many consider to be the perfect example of comedic casting? Bottom line - they can't and they don't. Perhaps if the original cast members weren't still so vividly etched in our memories, the new experience would be more enjoyable. But, as it is, these new Honeymooners have been eclipsed by the stellar work done by their predecessors more than 50 years ago.

Cedric the Entertainer stars as Ralph Kramden, the big-dreaming bus driver whose get-rich-quick schemes never bears the fruit he desires. He dips into his meager savings account to buy an antique train car that sanitation specialist Norton (Mike Epps) found in an abandoned subway line. When that investment turns out to be a monumental error, he needs to replace the money before wife Alice (Gabrielle Union) finds out.

She is going to need that cash to finally realize her life's dream of home-ownership. With their savings, pooled with Trixie's savings, and a little help from Ralph's mother-in-law (Carol Woods), she just might be able to scrape enough together to make the required down-payment for a cute little duplex that's on the market.

So Ralph once more finds himself behind the eight ball and the only possible hope he has is a scrawny greyhound that pal Norton rescued from a dumpster. Using the rest of their savings to hire a dubious dog trainer, played by John Leguizamo who provides the only truly comically inspired performance, they plan to race the animal all the way to fame and (more importantly) fortune.

This being a Kramden plan, the outcome can easily be guessed. Ralph is so busy trying to strike it rich that he can't see that his actions are taking him in the wrong direction. There is a biblical principle at work here.

He that hasteth to be rich hath an evil eye, and considereth not that poverty shall come upon him. Proverbs 28:22 (KJV)

It is no surprise to find that Alice had the right idea all along. God isn't "anti-wealth." It is just that He is more concerned with what we carry in our hearts than in our pockets. Remember it is "love of money," not money itself, that is the root of all evil.

God has established the way to prosperity. If we work hard, live within our means, faithfully tithe and share our abundance, the promise of God is that we shall prosper. Being rich is not the goal or motivation of our lives - it is the fruit that is reaped as we stay biblically-minded in all that we do.
