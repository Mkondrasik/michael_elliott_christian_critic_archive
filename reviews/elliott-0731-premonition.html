Author-verified transcription

The problem with Premonition has nothing to do with the talent assembled or with the story they attempt to tell. The flaw in this film is more basic. It asks us to accept a contradiction in the laws of the physical universe without providing an explanation for it.

Sandra Bullock plays Linda, a young mother of two who must live through one of the most horrifying of life's situations: being told that her husband has died in a car accident. Linda tries to cope with the aftermath that includes telling her daughters and imagining a life without Jim, her husband (Julian McMahon).

The odd thing is that, although she goes to bed as a widow, when she awakens, her husband is sitting there at the kitchen table... alive and well and wondering why his wife has a stricken look on her face. She convinces herself that his death was simply a morbid dream but the next time she awakens from sleep, he's dead again. The next morning, he's once again alive.

This back and forth scenario continues for far too long before she comes to the realization that she's inexplicably bouncing around in time. As she begins piecing the events together in the correct chronological order, she discovers some things that call everything she knows into question.

The film takes a very long time to establish any kind of tension or excitement. The first third of the film simply establishes the young couple's relationship and the subsequent shock and grief that follows the untimely demise that ended it.

It isn't until the time bouncing starts that things get interesting but ironically, this is also the film's largest problem. Audiences need a reason to believe, (no matter how implausible that reason may be), the events they see unfold before them. The filmmakers offer no such explanation for the abandonment of natural law.

Bullock underplays her role which, in a different film, could have proven to be effective. Here, it simply underscores the fact that we're waiting for something to happen.

Julian McMahon, as her now-he's-dead, now-he's-not husband, is likable enough. But there's not much more needed from his character.

The filmmakers also try to inject a little spiritual/religious subplot but they completely fail to provide any substance that would give it credence. They seem to be saying that Linda's ability to transcend time is a reaction to her loss of faith. In other words, "premonition" is granted to those who need to find their way back to what they once believed.

That's quite a stretch to make and one that cannot be supported by those who study the scriptures. The closest thing to "premonition" that is recorded in God's Word is the revelation that comes to those who believe. But since believing is the key ingredient to receiving revelation, it kind of argues against the point to which the filmmakers allude.

Revelation is the receiving of information that cannot be naturally known and has always been the purview of God. He communicates to those who believe via the spirit which dwells within us. His Spirit speaks to our spirit which communicates to our mind.

But God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit: for the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God. For what man knoweth the things of a man, save the spirit of man which is in him? even so the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God. Now we have received, not the spirit of the world, but the spirit which is of God; that we might know the things that are freely given to us of God. 1 Corinthians 2:10-12 (KJV)
