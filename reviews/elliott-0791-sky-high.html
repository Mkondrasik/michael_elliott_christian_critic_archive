Author-verified transcription

Following in the footsteps of one's father can be a difficult goal to achieve. It would be even more difficult if you were the son of a superhero. In Sky High, that is exactly the situation in which Will Stronghold (Michael Anagaro) finds himself.

Not only is his father, The Commander (Kurt Russell), blessed with superhuman strength, his mother, Jet Stream (Kelly Preston), can fly. Together his parents form the greatest superhero team on the planet and everyone expects their son to one day join them in the family business. There's only one problem... young Will has no super powers.

Even so, because of his pedigree, off he goes to the elite Sky High where the teen age kids of today's superheroes learn how to use their powers for good or for evil. Because he has yet to develop powers, Will is assigned to the "hero support" program. It is familiarly known as being for "sidekicks" but widely referred to by the rest of the student body as being the final stop for losers.

It is there that Michael forms friendships with the "B-list" of the student body. Even for sidekicks, their powers are a bit mild. One guy glows. Another can melt. One girl is able to shapeshift into a guinea pig. And Lyla (Darell Panabaker), Will's best friend since the first grade, can control plant growth. These are not the kind of powers that are commonly associated with saving the world.

When Will's super strength finally kicks in, he is given a taste of how "the other half lives." Transferred to the hero track, Will makes new friends and receives the attention and acclaim so often denied to sidekicks. But it comes at a price.

A superhero is nothing without an arch-enemy and Sky High has a couple of them... along with a plot twist or two that not only keeps things interesting but also delivers a message appropriate to its PG targeted audience.

More than a summer special effects movie, Sky High blends the genre with a character driven coming of age tale that will connect with teens and preteens alike. Director Mike Mitchell keeps the characters and relationships based in reality even though the situations revolving around those characters are pure fantasy. He delivers a very entertaining film suited for a family outing.

Michael Anagaro (Little Secrets) makes a very appealing central character - a kind of Everyman superman. Danell Panabaker is adorable as the girl next door holding a secret crush. Mary Elizabeth Winstead is alluring as an attractive senior who has set her sights on Will.

The adult actors also add to the fun. Kurt Russell has some amusing moments as a proud papa champing at the bit to bring his son into his "secret sanctum." Kelly Preston plays the ultimate in multi-tasking moms. Cloris Leachman has a brief show-stealing scene as the school nurse who treats Will for being a late bloomer.

Layla has a problem with the school's practice of dividing its students into two groups (heroes and non-heroes) based upon the type of power or talent they display. She's right. That kind of discrimination leads to the belief that one kind of power or talent is superior to another.

God has a different way of looking at the topic. He has given certain gifts and the ability to operate (or manifest) those gifts to men. These supernatural powers are listed in 1 Corinthians 12 but we should note that He never assigned a ranking preference to them. Instead He gave us this exhortation:

"But covet earnestly the best gifts: and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way." 1 Corinthians 12:31 (KJV)

What are the best gifts or powers? As the kids in Sky High learn, they are the powers that best meet the need at hand. The ability to glow may not sound like much of a superpower until the day we find ourselves locked in a dark space. That day it could very well save our lives.

We should never treat anyone as being insignificant or inferior. Every single person has the ability to make a valued contribution. We just make them in different ways and at different times.
