Return With Honor

Author-verified transcription

"Return With Honor" was the motto of hundreds of pilots who were captured during the Vietnam conflict and held for up to eight and one half years in North Vietnam as prisoners of war.

The phrase now also serves as the fitting title for a long overdue documentary that tells not only their riveting story of survival, but touches upon the effect their capture and long imprisonment had on their wives and families back home.

The story is told using the personal reports of these men and their loved ones as well as amazing film footage of the time. In their own words, we hear them relate what it was like to be shot down, treated as an object of scorn and derision, and tortured beyond their human endurance to withstand.

They also tell us of the methods they used to stay strong, to keep their hope and courage alive, and to maintain their sanity in an increasingly insane situation.

Many of them testified to their unwavering faith in God. In one chilling recollection, a former POW was relating one particularly rough torture session. Knowing he could not physically continue absorbing the pain being inflicted, he turned the entire situation over to God, believing for deliverance. What happened next is a reminder for all of us that God never leaves us or forsakes us.

The political rationalizations for America's involvement in Vietnam are, appropriately enough, not handled in this documentary. Such musings are better left to others. The men we get to know through this film were not politicians. They were soldiers called to serve our country. They did so in the harshest of circumstances and in the most confusing of times.

In the midst of such hellish conditions, their only desire was to "return with honor" to the country they loved and served. They did so without question. Ms. Mock and Ms. Sanders have done well to not only bring their story to the screen, but to allow them to tell it themselves.
