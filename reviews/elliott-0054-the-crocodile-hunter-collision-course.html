The Crocodile Hunter: Collision Course

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 12, 2002
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Creation; dominion; stewardship; care for animals

Author-verified transcription

“Crikey.” That seems to be the word of the moment now that The Crocodile Hunter: Collision Course, the latest Australian cinematic effort to reach mainstream release in the US, has begun its theatrical run.

Australian adventurer Steve Irwin stars alongside his wife Terri. Together they serve as wildlife rescuers/conservationists traversing the outback looking for animals in need of human assistance to protect them from human interference.

While known to a global audience due to their numerous documentaries and television specials, this marks the Irwins’ first entry into the realm of motion pictures. Wisely, they stay within the characters that have brought them such widespread acceptance... they play themselves.

The other actors are not as fortunate. They are saddled with an implausible plot and superficial characters. This, of course, makes Steve and Terri more interesting and real by contrast.

Here’s the plot line: A satellite self-destructs while in orbit, jettisoning its surveillance photos contained in a metal ball. Should these photos wind up in the wrong hands, it would change the balance of power currently in the world.

That metal ball, naturally, gets swallowed by a 12 foot long crocodile. The same croc falls into the care and protection of Steve and Terri Irwin, who are determined to relocate it to an area free from poachers.

When the federal agents arrive to claim their top secret object, Steve mistakes them for poachers out to kill the croc in his care. Crikey.

To be fair, Collision Course is both amusing and educational. Who knew that the bird-eating spider of Australia could be so fascinating? Irwin, due to his goofy but engaging and personable manner which has long been appreciated by his TV fans, makes the film far more enjoyable than it otherwise might have been.

Magda Szubanski (Babe: Pig in the City) is on board as Brozzie, a croc-hating cattle rancher who uses a shotgun to protect her herd. Most of her scenes are with David Wenham (Seachase) who plays a ranger who tries to keep Brozzie under control until the Irwins arrive.

Lachy Hulme (The Matrix II) and Kenneth Ransom (Crocodile Dundee in Los Angeles) play a pair of relatively incompetent CIA agents out of their element in the Aussie wilds. Kate Beahan (Chopper) is their attractive Australian counterpart.

The extraneous plot is immediately forgettable. It exists merely to put a framework around Irwin’s antics with poisonous snakes, toxic spiders, and deadly crocodiles. Throughout the film, the Irwins never drop their documentary personas. This is a real hoot as the silly spy subplot enters into their domain. Irwin describes the behavior of his two legged pursuers with the same sense of outback awe and wonder that he uses with every other form of life he encounters.

It should be said that, from all appearances, Irwin is the real deal. His handling of the various deadly creatures in this film do not look at all staged. Whether he’s trying to lasso a 12 foot long crocodile, or keep an 8 foot long King Brown snake from sinking his fangs into his legs as he’s holding it by the tail, there is a very real danger which heightens the tension and thus, our interest and fascination.

Irwin’s knowledge, care and mastery of the “beasts of the field” is a demonstration of man’s superiority over the animal kingdom. It is a superiority which has existed since creation itself.

“And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.” Genesis 1:26 (KJV)

With that dominion or superiority comes an additional responsibility of stewardship. That is the main message that the Irwins hope will be communicated through their film.
