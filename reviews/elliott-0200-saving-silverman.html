Saving Silverman

Author-verified transcription

It is a good thing for Saving Silverman that I am not God. After watching this ridiculous film, I've come to the conclusion that some things are simply not deserving of salvation.

Saving Silverman is centered around the close friendship shared by Darren Silverman (Jason Biggs, American Pie), Wayne Le Fessier (Steve Zahn, Happy Texas), and J.D McNugent (Jack Black, High Fidelity). These three numb-brained dorks have managed to stay friends since the fifth grade... most likely because their crude behavior and immature personalities have prevented their social circle from growing any larger.

When the beautiful and strong-willed psychiatrist Judith (Amanda Peet, Whipped), inexplicably sets her sights on Darren she makes him leave his friends behind and sets about remaking him to her liking. He, of course, is hormonally incapable of resisting her overbearing demands.

Wayne and J.D., unwilling to see their friend lost to the whims of this she-devil, concoct an elaborate plan to kidnap Judith and reunite Darren with his high-school fantasy Sandy (Amanda Detmer, Boys and Girls) even though she is one week away from becoming the first nun ever to wear a pushup bra.

Because the level of humor which runs throughout the film is so childish it is rather shocking to realize that the film is rated PG-13 because its content is inappropriate for children. If our average intelligence as a society has truly shrunken to the point that films such as these reach widespread acceptance or appreciation, it does not bode well for the future of art, literature, or the moral climate of our culture.

The cast has done far superior work in the past but seeing as they are saddled with a script by newcomers Greg DePaul and Hank Nelken, which rivals the recent Head Over Heels for the distinction of being the worst written film of the year, one can argue that they are not to blame.

Whether it is depicting Sandy's family as a group of circus freaks, inserting a near cult worship of Neil Diamond as a recurring plot device, or depending upon a continual stream of sexually oriented innuendoes and sight gags, Saving Silverman demonstrates its lack of taste and wit in just about every scene.

Steve Zahn brings energy and conviction to whatever role he is in. But here, we just don't buy the level of stupidity in which his character operates. Likewise, Jack Black, as the dimmest bulb in the already low-wattage pack, overplays the dunce-like nature of his character to the end that it is neither amusing nor of interest. Jason Biggs plays a basically good guy who is awkward around girls, not unlike his previous roles.

Director Dennis Dugan (Big Daddy) cannot convince us to suspend our disbelief long enough to accept, even for a moment, the incredible leaps of logic necessary to follow the action.

While my opening paragraph was a tongue-in-cheek comment playing off of the title of the film, there is a kernel of truth in it. None of us are "deserving" of the salvation we have received. And yet God has freely offered it to all of us out of the goodness of His grace. But in order to accept his salvation, we must become meek to His truth and repent or change our erroneous thought patterns and behavioral patterns.

Now when they heard this, they were pricked in their heart, and said unto Peter and to the rest of the apostles, Men and brethren, what shall we do? Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. Acts 2:37-38 (KJV)

Notice that the repentance had to come before the promise of deliverance. If Hollywood wants to save itself from the immoral and nonintellectual sewer into which it has been progressively sinking, there must be a fundamental change in the way they approach their movie making, script development, and marketing.
