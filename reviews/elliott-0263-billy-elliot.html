Billy Elliot

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 13, 2000

3 stars

1 hr: 50 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Gifts; talent; passion; discipline; developing abilities

Author-verified transcription

Is there anything so dreary as an impoverished English mining community whose miners are on strike? But as "Billy Elliot" reminds us, even in the midst of hard times, our spirits can lift us up and our souls can soar.

Billy (Jamie Bell in his feature film debut) is an eleven year old child growing up in a predominately male household in the mining village of Durham in 1984. It is a hard life made harder by the workers' strike which has closed the mine. As Billy's dad and brother deal with the rancor and violence caused by the "scabs" which are crossing the picket lines, Billy shuffles off to boxing class presumably to learn the manly art of fisticuffs. Little did he suspect that his education would soon run in a completely different direction.

Arriving at the gym, he finds it being shared by a dance class run by Mrs. Wilkerson (Julie Walters, "Titanic Town"), a chain-smoking, wisecracking dance instructor who challenges Billy to drop his gloves and put on ballet shoes. Billy's natural exuberance for the dance shines through even his first clumsy attempts to discipline his movements by mirroring the other students.

If Billy's new found love for the expressiveness of dance takes him by surprise, it completely shocks his father and brother. Dancing, especially ballet, is something done by "poofs." "Lads, they reason, "are to do things like boxing, football, and wrestling, not (expletive deleted) ballet!"

Billy is undeterred however and, with Mrs. Wilkerson's encouragement, starts secretly preparing for an audition with the Royal Ballet School in London.

Director Stephen Daldry ("Eight") takes this rather simplistic and oft-used story line ("Brassed Off," "Flashdance") and with some help from his relatively unknown but highly accomplished cast, manages to elicit more than a few smiles from the audience.

Fourteen year old Mr. Bell turns in a natural and engaging performance. His dancing is raw and barely controlled but there is no denying the joy and freedom of emotional release which his character gains through the dance.

Julie Walters, in a pivotal but relatively minor role, shines magnificently. Her character recognizes both the unique passion that Billy has for the ballet as well as the family's natural and unfounded resistance to the stigma attached to it. Hers is a layered performance which is at once interesting and enjoyable.

Gary Lewis ("Gregory's Two Girls), as Billy's dad, is first outraged and indignant at the thought of his son involved in something perceived by him as being as foppish as the ballet. But he also portrays a man who loves his son and family dearly and comes to realize that perhaps Billy's passion for the dance may be what enables him to build a better life for himself.

The film makes it clear that Billy is no "poof" but then immediately clouds the issue by having his best friend (Stuart Wells in his feature film debut) "out" himself once he hears of Billy's interest in the ballet.

The homosexual aspect to the film is a needless element which doesn't much add to the real story about a working class son who desires to break free of the social class conventions which have bound his father and brother. By all accounts, Billy should have followed the steps of most of the men in Durham and become a miner. But he discovered a natural ability which he was able to refine and develop into a real talent because of the passionate desire he had to pursue that goal.

Social stigma notwithstanding, as we practice and develop our God-given abilities, the world will take notice. The Word of God promises:

"A man's gift maketh room for him, and bringeth him before great men." Proverbs 18:16 (KJV)

As believing Christians, we should never shy away from pursuing the passion of our hearts, keeping always the mindset that our accomplishments, performed in alignment with God's Word, will bring glory to God. As we excel in this world because of the discipline, encouragement, and power which the Word of God teaches us, we will be brought before great men and be given the opportunity to share with them the real reason for our successes.
