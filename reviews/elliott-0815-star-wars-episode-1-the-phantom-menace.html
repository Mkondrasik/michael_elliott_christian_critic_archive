"A long time ago in a galaxy far far away..." With those words, accompanied by the familiar strands of John Williams' theme music, another installment of the Star Wars saga finally begins.

The original Star Wars (Episode 4 - A New Hope) was released twenty-two years ago, giving birth to a cultural icon that continues to endure and grow as new generations discover the power of "the force." In The Phantom Menace, fans now have an opportunity to go back to the beginning of the tale to see the origin of their favorite characters.

Young Obi Wan Kenobi (Ewan McGregor) is a mere Jedi apprentice under the tutelage of Jedi master Qui-Gon Jinn (Liam Neeson). They have been sent to the small planet of Naboo to mediate a trade dispute. Upon their arrival, they find that the powerful Trade Federation is preparing their battle droids for a massive invasion. Why? We aren't told... yet.

Using their Jedi training, they rescue the young Queen Amidala (Natalie Portman) and plan to escort her to the legislative capital planet of Coruscan where she will make an appeal for action against the invaders.

Along the way, they meet young Anakin Skywalker (whom we know will grow to become Darth Vader). Qui-Gon Jinn senses that the force is strong with the young boy. Convinced that he is the "chosen one" prophesied to bring balance to the force, Qui-Gon Jinn arranges to have the boy brought to the Jedi council for testing.

It appears to me that writer/director George Lucas is a victim of his own success. The expectations for this film are so high, nothing short of a cinematic masterpiece will satisfy the hordes of fans who are camping out for tickets. If they are waiting for a masterpiece, the wait continues... The Phantom Menace isn't it.

What it is, however, is an entertaining piece of science fiction fluff. Visually, the movie is extremely pleasing. The special effects are every bit as dazzling and amazing as one would expect.

Where the film excels is in the depiction of the various worlds visited by Qui-Gon Jinn and Obi-Wan Kenobi. From the majestic structures of Naboo, to the underwater world of the Gungan, to the chambers of the senate, the settings that Mr. Lucas provides for us are imaginative and visually wondrous.

Would that the same care was taken with the characters. Mr. Neeson and Mr. McGregor act as if they were constantly aware of the weight that they carried as leads of the Star Wars legend. They generate little energy, less life, and absolutely no humor. (Where is Hans Solo when you really need him?)

For humorous relief, Mr. Lucas gives us Jar Jar Binks, a computer generated, clumsy, lizard-like native of Naboo who speaks in a fractured form of English (which at times sounds disconcertingly like Mary Kate and Ashley Olsen early on in their acting career. "How rude" is pronounced "how wude.")

Spiritually, the Star Wars series has long been used as a source for sermons. Good vs. Evil. Reliance upon an invisible force...

In this movie, special attention could be paid to the fact that young Anakin exhibits none of the dark qualities that we know he will one day represent as Darth Vader. Here, in Episode I, he is simply the loving son of a slave mother on the Hutt-ruled planet of Tatooine. Generous to a fault, kind, self-assured without being egotistical... there is even a hint that he was immaculately conceived. It is no wonder Qui-Gon Jinn considers him to be the one who will bring unity and balance.

The Christ parallels are obvious. And yet we know that, for some reason, Anakin will turn evil. What many fail to recognize is that Jesus Christ had free will and could have, at any time, turned to "the dark side of the force" if you please. He never did. He was tempted in all things, but he never sinned. Because of his obedience and steadfastness, we have access to the spiritual life that God has made available.

Like Anakin and Jesus Christ, we too have free will. Freedom to choose godliness. Freedom to reject evil. Freedom to please God. Within that freedom, when we choose correctly, the force (or the power of God) will be with us.
