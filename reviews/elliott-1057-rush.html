Sermon / spiritual topic: Competition; Envy; Excellence; Humility

Author-verified transcription

What kind of person risks life and limb in an attempt to drive around in circles faster than someone else trying to do the same thing? We discover the answer to this question (times two) in Rush, Ron Howard’s film that takes a look at an intense rivalry that existed in the dangerous world of Formula One racing back in the 1970s.

Early in the film we are introduced to James Hunt (Chris Hemsworth), a charismatic English playboy race car driver with a larger than life personality who approaches each track the way he lives life – by pushing himself to the absolute limit. Recognized by most as being in a class above his competition, he is taken aback when a newcomer, the staid, unpopular, and thoroughly analytical Austrian upstart Niki Lauda (Daniel Bruhl), actually gives him a run for his money. He is aghast when he learns that Lauda has landed a spot on the Ferrari Formula One team, leaving him to wallow in the lower classes.

It doesn’t take long for Hunt to find a ride and join Lauda at the top of the racing world, a place we learn that two out of a field of only twenty-four drivers are killed each season. Hunt and Lauda quickly see each other as their strongest competition and are driven to outrace one another on a quest to be crowned world champion. It is a journey that is proven not to be without tragedy (personal as well as professional).

Healthy competition is actually a very good thing as it pushes us to strive to improve ourselves and grow in the development of our natural ability. Hunt and Lauda both became better drivers because they were forcing themselves to be as good as they saw the other to be. God says that we can do the same for our brothers and sisters in Christ.

“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.” Proverbs 27:17 (KJV)

The danger of this, as we can see evolving in the ongoing rivalry between Hunt and Lauda, is when healthy competition gives way to envy or jealousy. It is one thing to be pushed to improve one’s self through the awareness of another’s achievement or success. It is quite another to be obsessed with matching it. We can let ourselves be inspired by another’s success but we should never hold ourselves in condemnation or judgment through the prism of someone else’s life.

“Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit. Rather, in humility value others above yourselves,” Philippians 2:3 (NIV)

This is not to say we are not to strive to be our best. Quite the contrary is true. We just do not need to define ourselves by the outcome of our personal race. What actually defines us is how well we race it.

“Know ye not that they which run in a race run all, but one receiveth the prize? So run, that ye may obtain.” 1 Corinthians 9:24 (KJV)
