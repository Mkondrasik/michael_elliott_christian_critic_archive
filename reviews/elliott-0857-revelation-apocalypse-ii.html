Revelation: Apocalypse II

Author-verified transcription

As The Christian Critic, I have long supported the idea of dramatizing the vivid and exciting records that can be found in the Bible. That idea saw fruition last weekend as Orlando, FL played host to the limited release of Revelation: Apocalypse II from Cloud Ten Productions.

The movie sets itself in the months following the "gathering together" or "rapture" that is described in the book of Thessalonians. Scripture states that when Jesus Christ returns for his saints, the dead in Christ will rise first. Believers who are living at that time will then be changed, receiving new spiritual bodies and joining their Lord in the air. Everyone else remains on the earth, where the antichrist will begin his reign.

Thorold Stone (Jeff Fahey, No Tomorrow) is an enforcement agent for O.N.E. (One Nation Earth), the organization that serves as the front for Franco Macalousso (Nick Mancuso, Under Siege II), a self-proclaimed Messiah and new global leader. Months earlier, Stone's wife and child vanished from the earth along with millions of others.

Much of Stone's work deals with the suppression of a suspected underground terrorist group called "The Haters." While investigating yet another terrorist attack, Stone uncovers evidence that leads him to believe The Haters are being framed by O.N.E.

The proof could very well lay hidden in an encrypted code on a computer disk given to Stone. It supposedly describes the true meaning behind the upcoming "Day of Wonders" prophesied by Macalousso.

Stone begins to seek answers with the help of computer wizard Willie Spino (Tony Nappo, Murder at 1600), and a band of the renegade Haters led by Helen Hannah (Leigh Lewis, Apocalypse: Caught in the Eye of the Storm).

The Haters turn out to be those who have come to accept Jesus Christ as their Lord and are working to bring his message to a world now being deceived by Macaluso.

The filmmakers are to be commended for this attempt to illustrate biblical truths via this entertaining medium. While Revelation does not yet match the quality of the big budget Hollywood films, it is certainly headed in the right direction.

The script starts off a bit "preachy," somewhat like a Sunday morning TV religious dramatization, but it eventually settles into its mystery, using biblical truths as the framework within which the story is told.

There is a great deal of figurative language used in the book of Revelation that is not intended to be taken literally. While some interpretive liberties have been taken by the filmmakers, the overall message communicated by this film is sound.

There's a wide range of talent levels mixed in the cast. Standouts include Nick Mancuso as the manipulative world leader and Marium Carvell (Down in the Delta) as one of the cell leaders for the underground movement.

Tony Nappo is gratingly amusing as the wheelchair bound computer geek. Supermodel Carol Alt (Private Parts) appears as a blind woman who must choose between sight and salvation.

Jeff Fahey is a bit bland as Stone but manages to convey the conflict and self-doubt he holds within his soul. His character is willing to explore every possible explanation (including alien attacks) rather than accept the spiritual answers held forth by The Haters.

His problem is that he wants proof in order to believe. God wants us to believe first, after which He promises we will see. The most salient line in the movie comes in response to Stone's demand for proof of God's existence. Helen calmly replies, "No proof is enough if the heart is not ready."

How many of us would believe if we really saw a miracle. And how many would then accept the first plausible explanation for the miracle so we wouldn't have to believe? God shows Himself daily to those who are willing to see Him. To the skeptic, God will always remain a mystery or a fable.
