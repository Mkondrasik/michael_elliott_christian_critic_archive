Author-verified transcription

Money is a tool that can make many things possible but it is not a panacea to cure all ills. For example, it cannot buy happiness. It is no guarantee for contentment. And, as we are so lyrically reminded, money can't buy us love.

In Friends With Money, we are shown the lives of four sets of individuals - three of whom have reached a certain level of financial security and one who most certainly has not. But all have issues that make their lives less than prosperous.

Olivia (Jennifer Aniston) is the poor one. An ex-teacher who quit because the students she was teaching had more money than she, she is now working as a maid; cleaning up after other people's messes even as she makes a mess of her own life. Her love life is pathetic as she continually chooses the worst kind of guy to date.

Joan Cusack is Fran who, with her husband Matt (Greg Germann), have the most money of them all. She also has a tendency to lapse into the "guilt of the affluent" whenever reminded that the vast majority of the world must make do with far less.

Jane (Frances McDormand) is a clothes designer on the verge of an emotional breakdown. Apt to explode into irrational tirades at the slightest provocation, she hasn't washed her hair since (apparently) the last presidential election. Her husband Aaron (Simon McBurney) is exceedingly patient and tolerant and often mistaken as being gay.

The fourth and final friend is Christine (Catherine Keener) who partners with her husband David (Jason Isaacs) as a screenwriting team. Their professional partnership may be successful but they have long ago forgotten to put any effort into their personal relationship and their marriage is on the rocks.

None of these friends have found full contentment from their affluence (or lack thereof) and perhaps that is the message that writer/director Nicole Holofcener (Lovely and Amazing) is attempting to convey. She does so by using slice of life reality where little is explained or resolved. We're simply to watch these lives unfold and observe them for what they are... works in progress.

The acting, across the board, is well above average... as well it should be with this multiple award winning cast. Because Holofcener doesn't use a strong narrative line the film may not have a mainstream appeal but cineastes may be drawn to it for its honest look at relationships and how money (or the perception of money) may affect them.

Those without money will often live their lives with an "if only" mentality. "If only I win the lottery..." "If only I had a job that paid 6 figures.." They see money as the way out of whatever problems they may have and their way into the satisfaction they are missing. What they don't know is that money, by itself, will never deliver what it is that they seek.

He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver; nor he that loveth abundance with increase: this is also vanity. Ecclesiastes 5:10 (KJV)

When we set our heart's affection on material things - or look upon our financial status as the gauge to our happiness and contentment, we set ourselves up to be continually disappointed. Greed and envy are born from such a mindset and they are destroyers to the soul. It is far better to find contentment in what we have and trust in God to meet our needs and fill our hearts with joy.
