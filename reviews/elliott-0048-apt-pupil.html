Apt Pupil

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 14, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Philippians 4:8 1 Corinthians 15:33 Matthew 12:35

Translation: KJV; Amplified
Sermon / spiritual topic: Thought life; evil influence; guarding the mind; resisting evil

Author-verified transcription

What do we think about when we don’t have to think about anything? The answer to that question gives us an indication of the quality or state of our hearts. Heart, biblically speaking, is located at the center of our mind. It is the core of our being. In Apt Pupil, the main character’s heart becomes tainted with the devilish thoughts and evil images which he allows to remain in his mind.

Based on a Steven King novella, Apt Pupil describes the unusual relationship between a high school student on the verge of becoming the valedictorian of his class and the elderly neighbor who turns out to be a Nazi war criminal.

Having studied the holocaust in school, Todd Bowden becomes obsessively fascinated with the subject. Upon discovering that old Mr. Denker is actually Kurt Dussander, ex-commandant of one of the Nazi death camps, he seizes the opportunity to hear the war stories first hand by making a perverse pact with Dussander. In exchange for telling him about the atrocities committed during the war, Todd won’t reveal Dussander’s true identity to the authorities.

And so, each afternoon after school, Dussander relates gruesome tales of death and brutality. The effect these morbid tales have on Todd gradually become apparent. His grades begin dropping. He becomes quick-tempered and easily distracted. Before long, an unspoken, perhaps unwanted, but nonetheless very real bond has developed between Todd and Dussander. Todd’s heart (the innermost part of the mind) is changing.

Brad Renfro (Telling Lies in America) does an excellent job as the young straight A student who embarks on the dark psychological journey into the depths of evil. It is a road that would have been best left untraveled.

Ian McKellen (Gods and Monsters) plays the ex-Nazi whose past is revisited upon him. Todd is not the only one affected by these suppressed memories brought to the light of day. Dussander had managed to shut the door on his past until Todd came along to reopen it. The scene where Todd has Dussander dress in a Nazi uniform and march in place (“just because I want to see it,” the young man says) is chilling, largely due to the level of intensity which McKellen achieves.

David Schwimmer (TV’s Friends) makes a brief appearance as Todd’s guidance counselor who unknowingly helps this devilish relationship to continue. Elias Koteas (Living Out Loud) is a down-on-his-luck stranger whose “luck” runs out upon meeting Dussander.

Director Bryan Singer (The Usual Suspects) manages to keep the tension high while raising some interesting questions regarding the nature of evil.

Of course, anyone who studies the Bible knows that evil has a spiritual source. That spiritual source has life and because it has life, it has a hunger. That hunger is unrelenting and indiscriminate. Devil spirits will devour any who do not resist.

And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: Luke 22:31 [KJV]

Our spiritual adversary is not impressed with our physical accomplishments or educational merits. It makes no difference if we are straight A students or in remedial classes. He desires entry into our minds.

Do not be so deceived and misled! Evil companionships (communion, associations) corrupt and deprave good manners and morals and character. 1 Corinthians 15:33 [Amplified]

If we allow bad or evil thoughts to linger in our minds, we will incorporate them into our doctrine... our logical thinking patterns. Eventually, they will be made manifest, coming into physical reality as we act upon them. Bad thoughts do affect us in a very real way. The only answer (and protection!) is to resist.

Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. James 4:7b [KJV]

God has given us the wonderful ability to choose what it is we will think about. Todd chose poorly and paid the price for that choice. We need not repeat his mistake. Philippians 4:8 tells us what we should be thinking regularly.
