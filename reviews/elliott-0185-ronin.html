Ronin

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 25, 1998
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Lordship; discipleship; guidance; following Christ; purpose

Author-verified transcription

Ronin is a Japanese term used to describe a type of samurai whose master was killed. Masterless, these highly trained warriors would wander around the countryside, hiring themselves out for various jobs requiring their unique skills.

The end of the Cold War has resulted in a type of modern day ronin. Men and women who, once gainfully employed by their respective governments which trained them to be experts in their unusual fields, are now forced to work freelance, attaching themselves to whoever is willing to meet their price.

A number of these mercenaries have been hired and brought together by a mysterious Irish lass for the purpose of stealing a metal briefcase, the contents of which are unknown to them. What sounds like an easy enough task isn't. Whatever is in that briefcase is valuable enough to die for... which people do with alarming frequency.

John Frankenheimer (The Island of Dr. Moreau) has directed some first rate scenes in this action/mystery. The car chases in particular are outstanding. Ronin is set in Europe, where the streets are much narrower than their American counterparts. This makes the speed and danger of the chases greatly emphasized.

Ronin, like the mercenary force in its story, uses a multinational cast. Robert DeNiro, (Wag The Dog) is Sam, an ex-CIA operative who plays his cards so close to his chest, he has to unbutton his shirt to discard.

Jean Reno (Godzilla) is Vincent, a resourceful Frenchman who owes his life to Sam. Natascha McElhone (The Truman Show) is Dierdre, the beautiful Irish woman who has compiled this team under the orders of Seamus (Jonathan Pryce, Evita), a rogue Irish patriot. Stellan Skarsgard (Good Will Hunting) is Gregor, an electronics wizard. Even Katarina Witt makes a cameo appearance as a Russian Olympic skater named Natacha Kirilova.

As the mercenaries' plan starts to fail, it becomes clear that 1) not everyone is who they appear to be; and 2) they've been used by someone who means to take the briefcases for his or her own self.

Unlike other movies in this genre, Ronin is not a mindless two hours in front of an exploding screen. Screenwriters Richard Weisz (a pseudonym used by David Mamet) and J.D. Zeik, by not telling us all, force us, as an audience, to exercise our logic and reasoning in order to follow their complex story line and keep up with the action.

Granted, there are some risks in taking this approach and the filmmakers are not always successful in their execution, but the action scenes are thrilling and the mystery just titillating enough to hold our interest and have us suspend our disbelief... at least up until the last five minutes or so.

The ronin, because they had no master or lord, also had no guidance or direction. They did what was necessary to survive, using their warrior skills most often for evil or unseemly deeds.

Like the ronin, our lord and master was betrayed and killed. But there, the resemblance ends. God raised our lord from the dead and seated him at His right hand.

Jesus Christ is our lord and master:

Ye call me Master and Lord: and ye say well; for so I am. John 13:13 [KJV]

As the disciples of the living Christ, we do not wander aimlessly. We have a clearly defined path to follow.

For even hereunto were ye called: because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that ye should follow his steps. 1 Peter 2:21 [KJV]

We are to eschew evil and follow that which is good. As we do, God, who is the Lord of our lord, promises to meet our needs and desires of our hearts. Our lives may not contain any high speed chases through the narrow streets of Nice, but living for our Lord is just as exciting and thrilling (and a hundred times more rewarding) than going out freelance.
