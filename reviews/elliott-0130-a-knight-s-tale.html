A Knight's Tale

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 11, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Salvation; equality; bloodline; identity in Christ; sonship

Author-verified transcription

Early on in A Knight’s Tale, writer/director Brian Helgeland (Payback) makes it abundantly clear that this medieval story of knights and nobility is not to be viewed as being historically accurate. How else to explain the 14th century peasant crowd breaking into a raucous rendition of Queen’s 20th century anthem, “We Will Rock You” during a jousting match?

Heath Ledger (The Patriot) stars as William Thatcher, a young commoner who dreams of becoming a knight and competing in the tournament games. Alas, such activity is forbidden to those who are not of noble bloodlines. Thus, young William is relegated to the sidelines along with his fellow squires, Roland (Mark Addy, The Flintstones in Viva Rock Vegas) and Wat (Alan Tudyk, 28 Days) to watch and cheer the armor-suited knights as they do valiant “battle” against one another.

When their master suddenly dies during a tournament which he was winning, William decides to take his master’s place, finish the tournament, and collect the prize money. A taste of victory whets the appetite and William adopts the fictional persona of Sir Ulrich von Lichtenstein. Assisted by his fellow squires, he begins making the rounds of the jousting circuit, racking up tournament wins and quickly becomes a crowd favorite.

Along the way they meet Geoff Chaucer (Paul Bettany, Dead Babies), a struggling writer whose gambling debts have cost him the shirt off his back along with every other stitch of clothing he once owned. In exchange for a pair of pants, Chaucer not only provides William with a set of forged lineage papers, he appoints himself the pretended knight’s herald, introducing him to the tournament crowds with all the blustery bravado of a WWF announcer.

The film, which can be likened to a Rocky tale for the middle ages, is actually quite enjoyable in a popcorn munching kind of way. There is a great deal of humor, the violent action is not graphic, and the love story and sexual references are tastefully handled.

Heath Ledger is fine as the rugged young peasant who pretends to be a knight. His athleticism and leading man looks fit the heroic character well.

Rufus Sewell (Bless the Child) as Count Adhemar serves as the antagonist. As William’s rival, not only for the championship title but also for the heart of the fair Jocelyn (Shannyn Sossamon in her feature film debut), Sewell’s Adhemar is a suitable villain - oily, duplicitous, and eminently hissable.

Mark Addy, Alan Tudyk and Paul Bettany all lend able support in their roles as “Sir Ulrich’s” serfs and squires. The interplay amongst them is quite amusing. Bettany is especially humorous as the highly intelligent and verbose Chaucer.

The inclusion of a modern rock and roll soundtrack is at times a bit disconcerting but also manages to inject a playful, tongue-in-cheek tone to the production. What Helgeland was going after was obviously mere entertainment, and on that score he delivers.

A major issue in the film was that of one’s bloodline. Only those born into nobility were eligible to become knights and compete in the games. William, though he manifested more noble qualities than any other character, was unable to ascend to the exalted status of knighthood because of his parentage.

There once was a time when God’s people also were deemed “able” or “unable” to receive God’s promises based solely upon their bloodline. It is one reason why the Old Testament records has so many genealogies recorded. It was important that people could trace their lineage back to one of the twelve tribes of Israel.

The New Testament indicates that there is now a better way. Today, there is only one “bloodline” that is important. By it all men are able to receive salvation and be welcomed into the family of God:

But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ. Ephesians 2:13 (KJV)

The perfect blood of Christ is the ticket to our “nobility” for by it are we made the sons of God.
