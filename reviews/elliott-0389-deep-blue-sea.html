Deep Blue Sea

Author-verified transcription

Regular readers of Movie Parables know that I rarely review horror films. The reasons are quite simple: (a) I don't like horror films because (b) their primary purpose is to evoke fear from anyone watching them and (c) I don't like fear.

But, with a free evening on my hands and limited choices, I decided to go see Deep Blue Sea on its first day of theatrical release thinking that, like Jaws, it might be more action/thriller oriented. Sitting in the audience waiting for the next victim to become "chummy" with (or should I say for) the shark, I remembered why it had been so long since the last time I subjected myself to this kind of "entertainment."

If cheap frights are what you seek, Deep Blue Sea delivers them, despite some implausible physical feats, lapses in character judgment, and some major leaps of logic.

Dr. Susan McAlester (Saffron Burrows, The Loss of Sexual Innocence) and her team at Research Station Aquatica are on the verge of a major breakthrough which may forever eliminate the effects of Alzheimer's Disease. Noting that sharks never show any deterioration of brain activity, they have been harvesting test sharks, increasing their mental acumen in order to extract enough protein to develop a serum.

This, of course, results in turning nature's oldest and most perfect killing machine into a Brainiac with teeth. Throw in a tropical storm, 3 Mako sharks with revenge on their newly developed minds, and about a dozen people to serve as shark bait, and you've got the general gist of the movie.

Director Renny Harlin (Cliffhanger, Die Hard II) seems to like the big epic-scaled action thrillers and Deep Blue Sea is no exception. With 25 years of technological advancements in terms of animatronics and CGI, Mr. Harlin is able to show more of the sharks and their attacks than was Stephen Spielberg during the filming of Jaws. This means that not only did you know that some of the characters weren't going to survive, you also knew that you'd see them become fish food.

The filmmakers chose to cast relative unknowns in order to increase the suspense of not knowing who would be next to die. The one exception to the unknown cast is Samuel L. Jackson (The Red Violin) as Russell Franklin, the man financially backing the project who decides to visit the underwater lab to either protect his investment or shut the project down.

The rest of the cast includes: Thomas Jane (The Thin Red Line) as Carter Blake, a shark wrangler with a past; LL Cool J (Halloween H20) as "Preacher," Aquatica's cook and spiritual force; Michael Rapaport (Palmetto) as facility engineer Todd Scoggins, Jacqueline McKenzie (Angel Baby) as marine biologist Janice Higgins, and Stellan Skarsgard (Ronin) as research physician Jim Whitlock.

I'd advise you not to get too attached to any one character as the odds are against him or her making it to the end of the film.

The attacks, when they come, come suddenly and without warning. The sharks show no mercy and are relentless in their methodical pursuit of their human captors. At one point, Preacher tells Dr. McAlester that the mentally enhanced shark is "the devil." It is understandable how that comparison was made.

Like the sharks of the film, our spiritual adversary is relentless, single-minded (he "cometh not but for to steal, kill, and destroy"), and offers no compassion or mercy. He will attack suddenly, without provocation or warning.

The keys to successfully countering his attacks include (a) not succumbing to fear, which is his device to keep you from taking action; (b) not losing hope but continue believing that God will show you a way to escape; and (c) working together with like-minded individuals in order to defeat him.

All of the above keys are exhibited in the movie... It's just that you have to sit through a lot of bloody screaming to see them.
