Yours Mine and Ours

Michael Elliott

November 23, 2005

2 stars

Author-verified transcription

At this rate, any film ever made will eventually be remade - if it had even a modicum of success. Yours, Mine, and Ours, a slight and frivolous 1968 family comedy starring Henry Fonda and Lucille Ball has been given a 21st century facelift to become a slight and frivolous 2005 family comedy starring Dennis Quaid and Rene Russo.

To be honest, I remember nothing about the original film. Give me 48 hours and no doubt I'll be able to say the same thing about the remake.

While memory serves, here's the plot: Quaid plays Frank Beardsley, a widowed Coast Guard admiral raising a family of eight. Russo plays Helen North, a widowed eccentric raising a family of ten. When they meet; as it turns out not for the first time - they were once high school sweethearts; sparks fly. It's when they decide to get married that sparks aren't the only thing in the air. Something else has hit the fan.

With 18 kids between them, happily ever after just doesn't "happen." It takes a wee bit of planning. And even then, the best laid plans can go astray... especially when the youngsters decide that they liked things better before their respective parents got hitched.

Chaos rules the roost as the children try various ways to break up the newlyweds. They have so much fun doing it that, when they actually succeed, they have to figure out a way to undo the damage so they can stay together as a family.

There isn't a lot of depth to the story or to the characters and everyone involved knows it. Quaid and Russo, who have both been in films showcasing their talents, recognize that an easy going charm is all that is required of them here. They're relaxed, affable and pretty much coasting through this old-fashioned piece of movie fluff.

Director Raja Gosnell (Scooby Doo) spends a lot of time focusing on the kids and their antics but didn't figure out exactly how to achieve the impossible task of communicating separate and distinct personalities for the underage mob that comprise the "Family Beardsley/North." With the exception of the oldest ages (Sean Faris, Danielle Panabaker, Katija Pevic, Drake Bell), the rest of the children kind of just lump together to form a single screaming, out-of-control mass of young humanity.

Some attention is given to the different approaches to parenting that Frank and Helen have developed. Frank is a firm believer in discipline and order. Helen welcomes free expression and group hugs as a constant reminder of the love that lives within their house. Neither approach is completely wrong but neither is completely effective. Discipline must be balanced with love so that obedience is offered freely - out of love and respect instead of fear of reprisals. Free expression can be rewarding but it must not come at the expense of responsibility or the teaching that actions bring consequences.

Raising a large family, even one with 18 children, is not an impossible task. It can, of course, be extremely challenging. It would be important to raise those children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord. The Bible has many passages that are addressed specifically to children. Most Christian parents teach the following verse to their offspring as soon as they are old enough to understand:

Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. Honour thy father and mother; (which is the first commandment with promise;) That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth. Ephesians 6:1-3 (KJV)
