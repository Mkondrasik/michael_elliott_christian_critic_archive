Mission: Impossible 2

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 24, 2000
Rating
3½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 45:7Deuteronomy 30:15Hebrews 5:14

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Good and evil; free will; choices; knowledge of God; discernment

Author-verified transcription

No need to check the Farmers' Almanac... Summer has definitely arrived. The telltale sign is the highly anticipated summer blockbuster MISSION: IMPOSSIBLE 2 which is exploding on a movie screen near you.

Directed by John Woo (Face Off) in his inimitable style and starring Tom Cruise (Eyes Wide Shut) as the IMF specialist Ethan Hunt, MISSION: IMPOSSIBLE 2 is given the high-gloss Hollywood treatment, every dollar of which is seen on the screen. Instead of being simply derivative, M:I2 out-Bonds 007's now tired series at every turn.

The plot is simple. A killer virus has been stolen and is being sold on the international market for millions. The impossible mission, should they choose to accept it, is to steal the virus back along with the antiserum.

Joining Ethan's team is computer expert Luther Stickell (Ving Rhames, Mission: Impossible), and helicopter pilot/comic relief Billy Baird (John Polson, The Sum of Us). An integral part of the plan requires the recruitment of a civilian, the beautiful master thief Nyah Hall (Thandie Newton, Beloved) to whose undeniable charms Ethan immediately succumbs.

She once had a romantic relationship with the maniacal Sean Ambrose (Dougray Scott, Ever After: A Cinderella Story) who is behind the virus disappearance. Her job is to resume that relationship to discover whatever information he will reveal. It is a job which causes the eavesdropping Ethan more than a little consternation.

While the plot is indeed simple... the execution of the plot is anything but. The stunts and special effects are mind-boggling, the high-tech gadgetry is fanciful, and Mr. Woo has even tossed in some Mission: Impossible trademark devices like the latex masks which characters don to disguise themselves. A voice altering computer strip taped across the throat allows characters to pass themselves off as any other character, a plot device which is used throughout the film.

After the first Mission: Impossible, the general complaint was that the story line was too confusing or difficult to follow. The filmmakers have corrected that problem for the sequel, perhaps going a bit too far in the other direction. There are very few surprises in the plot itself, not that it matters. The jaw-dropping action sequences are the primary reason to see the movie. The acting is more than adequate but still secondary to the visuals. Whether it is seeing Tom Cruise scale a huge rock formation in Moab, Utah or free fall down a Sydney skyscraper's atrium, the images presented to us are amazing and enthralling.

Be advised that MISSION: IMPOSSIBLE 2 is an intensely violent film. Mr. Woo uses slow motion photography and haunting musical strains during scenes of heavy gunplay in ways to try and make the violence appear more palatable or acceptable to our senses.

A line repeated at least three times, maybe more, by the developer of the virus is paraphrased as such: "In our search for a hero, we had to create that which is vital for his existence... A villain." In the context of the movie, the character was referring to the virus and its antidote.

The phrase reminded me of:

I form the light, and create darkness: I make peace, and create evil: I the LORD do all these things. Isaiah 45:7 (KJV)

The scripture of course refers to God's willingness to let us, His creation, choose between good and evil of our own free will. In order to give us the freedom to choose, he had to make the choice itself available. The reason the world becomes so ugly and dark is due to men and women making the wrong choices in life. Oftentimes it is done out of ignorance... They just don't know that there is a better way. Obtaining a working knowledge of the goodness of God is not "impossible." Our mission, should we choose to accept it, is to seek after that knowledge.
