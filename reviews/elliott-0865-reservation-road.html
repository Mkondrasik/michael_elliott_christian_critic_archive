Reservation Road

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 19, 2007
Rating
2½ stars

1 hr: 42 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Forgiveness; Grief; Healing

Author-verified transcription

One of the worst things imaginable occurs to two men, changing their lives irrevocably in the talent-heavy drama, Reservation Road.

Ethan Learner (Joaquin Phoenix) is enjoying a family night out with his wife Grace (Jennifer Connolly), daughter Emma (Elle Fanning) as they watch his sweet-natured son Josh (Sean Curley) perform in a school concert.

Meanwhile, divorced dad Dwight Arno (Mark Ruffalo) is watching his beloved Red Sox and bonding with his son Sam (Eddie Alderson). When the game runs long, Dwight is rushing home to return his son to his ex-wife Ruth (Mira Sorvino) so as not to violate his visitation rights. In an instant, that will become the least of his troubles.

Rounding a curve, Dwight has a moment of inattention and in that moment, Ethan loses his son. Dwight, being scared, shaken, and concerned about the prospect of losing his own son, drives on, leaving the tragedy behind him on the road but carrying it with him in his mind’s conscience.

Director and co-writer Terry George dispatches this section of the film very quickly because the story is not really about the tragedy but rather what happens to both these men as a result of it.

Ruffalo plays Dwight as a good if weak man torn by his desire to do the right thing weighed against the knowledge that if he does, everything he’s worked to rebuild in his life may be destroyed. It is a compelling if somewhat internalized performance.

Phoenix, on the other hand, turns Ethan into a driven vigilante, frustrated by the perceived lack of effort being made to find the animal that killed his son. As he becomes more and more single-minded towards becoming a one man detective, judge, jury and executioner, he doesn’t realize that he’s neglecting the rest of his family who are also hurting but who are trying to get on with their lives.

Jennifer Connolly, always a presence in any film she’s in, isn’t given much screen time here but she makes the most of what little she has. Though devastated by her loss, she recognizes what Ethan fails to see: In order to heal, one has to let go of the anger and bitterness.

Negative emotions are never profitable in the long term. Although we all experience them, it is the indulgent harboring of them that can poison a soul. Grief is never pleasant and tragic events are always difficult to live through. But the key may be to live through them – not to turn them into the center of one’s life around which everything else must revolve.

God has many words of comfort that He provides for us to use in moments when we may be experiencing emotional pain or loss. Common to all is not to let the pain control our minds, our actions, and our thoughts.

Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you. Ephesians 4:31-32 (KJV)

I know... easier said than done. Perhaps that is why God reminded us that He forgave us for our sins even though they required the death of His son. As hard as it may be to accept while we are in the middle of our grief, the truth is the truth. The sooner we forgive people for their weaknesses - even if those weaknesses caused us to personally suffer - the sooner our healing may begin.
