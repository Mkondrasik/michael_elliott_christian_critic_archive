Nutty Professor II: The Klumps

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 28, 2000
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Colossians 3:9-10Ephesians 2:3-6Ephesians 4:22-24

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Putting on the new man; self-control; renewing the mind; overcoming the flesh; Christian conduct

Author-verified transcription

After watching the sequel to Eddie Murphy's remake of Jerry Lewis' The Nutty Professor, it became clear to me that the filmmakers misnamed their work. This new material is so crude it should have been called "The Smutty Professor."

NUTTY PROFESSOR II: THE KLUMPS revisits the familiar territory explored in the first remake. Eddie Murphy (Bowfinger) returns, playing no less than 6 roles with the help of special make up artist Rick Baker. As the obese and brilliant genetic scientist Sherman Klump, Mr. Murphy shows a sweet and gentle nature hidden behind multiple folds of flesh. But the movie neither shares nor appreciates Sherman's wholesome qualities, as its comedic focus rests squarely upon his dysfunctional, weight-challenged, and sexually-minded family who are also played by Mr. Murphy.

In the first film, Sherman's svelte but testosterone-imbalanced alter-ego, Buddy Love, was manifested after Sherman drank his own genetic transformation potion. Now, vestiges of "Buddy" keep rising to the surface of Sherman's consciousness despite Sherman's attempts to suppress him.

When Buddy's "presence" threatens to ruin the budding romance between Sherman and his beautiful colleague, Denise Gains (Janet Jackson, Poetic Justice), Sherman decides to remove "Buddy" from his DNA forever. This decision proves to be ill-advised... Not unlike the one which caused this sequel to come into being.

Sherman soon has to protect his new discovery, a youth serum, against a free and rampaging Buddy as well as against his own diminishing faculties.

Mr. Murphy once again proves his versatility as an actor by not only developing six different characters, but by developing them with such depth and distinction. He truly does a yeoman's job and deserves the accolades he should receive for his performances. It is therefore six times as sad that the script which provides the framework in which these performances are encased was written to be so offensive that the excellent work of the film's lead actor is overshadowed by the crudeness of the material.

Whether it is the constant and overt sexual references made by Grandma Klump, the flatulence that once again is used as a source of comedy, the fat jokes which run a bit thin in this film, the fecal-projectile giant hamster which gives a new meaning to the phrase "pellet gun," or any of a number of coarse and inappropriate moments, NUTTY PROFESSOR II: THE KLUMPS fails to touch the heart because it spends so much time aiming for the lower regions.

The summer of 2000 may be remembered for the success of gross-out comedies. Me Myself and Irene, Scary Movie, and now this one all share a certain crude humor which have undeniably found an audience. This fact, of course, means that studios will soon be making more of the same.

But what is the appeal? Why reach into the comedy gutter to find humor? Why not take a higher road? Sherman battles within himself to "take charge" over the baser part of himself (Buddy). As Christians, it is a battle we wage on a daily basis. Scripture calls it putting on the new man.

Lie not one to another, seeing that ye have put off the old man with his deeds; And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him: Colossians 3:9-10 (KJV)

Putting off the old and putting on the new is something we can do daily.. hourly... even minute by minute. We do not have to lower ourselves to respond to the baser impulses of the flesh. We can aspire to a higher plane of consciousness; a place where humor is born of wit and intelligence instead of shock and cruel jokes at the expense of others.

Call me nutty, but laughter should be genuine and heartfelt followed by a lingering sense of joy instead of the dirty shame we feel when responding to the prurient humor of films such as these.
