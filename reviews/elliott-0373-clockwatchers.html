Clockwatchers

Author-verified transcription

There are those among us who merely "tiptoe through life." Clockwatchers' main character is one of them. Iris Chapman, played by Toni Collette (Muriel's Wedding), actually does less than tiptoe... she will literally freeze in place and wait statue-like instead of taking any step lest she, to her great horror, should bring attention to herself.

Iris belongs to that unique breed of employees known as the "temps". Office work, especially of the entry-level kind which the temps most often perform, can be mind-numbing. Then again, so can a movie that attempts to dramatize it.

Somehow, Clockwatchers manages to be oddly compelling. Yes, it is slow moving. Yes, there are some awkward moments. But, like a train wreck, you just can't help but watch this story of four female "losers" stuck on the lower rungs of the corporate ladder.

Ms. Collette is painfully shy as Iris. Slowly, she allows her character to emerge as she forms a friendship with the other temps in the office: Parker Posey (The House of Yes) as the outrageous Margaret who is waiting for the recommendation that will launch her into the "real world" of a permanent position; Lisa Kudrow (The Opposite of Sex) as Laura who is "temping" until she lands an acting job that is always just around the next corner; and Alanna Ubach (Virtuosity) as the prissy and perpetually engaged Jane.

These four outcasts form a bond that allows them to endure the monotony and excruciating boredom of their office jobs. It's a boredom that we get to experience with them in a remarkably brave but ill-advised decision on the part of novice director Jill Sprecher, who also shares writing credits with her sister Karen. We know these office jobs are dull and tedious... we really didn't have to hear the Musak droning in the background of every office scene to establish that point.

It was difficult to gain an empathy with any of these characters. As Christians, we are called to be bold and brilliant ambassadors, shining as lights in a dark and perverse world. The dim bulbs in this film barely give off a flicker.

The title itself comes from the practice of "temps" to sit idly at their desks, watching the clock for the five o'clock quitting hour to strike, at which time they are free to bolt from the office, escaping the prison in which they freely encage themselves.

We, as Christians, look at work differently. We've been taught by God that all work has value and anything of value requires work. Scriptures like Colossians 3:23 [KJV] come to mind:

"And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men."

Also Ephesians 5:16 [KJV]:

"Redeeming the time, because the days are evil."

As we apply ourselves diligently, making every moment count for the glory of God, whether at work, at home, or somewhere in between, we can shine as the lights God wants us to be. As we do, people will take note and be drawn to that light.

Outside of the light is darkness. Within that darkness, doubt hides. If doubt is allowed to linger, worry develops. If worry is allowed to incubate, it culminates in fear. Fear destroys lives. Especially such mundane lives as these Clockwatchers have.
