Deja Vu

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 22, 2006
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Past

Author-verified transcription

Déjà vu is that strange feeling that you’re doing something again for the first time. Tony Scott has made a film called Déjà Vu that replicates that feeling. We may not have seen it before but it sure feels like we have.

When a ferry blows up in New Orleans killing hundreds of servicemen and their families, ATF agent Doug Carlin (Denzel Washington) arrives on the scene and single handedly uncovers key evidence and the first clues to cracking the case. So impressed is the FBI that they offer him a spot on a secret task force with special access to what they call advanced satellite imagery.

It doesn’t take long for Carlin to uncover the truth. The images he’s watching are not from a satellite playback. Scientists have discovered how to fold time back on itself. This task force has the ability to watch events from any angle and at any location within their range that transpired exactly 4 ½ days ago. The catch: they only have one opportunity. There’s no rewind… no third chance… and they need Carlin’s intuitive ability to tell them where to look to catch the terrorist in the act.

Naturally, Carlin isn’t content to simply watch the tragedy of the past reoccur. He fights to find a way to change the past and save the ferry.

Director Tony Scott brings a technophile’s passion to his direction and the film is filled with gadgets, lights and machinery all humming away in frenetic action mode. He allows the film to lag a bit in the middle but recharges his batteries and the film’s third act is best.

Once again, Denzel Washington provides a smart, thoughtful hero upon which to hang the story and he carries it with his usual skill. Paula Patton makes for an attractive leading lady and Jim Caviezel is both creepy and effective as the domestic terrorist with an agenda.

Val Kilmer is surprisingly cast in a role that is neither challenging nor all that exciting. As head of the task force, he recruits Carlin and then sits by as Carlin and the humorously sarcastic Denny (Adam Goldberg) figure out how to catch the bad guy.

The screenplay by Bill Marsilii manages to sidestep most of the pitfalls that are inherent in a “time-bending” sci-fi thriller although a healthy suspense of disbelief will benefit an audience member to no uncertain degree.

The reality of this world is that there is no changing the past. There is no machine nor is there a theory that can be developed into a machine that will allow for the changing of events that have already occurred. Therefore we should not live with regrets of what cannot be changed. Learning from the past while living in the present is the only way to develop a future worth having.

Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind (past), and reaching forth (present) unto those things which are before (future), I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus. Philippians 3:13-14 (KJV)
