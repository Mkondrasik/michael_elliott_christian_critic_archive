Author-verified transcription

I suppose I need to issue a few disclaimers for this review of Wallace & Grommit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit. After all, it is a family film (one that is being roundly acclaimed by over 95% of all reviewers - judging by the current RottenTomatoes.com analysis) and, as a G-rated piece of entertainment, it will certainly appeal to the majority of my readers.

So why have I only given it 2 stars?

Disclaimer 1: I certainly recognize the laborious effort behind the film. Stop action animation is painstakingly hard and the quality of the end result is amazing. Five years of working one frame at a time is, to me, a great example of discipline and vision.

Disclaimer 2: I appreciate the lack of offensive content. There are few elements contained in the movie which would draw the ire of even the staunchest conservatives.

Disclaimer 3: I saw the film at a 9pm showing after working a physically exhausting day. Added to that, knowing the critical adoration that has followed this film, my expectations were set astronomically high. My physical state and heightened anticipation may have clouded my perceptions.

Bottom line: I wasn’t that enthralled. It was cute and sometimes funny but it failed to captivate or even hold my interest more than a few minutes at a time.

The film is the work of Aardman Studios whose animated shorts of a cheese-loving inventor and his silent but ever communicative dog has wowed audiences since 1989. The Curse of the Were-Rabbit marks the first full length feature film for this Academy Award winning duo of Wallace and Grommit.

Wallace, whose love of technology and Rube Goldberg-like inventions, has led him to a niche in the pest control industry where he helps his English clientele with their vermin (ie. rabbit) problems. Being a humane person, he captures the critters and keeps them at his home where he occasionally dabbles in some extra-curricular experiments. His latest one results in one humungous bunny that terrorizes the community on the eve of their giant vegetable festival.

OK, so there’s not much point to describe the plot. The animation is high-tech crude. Claymation, even at its highest level, is an art form with many limitations. Those limitations unfortunately become even more noticeable over an 85 minute running time. Even with talent like Helena Bonham Carter as the dotty Lady Tottingham and Ralph Fiennes as a proper though maniacal game hunter providing the vocal talent, for me the film just never got its legs - furry or otherwise.

But I am in the minority on this one so who knows - your experience might be quite different.

We do observe that the “beast” which terrorizes comes from an internal source. It is something familiar to us as each of us deals with a Dr. Jekyl/Mr. Hyde dilemma on a daily basis.

Lie not one to another, seeing that ye have put off the old man with his deeds; And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him: Colossians 3:9-10 (KJV)

Unlike the were-rabbit which only comes out at the full moon, our “old man nature” is capable of resurfacing at any time. Thankfully we have been given the freedom and ability to cast it off and, in its place, adopt our “new man nature” which is patterned after Christ Jesus.
