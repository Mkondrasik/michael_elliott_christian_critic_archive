Author-verified transcription

Django Unchained takes an over-simplified look at the issue of race as it existed a couple of years prior to the outbreak of the U.S. Civil War. Yes, there were slaves… and slave owners. But there were also black men who had been granted their freedom… and white men who resented it. It was an odd time to be alive. It was certainly a dangerous time to be black… or a white man who didn’t believe in slavery.

Django (Jamie Foxx) was a slave purchased by Dr. King Schultz (Christolph Waltz), a German dentist turned bounty hunter, in order to track down a trio of brothers who worked the plantation circuit. The deal they agreed upon was once Dr. King gets his men, Django gets his freedom… and the ability to try to free his wife who is enslaved on the plantation owned by Calvin Candie (Leonardo DiCaprio). Seeing as the three brothers are dispatched post haste, Django tastes his freedom early in the film. And Dr. King, feeling somehow obligated to this newly freed slave, decides to join him in his quest.

Waltz, who was “discovered” by American audience in Tarantino’s Inglourious Basterds, actually plays the most sympathetic character in the film. His work is impeccable, bringing humor into scenes of tension and threatening danger. He is no saint but manages to show, at times, saintly qualities. Not so with the other characters.

This being a Tarantino film, there is violence a plenty, though exaggerated and stylized There is also a great deal of language used that to this day could still be considered racially offensive.

I had difficulty embracing this film and I believe the cause was due to the perceived lack of a moral compass within the characters. Waltz, who was by far the most sympathetic figure in the movie, still had no hesitation in having a man shot to death in front of his young son. Django, who had the noblest cause of all, still had no trouble condemning a fellow slave to death in order to maintain his “cover” as he tried to gain access to his wife. Candie, who was arguably the film’s most charismatic character, also demonstrated hateful and despicable qualities.

The potential for good and evil exists in all men, even Christians. But to tell a story where the line between good and evil is so blurred that they become indistinguishable is leading an audience down a slippery slope. God would have us more clearly discern between the two.

“But solid food is for full-grown men, for those whose senses and mental faculties are trained by practice to discriminate and distinguish between what is morally good and noble and what is evil and contrary either to divine or human law.” Hebrews 5:14 (Amplified)
