A Bug’s Life

Who knew that bugs could be box office gold? For the second time this year, an animated feature film about ants has hit the theaters. In the first film, Dreamworks’ Antz, which featured the voice of Woody Allen as “Z,” a neurotic ant was called on to save the colony from an inner conspiracy which threatened to destroy it.

In the new film, A Bug’s Life by Walt Disney Pictures and Pixar Animation Studios, Dave Foley (TV’s News Radio) gives voice to Flik, an inventive ant whose inventions somehow seem to misfire. Here, the danger to the colony comes from an outside force... A band of evil grasshoppers who insist that the ants collect their food for them.

“Ants collect the food. Grasshoppers eat the food,” snarls Hopper, leader of the terrorizing band, as he explains the bugs’ facts of life.

After destroying this season’s “offering,” Flik must come up with a plan to save the colony from the terrible grasshopper reprisals. He sets off in search of warrior bugs – mercenaries who can stand up to the mighty foes. What he finds (though he doesn’t realize it at first) is an inept group of circus performers (flea circus, of course).

Together this unlikely alliance of insects must find a way to save the Flik’s colony... without getting squished in the process.

As with Antz, the filmmakers have attracted some recognizable names who lend their voices to the project. Kevin Spacey (The Negotiator) is deliciously evil as the villain, Hopper. Julia Louis-Dreyfus (TV’s Seinfeld) is the queen-in-training, Princess Atta. David Hyde Pierce (TV’s Frasier) is Slim, the walking stick who laments his role as a flea circus clown. Denis Leary is very amusing as Francis, the lady bug who ain’t no lady.

A Bug’s Life combines the best of style and substance which is not surprising considering that this is the same team that gave us Toy Story a few years back. As with that film, A Bug’s Life presents a story that is both engrossing and entertaining via a medium that is technologically advanced and amazingly effective.

Highlights include a rainstorm, shown from the ants’ perspective (the raindrops fall like mortar shells), and a run in with the enemy of all bugs... a bird. And don’t leave until after the credits as you’ll miss the animated “bloopers” that roll at the end of the film. They are almost worth the price of admission.

The filmmakers were very clever in introducing an assortment of bug characters through the plot device of the flea circus performers. In addition to PT Flea himself (John Ratzenberger, TV’s Cheers), we meet Gypsy, a moth (Madeline Kahn, Blazing Saddles); Heimlich, an overweight caterpillar (Joe Ranft, Toy Story); Francis the male lady-bug; Manny, a praying mantis magician (Jonathan Harris, TV’s Lost in Space); Dim, a rhino beetle (Brad Garrett, Suicide Kings); and Rosie, a black widow spider (Bonnie Hunt, Jerry Maguire).

Together, with Flik as their fearful leader, they rise to save the day and end the terrorizing of the colony. The story and humor is on a level that children of all ages will enjoy. (Note: the demise of Hopper, while not shown in graphic detail, may conjure images disturbing to the very young. I know I shuddered involuntarily.)

There are lessons that can be had in the watching of A Bug’s Life. After a while, Flik began to believe the constant negative reinforcement the colony threw at him and his ideas. If not for words of encouragement from Princess Atta’s kid sister ant, Dot, Flik would have left the colony for good.

We should always be aware of the impact our words have.

Do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building others up according to their needs, that it may benefit those who listen. Ephesians 4:29 [NIV]

We have the ability to help each other. We can build each other up. We can strengthen each other by choosing our words carefully to achieve a desired positive effect. It may be a lot harder to build up than it is to tear down... but the rewards are so much sweeter.
