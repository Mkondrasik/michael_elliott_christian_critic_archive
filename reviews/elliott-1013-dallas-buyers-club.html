Author-verified transcription

Based upon a true event, Dallas Buyers Club tells the story of how one man, diagnosed with HIV and unhappy with the limited medical options available to him, decides to take his treatment into his own hands. His journey not only extended his own life, it changed the lives of those around him and presented hope to those who may have believed hope was not within reach.

In the 80s, Ron Woodroof (Matthew McConnaughey) was a self-styled cowboy electrician whose skirt chasing, alcohol guzzling, and drug taking lifestyle had finally caught up to him. After an accident sends him to the emergency room, blood tests reveal the devastating truth. He has contracted the HIV virus – which much to his dismay he discovers is not just a gay disease. Doctors give him 30 days tops… enough time to “put his affairs in order.”

Ron instead spends that time learning all he can about his illness, refusing to accept the death sentence handed down by medical professionals. He begins to search for alternative treatments, finally tracking down an unlicensed doctor who prescribes a combination of protein supplements and vitamins – a pharmaceutical cocktail that, while not illegal, is certainly not approved by the American Food and Drug Administration.

When Ron responds to the treatment, he sees an opportunity to build a business. But to make that business work, he has to reach out to those in the most need – the homosexual community. Not surprisingly for the bigoted redneck that Ron is presented to be, he initially and unapologetically rejects anything that might cause him to be associated with homosexuals. His attitudes slowly change through an unlikely alliance with Rayon (brilliantly portrayed by Jared Leto) a cross-dressing transgender whose sassy style breaks through Ron’s prejudices. His growth, which is the central focus of the film, eventually enables him to see the person who exists behind the behavior and thus he is able to discover the common thread which binds us all together.

McConnaughey and Leto both give mesmerizing performances. They, each in their own right, play flawed and troubled personalities whose personal choices have resulted in tragic consequences. But even in their flaws, a sense of nobility (or perhaps better said humanity) manages to break through the layers of learned posturing they have adopted during their lifetimes. In the end, facades mean nothing. Truth is the only reality that matters.

The film does address a truth that often gets overlooked in our politically correct world. Actions do have consequences. At risk behaviors (intravenous drug use and unprotected sex - whether homosexual or heterosexual) increases one’s odds of contracting this insidious disease. Case in point: Even after Ron learned that he contracted the HIV virus, his continued abuse of his body weakened his immune system to the point that he developed a full-blown case of AIDS… a situation that might have been avoided had he made different choices.

While we should continue to exhibit and display compassion and empathy to people who are in need or are suffering through an illness; at the same time, we shouldn’t turn a blind eye to the fact that the choices they are making may be part of the problem they are trying to escape.

God gives us guidelines through His Word… a path through the darkness that, if we choose to follow, helps to keep us protected from many of the harmful consequences that can befall man.

“The integrity of the upright shall guide them, but the willful contrariness and crookedness of the treacherous shall destroy them.” Proverbs 11:3 (Amplified)
