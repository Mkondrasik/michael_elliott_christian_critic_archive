Author-verified transcription

As Sir Walter Scott once observed, "Oh what a tangled web we weave when first we practice to deceive." Who better to teach us a lesson in the dangers of weaving tales of deceit, than a lying tailor?

Set in late 1999, Author John le Carré's The Tailor of Panama is a strange mixture of comedy, intrigue, and political satire.

Pierce Brosnan (The World Is Not Enough) of James Bond fame spends some time on the opposite side of the spy spectrum as disgraced MI6 operative Andy Osnard. After his blatant womanizing blew his cover during his last assignment, Osnard is banished to Panama ("a Casablanca without heroes") and told to keep an eye on the handling of the canal which was just recently came under Panamanian control.

Harry Pendel (Geoffrey Rush, Quills) is a popular but debt-ridden English tailor whom Osnard uses to get information relating to the political climate of Panama and most importantly, how the Panamanians intend to operate this important waterway which was given to them by the Americans. Of course, Harry knows nothing but as an accomplished storyteller, is able to make up plausible tales and thus become Osnard's paid informant. One outlandish lie leads to another until finally, Panama finds itself in the center of a perceived international crisis.

Pierce Brosnan appears to be having a bit of fun exploring the darker side of the spy business. Manipulative, selfish, and cold-hearted, Osnard is as dashing as Bond but his motives have little to do with Queen and country.

It is Geoffrey Rush, however, who turns in the performance to watch. An ex-con, Harry came to Panama to reinvent himself as a proper English tailor catering to the upper class society. Everything about Harry is a lie except for the fact that he loves his wife (Jamie Lee Curtis, True Lies) and family. He lies to them because he feels the truth makes him unworthy of their love. Ironic that his penchant for lying may cost him that which he holds most dear.

Brendan Gleeson (The General) and Leonor Varela (The Man in the Iron Mask) are a pair of Harry's friends, patriots who once resisted Noreiga's tyranny and are now living quiet, damaged lives which will be turned upside down by Harry's tall tales.

Harold Pinter (Mansfield Park) makes a rare acting appearance as Harry's deceased mentor, Uncle Benny, who humorously appears to Harry (in his mind's eye) from time to time offering advice and counsel.

John Boorman has not crafted a typical spy thriller. There is very little action/adventure, special effects or pyrotechnics inserted into the plot. What it does have is an intelligent, sophisticated approach to storytelling where the audience is never quite sure until the end just how much the characters know about what is going on. Fans of James Bond films may be disappointed but those familiar with le Carré's novels should be elated.

There is a high degree of profanity and sex-related scenes including nudity so be advised that the R rating given to the film is appropriate.

"The man who tells the truth has to be found out eventually." This ironic line from the film indicates why lies tend to propagate themselves. The characters believe that the only way to hide a lie is through more lies. The truth is that lying will always lead to discovery... if not in this world, then certainly in the next.

A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall not escape. Proverbs 19:5 (KJV)

Life is far easier to manage when one is truthful. By always telling the truth, one only needs remember the truth. Once even one lie is told, we must then always remember what it was we told to whom. The easiest way to avoid getting caught in a lie is by not telling them.
