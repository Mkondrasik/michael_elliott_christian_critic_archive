When Kermit sang "It's not easy being green," he didn't realize just how easy he had it. In Muppets From Space, color is the last thing on Gonzo's mind... He still hasn't figured out what species he is.

Suffering from a recurring nightmare where he sees himself barred from entering the ark by Noah himself (F. Murray Abraham, Amadeus), Gonzo is tired of being the only "whatever" on earth. The answer finally presents itself via his breakfast cereal which begins to spell out clues to his origin, Gonzo is... An alien!

It isn't long before Gonzo and his roommate, Rizzo the rat, are detained by a secret government organization headed up by K. Edgar Singer (Jeffrey Tambor, The Larry Sanders Show) who intend to get to the bottom of the alien invasion. It's up to Kermit and the gang: Fozzie Bear, Miss Piggy, Animal, Pepe the Prawn and the whole Muppet family to rescue Gonzo and Rizzo from the evil clutches of this government agency.

The film takes the opportunity to make irreverent references to many well known films. Among them: Men in Black, Close Encounters of the Third Kind, Shawshank Redemption, Field of Dreams, Independence Day, and Star Trek. These references are only slightly amusing to the parents in the audience and most likely will remain unrecognized by their children who are, after all, the target audience.

More successful are the celebrity cameos that are sprinkled throughout the film: David Arquette (Never Been Kissed) as a mad scientist who uses the lab rats (Rizzo among them) for his cruel experiments; Andie MacDowell (Michael) as a TV reporter whose job is jeopardized by Miss Piggy's inside track on this alien story; Ray Liotta (The Rat Pack) as the gate guard at the government installation whom Miss Piggy tries to seduce; Kathy Griffin (TV's Suddenly Susan) as another guard who fixes her amorous attentions upon Animal; and Hollywood Hulk Hogan as... well, as himself.

While Muppets From Space does provide an afternoon's pastime for parents frantically seeking G-rated entertainment for their kiddies, it is a pale imitation of the quality once seen from the late Jim Henson's felt-covered “family.” Much of the wit and cleverness, once a trademark, now feels old and tired. I could not help but notice that a few of the jokes have even started to lean towards inappropriateness.

“I've always had alien tendencies. It feels natural to me,” Gonzo explains, thus coming out of the “alien closet.” When the government agent puts on a rubber glove to examine Gonzo, Rizzo pipes up, “Better check the final destination of that finger.” Even Miss Piggy resorts to kicking a man between his legs during one of her karate demonstrations.

I hope that this detour into borderline PG humor is merely a temporary lapse and not an indication of things to come from this studio.

Gonzo's desire to find out what he is and where he came from is certainly understandable. The loneliness that stems from his feeling of isolation is something that each of us has experienced at one time or another. But in searching for his past, Gonzo nearly forgets his present.

Every member in the body of Christ is uniquely different. We come from different cultures, speak different languages, live in different countries, have different backgrounds... And yet the Word of God tells us that we can now be and live in one accord. “There is neither Jew (Judean) nor Greek (Gentile), there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.” Galatians 3:28 [KJV]

Our sense of belonging is not dependent upon where we came from... It is dependent upon where we are going. Through the grace of God we can belong to something larger than ourselves even while remaining as different and unique as... as a pig, a frog, a bear, a prawn, and a “whatever.”
