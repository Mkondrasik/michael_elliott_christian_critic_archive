Akeela and the Bee

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 29, 2006
Rating
4 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Thessalonians 5:11Romans 15:2Proverbs 27:17

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Encouragement

Author-verified transcription

I don't know where I was during its theatrical release but, if you were like me and had missed Akeelah and the Bee on the big screen, go to your local Blockbuster to pick up the DVD or order it from Netflix or buy it from Amazon. It is a wonderful family film filled with meaningful lessons - packaged in an entertaining and emotionally fulfilling way.

It's a terrible title, I know, but it refers to the central character, Akeela Anderson (Keke Palmer), and her goal - to win the Scripps-Howard National Spelling Bee. Akeela is a natural born speller. She began spelling words as a way to ease the pain of losing her Scrabble-loving father at an early age. When her South Central LA school, badly in need of some positive media attention, learns of her ability, they push her relentlessly to represent them at the nationally televised event. She'd be the first student they'd be sending.

At first, she isn't keen to the idea. And her mother (Angela Bassett) is dead set against it. But Akeelah decides enter as a way to honor her father who had always loved words. But to succeed at the national level, she's going to need help. Enter Dr. Larabee (Laurence Fishburne) an ex-Harvard professor who agrees to coach Akeelah using his unusual methodology.

As she begins to advance through the various local, state and regional contests, the pressures mount and the expectations of the community begin to weigh heavily upon her.

This delightful film succeeds on so many levels it is difficult to know what to praise first. Director Doug Atchison should be commended, first and foremost, for his casting. Keke Palmer, who plays Akeela, is a formidable actress for her tender age (13 as of this writing). She is the emotional anchor to this film and it is impossible not to feel a connection with her character. She's a remarkable young performer whose future looks very bright indeed.

Another young performer who shines is JR Villarreal who plays another spelling bee contestant. The joy of this young boy is infectious and lightens the film considerably.

Laurence Fishburne, as the emotionally repressed adult who agrees to coach Akeela, shows a great deal of restraint and control in his performance. It isn't a showy role but he brings levels to it that resonate with us and the other performers.

The only actor that proved troublesome to me was Angela Bassett as Akeela's mother. A hard-edged single mom trying to raise her children in a less than desirable atmosphere, she struck a shrill tone and maintained it throughout the film. I would have liked to have seen more levels from her that might have allowed us to warm up to her character.

Akeela knows that she cannot perform to the best of her abilities without someone to help her. She needs teachers and coaches. She finds one in Dr. Larabee but he is not the only one who assists her. Akeela learns that she can learn from and gain support from everyone in her community that loves her and loves what she's doing.

Having this kind of support is not a unique concept. God instructed us specifically to do this for one another.

Therefore encourage (admonish, exhort) one another and edify (strengthen and build up) one another, just as you are doing. I Thessalonians 5:11 (KJV)

As John Donne so famously put it, "no man is an island, entire of itself...." By drawing support and encouragement from our friends, neighbors and families, we strengthen ourselves far more than we could by going it alone.
