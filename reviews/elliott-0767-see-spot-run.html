Author-verified transcription

The movie, See Spot Run is to cinema what the phrase, "See Spot run" is to literature: geared towards an elementary school mentality. The film can be appreciated only within that context. In other words, we should keep our expectations low.

See Spot Run stars David Arquette (Scream) as Gordon, a goof ball postal carrier who spends most of his day trying to outwit the dogs on his route. It's a close contest with the dogs winning as much as Gordon.

Enamored with his beautiful neighbor, Stephanie (Leslie Bibb, The Skulls), the irresponsible Gordon agrees to baby-sit her six year old son, James (Angus T. Jones, Simpatico), while she goes out of town. Though she recognizes that her son is more mature than Gordon, she has no other option and reluctantly agrees to the arrangement.

Meanwhile, mob boss Sonny Talia (Paul Sorvino, That's Life) has a personal score to settle with FBI Agent 11, a Bull Mastiff (Bob, 102 Dalmatians) who has been hounding the mob for the last two years. He orders a hit on the canine which sends Agent 11 into the witness protection program. When things go awry, Agent 11 escapes from both the mob and the FBI to find refuge with the goofy postman and his six year old charge who rename him Spot.

Not only are hit men Gino (Joe Viterelli, Analyze This) and Arliss (Steven R. Schirripa, The Sopranos) on their tail, but the increasingly emotional FBI Agent Murdoch (Michael Clarke Duncan, The Green Mile), is desperately trying to track and save his four-footed partner.

During all this, Gordon discovers that responsibility has a plus side to it as he bonds not only with the boy, but with a postman's number one nemesis... a dog. Gordon isn't the only character who grows. The FBI-trained Agent 11 learns how to stop and smell the roses... and then roll around in them. If only they can stay one paw ahead of the bad guys, they might learn to be a family.

David Arquette brings a high energy zaniness with a humor which is broad enough to appeal to the tricycle crowd and yet still portray a sweet if immature vulnerability. The beautiful Leslie Bibb deserves a good sport award for allowing herself to be pushed, prodded, splattered, drenched, and blown about as she encounters one obstacle after another trying to return to save her son from the incapable hands in which she left him. Paul Sorvino and company play the Italian Mafioso shtick in humorously stereotypical fashion.

The downside to this rather frivolous kid's movie is the inclusion of humor that can best be described as being of questionable taste. Is it not enough that Spot locks Gordon out of the house? Must we have him repeatedly fall in doggie droppings until he is covered in fecal matter? Must the personal canine vendetta of mob boss Sonny stem from a dog bite injury that made him a soprano... literally? Because of the oftentimes crude nature of the humor, I can't recommend this family film for all family members.

It is a shame really because as inconsequential movies go, this one does have some bright moments as well as some character development worth watching... even though the characters resemble cartoons rather than anyone in real life.

The spiritual lesson can come from observing the opposites in the film that set the two extremes: Gordon, the irresponsible, undependable but childishly playful postman and Spot, the over-trained, over-disciplined, unplayful workaholic dog. The first time Gordon tries to interact with Spot, he thinks the dog is "broken" because it doesn't know how to chase a ball. Most people who look at Gordon think that he is "broken" because he doesn't know how to act like an adult. As it is for so much of life, the truth for these characters lies somewhere in the middle.

Let your moderation be known unto all men. Philippians 4:5a (KJV)

Finding the proper balance in one's life is important. As the worldly saying goes, "All work and no play makes Jack a dull dog." See Spot Run is not dull. But neither is it properly balanced.
