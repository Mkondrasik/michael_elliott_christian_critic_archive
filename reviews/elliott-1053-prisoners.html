Prisoners

Author-verified transcription

What is a father to do? In Prisoners, two girls disappear from their homes and the only suspect is a young man whose camper van was spotted in the vicinity. When the police are unable to hold or charge him with anything due to a lack of physical evidence, the father of one of the girls decides to take matters into his own hands.

Hugh Jackman stars as Keller Dover, a working class man who is driven to desperation after the investigation into his daughter’s disappearance hits a dead end. Keller remains convinced of the guilt of Alex Jones (Paul Dano), a mentally challenged boy, who was seen in the area. Frustrated by the inability of the authorities to uncover the truth, he believes he has no choice but to take action himself.

Dragging Franklin Birch (Terrence Howard), the father of the other missing child, into his plan, Keller abducts Alex and, using increasingly violent and inhumane forms of torture, tries to force him to reveal the whereabouts of the missing girls. Meanwhile, Detective Loki (Jake Gyllenhaal) continues his investigation by figuratively lifting one rock after another and dealing with the vermin he finds underneath.

This is a disturbing and unflinching film that is often uncomfortable to watch. The violence that Keller exhibits is not made easier to watch with our understanding of his underlying motivation. Jackman handles the role with great finesse, showing us Keller’s genuine concern for his family while the violent potential of his character is always evident and believable.

Gyllenhaal adds a nervous tick to his character that is never explained or mentioned in the film but, as we see the human depravity he must wade through during his investigation and the increasing frustration that comes from having to work within the system, we can certainly understand how it developed. The supporting cast is quite strong with Maria Bello as Keller’s wife, Viola Davis as Franklin’s wife, and Melissa Leo as Alex’s mother.

Principles matter most when the stakes are the highest. It is relatively easy to follow a righteous path when we are not pressured or challenged. Unfortunately we live in a world where pressures and challenges arise unexpectedly and without mercy. It is in dealing with them that we must take care not to allow ourselves to lose sight of who we are and what we represent.

While we certainly sympathize with Keller’s plight, the impulse to fight evil with evil or violence with violence does not reflect a godly solution to his problem. Quite the opposite is true. Everything we do carries an emotional and psychological cost that often lingers long after the event itself. What the film does not satisfactorily address is how Keller’s life has been irrevocably changed as a result of the steps he took. Regardless of the outcome, Keller must live with the knowledge of what he did… what he became.

No matter what despicable horrors we may have to face in this world, becoming part of that horror show by adopting the methods or means of our enemies, should not be an option for us.

“For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?” Mark 8:36 (KJV)

Justice, vengeance, and restitution are things best left to God. He promises that He will make all things right. Man’s attempts to do so usually result in sadness, pain and dissatisfaction.
