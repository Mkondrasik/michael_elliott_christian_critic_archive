Friday Night Lights

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 8, 2004
Rating
3 stars

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Perfection; giving your all; service; excellence

Author-verified transcription

It would be easy to think that the religious-like fervor the characters in Friday Night Lights display towards high school football games is just another Hollywood exaggeration. At least it would be easy for those who have not spent any time in the heartland of America. The people there take their football very seriously.

So when the high school coach (Billy Bob Thornton) opens the season by telling his team, “We're in the business of protecting this town,” and “the hopes and dreams of an entire town are riding on your shoulders,” his words are spoken in deadly earnest, without the slightest bit of irony or whimsy.

Based upon a true story and the novel by Buzz Bissinger which told it, Friday Night Lights relives the 1988 season of the Odessa-Permian Panthers. A perennial powerhouse in Texas high school football, the town's expectations of this particular season are so high that anything less than a state championship will be considered a failure.

That expectation exists due to the presence of the star running back and human highlight reel known as Boobie Miles (Derek Luke). Coasting behind Boobie's considerable talents, the Panthers are virtually guaranteed an undefeated season.

When Boobie suffers a season ending injury early in the year, all bets are off and the bone-crushing pressure, intensely felt by the coach and his players, is on.

Directed by Peter Berg and filmed in a gritty, naturalistic way, Friday Night Lights is surprisingly effective in capturing the small town fascination and nearly obsessive-behavior associated with high school sports. It is more realistic than Remember the Titans or Varsity Blues, both of which dealt with the same subject. It is probably most reminiscent of Hoosiers, which did for Indiana basketball what Friday Night Lights might do for Texas football – provide a video testament to the heart behind the sport.

Billy Bob Thornton plays the coach in a low key manner. It often seems as if he's embarrassed by the degree to which the town worships the game and his players. All of that is forgotten when the game begins. At that point, the focus is on the game and how it is to be played.

The film focuses on a number of young men who are on the team. Lucas Black, who costarred with Thornton in Sling Blade, is the team's quarterback, thrust into the limelight after Boobie's injury. Derek Luke is wonderful as the vociferous Boobie, left speechless when the injury takes his life's plans from him. Tim McGraw, in his feature film debut, does a decent job as an ex-jock trapped in the memories of his glory days.

The coach teaches his team the meaning of perfection. “Be perfect” is an admonition he often uses when sending them onto the field. To him, perfect does not mean error-free. It means to perform at the highest heights of one's abilities... to give 100% and leave no reserve untapped.

We are commanded to give of ourselves in the service of God. He too, tells us to be perfect.

Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect. Matthew 5:48 (KJV)

The word perfect, in this King James' version, literally means complete. In whatever we do, God only asks is that we give it our all.
