Author-verified transcription

The title of 21 Grams refers to the weight that is supposedly "lost" at the time of one's death -- the weight of a soul as it leaves the body. It is a surprisingly slight amount. Judging by the hefty lives that are presented to us in the film, we might be inclined to think that these souls would have a bit more substance to them.

Told in a disjointed manner, with no adherence to traditional linear timelines, 21 Grams introduces us to three characters who are living through their individual and personal tragedies. As the story unfolds, the lives of a young widow who turns to drugs in order to relieve her grief; a heart patient with a failing marriage; and a born again ex-con are going to become forever intertwined as their tragedies touch upon and indelibly mark each of the others.

By jumbling the sequence of events, director Alejandro Gonzalez Inarritu demands of the audience their undivided attention. Those who give it will be rewarded with a film that contains three of the most powerful performances to be seen in this year.

Sean Penn has been receiving a lot of critical acclaim for his role in Clint Eastwood's Mystic River but, for me, the job he does here as an ailing heart patient results in a far more complex and interesting character.

Naomi Watts continues to impress with this gritty turn as a woman whose life is tragically altered in the blink of an eye. Because we see her character on either side of the pivotal event in her life, she has the most opportunity to demonstrate her emotional range. She doesn't disappoint.

Completing this acting triangle is yet another fine performance from Benecio Del Toro. An ex-con who has found Jesus, he finds his newly developed faith being sharply tested when a single act sends him on a downward spiral of guilt and condemnation.

To go into more detail of the plot would be to diminish the experience of seeing the film. Please note that this R rated film includes profane language, scenes of violence and sexuality, as well as some drug use... it also contains some intensely emotional scenes that can be uncomfortable to watch. Thus, 21 Grams may not be for all tastes. But there's no denying the talent behind it.

The supposition behind the title is based upon scientific experiments conducted by Dr. Duncan MacDougal in the early 1900s. Dr. MacDougal observed, by placing terminal patients on a scale, that at the time of their death there was a measurable decrease in weight. He drew the conclusion that the variance could only be explained by the departing of the patient's soul, something confirmed for him by the fact that a dying dog had no such weight loss.

Whether Dr. MacDougal's conclusions are valid or not isn't an issue I'm prepared to debate. But his terminology is flawed. Biblically speaking, the soul refers to a person's breath life. When God breathed into Adam's nostrils, man became a "living soul." When a man dies, the breath is expunged from a person's lungs and he ceases to live.

In the acts of creation, there is no distinction between the soul life of animals and humans. Dogs have breath life which originate from the same source as ours. What distinguishes us from the animals on this planet is our ability to have spirit life. Spirit is something God created uniquely for man so that we might have fellowship with Him. There is a distinction between soul and spirit but it requires a biblical understanding to see it.

For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart. Hebrews 4:12 (KJV)

Whether the soul or spirit of man has a measurable weight is immaterial in the grand scheme of things. What's important is what we do with it.
