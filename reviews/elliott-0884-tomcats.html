Author-verified transcription

The literal dictionary definition of a "tomcat" is simply the male of the species. However, for me, the term has always conjured up an image of an undesirable, uncontrolled, mangy animal running free to wreak havoc on the neighborhood. Such a description better fits the movie Tomcats... especially the mangy part. Watching it may very well get your skin to start crawling... hopefully towards the exit.

These "Tomcats" are a group of over sexed, immature men who make a pact with one another not to get married. They even put up a few hundred dollars each and invest it with the understanding that the money will go to the last man to walk down the aisle.

A decade later, thanks to the bull market of the 90s, the pot has grown to a half-million dollars and the eligible bachelors have dwindled to two remaining holdouts... Michael (Jerry O'Connell, Mission to Mars), a down on his luck cartoonist, and Kyle (Jake Busey, Contact) a thoroughly obnoxious individual whose main goal in life is to have sex with every woman in the world.

When Michael stupidly racks up a $50,000 gambling debt, the only solution out of his predicament is to convince Kyle to get married so he can use the Tomcat money to pay off the casino owner. He solicits the help of Natalie (Shannon Elizabeth, American Pie), an undercover cop who agrees to help for reasons of her own.

Naturally, Michael and Shannon fall for each other but won't admit it and so they continue with their scheme to defraud Kyle and... blah, blah, blah... Really, the plot only exists as a flimsy framework upon which to hang the various sexual gags and shock humor which continue to prevail in the more unimaginative minds populating Hollywood.

One of the film's promoted "highlights" has Michael chasing a wayward body part through the halls of a hospital following a friend's surgery for testicular cancer. Other moments include hijinks at the sperm bank; a sadomasochistic encounter with a mousy librarian and her granny; a man, suspecting his wife is having lesbian sex, tries to catch her in the act; and the list goes on.

Jerry O'Connell is affable enough as an actor, but because the script is so demeaning and disrespectful of woman, we are repelled by his character as much as any of the others.

Screenwriter Gregory Poirier (Gossip) makes his directorial debut with Tomcats. His partiality for obvious sexual references and sight gags become quickly tiresome. I would think that the audience old enough to see this R-rated film would have long stopped being titillated by such immature and misogynic humor. Additionally, Poirier tries to tack on a romantic relationship (between Michael and Natalie) in the midst of his film which so belittles romance or adult behavior of any kind. It simply doesn't work. We care nothing about these characters.

To be fair, the script does have Michael coming to an awakening of sorts in which he admits that in Natalie he may have found "someone who matters more to me than I matter to myself." Though his actions do not convey that he believes those words with any kind of conviction, they are meaningful words when compared to how God designed the marriage relationship.

Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; Ephesians 5:25 (KJV)

To use Christ's unselfish sacrifice as the standard for the way a man ought to love his wife is testimony of the level of importance God places upon the institution of marriage. However, contrary to Michael's statement, God does not want men to love their wives greater than they love themselves. He wants us to have a love that goes far deeper than that.

So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church: Ephesians 5:28-29 (KJV)

Men who follow this standard are the ones that women should be seeking. Let us leave the tomcats on the streets, rummaging through the garbage cans.
