Author-verified transcription

What would cause a Superman, beloved by children the world over, to put a gun to his head and blow his brains out? That's the question that private eye Louis Simo is asking in the new period mystery, Hollywoodland.

In 1959, George Reeves, a small time actor who gained reluctant fame when he donned the blue and red tights for a TV version of the Man of Steel, committed suicide. In this hypothetical account, a small time private detective, using media savvy and unfounded allegations, asks enough questions to raise a specter of doubt as to what might have really happened.

Was Reeves (Ben Affleck) shot by a jealous girlfriend (Robin Tunney) after he told her that their relationship was over? Was he killed by a studio boss (Bob Hoskins) because of the affair he was having with his wife (Diane Lane)? Or was he simply despondent over the fact that his acting career stalled and crashed due to his low-paying but high-profile TV role?

Which of these scenarios is true? We don't know - nor do we find out. Director Allen Coulter and screenwriter Paul Bernbaum leave things ambiguous enough to force us to draw our own conclusions.

But even though loose ends are left dangling, they are at least being dangled by an extremely competent cast working with an intelligent script in an authentic looking retro-world.

Much attention is being given to Ben Affleck for the work he has done as the charming but frustrated Reeves. Perhaps he feels an affinity for the man who wanted to be seen as a serious and important actor but never got the opportunity. Putting on an extra twenty pounds for the role, he may have also finally shed some of the publicity-driven baggage to which he has been shackled.

But while Affleck turns in a credible performance, it is Adrien Brody who really makes the piece work. Simo is more snake oil salesman than detective and is, in his own way, just as charming as Reeves. He's also just as frustrated as he also feels he's on the fringes of success, not yet seen as a "player" in the game of Hollywood law and order. Brody's look and delivery is perfect for the role and sets the tone for this quasi-noir film.

Diane Lane gives another solid performance as the older woman who keeps Reeves housed and fed in exchange for his sexual attentions. She is able to play sexy, scared, and scorned with equal degrees of effectiveness. Bob Hoskins plays a studio boss with the chilling menace of a Mafia don.

The pacing of the film is slightly affected because the filmmakers have decided to constantly switch back and forth between past and present as they try to tell the stories of Simo and Reeves virtually simultaneously.

It should be noted that their stories aren't the happiest. Both central characters are dissatisfied with their lives even though both had reasons or opportunities to find happiness and contentment. When we fixate on a single dream, we often miss the many blessings being offered us. Living a contented life, no matter what that life may bring, is available. It requires a sound mind and an unwavering trust in the God who provides all things.

Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me. Philippians 4:11-13 (KJV)
