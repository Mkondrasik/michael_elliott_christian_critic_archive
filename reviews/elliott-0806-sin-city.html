Sin City

Author-verified transcription

You don't visit a place called Sin City with the expectation of finding saints. Likewise, when we prepare to watch a film that goes by the same name we shouldn't be surprised by the kind of behavior engaged by its characters.

Rooted in the stylized sensibilities of Mickey Spillane and Damon Runyon, Sin City is a brilliantly realized cinematic representation of Frank Miller's pulp fictionalized comic book world. It was co-director Robert Rodriguez (Spy Kids) who convinced an initially skeptical Miller that new digital techniques would be capable of capturing the unique look and feel of his graphic novels and effectively transfer them from page to screen.

Three of Miller's stories are used to form the spine of the film and each tale features a different tough guy hero whose actions often mirror the unspeakable horrors performed by those he opposes. Such is the world of Sin City where:

Marv (Mickey Rourke) is an ugly brute of a man who enjoys one night with a beautiful blond. When he wakes up next to her corpse, he moves heaven and earth to find her killers and avenge her death.

John Hartigan (Bruce Willis) is an honest cop with a bad heart who refuses to allow a young girl to be the next victim of a sick and twisted individual with friends in high places. His devotion to the girl spans years and places him at odds with an all powerful political machine.

Dwight (Clive Owen) is simply trying to help protect a band of cop-killing prostitutes by hiding the evidence of their crime. He is up against a number of unscrupulous men who seek the same evidence so they might use it to gain power and control over the women's turf.

In each case, the heroes beat, maim, shoot, and dismember anyone who is foolish enough to stand in their way. Such gruesome activity is somewhat softened by the black and white cinematography (interrupted by brief and well-placed flashes of color). As in the printed version of the stories, some of the action is even viewed in silhouette. Still, the film is exceedingly violent and graphic in nature.

It is nonetheless a remarkable artistic achievement which showcases the improvements that have been made in the field of digital filmmaking. It is also enhanced by a number of effective performances. Mickey Rourke is totally in command of his character. It is a performance that could easily lead to a resurgence of his flagging career. The actors, save one, slip rather naturally into the 1940s retro style of the film. The exception is Brittany Murphy who, as a sassy barmaid in one of Sin City's seedier dives, cannot lose the modern cadence of her speech patterns and seems woefully out of place and time.

One way to define sin is a state of broken fellowship between man and God. Sin City gives us an opportunity to imagine what a godless world might look like - filled with violence and ruled by chaos. It's a world prophetically described in the book of Isaiah.

The way of peace they know not: and there is no judgment in their goings: they have made them crooked paths: whosoever goeth therein shall not know peace. Therefore is judgment far from us, neither doth justice overtake us: we wait for light, but behold obscurity; for brightness, but we walk in darkness. Isaiah 59:8-9 (KJV)
