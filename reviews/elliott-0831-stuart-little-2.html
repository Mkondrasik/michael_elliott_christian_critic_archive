Stuart Little 2

Author-verified transcription

Who knew that a mouse could roar this loudly at the box office? In the year 2000, Stuart Little managed to win the hearts and wallets of audiences around the globe, eventually collecting over $300 million at the box office. Get ready for a repeat performance because Stuart Little 2 is just as warm, fuzzy, funny, and family-oriented as its minuscule CGI star... and that is saying something.

Stuart (voiced by Michael J. Fox, Spin City) has settled in at the Little household. He shares a room with his adoptive brother George (Jonathan Lipnicki, Like Mike); has father to mouse talks with his adoptive dad (Hugh Laurie, The Piano Tuner); and endures the overprotective nurturing of his adoptive mother (Geena Davis, A League of Their Own). Even Snowbell the cat (voiced by Nathan Lane, Titan A.E.) has reluctantly come to accept him as a part of the family.

Still, Stuart senses that he's different. It may be because, as a mouse, he's about 1,000 times smaller than anyone else and has no friends his own size. And then, as if in an answer to prayer, into his life drops Margalo (voiced by Melanie Griffith, Crazy in Alabama), an injured little bird being pursued by a fierce, predatory falcon (voiced by James Woods, John Q). Before we can say "Little hi, little lo, little hey, little ho," Margalo is welcomed into the Little household, much to the flustered chagrin of Snowbell and to the heart-thumping delight of Stuart.

But all is not as it seems and the entire Little clan will have lessons to learn about honesty, trust, love, and letting go.

This relatively short film (the entire running length of the film is only 72 minutes) is just long enough to totally enthrall both old and young without its becoming tedious or repetitive.

As with the original film, a combination of witty writing and delightful characters make for an irresistible mix. Michael J. Fox reprises his vocal performance as the irrepressible Stuart who so badly wants to be accepted as one of the kids that he's actually excited to be on the school's soccer team even though the ball towers over him like a Goodyear blimp.

Much of the humor comes from Nathan Lane, who intones the pampered pet Snowbell with such sarcastic archness that he quickly becomes an audience favorite. Melanie Griffith's "little girl voice" is perfect for Margalo, the free-spirited bird who flies into Stuart's heart. James Woods is appropriately menacing as the voice of the Falcon, the only bad guy in the film.

Although screenwriters M. Night Shyamalan and Gregory J. Booker have not returned to pen the sequel, Bruce Joel Rubin (Ghost) has been able to capture the feel good attitude of the original film. We like these characters and there is nothing in this film to stop us from liking them even more.

Perhaps more than the original, Stuart Little 2 has messages that will resound in the hearts of any family member. The most obvious being the ultimate responsibility of parents to prepare to send their children out into the world. One of the recurring themes of the film is that Mrs. Little may be holding on too tightly to Stuart for fear that he may be hurt if he actually participates in worldly activities. Scriptures provide Christian parents who may be similarly inclined with both direction and comfort.

Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it. Proverbs 22:6 (KJV)

All Mrs. Little can do... all any parent can do is to instill in her child the difference between right and wrong, the understanding that actions will bring consequences, the knowledge that it is better to give than receive, and all the other truths that God would have us know, remember, and use.

Keeping a bird from stretching her wings to fly will only make that bird easy prey for her adversaries. We cannot teach our children to win in the competition of life if fear keeps us from letting them play the game. Neither should we toss them into the game unprepared or ill-equipped. We train them, we trust them, then eventually we must let them go.
