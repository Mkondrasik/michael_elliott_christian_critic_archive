Stepmom

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 25, 1998
Rating
3½ stars

2 hr: 5 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Family; Long-Suffering; Marriage

Author-verified transcription

One tissue won't do. If you're planning to see Stepmom, you'd best bring the whole box. You're going to need it.

Director Chris Columbus (Nine Months) uses all the tricks of the trade to wring the tears out of us. Well... maybe not all of us. Most men will be immune to the cinematic manipulation... some women may be able to avoid an emotional outburst... but mothers?? Mothers should prepare themselves for a torrent of tears that will make Noah's flood look like a little gully-washer.

As our culture became more and more tolerant of "broken marriages", we have also had to learn how to deal with new and unusual relationships. There are few relationships that are as unusual and strange as the ones between a natural mother and the step-mom.

Susan Sarandon (Dead Man Walking) is Jackie, the natural mother of two delightfully normal children, who has to share her children with the new woman in her ex-husband's life.

Julia Roberts (My Best Friend's Wedding) is that new woman, Isabel. She is younger, prettier (if you like big teeth, Jackie says), and struggling to make a connection with two children who naturally reflect the animosity and hostility of their mother.

Ed Harris (The Rock) is the man in the middle. An uncomfortable position, to be sure, but Mr. Harris does a fine job in a thankless role. This movie is for and about the women.

The heartbreak of divorce and "broken homes" not being enough for a full cathartic release, Mr. Columbus has Ms. Sarandon diagnosed with a terminal form of cancer. Ms. Sarandon is quite deserving of the Golden Globe nomination that she received for this role. It is a gutsy performance by an actress seemingly unafraid to take risks.

Jena Malone (Contact) and Liam Aiken (Henry Fool), as the children caught in the emotional tug-of-war, could not have been more perfectly cast. These are not children who are wiser than their years or perfect little dolls with no personality hiding in the background of the scenes. Their naturalness on screen is refreshing.

Spiritually, while a child should grow up in an environment where he or she is surrounded by love, it is important to remember that the child is not the foundation nor the center of a family. The center of the family must be the marriage relationship. And the foundation of the marriage must be God and His Word. Without God, there is no standard strong enough to keep a marriage together. With God, there is nothing strong enough to rip that marriage apart.

Perhaps if Jackie worked on her relationship with her husband as much as she worked on her relationship with her children, her marriage could have been saved.

Of all the characters on screen, Isabel acted in the most godly fashion. Continually abused or snubbed by Jackie, she never retaliated in kind. Rejected by the children, she persisted in reaching out to them - not as merely a friend, but as a loving parent. She manifested the godly quality of long-suffering.

Long-suffering is more than patience. It is patience with endurance. If someone doesn't respond the first time, you try again. If they don't respond the second time, you try again.

It would have been easy for Isabel to quit and resign herself to the fact that her boyfriend's children hated her. It would have been easy, but it would not have been best.

Stepmom may not be the best movie of the year, but judging from the choked back sobs coming from the women in the audience of the movie I attended, it certainly was effective. Ladies, please heed this warning... when seeing this movie, tissue is not optional.. it is a simple necessity.
