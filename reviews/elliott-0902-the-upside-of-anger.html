The Upside of Anger

Original publication date
April 1, 2005

3 stars

1 hr: 58 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Anger; Wrath; Control

Author-verified transcription

We are told that "hell hath no fury as a woman scorned." The Upside of Anger gives credence to the saying and keeps that fury always simmering - ready to boil over at any given time in any given scene.

After a brief prologue, the film flashes back in time to tell the story of Terry Wolfmeyer (Joan Allen), an attractive mother of four teenaged daughters who is trying to come to terms with the realization that her husband has left her for his Swedish assistant. Unfortunately her way of dealing with the emotional pain is to drown it with alcohol. It becomes her preferred method of dealing with all stress... and with four teenagers in the house, stress is a daily occurrence.

She has company. One of her neighbors is Denny Davies (Kevin Costner), an ex-ballplayer turned radio talk show host, who is more than happy to become Terry's drinking buddy. Together they drink their way through life's disappointments, oblivious to the fact that most of those disappointments are ones that they have helped to make.

Meanwhile, the four daughters (Erika Christensen, Evan Rachel Wood, Keri Russell, and Alicia Witt) are making their own life decisions - most of which send mom further into her state of apoplectic rage.

At the heart of this film is the wonderful Joan Allen whose intensity simply burns up the screen. I don't know anything about Ms. Allen's personal life or whether she has any children but she certainly has a mother's nonverbal disapproving "look" down pat. It is especially amusing in one scene where the look has the effect fantasized by every mom who has ever given it.

Kevin Costner is a nice comic counterpoint to Allen's controlled (and sometimes uncontrolled) anger. He plays a goofy but gentle man who may not be the most intelligent player ever to swing a bat.

All four of the daughters are given fully developed characters and individual moments where the respective actresses are able to shine.

Writer director Mike Binder also appears as Shep, Denny's radio producer and a rather pathetic man who tends to date girls half his age... including one of Terry's daughters.

Even though Binder has written some very clever dialogue and crafted some interesting characters his screenplay is far from flawless. Although I admire Allen's performance some of her reactions to what is going on around her seem to me a bit contrived. Binder also bookends his film with a voiceover narration given by the youngest daughter with a whacky philosophical overview on the "upside of anger" that holds little weight.

There's not much of an upside to anger - especially an anger that is uncontrolled and irrational. Examining Terry's life we can easily see that her unresolved anger poisons or deleteriously affects every relationship she has. God exhorts us to put aside our wrath to seek the peace of heart and mind that comes when we live according to His standard.

Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God. James 1:19-20 (KJV)
