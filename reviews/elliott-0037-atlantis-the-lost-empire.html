Atlantis: The Lost Empire

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 15, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 1:12 2 Timothy 1:6-7 Acts 10:38

Translation: NRSV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual power; gifts of holy spirit; studying God's Word; Christian parenting

Author-verified transcription

Heavily steeped in mythology and borrowing from sci-fi classics such as 20,000 Leagues Under The Sea and Journey To The Center of The Earth, Disney’s newest animated adventure, Atlantis: The Lost Empire, is eye-catching and full of heart-pounding action.

The nerdy Milo Thatch (voice by Michael J. Fox, Stuart Little), is a young museum employee who dreams of discovering the whereabouts of the fabled lost city of Atlantis. Milo, a linguistics expert, is trying to complete the work begun by his late grandfather who was a famous explorer.

When an eccentric tycoon (voice by John Mahoney, Frasier) agrees to fund a massive expedition, Milo finds himself joining the A-Team of scientific exploration led by the burly Captain Rourke (voice by James Garner, Maverick). Following the clues contained in an ancient journal, Milo directs the team to the very gates of Atlantis where they are attacked by the city’s guardian, a giant lobster-like machine known as Leviathan.

The surviving handful of explorers continue on to discover that Atlantis has survived because of a powerful crystalline life energy which has kept the undersea city protected and provided its people with health and longevity. Trouble ensues when the more mercenary members of Milo’s expedition decide to steal the crystal energy and take it back to the surface to sell to the highest bidder. It will be up to Milo and Atlantis’s Princess Kida (voice by Cree Summer, A Different World) to try and stop that from happening.

Atlantis is more reminiscent of Disney’s live action adventures than any of its recent animated features. There are no songs (save the one that plays over the end credits), and no talking animals. There is, however, much action, sometimes rather intense action that results in death.

The visual imagery is quite impressive. Traditional 2D hand drawn animation with 3D computer graphics effects were blended extremely well. The depiction of Atlantis itself as well as many of the vehicles which were used in the film were exquisite in their detail.

The vocal talent was equally impressive. Standouts include the unusual cadence and dry humor of Don Novello (perhaps best known for his Father Guido Sarducci character on Saturday Night Live) which he brings to the character of demolition expert Vinnie Santorini. Florence Stanley (Fish) riotously deadpans her way through her role of the unflappable Mrs. Packard.

Of course, at the center of the film is the fantasy of the legend Atlantis and its crystal energy power source. Such mysticism has long been and most likely will continue to be a staple of Disney’s animated films. Christian parents should be prepared to address such issues with their children.

To look at this element from a positive perspective, there are some analogies that can be made to bring our minds back to reflect upon biblical truths. If we liken the Atlantian crystal energy to the awesome power of God, the shards of crystal which the Atlantians wear about their necks would then represent God’s gift of holy spirit which He gives to all who believe.

As we see in the film, the shards of crystals were used to direct the power that comes from the energy source towards specific purposes, just as we can manifest the power of God in our lives through the operation of the gift of holy spirit within us.

Furthermore, the Atlantians had lost much of the knowledge regarding the crystals and how they functioned. That is something that we should be careful not to emulate. It is why God so often exhorts us to study and do His Word. So that we retain the knowledge of how to live with His power in our lives.

But to all who received him, who believed in his name, he gave power to become children of God. John 1:12 (NRSV)
For this reason I remind you to rekindle the gift of God that is within you through the laying on of my hands; for God did not give us a spirit of cowardice, but rather a spirit of power and of love and of self-discipline. 2 Timothy 1:6-7 (NRSV)
