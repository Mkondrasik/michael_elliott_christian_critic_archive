All Is Lost

Author-verified transcription

It is a brave concept for a film. Largely consisting of little to no dialogue with a single actor playing a character we don’t know and don’t learn much about, All Is Lost is a riveting tale of survival as one man battles the elements in a valiant struggle to see another day.

The film opens with a voiceover as the man (Robert Redford) pens a letter of apology to a person (or persons) unknown for reasons not clear to us. It then jumps back eight days to lead us through the conditions that led to the letter.

The man is on a solo journey in the Indian Ocean when his sailboat runs into an abandoned shipping container causing a hull breach. Though he successfully repairs the damage, he is left without radio or navigation controls. And there’s a storm brewing directly in his path.

Writer/director J.C Chandor should be commended for bringing this story to the screen. The dramatic intensity builds due to the situation of the character. There’s no need to overstate the obvious. Each decision the character makes is easily one that could result in life or death.

Robert Redford was the perfect choice to play the character. He conveys the rugged individualism, and stoic resourcefulness needed as well as being an iconic presence to fill the screen when there is no other human character within reach of the lens. And he has the awareness not to overplay the role but to let the story take center stage.

And what a tale it is. A story of survival; of a man who, at the end of his rope, is thinking “all is lost” is something to which we can all relate. Not that we have ever been adrift at sea or in space, or even cast away on an isolated island. But each of us has been in need of deliverance or salvation. Each of us has felt lost and alone at some point in our lives. The good news is that we aren’t.

God tells us that even at our lowest or loneliest points, we can reach out to Him and He will hear us. We may lose Him at times during our lives but He never loses us. And He will always be there for us when we call out to him for his guidance and salvation.

Save me, O God, For the waters have threatened my life [they have come up to my neck]. I have sunk in deep mire, where there is no foothold; I have come into deep waters, where a flood overwhelms me. Psalms 69:1-2

I went down to the bottoms and the very roots of the mountains; the earth with its bars closed behind me forever. Yet You have brought up my life from the pit and corruption, O Lord my God.” Jonah 2:5-6 (Amplified)
