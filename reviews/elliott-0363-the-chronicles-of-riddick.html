The Chronicles of Riddick

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 11, 2004
Rating
1 star
Running time
1 hr: 59 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Romans 12:211 Peter 3:91 Thessalonians 5:15

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Good and evil; overcoming evil with good

Author-verified transcription

Perhaps they should have called it The Chronicles of Riddick-ulous. It's the most apt description of this overblown, muscle-bound, explosion-happy, summer sci-fi adventure.

Vin Diesel returns to the screen as Riddick, the convict with the odd ocular disorder who first graced the screens in Pitch Black. The Chronicles of Riddick takes place five years later on a remote planet as Riddick is once again on the run from bounty hunters, known here as "mercs."

Searching for the person who led the mercs to his hideaway planet, he comes up against his old pal Imam (Keith David) who informs him of a conquering race known as Necromongers who are destroying planet after planet on their way to discovering the "underverse" (whatever that is).

He's also informed that Jack, the teenage girl he saved in Pitch Black, is now going by the name of Kyra (Alexa Davalos) and is a prisoner on the planet Crematoria. Riddick must save Kyra, defeat the Lord Marshal (Colm Feore) of the Necromongers, outrace the sun, and single handedly save the universe from certain destruction.

The story and dialogue is beyond laughable. How else should we receive lines such as "It's been a long time since I smelled beautiful" if not with incredulity? The names given to places and people would be more fitting in a Mad Magazine spoof of the film than in the film itself. Crematoria is a planet with a surface temperature of 700 degrees that will immediately incinerate anyone (except those who stay in the shade). The Necromongers are a race with a Borg-like mentality that are "half living and half something else. The only race they fear are the angry and defiant "Furians." Writers demonstrating such incredible lack of imagination and inventiveness should not be working in the realm of science fiction.

Diesel is all muscle and bass, flexing for the camera, growling out his inane dialogue, and taking off and putting on his dark goggles for no good reason. Colm Feore as the chief bad guy is bland and boring. The energetic Alexa Davalos is a bright spot among an otherwise dull and listless cast. Even Dame Judi Dench, who appears as a ghost-like oracle, fails to bring a sense of legitimacy to this project.

The special effects, which should be the saving grace to the film, are neither special nor effective. It is mindless gimmickry that cannot disguise the fact that this film should have been scrapped early on in the process.

The Necromongers are presented as a great evil that can only be defeated by another kind of evil. It is a silly premise that not only contradicts scripture - it defies common sense. We will never be able to counter a force by replacing it with a similar force. It is only by offering an opposing force that we can we repel that which is not desirable. It is why the scriptures tell us:

Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good. Romans 12:21 (KJV)

Good is the opposite of evil - the two cannot co-mingle. Therefore one must prevail over the other and God's promise is that good will always be stronger than evil.

To try to overcome evil with evil would be equivalent to putting out a fire by burning everything in its path. The fire will go out - but only because everything it could consume has already been destroyed. Meeting evil with evil will never lead us to the way of deliverance and salvation. It will bring nothing but destruction and despair.
