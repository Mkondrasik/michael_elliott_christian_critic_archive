Arlington Road

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 9, 1999
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Deuteronomy 20:3 1 John 4:18 Hebrews 2:15

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Fear; believing; bondage; peace

Author-verified transcription

It has taken a few months, but the psychological thriller, Arlington Road, is finally making its quiet way into the theaters. Far from being a dead end, this road contains enough blind alleys and twisting turns to make even the most experienced driver wary.

Jeff Bridges (The Big Lebowski) plays Michael Faraday, a professor of history with a special interest in domestic terrorism. Three years ago, his wife led a Ruby Ridge type FBI raid and was killed in a bloody shoot-out. The FBI never admitted culpability and the memory remains an open wound.

Faraday begins to suspect that his upper middle class neighbors, Oliver and Cheryl Lang (Tim Robbins, Nothing to Lose and Joan Cusack, In & Out) are not what they appear to be. As his suspicions grow, so does the level of risk Faraday takes in order to prove himself right.

To everyone else, the Langs appear to be the ideal suburbanites. Faraday becomes convinced that behind their apple pie exterior lies the potential for an Oklahoma City style terrorist attack. But it’s a hard sell. Faraday is unable to persuade his son (Spencer Treat Clark, Gladiator), who has become best friends with the Langs’ boy, or Brooke (Hope Davis, The Impostors), his graduate student girlfriend, or Whit (White Man’s Burden), his late wife’s FBI partner. If anyone is going to uncover this conspiracy, it will have to be Faraday.

But is it really a conspiracy? That is the question that director Mark Pellington (Going All The Way) and screenwriter Ehren Kruger (Reindeer Games) try to leave open as long as dramatically feasible. We’re never quite sure if Faraday has stumbled onto a diabolical plot to strike terror into the heart of the nation, or if he has loosened his grasp of reality just enough to give his mind completely over to paranoia.

Jeff Bridges gives an unquestionably brave performance by portraying Faraday as a man teetering on the brink of sanity. He walks a fine line and some may make the call that he goes over the top, but it is a brilliantly crafted performance in that he never plays the movie’s trump card. Faraday could be crazy, or he could just be enormously stressed from the knowledge he alone possesses. Mr. Bridges’ performance does not lean us in either direction, but rather draws us in closer, keeping us on the edge of our seats.

Tim Robbins provides a fine compliment to Mr. Bridges’ high-strung performance. There’s a steely coldness that Mr. Robbins brings to Lang that is calmly unnerving. Joan Cusak adds another excellent supporting role performance to her repertoire as well.

The script by Mr. Kruger is far from unflawed. There are gaping holes of logic that stretch the plausibility of the plot, but at Mr. Pennington’s hand, these moments are glossed over quickly and the pace and tension rarely abate.

It becomes evident that Mr. Pennington desires to shake us from our complacent mood. The opening credits, following a disturbing first scene, are jarring and unsettling. His use of music, lighting, and camera angles are designed to keep us from getting comfortable.

Terrorism, by its very name, is designed to engender fear. Fear is a crippling, debilitating device meant to freeze an opponent and keep him from functioning properly. It is why God so often reminds us in His Word that we are to have no fear. “Be not afraid, only believe,” are words which Jesus spoke to a man who came to him for help.

Fear is the opposite of believing. Since believing is the key to receiving the promises of God, no wonder our enemy wants to keep us encased in fear. Fear is a kind of bondage.

And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage. Hebrews 2:15 [KJV]

A life without fear is available. God would not remind us to “Fear not” if it weren’t available to obey Him. Faraday gave in to his fear and his decisions were made from a fearful mindset. Had he chosen to stay peaceful, rejecting the fear, we would have undoubtedly seen a far different film.
