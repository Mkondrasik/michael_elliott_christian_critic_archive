Author-verified transcription

Is there a more traumatic experience for a parent than the loss of a child? Brothers Michael and Marc Linn of Rapid City, South Dakota have used this scenario to explore the role faith plays when tragedy strikes.

The production values of this locally produced film belie the fact that its budget was so minuscule as to be insignificant by Hollywood standards. Obviously fueled by a belief in the project and supported by a cast of volunteer actors, the Brothers Linn manage to present a finished work of which to be rightly proud.

Although, as should be expected, the acting of the cast is uneven, there are standout performances. Marcia Welsh-Kahler as Sharla Richards, the distraught mother of a seven year old girl named Jennifer who vanishes during a shopping excursion, provides a textured, controlled performance. Irene Handren-Seals as Bren, a family friend coping with her own loss also offers a strong supporting hand.

The musical score by James Jacobson is a wonderful addition, setting an appropriate mood or emotional base upon which each scene is built.

The Linns demonstrate a definite proficiency regarding camera angles, scene construction, and editing. The opening shot of a child drawing a lovely picture of a family is quite effective as is the scene where Sharla discovers her 7 year old to be missing from the clothing store where they were shopping.

The weaknesses of the movie deal primarily with the aforementioned unevenness of the acting quality and with the ambiguity of the script's resolution. Sharla, who had put her trust in God to protect her children, tries to steadfastly believe that God will allow no harm to come onto Jennifer. Even as those around her prepare themselves for the possibility that they may never see Jennifer alive again, Sharla continues to live in denial, refusing to believe that God would permit anything bad to happen.

Sharla's inner struggle to continue to believe in God despite all that has occurred to her family deserves to be kept as the dominant storyline of the film, but two thirds of the way through the movie, the focus shifts to a more action-oriented detective story. When we get back to Sharla, it is unclear how she becomes reconciled with God. A scene near the end of the film, which is obviously intended to indicate comfort to the character, is left deliberately ambiguous. People will undoubtedly draw their own conclusions as to what this scene will mean. I daresay that most will choose to believe something that is contradictory to the scriptures.

Bad things do happen in life... Even to people who are good. We have a personal adversary whose desire is to "steal, kill, and destroy." Praise God that we have the means to resist him but it is important that we never lose sight of his real and dangerous nature. We must stay ever vigilant to prevent him from prevailing over us or our families.
