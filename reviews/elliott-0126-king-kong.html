King Kong

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 14, 2005
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love

Author-verified transcription

Allow me to state the obvious: At over 3 hours in length and a production cost of over 200 million dollars, director Peter Jackson’s remake of King Kong is one monster of a movie.

With the clout gained from the phenomenal success of his Lord of the Rings trilogy, Jackson decided to revisit a film that has been a longtime favorite of his. Even if you’ve somehow missed the original 1933 version or the less noteworthy 1976 remake, Kong is such an iconic figure that the story will still seem familiar.

Set in the depression-laden 30s, the film begins with Carl Denham (Jack Black), a financially struggling filmmaker, taking his cast and crew to the mysterious Skull Island to discover Kong, whom he promptly names the Eighth Wonder of the World.

This gigantic ape is Lord of the Forest by being the strongest, smartest and fiercest creature on an island populated by dinosaurs, prehistoric insects, and fearful natives.

Speaking of those natives, they use Denham’s expedition as an opportunity to make one more sacrifice to their ape god. This time, the victim, Ann Darrow (Naomi Watts), touches an emotional cord within Kong that will eventually lead to the original film’s best remembered line, “It was beauty killed the beast.”

Kong’s protective fascination with Ann leads to his capture and eventual exploitation by master showman Denham. As we know, it was an exploitation doomed from the start as Kong frees himself to cause panic in the streets of New York City.

The special effects are mind-blowing – vastly superior to the cheesy camera tricks of 70 years ago. This Kong looks real and believable. But Jackson shoots himself in the foot by giving us too much: Too many stories, too many action scenes going on for too long all of which lead to a running time that is out of proportion for what is, despite its cost and name cast, a B-monster movie.

Watts is resplendent as the doe-eyed actress who captures the heart of Kong. It is their relationship (beauty and the beast) that gives the film whatever gravitas it musters. All the rest is action/adventure and edge of the seat suspense. Adrian Brody is fine as the Kong’s human rival for Ann’s affection. He isn’t given that much to do except rush headlong into danger in a mindless attempt to rescue his girl from the clutches of the big hairy ape who is more than willing to fight for an exclusive relationship.

Jack Black is best suited for his over the top comedic performances. There are vestiges of that persona (or else lingering perceptions of it) in his portrayal of Denham and they seem somewhat out of place here.

It was Denham’s compulsive desire for fame and fortune that drove him to try (unsuccessfully) to control an uncontrollable force. Once again we see through the illustration of film that love is the greatest power of all. It’s far greater than greed or lust for fame.

Ann was not the strongest or smartest character on the screen. But she was the only one to tap into the one thing that could alter Kong’s behavior – love. Kong responded to aggression with aggression. It was love that “tamed” him just as it will change anyone who gives himself over to love’s infinite possibilities.

We love him, because he first loved us. 1 John 4:19 (KJV)
