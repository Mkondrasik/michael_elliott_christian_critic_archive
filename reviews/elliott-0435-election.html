Author-verified transcription

Most of the reviews that I have seen for the new film, Election, describe it in much the same way: as being "wickedly funny." After viewing it for myself I was very disappointed to find that these "pre" viewers were only half right. This film tries too hard to be wicked for me to appreciate any of its so-called humor.

Tracy Flick, as played by Reese Witherspoon (Cruel Intentions) is the rah-rah queen of Carver High School. Much like Max Fisher, the lead character in Rushmore, she belongs to every club available, chairing most of them. In this her senior year, she has decided that it is now time to leave her most indelible mark on Carver High. She is running for President of the Student Council, much to the dismay of Jim McAllister (Matthew Broderick, Godzilla) who is the council's faculty advisor.

Maybe it's the fact that Tracy had an affair with a fellow teacher, destroying his marriage and career in the process. Maybe it's the fact that Tracy's gung-ho attitude is exhausting to be around. Maybe it's just not wanting to be a part of allowing this overachieving, overambitious, walking time bomb of a self-advancement-at-any-price creature to launch a political career. Whatever it is, Mr. M is not about to let Miss Thang anywhere near the presidency of the student council.

But it's not going to be easy, seeing how she's running unopposed. Eventually Mr. M is able to convince an injured but popular jock (Chris Klein, American Pie) to mount a campaign, even though he lacks the most rudimentary qualifications for the job... such as intelligence. Things get further complicated as the jock's lesbian sister (Jessica Campbell in her feature film debut) throws her hat in the political ring as a show of disdain for the entire process. Her platform is one that is immediately appealing to the high school body... The best student government is no student government. A vote for Tammy Metzler is a vote for anarchy.

As the election day approaches, the frustration and desperation of these characters boils to the surface. Before the movie is over, somebody is going to suffer greatly. By somebody, I mean besides the audience.

That is already a given.

All of the actors do commendable jobs, most notably Reese Witherspoon and Matthew Broderick. It is just that the roles they have been asked to play are self-serving, unattractive and unappealing. Each character is motivated solely by selfish interests.

The subject matter is also designed to shock and titillate. Let's see... We have the teacher - student affair; we have the husband - best friend's wife affair; we have the brother - lesbian sister's girlfriend affair... All in all, there's not a good fidelity rate going on at Carver High. In fact, everyone appears to be majoring in sexual studies, there's so much of it going on.

In one scene Mr. McAllister tries to teach his students the difference between ethics and morals. Because that scene appears early on in the movie, the irony of the moment could be missed. As time progresses, we come to learn that both morals and ethics are noticeably absent from the film.

This complete lack of moral center is, for me, the fatal flaw of the film. The filmmakers took what should have been positive, role-model qualities like drive, perseverance, preparation and planning, and even, yes... ambition, and turned them into the negative and selfish traits of Tracy. In a society where so many of our young people have lost the work ethic of their parents and grandparents, where the word "slackers" has been used to describe an entire generation, the portrayal of persistence and hard work to achieve success deserves better treatment.

Tracy laments at one point in the film, "The weak are always trying to sabotage the strong..."

Watching the weakness displayed in this film, I tend to agree. There is nothing in this film to help strengthen your moral commitment to live life in a way acceptable to God.

Every year we are encouraged to support our system of government by exercising our right to vote. Sitting out this Election would be the wisest and strongest "vote" you could make.
