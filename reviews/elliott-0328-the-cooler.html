The Cooler

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 26, 2003

2½ stars

1 hr: 41 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Believing; fear; self-fulfilling prophecy; victory

Author-verified transcription

The unluckiest man in town is king... especially when the town happens to be Las Vegas where casino owners are looking for any means possible to keep people from a winning streak. In The Cooler, William H. Macy is the means - a man so unlucky that people start losing whenever he's around.

His character, Bernie Lootz, works the floor of the Shangra-La casino, run the "old school" way by Shelly Kaplow (Alec Baldwin). Years before, after Bernie had run up an enormous gambling debt, Shelly broke his kneecap and then forced him to work off the debt by acting as the casino's "cooler." His job is to simply be himself and let his bad luck rub off on whoever is next to him.

He has been so successful that Shelly isn't about to let him go, even though Bernie has just about paid off his debt and plans to move on with his pathetic life.

Magically, Bernie's luck begins to change. Into his life walks Natalie, one of the Shangra-La's cocktail waitresses, who falls for the sad sack and begins to romance him. As Bernie's outlook changes, so does the effect his presence has on those around him. Suddenly, everyone's a winner. Everyone except Shelly who really doesn't like to lose. With added pressure from his partners to change with the times, the last thing Shelly needs is for his "cooler" to suddenly get "hot."

William H. Macy again finds a character which fits well within his strengths and screen persona. Bernie is a likable guy, resigned to his imagined role in the universe. He's not so far gone as to have stopped dreaming of a better, "luckier" life but he doesn't really expect it to happen so when it does, it comes as a pleasant surprise.

Maria Bello, as the catalyst which changes Bernie's perspective on life, plays a rather stereotypical role: a girl with the rough exterior discovers a soft spot in her heart as she learns that love can redeem and heal.

Alec Baldwin is perfectly cast as a mobster pretending to be a businessman. Though his partners try to drag him into the new millennium, it is clear that he's perfectly comfortable staying with the old ways where persuasion comes from wielding a baseball bat.

The film, originally rated NC-17, has gone through a reedit in order to garner a more marketable R rating. Viewers should still be advised that graphic sex scenes do remain in the film as well as extensive profanity, and violence.

Natalie, in one of her conversations with Bernie, unknowingly hits upon a biblical truth. After Bernie has shared what his job at the Shangra-La entails, and how he has always been unlucky in everything he's tried to do, she replies by saying, "Sounds like a self-fulfilling prophecy."

The power of believing is an immutable law that God has woven into the universe, working for saint and sinner alike. Job certainly came to recognize the consequences of negative believing:

For the thing which I greatly feared is come upon me, and that which I was afraid of is come unto me. Job 3:25 (KJV)

God continually exhorts us to think of ourselves as victors; more than conquerors; armed with his power and might. Luck is a superstitious belief which excuses a lack of effort or a will to accomplish one's goals and desires. As the adage goes, "The harder we work, the luckier we get."
