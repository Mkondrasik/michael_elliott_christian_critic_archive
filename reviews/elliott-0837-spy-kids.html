Spy Kids

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 30, 2001
Rating
4 stars
Running time
1 hr: 28 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 4:1-31 Corinthians 1:10Colossians 3:14-15

Sermon / spiritual topic: Family; Unity; Love

Author-verified transcription

James Bond for the junior set. It's a brilliant concept wonderfully executed by writer/director Robert Rodriguez (From Dusk Till Dawn).

Juni and Carmen Cortez are nothing extraordinary. Just a pair of typical grade school kids whose lives consist of little more than homework, boring parents and sibling squabbles. Never, in their wildest dreams, did they ever suspect that their parents might be the world's greatest secret agents! "My parents can't be spies... they're not cool enough!" Carmen exclaims upon learning the truth.

When mom and dad are kidnapped by the evil Fegan Floop, who is masquerading as the host of a children's TV show featuring deformed characters called floogles (discovered to be mutated secret agents), it's up to the kids and the neat spy gadgets they find around the house to save their parents and the world.

Soon, the preteens are jet-packing and scuba diving their way across the world, trying to keep one step ahead of the evil "thumb-thumbs" and robot children which have been programmed to stop them.

Granted, the plot description makes the film sound silly and even a little cheesy, but the high production quality, witty writing, accomplished direction, and winning performances make Spy Kids the most enjoyable family film experience that I've seen in the past year.

Antonio Banderas (The Mask of Zorro) and Carla Gugino (Judas Kiss) head the cast list as Gregorio and Ingrid Cortez, super spies who retire from the OSS to embark on their most exciting adventure yet... parenthood. They maintain the aura of confidence we require of our movie secret agents and yet are able to find the self-effacing humor that makes this respectful spoof of the genre so much fun to watch.

Alan Cumming (Titus) plays Floop with a delightful Willie Wonka wistfulness. More concerned with his fanciful TV show than his involvement in world domination, Floop is kept on track by his evil assistant, Minion (Tony Shalhoub, Galaxy Quest).

Alexa Vega (The Deep End of the Ocean) and Daryl Sabara (in his feature film debut) are filled with the wide-eyed wonder of children in the midst of a fantasy adventure. But they also communicate a recognizable and believable sibling relationship that contains a healthy mixture of love and annoyance. They may grate on each other's nerves, but when the chips are down, they are there to help and support each other. After all, that is what family is for.

The special effects and visual elements to the film are first rate. Robert Rodriguez keeps the action flowing, the humor lively, and the content family friendly. There are some mild innuendoes that creep into the script from time to time. (When Carmen realizes that she's about to face a super-powered robot, she blurts out, "Oh, shhhhh...itake mushrooms!" instead of the more familiar epithet.) But overall, Spy Kids is a true family-oriented film. One which children will want to see more than once and parents might not even mind if they do.

The focus on the family is even built into the script as a thematic device. The two master spies decide to quit the spy business with all its dangers and excitement when they realize that there's even a greater challenge. Later on, Carmen agrees, "This spy stuff is easy. Keeping a family together is hard."

Individually, we are members of the body of Christ, a part of the household of God. Together, we must strive to keep that household functioning in united harmony.

I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called, with all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love; endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. Ephesians 4:1-3 (KJV)

It takes a like-minded effort and a great deal of love to continually keep a family together... especially one that is as large and disparate as the family of God. As Carmen notes, it is indeed hard, but it's a mission that is well worth accepting.
