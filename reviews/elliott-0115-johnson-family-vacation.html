Johnson Family Vacation

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 7, 2004
Rating
1 star
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Fatherhood; family; responsibility; example; God as Father

Author-verified transcription

Be prepared for the worst “vacation” imaginable. Johnson Family Vacation is billing itself as a comedy but it is so tediously dull and unfunny that it leads us to wonder if the folks out there in Hollywood have completely lost their judgment, their taste, or their minds. From the looks of this embarrassment for everyone involved, I wouldn’t rule out the possibility of all three.

Cedric the Entertainer (who may have to reconsider a name change after this stinker) stars as Nate Johnson. Separated from his wife (Vanessa Williams) for the last three months, he nevertheless convinces her to accompany him and their three children (Solange Knowles, Bow Wow, and Gabby Soleil) on a cross country trip to attend his family’s annual reunion. At stake is a trophy for “family of the year” - an honor that his overly competitive brother (Steve Harvey) has claimed for the past three years and has every intention of keeping.

The three day trek is filled with one tired gag after another as Nate experiences road rage with a semi, is stuck nude in a Jacuzzi with a half dozen fat women, picks up a bizarre female hitchhiker (Shannon Elizabeth), completely destroys his new “pimped out” Lincoln Navigator, and predictably reconciles with his wife and family by the time the movie ends. It may take longer for Cedric to reconcile with his fan base.

The film is a disappointment because Cedric is an extremely funny and talented man. Known more for his edgy supporting character roles, this is his first time headlining a cast and to appear in something so inanely stupid is a career miscalculation of the first degree. He deserves better. For that matter, so do we.

It probably comes as no surprise to find that this is the first feature film for director Christopher Erskin and screenwriters Todd R. Jones and Earl Richey Jones. While they should be commended for selling themselves in what is admittedly a very competitive and high profile industry, they cannot be commended for foisting this piece of recycled trash upon a paying public.

The one redeeming element to the film is the cute and (as yet) unspoiled presence of little Gabby Soleil who plays the youngest Johnson. I don’t blame her for appearing in this film - she’s too young to know better. The rest of the cast isn’t.

I even hesitate to use the film (as I always do) to illustrate biblical principles because the film is in no way, shape, or form based in what we might recognize as the real world. The actions and decisions of these characters are so insipidly caricatured that they can’t be taken seriously. If only they were funny. Instead, they float in an in-between state of limbo, neither serious nor funny. Pathetic might be the closest adjective.

So, how to redeem the time we’ve spent with this film? Perhaps we can do this by reflecting on what a father’s role in the family should be. Nate is not a great example from which to learn. He insults and denigrates his son and eldest daughter; he doesn’t recognize or meet the needs of his wife; he acts impulsively and stupidly which affects his family and even puts them in harm’s way. A father has the responsibility to do better than that.

A father should be an example to his children and a leader as well as a partner to his wife. He should conduct himself in a way that demands respect and honor. A father’s love is selfless and without equal. No wonder our God chose this relationship (a father and his child) to be the one he would share with us.

And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty. 2 Corinthians 6:18 (KJV)
