Author-verified transcription

During the past 50 years or so Star Trek has taught us that it doesn't matter who is sitting in the captain's chair onscreen as long as there is a competent hand at the helm off screen. Director J.J. Abrams steers this franchise where man has gone many times before but somehow makes the familiar seem brand new.

A new threat to the Federation sends James T. Kirk (Chris Pine) and his stalwart crew to the very edge of the neutral zone with the unusual orders to locate and terminate a dangerous fugitive. When Kirk breaks regulations and ignores those orders, opting instead to capture his target, he discovers that he may very well have placed himself and his crew in grave danger through his brash and headstrong actions. He may have also done the only thing possible to save the Federation from self-annihilation.

The entire cast from the last film rejoins the Enterprise for this mission including Zachary Quinto as Spock, Zoe Saldana as Uhura, Karl Urban as McCoy, and Simon Pegg as Scotty. They continue building upon the character traits molded for them from the original series while exploring the new relationships and personality quirks afforded by the inventive premise under which they now operate.

There is plenty of harrowing action, self-deprecating humor, nail-biting suspense, and the more than occasional nod to the past as screenwriters Roberto Orci, Alex Kurtzman, and Damon Lindelof continually bring the crew of the Enterprise to the brink of death and back.

While there are a few minor missteps along the way, the casting of the villain was not one of them. Benedict Cumberbatch plays the object of the manhunt with chilling effectiveness. An unstoppable force of engineered power, he makes us believe that he could single-handedly defeat the Federation.

Kirk finds that in order to live another day, he must trust the man he was ordered to kill. He rationalizes by quoting, "An enemy of my enemy is my friend." Spock logically counters by observing that the originator of the quote was killed by those he thought were his "friends."

It is an ill-advised practice to trust an enemy. God warns us in Proverbs.

"Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful." Proverbs 27:6 (KJV)

At the same time, we must guard against acting out of hatred or vengeance towards our perceived enemies. We must remember that our true enemy is spiritual. And whenever we step into "darkness" to smite our physical enemy, we have stepped out of God's light and protection. It is a fine balance that we have been called to walk but it is a walk upon the highest ground there is.

"Do not repay evil with evil or insult with insult. On the contrary, repay evil with blessing, because to this you were called so that you may inherit a blessing." 1 Peter 3:9 (NIV)
