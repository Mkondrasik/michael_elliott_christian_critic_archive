Author-verified transcription

When discussing the legal fight looming before them, the title character of Erin Brockovich muses, “It’s like David and ‘whatsisname’ (from the Bible).” Her ever-pragmatic boss corrects her with “It’s more like David vs. ‘whatsisname’ and ‘whatisname’s’ whole (expletive deleted) family.”

The Goliath in question is Pacific Gas and Electric, a 28 billion dollar utility which, because of environmentally unsound practices, has been slowly poisoning the small town of Hinkley, CA. Fighting for the rights of the simple undereducated residents of Hinkley is a brash, twice divorced mother of three with a penchant for push-up bras, thigh-high skirts, and a dockworker’s vocabulary. The truth is, because of her unyielding nature, PG&E doesn’t have a prayer.

Unemployed, with dwindling savings and mounting bills, Erin Brockovich bullies her way into a file clerk’s position with a small, overworked law firm. After uncovering PG&E’s malfeasance buried in a routine real estate transaction, she bullies her boss into taking action. Like a pit bull in spiked heels, she sinks her teeth into this case, determined to see it through to its end, personally visiting with each and every claimant, getting to know them, their families, and the various illnesses they’ve contracted as a result of the industrial contamination of their water supply.

For Erin, this fight becomes a personal one. And that, of course, is what gives this movie its heart.

Julia Roberts (Notting Hill) plays Erin, an ex-beauty queen whose questionable taste in dress and language belies a quick intelligence and abundant compassion. To be honest, Erin Brockovich is not as much about the legal battle against PG&E as it is about Erin’s fight for respect and acceptance from a world which tends to judge from appearance only. Ms. Roberts is immediately appealing - humorous and attractive - despite the tacky outfits and uncensored mouth.

Albert Finney (Simpatico) is Ed Masry, an attorney almost ready for retirement after a long and arduous career in litigation. He fully understands the import of what Erin has discovered but also recognizes the limitations of his own small firm in handling it. Finally, the irrepressibility of Erin cannot be denied as he, against his professional judgment, is taken up in her passionate conviction to do what is right regardless of the personal cost.

Aaron Eckhart (Any Given Sunday) is George, Erin’s biker neighbor turned boyfriend whose own appearance is also misleading, giving Erin a lesson in quick judgments. His gentle heart and giving nature allow Erin the freedom to invest the time required to help the families of Hinkley.

Based upon a true story, (the real Erin Brockovich appears briefly as a diner waitress), the film shares many of the same themes as last year’s A Civil Action which starred John Travolta. But whereas the latter work appealed to us cerebrally, Erin Brockovich is looking for us to respond on a more visceral level. Thanks to Ms. Roberts, it largely succeeds.

Director Steven Soderbergh (The Limey) keeps Erin Brockovich out of the courtroom, focusing instead on the painstaking work that Erin, a non-lawyer, did which allowed the case to be brought to arbitration. We see repeatedly that the key to her success lay in gaining the trust of the victims who were feeling used and abused by a corporate system which did not care about them or their situation apart from whatever gain could be derived from their suffering.

Erin, by giving so completely of herself, earned their trust to the point that they would accept the help she was offering. Her motivation was not feigned or born of self-interest and the residents of Hinkley perceived and responded to that. As we reach out to people with God’s Word, offering them the help and deliverance of our heavenly Father, our motivation of heart should be as pure and our commitment level should be as strong as Erin’s.

Even as I please all men in all things, not seeking mine own profit, but the profit of many, that they may be saved. 1 Corinthians 10:33 (KJV)

Erin brought hope to an entire community. The Apostle Paul brought hope to all of Asia Minor. When we serve God with a pure heart of giving, our lives can have the same impact.
