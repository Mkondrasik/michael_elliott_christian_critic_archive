Author-verified transcription

If it were easy to make an intelligent movie thriller, the world would be filled with them instead of the mindless, explosive-happy, stunt-heavy action pictures of which we are all too familiar. We can be thankful for director Sidney Pollack (Three Days of the Condor) who excels in the genre and uses the formula once again in The Interpreter.

Silvia Broome (Nicole Kidman) has left her fictional South African homeland to put her linguistic abilities to good use as a UN interpreter. Thinking herself to be alone in the General Assembly Room one evening, she accidentally overhears an assassination plot being discussed in an obscure African dialect. Unfortunately for her, she is one of the few people who can understand it.

Even though the assassination target is Edmond Zuwanie (Earl Cameron), a visiting African dictator she holds responsible for her parents’ death and the barbaric ethnic cleansing taking place in her country, she reports the incident to the authorities. Secret Service agents Tobin Keller (Sean Penn) and Dot Woods (Catherine Keener) take charge of the investigation and find no shortage of suspects who would like to make Zuwanie pay for his crimes against humanity.

Though immediately suspicious of Silvia’s story, Keller finds himself connecting with her through their shared experiences with grief. His wife died in a car accident 28 days earlier and has returned to work in order to deal with the devastation of his loss. She has lost her family due to Zuwanie’s genocidal practices.

By giving two gifted actors such emotionally rich back stories, Pollack is able to texture his film with enough layers to fill three films. Penn is especially adept in portraying a man dealing with a crisis of global proportions while in the midst of a personal soul-shattering experience.

The movie is further helped by the setting in which it was filmed. Granted unprecedented permission to shoot in and around the UN building adds a dimension of credibility and authenticity that ratchets up the suspense.

It is said that violence begets violence and there is a degree of truth in that maxim. However, we have been taught that there is another way. The way that was taught by Jesus Christ and his followers is not always easy to perform and requires a great deal of self-discipline.

But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also. Matthew 5:39 (KJV)

It is far easier and perhaps more “natural” to strike back when struck but as one of the characters in the film states, “Vengeance is a lazy form of grief.” We should recognize that anger is an emotion that accompanies grief but to submit ourselves to that emotion is to give place in our hearts to the devil. It will not make our grief any easier to handle.

God is the ultimate judge of all men and any wrongs done to us during this life will be made right at the time of his choosing. We need not avenge ourselves. That is a promise and a truth not open to interpretation.
