Author-verified transcription

The message from the original Spy Kids movie was "keeping a family together is hard work... but worth it." The message has changed slightly in Spy Kids 2 which seems to say "putting a sequel together is hard work... and sometimes not worth it at all."

Director/writer/producer Robert Rodriguez (From Dusk Till Dawn) hastily assembled together the original cast before its young stars hit the appearance-changing reality wall of adolescence. Rodriguez copies the look of his earlier film by filling the screen with brightly colored gadgets and gizmos but was unable to reproduce the special spark between the characters that made the first film such a delight.

Carmen (Alexa Vega, Deep End of the Ocean) and Juni (Daryl Sabara, Spy Kids) Cortez are now fully-fledged members of the Spy Kids unit of the OSS. When a plot is hatched to wrest control of the OSS (not to mention the entire world), Carmen and Juni take matters into their own hands and jaunt over to the titular Island of Lost Dreams in pursuit of a fancy looking thingamabob powerful enough to destroy the planet.

They are closely followed by their snooty chief OSS rivals, Gary (Mathew O'Leary, Domestic Disturbance) and Gerti Giggles (Emily Osment, The Secret Life of Girls) who want to claim this assignment as their own. Not far behind are overprotective mom and dad agents, Gregorio (Antonio Banderas, Original Sin) and Ingrid (Carla Gugino, The One) joined by a third generation of agents, Ingrid's parents (Ricardo Montalban of Fantasy Island and Holland Taylor of Legally Blonde) who are just as overprotective and overbearing.

On the island, Carmen and Juni discover the inventor of the sought-after device. Romero (Steve Buscemi (Ghost World) is an eccentric inventor who simply wants to be left alone with his genetic inventions, of whom he is terribly frightened.

Once again, the focus is on the Cortez children as they work together to save the world. Alexa Vega and Daryl Sabara reprise their roles but the freshness is gone and the sibling rivalry that was so amusing in the original is almost nonexistent here.

Antonio Banderas and Carla Gugino as the parents are more successful in recapturing the essence (and thus energy) of their roles but they don't have much screen time to make it matter.

The inclusion of grandparents Ricardo Montalban and Holland Taylor is a good idea wasted as they have even less screen time than the others.

While there is a certain amount of flair or style left over from the original film, there is nothing else to support this movie. There is no further story or character development, Once again, style over substance proves to be a relatively empty experience.

Rodriguez seems to want to send a message, although it is poorly formed and later abandoned as the film progresses. That message is one of self reliance. "An agent is only as good as his gadgets" the characters are fond of saying, yet their assignment takes them to an island where the gadgets don't work. With high tech solutions no longer available to them, Carmen and Juni must rely on themselves, their brains and their preteen muscles to save the world.

Dependence upon man-made devices is a trap into which we all may fall. They can be a convenience but also a crutch. Dependence upon others can also lead to disappointment. Even depending upon one's self is no guarantee of success. We may be thankful that there is another option.

" But blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, whose confidence is in him. He will be like a tree planted by the water that sends out its roots by the stream. It does not fear when heat comes; its leaves are always green. It has no worries in a year of drought and never fails to bear fruit." Jeremiah 17:5-8 (KJV)
