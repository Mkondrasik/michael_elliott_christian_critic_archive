Turbo

Author-verified transcription

With Turbo, Dreamworks Animation borrows liberally and unapologetically from the Pixar films Ratatouille and Cars to churn out a derivative, formulaic movie that, while it may not be original in tone or matter, is certainly not without charm.

Theo (Ryan Reynolds) is a garden snail and though he may dream of living in the fast lane, he can’t seem to escape the simple reality that, as a snail, speed isn’t his long suit. But when an accident with a canister of nitrous oxide turbo-charges our hero, there’s nothing to slow him down from realizing his fantasy – competing in the Indianapolis 500 against his idol, the racecar driver Guy Gagne (Bill Hader).

Despite the objections of his always negative brother Chet (Paul Giamatti), Turbo sets out to set the world ablaze at an accelerated pace. He comes across Tito (Michael Pena) and Angelo (Luz Guzman), brothers who own a broken down taco stand in a nearly abandoned strip mall. Fortunately, the owners of the mall pass the time with snail races and Theo (renamed Turbo) becomes the hit of the circuit. It isn’t long before the improbability of a snail racing at Indy becomes more than a possibility. It becomes the only hope the strip mall and its owners have of continued survival.

In addition to the aforementioned actors lending their voices to the project, Turbo also features the talents of Samuel L. Jackson, Snoop Dogg, Maya Rudolph, Richard Jenkins, and Ken Jeong. While Jackson and Giamatti manage to have a little fun with their vocal performances, the others simply blend into the background without making much of an impact upon us. The colorful 3D digital animation is quite accomplished and very impressive indeed.

Like the other films it copies, Turbo provides ample opportunity to reflect on various topics also handled in the Bible: the importance of not stifling the dreams of others; persevering towards an established goal; and the first step to achieving the desires of one’s heart is to believe that the desire is possible to achieve. As Guy Gagne likes to quote, “No dream is too big; and no dreamer is too small.” There is one other unspoken element that guarantees the successful attainment of one’s dreams.

“And he said, The things which are impossible with men are possible with God.” Luke 18:27 (KJV)

Whatever the dream, no matter how unlikely it may be or unrealistic it may seem, if it is within the parameters of God’s will as outlined in His Word, it is possible to achieve. All that remains is whether we have the necessary will to extend the required effort to run the race all the way to completion. The promise God extends to us is that if we continue to run the race with Him at our side, we are assured a victory.
