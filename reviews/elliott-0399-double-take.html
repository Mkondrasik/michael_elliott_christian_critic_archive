Double Take

Author-verified transcription

Double Take is the kind of formulaic comedy cum action thriller cum buddy picture that has been seen dozens of times before in different incarnations starring seemingly interchangeable actors. Still, if sharply executed, the formula can provide for an enjoyable if easily forgettable evening's entertainment.

Daryl Chase (Orlando Jones, The Replacements) is on top of his world. As a successful investment banker with a super-model girlfriend, Harvard education, Armani suits, and a prestigious New York apartment, he garners the respect and admiration from everyone he meets. That is, until one of his clients tries to launder a large sum of ill-gotten drug money through his firm. Daryl is set up to take the fall and is soon running from every acronymous agency that exists (FBI, CIA, DEA, ASPCA...) not to mention the Mexican drug dealers who want him silenced.

After continually crossing paths with an obnoxious, fast-talking street hustler named Freddy, Daryl switches identities with him in order to flee unnoticed into Mexico where authorities are waiting to take him into protective custody. What Daryl doesn't know is that Freddy is also on the lam, wanted for the murder of a high level Mexican official. Soon the Federales are joining the chase for this unlikely partnership.

Writer/director George Gallo, who penned the 1988 "buddy picture" Midnight Run, has tried to insert enough red herrings and plot twists into his screenplay to give Double Take a quality similar to The Sting, whereby the audience is kept guessing as to who is doing what to whom. The trouble is that the plot line is so far-fetched and problematic that we really don't care to give that much thought to the film.

The only element which is of any passing interest is the sometimes humorous exchanges between the two leads.

Comedian Eddie Griffin is an energetic, hard-working, live-wire of a performer who injects the adrenalin into the production. His street-savvy style of humor fits the character of Freddy well and contrasts nicely with the button-down persona of Daryl Chase.

The talents of Orlando Jones, as Daryl, are not as showcased because of the limitations of the high class character he plays. Being genuinely snooty will not garner many laughs. But when he must adopt the "hood" persona of Freddy to carry out his impersonation, he is able to cut loose a bit and have some fun. His incredulous response (while in disguise) to a waiter who informs him that the menu does not include Colt 45 Malt Liquor is the most life his character displays in the entire film.

Double Take was inspired by a rather obscure movie called Across The Bridge which starred Rod Steiger in a dramatic role. The filmmakers recognized the potential to remake the film as a comedy. Somewhere between the inspiration and the execution, the full potential was lost. The end result is a bland retread of the kind of action/humor combination seen in Rush Hour, Blue Streak, Nothing to Lose, Money Talks and a slew of others.

Some of the humor of Double Take comes from the switch of identities between the central characters. Daryl receives a rude awakening when, dressed in the t-shirt and ghetto cap of Freddy, he is not given the same respect he was once afforded when he was "living large." Freddy rubs the lesson in by saying, "It wasn't the brother in the suit, but the suit on the brother that got you that respect."

Appearances can be deceiving in either direction. We can miss seeing the genuineness of a man's heart because we don't like his appearance. Likewise we can put our trust in a man because he appears to be genuine even though his heart may be cold and dark and evil. Instead of a man's or woman's outward appearance, which can often be misleading, what we should consider and judge is the kind of spiritual fruit present in a man's life... for that cannot lie.

Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them. Matthew 7:20 (KJV)
