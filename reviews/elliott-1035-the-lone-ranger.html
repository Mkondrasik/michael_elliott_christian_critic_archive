Author-verified transcription

“What’s with the mask?” seems to be the question of the day for the characters of Gore Verbinski’s retooling of The Lone Ranger. The fact that no one seems to be able to give a logical explanation might be a telltale sign that something is amiss with this adaptation.

In the 2013 telling of the iconic western tale, an ancient Tonto (Johnny Depp) spins a yarn quite different from the one we remember. He tells the story of an unassuming man, educated in the law, who becomes an unlikely legend of the Old West. After surviving a criminal ambush that claimed the life of his brother, John Reid (Armie Hammer) finds himself donning the mask and teaming up with a Cherokee outcast to track down and stop an unspeakable evil from doing further harm.

The fact that this Lone Ranger is a reluctant hero and that his Indian sidekick is somewhat addle-minded are among the conceptual flaws of this high-concept film. Action sequences alone (and this film has its fair share of them, including a pair of intense train battles) do not make a good film. The characterizations of the central figures needed greater delineation and, quite frankly, more heart. Ironically, the villains, as played by William Fitchner and Tom Wilkenson, are much more clearly drawn and defined than the protagonists who were set up opposite them. Perhaps had the filmmakers shown more respect and appreciation for the source material, the outcome would have had greater appeal.

Fran Striker, the original writer for the 1933 Lone Ranger radio program, built the character around a very strict moral code that remained constant through its long history. This creed was stated as follows:

I believe… • That to have a friend, a man must be one. • That all men are created equal and that everyone has within himself the power to make this a better world. • That God put the firewood there, but that every man must gather and light it himself. • In being prepared physically, mentally, and morally to fight when necessary for what is right. • That a man should make the most of what equipment he has. • That “this government of the people, by the people, and for the people” shall live always. • That men should live by the rule of what is best for the greatest number. • That sooner or later… somewhere… somehow… we must settle with the world and make payment for what we have taken. • That all things change but truth, and that truth alone, lives on forever. • In my Creator, my country, my fellow man.

These tenets belonging to The Lone Ranger can also be traced to biblical principles and truths. Had the makers of the film chosen to embrace this moral code and applied it to their remake, not only would they have remained true to the original vision of the story, they would have had a character worth building a movie around.
