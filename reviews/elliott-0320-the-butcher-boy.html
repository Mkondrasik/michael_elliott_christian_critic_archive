The Butcher Boy

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 3, 1998
Rating
2 stars

1hr : 50 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual warfare; evil influence; thought life; devil spirit possession

Author-verified transcription

It is hard to see the good in some movies. Sure, we can appreciate the skill that is apparent in the quality of the cinematography or the ability of the editor to shape and fit the various scenes to tell a particular story. We can even admire the acting or directing of a film without particularly enjoying the film itself. Such is the case with The Butcher Boy, directed by Neil Jordan (The Crying Game).

This movie tells the story of Francie Brady (Eamonn Owens), a highly imaginative and personable young man growing up in a small Irish town with an alcoholic father and mentally unstable mother. In such a dysfunctional environment, it is not hard to see why the boy begins to develop his own set of emotional problems.

Newcomer Mr. Owens was picked for the role after an extensive talent search prompted over 40,000 applicants. His winning charisma shines throughout the film even as his character ventures deeper and deeper into the darker side of "human nature."

Steven Rea (The Crying Game) plays his alcoholic Da as well as serving as the older Francie who narrates the film. It is within his narration that the cleverness of the screenwriters (Patrick McCabe, upon whose novel this movie was based, and Neil Jordan) becomes evident. The wit and amusing insight of the narration helps to curb the revulsion we should be feeling as we watch the downward development of what could have been an outstanding young man capable of making so many contributions to his society and community.

Fiona Shaw (The Avengers) plays the uptight and overly proper Mrs. Nugent who becomes the target of young Francie's misplaced wrath. Alan Boyle is Joe Purcell, Francie's best friend and blood brother who struggles at first to understand his friend's behavior only to later distance himself from it.

And, in a casting decision which in all likelihood did not exactly thrill the Roman Catholics of the world, Sinead O'Connor makes an appearance as none other than the Virgin Mary who Francie begins to see in visions a la Lourdes.

As we watch Francie spiraling downward, it would be wise to remember that life is spiritual. A senses man might look at this film and find ways to excuse Francie's progression into anti-social (and later criminal) behavior by saying that he is understandably reacting to an unhealthy childhood environment or that he is subconsciously crying out for help.

A spiritual man or woman sees it differently. We know that there is an unseen, spiritually evil force at work in this world. Thanks to Jesus Christ, we know a little about how it functions and operates. Without the proper parental control and protection over his life, Francie became susceptible to devil spirit infiltration. As Francie continued to obey the evil suggestions, the spirits controlling or influencing him became bolder and more in number.

That's how our spiritual adversary works. He marshals his forces and sends them out looking for weakness. Once a weakness is found, he exploits it and continues to expand his influence until, if unchecked, he can dominate an individual's life and actions. This is why God repeatedly warns us to guard our minds and not conform to the world.

By numbering the various devilish manifestations that can be seen in the actions or experiences of young Francie, we can see the progression:

1) Lying; 2) Stealing; 3) Depression; 4) Envy; 5) Obsession; 6) Filthiness; 7) Child Molestation; 8) Alcoholism; 9) Suicide; 10) Murder.

These attributes have nothing to do with God. They mark the presence of the evil one.

A movie about how an individual opens his mind and life to devil spirit possession may be educational, but it can never be uplifting. For all the artistic merit that The Butcher Boy may possess, it remains a dark, disturbing and spiritually unedifying film.

--Michael Elliott--
