Recess: School's Out

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 18, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Running time
1 hr: 22 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 13:11Proverbs 22:15Colossians 3:20-21

Sermon / spiritual topic: Childishness; Imagination; Youth

Author-verified transcription

I was ten years old, playing basketball by myself in the backyard. Shooting foul shots can be boring but the imagination of a ten year doesn't tolerate boredom long. Suddenly, I felt the world's eyes upon me as my mind concocted the scenario. An alien race is ready to blast the Earth with their destruction beam but if I sink this foul shot they will agree to leave in peace...

There isn't a ten year boy old alive who hasn't entertained himself at one point or another with childish fantasies and made-up stories of derring-do. Disney's new animated film, Recess: School's Out, appeals to that childish imagination inside each one of us.

Based upon the popular TV show, Disney's Recess, the film follows the antics of the fourth graders of Third Street School as they prepare themselves for the ultimate recess... Summer Vacation!

T.J. Detweiler (Andy Lawrence, Jack Frost) has it all mapped out - a summer filled with nothing but playing with his friends. That is until his friends drop the bombshell... Each one of them is going away to camp for the summer. T.J. is left behind with nothing to do and no one to do it with.

Then, riding his bike past the supposedly empty school, he sees a mysterious green glow and a ray gun emerging from the roof. When his parents and the police refuse to listen to him, T.J. enlists the help of Principal Prickly (Dabney Coleman, Inspector Gadget) who promptly dematerializes as soon as he approaches the school.

Frantic, T.J. calls his friends back from camp and they begin the ultimate kid experience... battling the bad guys to save their school, the world, and most importantly... summer vacation.

Producers Paul Germain and Joe Ansolabehere have taken great care to develop unique characters which are real and identifiable. They have also not lost that feeling of "anything can happen" which children, in their youthful exuberance, seem to collect in great abundance.

The relationship between the teachers and the students is also delightfully depicted. At first, they appear to be natural enemies, with the children playing harmless pranks in defiance of the seemingly overbearing and humorless adults. But as soon as an outside force threatens the school, they discover that they have more in common than they thought and what's more, they actually care about one another.

The animation used betrays the Saturday morning TV origins of the project but the narrative is so strong and the vocal talent so well cast that we are quickly caught up in the story and the characters. Some of the humorous references may go over the heads of the children but will bring smiles of fond recollections to their parents. Other bits, such as a pack of wild kindergartners who send the evil ninjas fleeing in terror, will amuse everybody. Though it runs a tad bit too long for some attention spans, Recess: School's Out is still an agreeable family picture that contains a little something for everyone.

What I most liked about this film is that it embraces the unique childlike imagination to which we can all relate because each one of us was, at some time, a child. Imagination, properly channeled, can be a wonderful thing. Today's imagination can be tomorrow's invention. Obviously, parents must keep their children in check because "foolishness is bound in the heart of a child." But they may do so in ways that will not stifle the child's God-given enthusiasm for life. We need not be in such a hurry to have our children grow up. There is a natural order to life which God has established.

When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things. 1 Corinthians 13:11 (KJV)

Our children deserve the opportunity to be children. By the way, since Earth is still in one piece, it should be obvious that I made the foul shot. Good thing the aliens allowed me to go best three out of five.
