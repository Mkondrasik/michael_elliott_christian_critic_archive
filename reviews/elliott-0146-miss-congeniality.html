Miss Congeniality

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 22, 2000
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 7:241 Samuel 16:7Matthew 23:27

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Judgment; appearances; discernment; acceptance; seeing beyond the surface

Author-verified transcription

An FBI agent, best described as a tomboy who never grew into her femininity, is assigned to infiltrate the Miss United States beauty pageant... as a contestant. Such is the one joke premise that serves as the structural foundation for Miss Congeniality.

Sandra Bullock (28 Days) stars as Grace Hart, a snorting, slovenly, kickboxing FBI special agent who has been trying so hard to fit in as one of the guys, she never saw much point to acting like one of the girls. When an investigation of a terrorist bombing spree leads the FBI to the upcoming pageant, Grace's patriotic duty is to subject herself to the makeover of the century and enter the contest as Miss New Jersey.

Pageant consultant Vic Melling (Michael Caine, The Cider House Rules) is saddled with the next to impossible task of turning this gun-toting, gum-popping caterpillar into a butterfly in stiletto heels. Everything from hair to toenails must be redone in a mere matter of hours. Even her posture and poise needs major work. "I haven't seen a walk like that since Jurassic Park," Vic complains cattily. He is soon singing a different tune because when his handiwork is completed, Grace has been transformed into a vision of armed loveliness.

The central story of a mysterious bomber on the loose, targeting a nationally televised beauty pageant is beyond absurd and serves only to place the ungraceful Grace in awkward and potentially embarrassing situations. The male members of the FBI investigative team are little more than sexist stereotypes. Their job seems to consist only of inventing politically incorrect labels for the various aspects of the case. Grace's pageant name becomes "Freebush" and the undercover surveillance is known as "Operation Thong." The only time these agents appear halfway alert is when Grace takes the hidden camera into the contestants' dressing area.

As juvenile as this sounds, and much of the humor is very childish, there is a saving grace. Sandra Bullock's slapstick performance is game enough to keep us from totally dismissing the film as pure nonsense. She's simply fun to watch... even in a film as slight as this one.

Michael Caine adds to the fun with his performance as a demanding perfectionist specializing in winning beauty contests. His sharply critical observations, sarcastically delivered, elicit some of the biggest laughs of the film.

Benjamin Bratt (Red Planet), as the FBI team leader and love interest for Grace, fills his suit nicely and delivers his lines on time, which is just about all we could ask of someone in such a shallow role. Candice Bergen (TV's Murphy Brown) and William Shatner (Star Trek) as the pageant's organizer and host, give wooden and hackneyed portrayals of characters for whom these descriptive phrases fit perfectly.

Director Donald Petrie (My Favorite Martian), working from a script by Marc Lawrence (Forces of Nature) manages to keep the mood light without descending into the depths of banality which is where this film could have easily landed. Wisely, they have chosen to avoid the most obvious of low humor targets by treating the Miss United States entrants with, if not respect, at least a lack of disdain.

In fact, Grace, who has never been able to find a peer group to accept her, surprises herself by responding to the fellowship which develops among the pageant contestants during the course of the competition. As they welcome her into their "family," she finds herself needing to reevaluate the negative preconceptions she has always carried towards the women who entered these kinds of "scholarship programs" (AKA beauty contests.)

Appearances can be deceiving. Judging books by their covers will often cause us to miss the greatness or potential for greatness that may lie within.

Stop judging by mere appearances, and make a right judgment." John 7:24 (NIV)

Whenever we dismiss someone for being too beautiful or not beautiful enough, we lose. There is so much that lies beneath the surface appearance of a man or woman. Only God can read the hearts and thoughts of man. The rest of us must take the time and demonstrate enough interest to inquire before we can discern the quality of someone else's life.
