Reindeer Games

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 25, 2000
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 2:9Matthew 6:34Ephesians 4:28

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: God's provision; the future; contentment; theft; living in the present

Author-verified transcription

Remember when you could count on holiday movies to be either comedies or romances? They were, for the most part, the sweet and simple "feel-good" films designed for family entertainment. Reindeer Games is technically a holiday movie but seeing as it opens with the bloody aftermath of a Santa Claus massacre, we'd be hard pressed to call it "cheery."

Ben Affleck (Dogma) plays Rudy, a car thief serving out what remains of a five year prison sentence. His plans, once on the outside, are simple: A mug of hot chocolate, a piece of pecan pie, and watching football games on TV with his Dad. His roommate, Nick (James Frain, Titus), who is also being released, has slightly different plans. He has struck up a pen pal correspondence with Ashley (Charlize Theron, The Cider House Rules), a beautiful girl who will be waiting for him outside the prison with keys to a nearby motel room.

On the eve of their release, during the confusion of a cafeteria food fight, Nick is attacked and brutally stabbed by a fellow prisoner looking for revenge. Rudy, after a brief wrestling bout with his conscience, decides that Nick's plans were infinitely better than his own so, once free, he assumes his ex-roommate's identity, getting the girl and a passel of troubles to boot.

Ashley's brother, Gabriel (Gary Sinise, Snake Eyes) AKA "Monster" has heard all about Nick from his sister. Especially interesting to him was Nick's two year employment as a guard at The Tomahawk, an Indian casino which has laughable security and lots of cash. Gabriel pays a surprise visit to the motel hideaway to force Nick/Rudy to help him and his ho-ho-holdup men rob the casino.

Of course Rudy knows nothing about The Tomahawk other than what Nick had mentioned to him behind bars. To stay alive, Rudy must improvise, bluff and otherwise stay one step ahead of his "partners" in crime. It might not be as hard as it first appears. We find that with these crooks, intelligence is not one of their strongest qualities.

The same could be said of the film itself. It is not a movie that gets better upon contemplation. Director John Frankenheimer (Ronin) uses his skill and adeptness at keeping the action moving and keeping our interest engaged but, in the end, the implausibility of the plot and the gaping holes in the logic win out.

Screenwriter Ehren Kruger (Arlington Road) has peppered the script with some witty and genuinely amusing one-liners, but he has failed to encase them in the strong framework of a believable story.

Mr. Sinise plays the smartest dumb villain to be seen in movies this year. He exhibits the intensity and threatening manner needed without losing the believable gullibility of the character.

Mr. Affleck works hard against his boyish nice guy image but doesn't quite succeed in portraying a veteran thief and criminal. Still, he is affable as the male lead and as his only real goal is to go home, forget the mistakes he made, and start his life again, we find ourselves rooting for him.

We all should have the desire to better ourselves - to improve our lives and standard of living. But not in the way these characters choose. As one of the thieves put it: "If you want a future, you have to stand up and steal it."

That is a direct contradiction of the Word and an insult to the God Who provides for all our needs. We know we already have a future because God has prepared it for us. While we do need to stand up, it is not to steal... but rather to claim what is rightfully ours and given to us by God.

But as it is written, Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared for them that love him. I Corinthians 2:9 [KJV]

Our future is assured. Our energies should be focused on the present. Rudy's initial thought was the proper one: A mug of hot chocolate, a piece of pecan pie, and spending time with his family. Take care of the needs of today and tomorrow will be much easier to face.
