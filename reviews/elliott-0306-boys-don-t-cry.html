Boys Don't Cry

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 22, 1999
Rating
Not recommended
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Corinthians 10:5Ephesians 4:18-23Proverbs 3:5-8

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Truth; feelings; thought life; discernment

Author-verified transcription

The promotional tag line for the film Boys Don't Cry is very misleading. By declaring the movie to be "a true story about finding the courage to be yourself," its promoters merely show how cavalierly Hollywood will twist perception, with little regard to reality or truth.

If this story about a young girl barely out of her teens who disguises herself as a man and lives her lie in a small town some 70 miles from her home is about "being yourself," then Mission to Mars is about the chocolate industry. The fact that Boys Don't Cry is based on a true story may heighten the tragic nature of the movie, but don't mistake this tragedy for an inspirational tale of courage. Because it isn't one.

Hilary Swank (The Next Karate Kid) stars as Teena Brandon from Lincoln, Texas, whose "sexual identity crisis" causes her to closely crop her hair, strap her chest down, stuff a sock in her jeans, and call herself Brandon Teena. As Brandon, she enters the small town Texas social scene, drinking beers, and looking for girls to seduce.

After meeting and befriending a handful of smaller town trailer trash refugees, Brandon wakes up to find herself in Falls City, Nebraska where she meets Lana (Chloë Sevigny, The Last Days of Disco), who will eventually respond to her/his charms... Much to the detriment of them both.

The acting has been widely recognized as being of the highest quality. Ms. Swank and Ms. Sevigny have both been nominated for Oscars in the actress and supporting actress categories. Their co-stars also provide very naturalistic portrayals of a band of small town losers whose personalities have long been defined by the booze they swill and the company they keep.

Yet, for this reviewer, the quality of the acting does not redeem the completely unedifying nature of the story being told. It was simply excruciating to sit through and I cannot in good conscience recommend it for viewing.

Director Kimberly Peirce (The Last Good Breath) is uncompromisingly direct in filming both the brutality and heavy sexuality of the story line. Anyone who remembers the news reports of this incident which took place in 1993 will recall that the crowd with whom Teena associated did not react with compassionate understanding to her sexual ruse. Her subsequent rape and murder was unconscionable, carried out by a pair of animals who deserve the harshest punishment our society is allowed to give.

But the fact that she was a victim of such heinous violence does not make her actions brave. When asked how she evolved from Teena Brandon to Brandon Teena, she responded that she started out as a normal girl, then reinvented herself as a boyish girl, and kept going until she found something that "felt right."

With no disrespect intended to the late Ms. Brandon, it takes little courage to go with what feels right. It also takes no thought. Our feelings are probably the worse litmus test we could use to judge proper or appropriate action. What Ms. Brandon needed... indeed, what we all need... is a source of truth outside of ourselves. A standard to which we may hold ourselves, ensuring that we are not deceiving our own selves because of our feelings, emotional responses, or individual perceptions.

Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ; 2 Corinthians 10:5 (KJV)

Ms. Brandon's "feelings" led her to lie (to herself and others), steal, and engage in homosexual relations. They brought to her violence, depression, enmity, and eventual death at the all too young age of 21. These are qualities associated with our spiritual adversary. An adversary which is merciless and unforgiving but conquerable if we arm ourselves with the power of God resident in His Word of Truth. To stand upon that truth in the face of our enemy despite how we may "feel" is a truer test of courage.
