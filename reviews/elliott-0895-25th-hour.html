Author-verified transcription

We live in a cause and effect world. Actions bring consequences and some are worse than others. Spike Lee's 25th Hour tells the story of a man about to face the consequences for his criminal acts.

Edward Norton (Red Dragon) stars as Monty Brogan, a drug dealer who discovers he waited too long to quit an ugly business. When DEA agents arrest him for possession and trafficking in narcotics, Monty realizes that he'll be looking at a very long and unpleasant time behind bars. He's got one last day as a free man before he has to turn himself in.

Wandering through a post 9/11 New York, Monty ponders his fate even as it draws inexorably closer. Joining him for a final farewell soiree are his childhood buddies, Jakob (Philip Seymour Hoffman, Punch-Drunk Love), now a disillusioned and insecure high school teacher, and Frank (Barry Pepper, Knockaround Guys), a hot shot, risk-taking stock broker. They, along with Monty's live-in girlfriend Naturelle (Rosario Dawson, The Adventures of Pluto Nash) and Mary D'Annunzio (Anna Paquin, X-Men), one of Jakob's nubile young students, spend Monty's last free night at a club owned by the drug kingpin for whom Monty worked.

Director Spike Lee (Do The Right Thing) expects us to overlook Monty's drug dealing history to sympathize with his plight. In the very first scene, he shows Monty saving an injured pooch, whom he later adopts as his own. It is as if Lee wants this one act of kindness to serve as an absolution of some kind for Monty.

Edward Norton puts forth an intelligent, introspective study of a man who made the wrong choice in life and is realizing it too late to undo the consequences of that choice. His story is the most compelling although we never really connect with his character.

Barry Pepper crackles with energy but is essentially a plot point rather than a fully fleshed out character. Philip Seymour Hoffman has more of a subplot working for his character but it is a rather unpleasant one having to do with his growing sexual desire towards one of his underage students.

This may be the most mainstream accessible picture to come out of Spike Lee since Do The Right Thing in 1989. Still, it is a flawed film. Lee indulges his artistic muses with a 5 minute profanity filled rant delivered by Norton in which his character blames the various multicultural aspects of the city for his downfall before he turns his venomous tongue upon himself.

25th Hour is the first major production to use post 9/11 images as part of its narrative. If only Lee had something more profound to say. A scene which overlooked the recovery operation at "ground zero," was a wasted opportunity. It is only one of many elements to be mishandled.

There is really no sense of the clock ticking down a la High Noon; the characters are stereotypical and largely uninteresting; and the question of who ratted Monty out to the DEA agents was not a burning issue even though it seems it should have been.

But through it all, there remains one message that was unmistakable... And it comes straight from the scriptures:

Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting. Galatians 6:7-8 (KJV)

Forgiveness and redemption are always available thanks to the grace of God. But we shouldn't delude ourselves to think that we might escape the consequences of wrongful actions. The actions Monty took, regardless of his motives, were wrong and harmful to others (though we do not see the negative impact of his drug dealing save for one brief scene with an ex-client). His illegal and immoral actions may have brought him immediate earthly rewards but such "fruit" is not long-lasting and will be followed by many regrets.
