The Big Kahuna

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 19, 2000

3 stars

1 hr: 30 min

Published Web Version

Sermon / spiritual topic: Christian witness; work; evangelism; integrity; conduct

Author-verified transcription

The small, character driven film, THE BIG KAHUNA, asks the deep philosophical question, "Is it possible to swim with the sharks and still be a fisher of men?" The film may not come up with the final answer, but it certainly gives us plenty to ponder and discuss once the movie ends.

The "kahuna el grande" in question is Mr. Fuller, the president of a major corporation whose firm uses copious amounts of industrial lubricants. Attending a business convention in Wichita, Kansas populated by industrial lubricant salesmen, Mr. Fuller is a hot commodity and anticipated guest of those who have set up hospitality suites to meet and greet any and all who may one day want to buy their product.

Among them are three men who have traveled to Wichita for the expressed purpose to meet Mr. Fuller and convince him to do business with their firm. They are Phil (Danny Devito, Drowning Mona), a tired 52 year old marketing rep who is on the downhill slope of his career and contemplating life's meanings in existential terms; Larry (Kevin Spacey, American Beauty), his long-term cynical and brutally honest partner who defines his life by the way he succeeds at his job; and Bob (Peter Facinelli, Can't Hardly Wait), a young, newly married man who is attending his first business conference but maintains a personal priority higher than the business he was sent to conduct.

You can be assured that any story built around the waiting for a title character (Waiting For Godot, The Iceman Cometh) is not going to be about that character... but rather those who are waiting for him.

In this instance, it is Bob's belief structure as a born-again evangelistic Christian that stands at the core and serves as the catalyst for all discussion.

The topics raised in those discussions include marriage, pornography, friendship, God, and the difference between selling something and conversing with someone. It is the latter topic which has the most impact upon us as it is used to make a spiritual point.

THE BIG KAHUNA marks the directorial debut for John Swanbeck, who originally directed a staged reading of the play on which it is based. He sent the script to Kevin Spacey, whom he met while working on the stage production of Hurlyburly. Mr. Spacey decided to use it to launch his new production company, Trigger St. Productions. Amazingly, the movie was filmed in a mere sixteen days.

Mr. Spacey has the showiest role as Larry. Sarcasm dripping from his smirking lips, he delivers his lines with a rapid fire staccato. Glib and smooth talking, he is a salesman on top of his game being forced to consider spiritual matters he has long avoided. Mr. Devito, has a quieter, more introspective role which he uses to turn in one of the best performances of his career. As Phil, he sees his career as having peaked and is now questioning the overall meaning of his life. It is a delicate yet meaty portrayal.

Mr. Facinelli more than holds his own with these two film veterans. He accurately captures the conviction of a true believer along with the tinge of condescension that often is included with the zeal of youthful certainty.

It is not Bob's conviction or belief in the scriptures that causes the film's conflict. Those qualities are rather begrudgingly admired by the other two characters who just may be beginning their own spiritual quests. Rather it is the manner in which he conducts his evangelistic pursuits that they find inappropriate. And rightly so.

Bob, who is apt to quote Bible verses to support his arguments, may have forgotten this one:

And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men; Colossians 3:23 (KJV)

Our witness, as believing Christians, extends beyond the words we speak. How we act or conduct ourselves is oftentimes more telling. Throughout the scriptures we are exhorted to work heartily as unto the Lord. When hired by an employer, we are to give him 100% of our time, energy and efforts during our work hours. We should perform our jobs heartily, as if we were working for Christ himself.

To be the best employee that is in us to be is its own witness. For when people approach us to ask us why we work so hard (and they will), we can answer them honestly, "Because God tells us to."
