Author-verified transcription

Iron Man returns to the screen following his intergalactic adventures in The Avengers and we can see that the weight of the near global catastrophe has taken its toll on Tony Stark (Robert Downey Jr.). In Iron Man 3, our hero suffers from insomnia, anxiety attacks, and a new threat, courtesy of a ghost from his past.

As the U.S. is held hostage by a terrorist named Mandarin (Ben Kingsley), Stark must confront his own fears and insecurities. Just what makes him a “super-hero?” Is it merely the suit? Or is it the man who wears the suit? He must discover that quickly before the Mandarin destroys everything he has built and everyone that he cares about.

Downey spends more time out of the iron costume than in it in this film resulting in more of a character-driven action adventure. While there is plenty of CGI-candy to make the franchise purists happy (notably the mid-air rescue of the passengers of an exploding airliner), the real story is in keeping with the flesh and blood characters.

Gwyneth Paltrow returns as Pepper Potts, Stark’s girlfriend and CEO of Stark Enterprises, and even gets an opportunity to wear the iconic suit. Guy Pearce shows up as a fellow inventor that Stark once shunned, now working on DNA manipulation. Don Cheadle reprises his role as Colonel James Rhodes AKA Iron Patriot when he puts on one of Stark’s suits appropriately designed in a red white and blue motif. Jon Favreau, who relinquishes his director’s seat to Shane Black, returns to play Happy Hogan, newly appointed chief of security for Stark’s company.

Stark’s internalized dilemma is not uncommon. Everybody ends up wearing many “hats” during their lifetime. We take on the role of child, student, friend, and employee. Many of us go on to become spouses, parents, employers and more.

There may be times in our lives when we feel unequipped for the role we’re asked to take. New parents can certainly experience this as the responsibility for the child that they just brought into the world begins to sink into their consciousness. Employees promoted into a new and unfamiliar position can become insecure in their abilities to handle their new roles.

One of the “hats” we are privileged to wear is that of an ambassador for Christ. But the act of speaking for God and teaching or leading people to a relationship with the Lord can be a daunting responsibility. We can certainly be excused for thinking we may not be up to the task. God disagrees and fortunately has an answer for us.

“Resolve and settle it in your minds not to meditate and prepare beforehand how you are to make your defense and how you will answer. For I [Myself] will give you a mouth and such utterance and wisdom that all of your foes combined will be unable to stand against or refute.” Luke 21:14-15 (Amplified)

As we continue to walk in faith, doing the will of our Father, we may be faced with opportunities that we are ill prepared to handle. God tells us not to be anxious but to trust in Him. As we remain peaceful and do the best that we know, God promises us a victory and will work within us and stand behind us until it is achieved.
