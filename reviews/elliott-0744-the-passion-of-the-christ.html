It seems like we've been talking about The Passion of the Christ for months. And many of us have. We have all read the articles and seen the cute "wordplay" headlines: "The Greatest Story Ever Sold;" "The Gospel According to Mel;" "Christ In All His Gory," etc.

But now, as the film begins to open in theaters nationwide, we have the opportunity to look beyond the hype and past the media spin to see for ourselves what we can draw from this film. It is, in a word, powerful.

Beginning from his prayer in the garden of Gethsemane, the film depicts in graphic detail the arrest, the torture and the execution of Jesus - carried out by the brutal Roman soldiers but endorsed by the religious leaders of Jerusalem.

There have been complaints that the film is too violent - too gory - too bloody. I agree. It is too violent, too gory and too bloody. It is not easy to watch - especially if you love God and His son. But it is true to fact. His actual "trial" and execution was also too violent, too bloody and too gory. If we read the scriptures we would know that the prophecy says that he:

"became an object of horror: many were astonished at him. His face and his whole appearance were marred more than any man's, and his form beyond that of the sons of men." Isaiah 52:14 (Amplified)

We, as people, have a tendency to downplay the unpleasant facts of life. Jesus paid the price for our sins and that price was higher than any of us could possibly imagine. Mr. Gibson, in his film, depicted a fraction of what Jesus had to endure.

There has also been a great deal of controversy regarding the anti-Semitic influences of the film. The question of who "killed Christ" is really immaterial. First of all, Christ laid down his life for us. Nobody "killed" him. He gave his life willingly. He had the power to escape at any time.

Secondly, arguing between whether it was the Romans or the Judeans that were responsible for carrying out his execution is a moot point. Scriptures clearly state that had the "princes of this world" (a figurative reference to the devil spirit realm) knew, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.

Jesus' life extended beyond our earthly understanding. There was a spiritual reality taking place behind the scenes. His execution was orchestrated by devilish influences which were able to deceive and manipulate people to do their bidding. This is something that is represented in The Passion of the Christ. But the devil did not understand the implications of his actions.

Which none of the princes of this world knew: for had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. 1 Corinthians 2:8 (KJV)

The devil erred. And because of that, and because of the sacrifice that Christ made, we have the ability to become "born again of God's spirit."

Mel Gibson is an accomplished filmmaker - one with an obvious and established artistic vision. God bless him. There are minor points with which I might find disagreement. But the overall thrust of the film - what Jesus Christ had to do to redeem us - I can find no fault.

There is a twofold part to the sacrifice of Christ. He died for our sins, yes. We, who have made Jesus our Lord and believed in our heart that God raised him from the dead, we have eternal life. But in addition to that, scriptures state that:

"he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed." Isaiah 53:5 (KJV)

We have the ability - the power - and the God-given right to claim physical wholeness in our day because of what Jesus Christ endured. "By his stripes we are healed."

We need not suffer physical maladies. Christ suffered for us. He endured the pain so that we might be delivered. As we believe in him, we receive the deliverance of God, whose power knows no equal.
