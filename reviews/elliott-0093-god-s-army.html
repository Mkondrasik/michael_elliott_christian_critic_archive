God's Army

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 10, 2000
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 3:4-7Galatians 6:3Colossians 2:8

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Teaching; humility; spiritual leadership; God gives the increase

Author-verified transcription

In “God’s Army,” the soldiers are not wearing camouflaged khaki but starched white collared shirts and solid colored ties. They aren’t carrying AK-47s. Instead their “weapons” are Bibles and religious pamphlets. And their battlefield isn’t a dense forest or a barren desert terrain, but rather the cities and suburbs of America.

Written, directed, and starring Richard Dutcher, the low-budget “God’s Army” takes a look at a group of people that few bother to get to know... the missionaries of the Church of Latter Day Saints. These men and women choose to devote two years of their lives to the proselytizing of their faith and religion. According to the film, they see themselves as spiritual warriors, battling and fighting on the side of God and Jesus Christ for the souls of the people who have not yet been baptized. Their morning exhortation to one another is, “Let’s do some good.”

Seen through the eyes of the novice Elder Allen (male Mormon missionaries refer to themselves as “elder,” females as “sister”), played by newcomer Matthew Brown, the film smacks of religious propaganda as he moves from doubtful follower to adamant believer. His development is helped along by the dogmatically secure 29 year old Elder Dalton (Richard Dutcher, whose only other film credit was the 1990 movie, “Tripwire,” where he played a delivery boy.) Because of his unusually advanced age Dalton is affectionately referred to as “Pops.”

The film takes us through the daily routines of missionaries on the field: Study time. Door to door testifying. Following up with contacts made. Practical joking. The latter is something which is done with great regularity. A favorite source of hilarity is taking a photograph of a fellow missionary when he’s sitting on the toilet. Who knew that Mormon missionaries could be so comically inspired?

But seriously, despite the blatant propagandizing which makes this film difficult to recommend, it does bring a number of things to the table worthy to be examined. Not least of all the fact that there are few other films playing in theaters today that will mention God and Jesus Christ in the same worshipful tones as this film does or depict men and women of faith who commit themselves wholeheartedly to prayer and study without being condescending.

In addition, the missionaries continually wrestle with their faith. Mr. Dutcher is not afraid to portray an opposing viewpoint to Mormon beliefs by including a character who, while on the mission field, continually reads the books and articles which denounce elements of the LDS belief structure and the history of the church itself. Oddly enough, as he shares with his other missionaries the flaws he is discovering in their religion, the explanations given to dismiss those detractors are insufficient to dispel the doubts which are raised in the minds of the audience.

The doctrinal error of Mormonism aside, there are many biblically based truths reflected in the film which are great reminders to anyone who uses the Bible as the source of truth. Confronting the newcomer who is uneasy knocking on doors of strangers, Elder Dalton asks him, “How many people are you going to convert in two years?” Elder Allen throws out a conservative number which Dalton quickly rejects. “Wrong! We don’t convert anybody. That’s between them and God. All we do is teach.”

For the most part, that’s all any of us can do for one another. Teach with meekness and humility. When man’s ego enters into the picture, God has to leave it. The human condition being what it is, there is a natural tendency to elevate a man, especially one who is teaching the Word of God, to a level which supersedes the power of God. The apostle Paul had to argue this point to believers who were going astray in their thinking:

For while one saith, I am of Paul; and another, I am of Apollos; are ye not carnal? Who then is Paul, and who is Apollos, but ministers by whom ye believed, even as the Lord gave to every man? I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase. 1 Corinthians 3:4-7 (KJV)
