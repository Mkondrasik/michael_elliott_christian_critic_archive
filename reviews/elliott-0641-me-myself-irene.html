Author-verified transcription

The problem with "shock humor" is that most of the time, once the shock wears off, all we are left with is the awareness that what we've just seen is not really that funny. Me Myself & Irene is an example of a film containing an embarrassment of riches... if one considers prosthetic sexual devices, toilet humor, and constant references to the anal cavity to be comic gems.

We should have seen the offensiveness coming from a mile away. The film was directed by the Farrelly Brothers (There's Something About Mary) and stars Jim Carrey (Ace Ventura, Pet Detective), neither of whom are particularly known for their restraint or self-censorship. In Me Myself & Irene, they don't just push the envelope of good taste, they fold, staple and mutilate it.

Mr. Carrey stars as Charlie Baileygates, a Rhode Island state trooper so mild-mannered that "milquetoast" is too aggressive a term to describe him. His wife dumps him for a diminutive black limo driver, leaving Charlie to raise their three black sons by himself. The townspeople do not respect him or the uniform he wears. Finally, after years of humiliation and abusive treatment, he snaps and manifests a second personality.

Hank is as obnoxiously aggressive as Charlie is passive. Crude, hateful, over sexed and incapable of self-control, Hank is the instigator of most of the outrageous actions in the film. But because he is a schizoid personality, the filmmakers apparently believe that no consequences to his illegal and immoral actions are merited.

When Charlie is asked to escort Irene (Renee Zellweger, Jerry Maguire) to upstate New York so she may answer to bogus charges her ex-boyfriend has filed, Hank comes along for the ride. What ensues is a very weird romantic triangle with Mr. Carrey playing two sides of the isosceles.

There is no denying that Mr. Carrey has highly developed comic abilities. If only he could learn to use his talents on more edifying material. Whether wrestling a cow that refuses to die or trying to redirect a wayward stream of urine, his physical contortions and degree of intensity amuse us at the same time the outrageous conditions which cause those contortions repel us.

Renee Zellweger, plays straight woman in the midst of the comic zaniness surrounding her in much the same way as Cameron Diaz's turn in There's Something About Mary. Unfortunately she doesn't offer enough of a sweet or innocent presence to rise above the crassness of the material.

As the trio of black geniuses who are being raised by single "dad," Charlie, Anthony Anderson (Romeo Must Die), Jerod Mixon (Bulworth), and Mongo Brownlee (Con Air) interact with each other as seamlessly as the veteran comedy troupes of old. That they do so by utilizing foul-mouthed street lingo associated with their ethnicity is part of the comic concept.

Mental illness has often been featured in films, both as a source of comedy as well as drama. Rarely, however, is it depicted from a scriptural or spiritual viewpoint. God's will for our lives is that we prosper and be in health. The devil's will for our lives is just the opposite. Jesus Christ understood this unseen conflict and was able to minister healing to those who were afflicted with all types of illnesses, including those affecting the mind.

And his fame went throughout all Syria: and they brought unto him all sick people that were taken with divers diseases and torments, and those which were possessed with devils, and those which were lunatick, and those that had the palsy; and he healed them. Matthew 4:24 (KJV)

The power of God knows no equal. Nothing in the devil's bag of evil tricks can withstand the manifested power of God administered by a believing man or woman. This truth may not be as "funny" as the film's showing us a man with poultry protruding from his posterior, but it surely is more beneficial.
