The Way, Way Back

Sermon / spiritual topic: Words; Encouragement; Parenting; Building Others Up

Author-verified transcription

Growing up can be hard. It can be even harder when those who are meant to help guide us have kind of lost their own way. In The Way Way Back, 14-year-old Duncan is left to figure life out on his own. And it looks like he’s got just one summer in which to do it.

Duncan (Liam James) is none too happy about having to spend the summer with Mom (Toni Collette) and her new boyfriend (Stephen Carrell), who just happens to be a bit of a jerk, at the jerk’s beach house. He’s miserable and makes no bones about letting it show.

As the grown-ups succumb to the libatious temptations of this summer resort town, Duncan is left with no friends and with nothing to do. That is, until he stumbles across Owen (Sam Rockwell), a free-spirited water park employee who becomes the most important and influential figure in Duncan’s young life.

The film starts very slowly and with surprisingly little humor but those who stay with it will find themselves connecting with the story and characters in a real and rewarding way. Much of the success must be laid at the feet of the very talented Sam Rockwell. His character’s playful nature, exuberance and zest for life are contagious, infecting not only Duncan but the entire picture with an unmistakable and irresistible energy.

Early in the film, Carrell’s character callously tags Duncan as being a “three” on a rating scale of one to ten. It is a comment thoughtlessly made and has a terrible effect on an already emotionally troubled child. God exhorts us to be more aware of the impact our words can have and choose the ones that will bless or benefit those to whom we deliver them.

“Do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building others up according to their needs, that it may benefit those who listen.” Ephesians 4:29 (NIV)

At the beginning of the film Duncan’s needs were obvious and apparent to all. Sullen, morose, obviously troubled… he needed help. Someone to care enough to talk to him; listen to him; engage with him. The man who was vying to be his stepfather was only successful in driving Duncan deeper into a sense of isolation and loneliness. It was Owen, through his good-natured and genuine kindness, who managed to bring Duncan out of his shell and introduced him to a better world. It is important to note that for all of Owen’s joking around, nothing that he did… no action that he took, was ever mean-spirited or worked to the detriment of any other person.
