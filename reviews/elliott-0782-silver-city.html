Author-verified transcription

When Colorado's leading gubernatorial candidate Dickie Pilage (Chris Cooper) reels in a corpse during a fly fishing photo op, his campaign manager (Richard Dreyfuss) suspects political foul play and hires an investigator to look into the matter. However, despite the film's structure and story line, it appears that writer/director John Sayles is more interested in making political commentaries than he is in crafting a murder mystery.

Investigator (and disgraced journalist) Danny O'Brien (Danny Huston) quickly finds that he is over his head and embroiled in such hot button issues as illegal immigration, migrant workers, environmental conservation, the influence of political lobbyists and yellow journalism. In telling this story, Sayles is giving us his jaundiced view of big party politics with a not-so-subtle jab at America's current president.

Dickie Pilage is a political opportunist -- a ex-frat boy son of a politician who failed in his business endeavors and was bailed out of insolvency by a wealthy "friend of the family" (Kris Kristofferson). We usually see Dickie in front of the media, often fumbling for the right words when he goes off the script prepared for him by his handlers. It is an obvious and unflattering caricature of George W. Bush and one more example of the disdain that the Hollywood left holds for the sitting commander-in-chief.

But Dickie is merely a secondary character -- this film centers around Danny and his investigation and that is its biggest failing. Danny Huston (son of director John Huston) is not much of an actor and his glaring weaknesses keep Silver City from totally engaging the audience.

Sayles does manage to escape a complete embarrassment by surrounding Huston with one of the strongest supporting casts seen this year. Cooper is, once again, brilliant in what will undoubtedly be Silver City's signature role. Richard Dreyfuss plays his smug and controlling campaign manager with adroitness. Maria Bello is a reporter assigned to the campaign who once (not at all believably) had a relationship with Danny. Rounding out this very talented cast are Kris Kristofferson, Mary Kay Place, Billy Zane, Thora Birch, Mel Ferrar, Ralph Waite and Darryl Hannah. All provide interesting side stories which helps to distract us from the anemic central character.

There's a principle that we must hold in our minds as we watch films like Silver City, The Day After Tomorrow, and Fahrenheit 9/11, all of which paint Bush (or a Bush surrogate) in an unflattering light. The principle of which I speak asserts that if something is repeated often enough, it is taken to be true. Films can and do have an influence on the way people view their world. Movies affect fashion, language, and our perception of history. There appears to be a movement afoot to use movies now to influence the course of American politics.

Regardless of one's political beliefs, we should always be cognizant of why we hold them. We should never allow ourselves to be manipulated into believing something is true simply because other people say it is.

Prove all things; hold fast that which is good. 1 Thessalonians 5:21 (KJV)

Political spin, whether it comes from the talking heads on TV or from an entertainment product in the theaters, is designed to make an impact upon us and affect our perception of reality. God has given us the ability to discern. We must become adept at doing so if we are to continue to live in this world of muddled facts and biased opinions.
