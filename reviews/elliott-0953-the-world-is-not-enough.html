The World Is Not Enough

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 19, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Running time
2 hr: 8 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ecclesiastes 5:101 John 2:15-17Proverbs 15:27

Sermon / spiritual topic: Greed; Lust

Author-verified transcription

With apologies to James Bond, it is time for the tired old format used in the making of his films to be both "shaken and stirred." Somewhere along the line, the attempts to make a bigger and better Bond film has been reduced to simply this: more explosions. The latest Bond picture, The World Is Not Enough, has plenty of explosions, gadgets, and girls. But to borrow from the title, they just aren't enough. Is it too much to ask of Mr. Bond to give us a simple and understandable plot?

Pierce Brosnan (The Thomas Crown Affair) returns as the super-suave super spy 007, James Bond. This time, the world's supply of oil appears to be in danger. I say "appears to be" because we're really not sure what the threat to the world is. But we know there must be one because 007 is busy chasing, shooting, dodging, punching, rappelling, and skiing his way across the world.

At the center of his actions lies one Elektra King (Sophie Marceau, Lost and Found), oil heiress/hot babe. After her father was blown up in an (gasp) explosion, British intelligence fears she may be the next target. Especially when Bond finds out that the mastermind of the recent spate of bombings might be Renard the anarchist (Robert Carlyle, Plunkett and MacLeane), a very dangerous criminal with a bullet lodged in his brain which is killing off his senses one by one, making him impervious to pain.

Thinking that the next explosion might be a nuclear one, Bond hooks up with Dr. Christmas Jones (Denise Richards, Drop Dead Gorgeous), nuclear physicist/hot babe.

Mr. Brosnan continues to bring out the hardness of the Bond persona. His spy kills without compunction, and uses women likewise. By not softening the character, Mr. Brosnan is probably offering a more realistic version than his predecessors, but at the expense of losing a certain amount of audience appeal.

Director Michael Apted (Extreme Measures) was given all the ingredients for a classic Bond film, but he failed to blend them together properly. What is left is an overlong, overloud, overblown series of unconnected action scenes with gadgets galore (parasailing snowmobiles, gigantic chain saws suspended from helicopters, etc.)

This is a bit of a disappointment because the film does show much promise. There were more than a few wasted opportunities. The idea of having a sensory-deprived villain could have played a much more prominent role than it did. Mr. Carlyle's Renard turns out to be a bit of a wimp. Smitten by Elektra King but unable to enjoy her touch, he shows too much weakness to be an effective Bond villain.

John Cleese (The Out-of-Towners) is introduced as R, and is obviously being primed to replace the aging Desmond Llewelyn who has played Q throughout the series. In this film, he is given relatively little to do. One would think that his role will expand in future appearances.

Greed always plays a major role in Bond films. The title of this most recent venture is most accurate. For those who lust after earthly things, the world is not enough. Scriptures say:

He that loveth silver shall not be satisfied with silver; nor he that loveth abundance with increase: this is also vanity. Ecclesiastes 5:10 [KJV]

There will always be something else, something more desirable. The world does not teach us how to be satisfied with what we have but rather how to desire what we do not have.

By placing our trust in our heavenly Father, and looking to Him as our sufficiency, we can escape this most devilish trap which serves only to enslave, ensnare, and lead people to disappointment and failure. The world is never enough, but we have been called to stand above the world and partake of the divine nature of God.
