Runaway Bride

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 30, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ecclesiastes 5:5Matthew 12:36Deuteronomy 23:21-23

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Marriage; vows; commitment; words; accountability

Author-verified transcription

It has been ten years since the collaboration of Garry Marshall, Richard Gere, and Julia Roberts formed to produce Pretty Woman. After seeing their second joint effort, Runaway Bride, we can only hope that we won't have to wait another ten years for a third outing.

Here, Mr. Gere plays Ike Graham, a USA Today columnist whose misogynist views are reflected in his published pieces. Working against a fast approaching deadline, he hears about Maggie Carpenter (Miss Roberts), a three time bride from the Eastern Shore of Maryland who has a habit of leaving her grooms at the altar. Without further investigation, he decides to feature her story in his column, describing her as a "devourer" of men.

Maggie strikes back with a letter to the editor that categorically lists numerous factual mistakes in Ike's reporting leading, of course, to his dismissal from the paper. Looking to vindicate himself, he travels to Hale, Maryland to cover this "Runaway Bride's" fourth attempt to make it down the aisle. What he finds in Miss Carpenter and the small Mayberry-like town of Hale is more than he ever expected.

Garry Marshall may never be known as a "great" director, but one of his greatest attributes is his ability and willingness to take a back seat to the story and its characters. Mr. Marshall is the best kind of storyteller, one who does not attract attention to himself. At his hand, Runaway Bride is wrapped in a small town charm that elicits an ease and naturalness from its performers.

Mr. Gere has never been as open and likable as he is here. By charming his way into the lives of the closely-knit community of Hale, he manages to win over the audience as well.

Miss Roberts literally lights up the screen with her presence and dazzling smile. Unlike Notting Hill, where much of her work was overshadowed by the ingratiating style of her costar, Hugh Grant, Runaway Bride is her star vehicle, from beginning to end. It is her performance that makes the film and its characters' relationships work. She is quite stunning.

Joan Cusak (Arlington Road) is quickly becoming renowned for being a most accomplished supporting player, having already received two Academy Award nominations in that category. Here, as Peggy, Maggie's best friend and perpetual bridesmaid, she again provides her unique blend of quirky humor and heart.

Hector Elizondo has appeared in every film that Garry Marshall has directed (including, of course, Pretty Woman). His dry, deadpan delivery is responsible for one of the biggest laughs in the film.

The script, written by the team of Sara Parriott and Josann McGibbon (Three Men and a Little Lady), has more than its share of laughs, much of them coming at the expense of Maggie's penchant for turning "Here Comes The Bride" to "There Goes The Bride." Using videotaped sequences of Maggie's first three wedding disasters was a brilliant and hysterically funny addition.

Of course, marriage is a big step and one that must be considered deeply. While it could be argued that she should never have gotten engaged to begin with, Maggie had a valid reason for not going through with her weddings. (Reasons which we won't go into here out of respect for the filmmakers, who would probably prefer to let you discover that plot twist for yourself). But even though she became the town's object of ridicule, she was right not to vow a vow before God which she was not convinced she could keep.

Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay. Ecclesiastes 5:5 [KJV]

"I do" may be two of the littlest words in the English language, but they carry enormous weight, reflecting as they do a lifelong commitment made before God to one another. The Word of God says that we will be held accountable for every idle word that we utter. [Matthew 12:36] What about the most important two words many of us will ever speak?
