Hanging Up

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 18, 2000
Rating
1 star
Source
Original Author Copy

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Rest; Sabbath; renewal; keeping God first; family

Author-verified transcription

Hanging Up could very well be the cinematic equivalent of an unwelcome telemarketing call from a timeshare salesman. Its title serves as an helpful exhortation to any audience searching for a proper response to its “call.”

Meg Ryan (You’ve Got Mail) plays Eve, the put upon daughter of curmudgeonly Lou (Walter Matthau, Grumpier Old Men). Lou is dying, but he’s taking his time about it. This is a fact which is becoming increasingly difficult for Eve to handle because it is into her care that his failing condition has managed to fall.

Her sisters, Georgia (Diane Keaton, The Other Sister) and Maddy (Lisa Kudrow, Analyze This) give little of their time to Eve or to their father except via distracted conversations on the ever present cell phone, fax phone, pay phone, etc. Georgia, a successful magazine publisher, is too busy building her empire and Maddy, a rising soap opera actress, is too busy... well... too busy being a rising soap opera actress.

Directed by Diane Keaton (Unstrung Heroes), the film seems to want to be about such universally understood themes as reconciliation, sibling rivalry, and family love. But the script by Delia and Nora Ephron (You’ve Got Mail) never digs deep enough to find the heart of such rich topics. Instead it chooses to superficially build its story around a forced and eventually irritating plot device centering on our society’s growing dependence on telephony devices.

Personally, I found the rudeness and lack of phone etiquette demonstrated in the film disconcerting and unappealing. Instead of commiserating with any of the characters, I was repelled by their apparent selfishness or self-serving natures.

Walter Matthau, in one of his most unpleasant roles, plays the father figure to the three girls. Apparently adored by them when they were small, he proved not to be the greatest example of proud parenting. Now, as his physical and mental abilities are becoming impaired, he is even more demanding, inconsiderate, and boorish. In a word, unlovable... A description that fits the movie as a whole.

Even Meg Ryan’s impish screen persona does little to salvage this misguided effort. She brings her customary high energy but scatters it across the screen in such an unfocused manner that her characterization is ultimately as interesting as a busy signal.

Both Diane Keaton and Lisa Kudrow have been given next to no opportunity to develop much of a relationship with either the father or their other sisters. Their aloofness throughout the film makes it all the more difficult to accept the confrontation and reconciliation which comes in the latter third of the picture.

The most effective screen performances are those given by Duke Moosekian (Stigmata) and Ann Bortolotti (in her film debut) who play a doctor and his mother who try to give Eve some salient advice: Disconnect yourself.

Everyone, regardless of age, position, or environment, should plan quiet time in his day or week to refresh and reflect. Time to contemplate or meditate. Time to give thanks and praise to God.

This was God’s initial reason for establishing a Sabbath Day. So that people would stop working for a moment and spend time with Him. So that they could rest and that He could strengthen them via His Word.

Six days thou shalt do thy work, and on the seventh day thou shalt rest: that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed. Exodus 23:12 (KJV)

The world can keep our lives so busy it seems that we can forget to take the time to refresh ourselves and draw strength from the unique relationship we have with our spiritual Father. When that happens, everyone loses. But when we take the time to keep God first in our lives, we are better equipped to deal with whatever challenge may face us.

But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. Isaiah 40:31 (KJV)
