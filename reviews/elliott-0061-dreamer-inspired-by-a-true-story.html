Dreamer: Inspired by a True Story

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 21, 2005
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Matthew 13:58Hebrews 3:15-19Matthew 17:19-20

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Dreams; belief; unbelief; faith; overcoming fear

Author-verified transcription

Dreamer is the kind of saccharine-laced, rooting-for-the-long-shot story for which I happen to be a sucker. Sure it’s predictable. And yes, it is manipulative. But it is also emotionally satisfying and a good yarn to boot.

Dakota Fanning stars as young Cale Crane, the pint sized daughter of down-on-his-luck horseman Ben Crane (Kurt Russell). They live on the only horse ranch in Lexington Kentucky that doesn’t have a single horse on it. It seems there was a falling out between Ben and his father (Kris Kristofferson) that has led to a quasi estrangement and a noticeable lack of equine activity on the Crane homestead.

When Sonador, the horse Ben is training for a heartless breeder (David Morse), is seriously injured in a high stakes race, Ben is saddled with the animal as part of his severance pay. His only hope to get back on top financially is to nurse the horse back to health so he can breed her.

It is Cale who has bigger dreams for Sonador. Her believing is not only for her to walk again – and not only for her to run again - but for the horse to heal the rift that has divided her family for so long.

If there’s a reason that 9 year old Dakota Fanning seems to be everywhere perhaps it is because she is. Since her 2001 breakthrough film (I Am Sam) she has appeared in no less than ten major films opposite some of our biggest box office stars (Sean Penn, Tom Cruise, Mike Myers, Denzel Washington, and Robert DeNiro for example). A young girl this personable who is also able to convey emotions that translate believably is not that common and Hollywood has taken notice by feeding her role after role.

Kurt Russell continues to be a reliable if somewhat unappreciated talent. His performance as Ben is nicely shaded as he plays a man whose inner light has been dimmed over the years by a string of misfortunes.

Writer/director John Gatins works with a formulaic blueprint that makes its outcome all too clear but he infuses the script with enough heart and soul to make the film do what it is intended to do – have us care about these characters and the story which is shaping their lives. That it does so in a PG friendly manner is an added bonus.

Dreams (or goals) are wonderful things but they don’t come to pass all by themselves. Believing in one’s dreams (or one’s heart’s desires) has a great deal to do with whether or not they ever move from dream to reality. Ben’s repeated disappointments and “bad luck” have caused him to grow leery of hoping that something good will happen. His doubts have led him to worry and worry has grown into fear. He allowed his fears to collect and magnify his unbelief to the point that he placed severe limits not only on his life but also the life of his family.

God is capable of providing so much for us if we would just increase our faith to believe for it. Believing activates God’s power and causes great things to occur. Unbelief does just the opposite.

And he (Jesus Christ) did not many mighty works there because of their unbelief. Matthew 13:58 (KJV)

Jesus Christ did no small number of mighty works – but where unbelief dominated, the spiritual power of God was not manifested. Scripture tells us that when we believe we can move mountains. It is our unbelief that keeps them right where they are – usually blocking our way from attaining what we desire.
