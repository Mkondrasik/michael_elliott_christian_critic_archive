The Country Bears

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 26, 2002
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Unity; spiritual leadership; messengers versus the message; church growth

Author-verified transcription

“They’re better than The Eagles!” That’s high praise, indeed... especially seeing as it comes from none other than Eagles’ frontman, Don Henley. More surprising still is that the group upon whom he bestows this compliment once got their start by beating an armpit musician in a talent show. We’re talking, of course, of The Country Bears.

Like many bands, fame and adulation take their toll and The Country Bears have long since let egos, heartbreak and a certain honey addiction come between them. The Bears broke up soon after the end of their successful Hiber Nation Tour.

It was a disappointment to their many fans, especially to Beary Barrington (voice by Haley Joel Osment, AI: Artificial Intelligence), a young cub who, now that he’s in the fourth grade, is beginning to suspect that he might not really be related to the human family with whom he lives. Feeling different and out of place, he sets out for Country Bear Hall where he knows, “you can be different and still fit in.”

Finding out that the Hall is about to be demolished by an unscrupulous developer (Christopher Walken, America’s Sweethearts), Beary discovers his higher purpose in life: to reunite The Country Bears, put on a concert and save the hall.

With the help of the Hall impresario, Henry (voice by Kevin Michael Richardson, Lilo & Stitch), Beary sets out in search of the scattered Bears. Close behind are Officers Hamm and Cheets (Daryl “Chill” Mitchell, Black Knight and Diedrich Bader, Jay and Silent Bob Strike Back) who erroneously believe that Beary might have been kidnapped.

To be honest, the story which serves as the framework for the film is instantly forgettable. That is something for which to be thankful. This entire experience might have been described in much the same way (forgettable) if it were not for the music of John Hiatt who provided six original songs for the movie. The musical numbers, enthusiastically staged and performed by such vocal talents as Don Henley, Bonnie Raitt, Brian Setzer, Krystal Marie Harris, and Jennifer Paige, make the movie come alive with energy. When the music stops, the characters become lifeless and uninteresting. When the “Bears” are not performing, our interest quickly begins to dwindle.

In the most enjoyable sequence, rockabilly sensation Brian Setzer squares off against Zeb Zoober (voice by Stephen Root, Office Space) in a winner-take-all duel between guitar and fiddle. The song, “I’m Only In It For The Honey” was written especially for the film by Setzer and it is a honey of a tune.

The Country Bears is inspired by, of all things, a theme park attraction. An estimated 75 million people have seen the auto-animatronic “Country Bears Jamboree” that plays at Disney Parks around the world. Although the film is no better than the attraction, there is a hidden blessing... at least they didn’t choose It’s A Small World.

As a fan, Beary has a tough time choosing a favorite Country Bear. That’s because he has no favorites. He just isn’t enamored with them individually. What makes them special to him is the music they collectively make.

It is likewise important that, as Christians, we don’t allow ourselves to place the messengers above the message itself. It seems like a simple thing but it is human nature to be attracted to personalities. It was something that needed to be addressed as early as the first century church:

I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase. 1 Corinthians 3:6-7 (KJV)

God, speaking by revelation to Paul, recognized that if the church formed factions (“I am of Paul or I am of Apollos”), divisions that would be detrimental to growth would set in. It was his desire, and it should be ours, that the church grow together as one united in faith.

God has set in the church gift ministries of apostles, pastors, prophets, evangelists and teachers for the perfecting of the saints (Ephesians 4:11). We must remember that they are there to help us grow to spiritual maturity so we may follow, not them exclusively, but Christ who is the head of all the church.
