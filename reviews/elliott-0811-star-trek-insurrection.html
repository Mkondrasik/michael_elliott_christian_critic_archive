Author-verified transcription

It looks like we're running out of places where "no man has gone before." At least that's the feeling we get from this ninth installment of the popular Star Trek series of films; this one subtitled Insurrection. Throughout the film, we're reminded that we've seen all this before in one episode or another. Still, the formula continues to work so why get your "tribble" in an uproar? Just sit back and enjoy the ride.

Captain Jean-Luc Picard (Patrick Stewart) is still at the helm of the Enterprise, in the midst of a never-ending stream of diplomatic missions. He even bemoans to his faithful crew at one point early in the film, "Remember when we used to be explorers?" One distress call later and they dash off to save the universe. Well, actually... not the entire universe... just one small portion of it.

It seems that the Federation has stumbled upon a unique planet with miraculous rejuvenating properties. For reasons best left unexplained, the Federation forms an alliance with a race of people called the Son'a. Their plan is to force the native inhabitants of the planet (the Ba'ku) to relocate to another section of the universe, leaving their planet and its mysterious properties to them.

Since such a plan obviously flies in the face of the Federation's prime directive, it's left up to Picard and his band of intergalactic Dudley Do-Rights to stand against the Federation and foil their plan (whereby we get the "insurrection" of the title).

Director Jonathan Frakes (who also plays Commander William Riker in the film) keeps the mood light and frothy, relying heavily on humor to keep us interested despite a plot that is thinner than one of Data's memory chips. And there is a lot of humor to go around. Most of it stems from the effects that the rejuvenating properties of the planet have on the crew of the Enterprise. Picard finds himself dancing the mambo, Data (Brent Spiner) gets lessons on how to be a child, and Worf (Michael Dorn) starts going through puberty (along with boxes of industrial strength Stridex pads). Even Riker's libido gets a jump start in Counselor Troi's (Marina Sirtis) quarters.

Mr. Stewart is at his stalwart best, whether facing death or singing Gilbert and Sullivan. Should this series' sequels continue well into the double digits, the next captain will have mighty big shoes to fill.

The regular cast members know their assigned roles and (as Data might say) they perform them well within normal parameters. There is so many of them that no one other than Mr. Stewart and Mr. Spiner have a real opportunity to shine. F. Murray Abraham (Amadeus) as Ru'afo, the obsessed leader of the Son'a, gives a new, skin crawling meaning to the term facelift. He is quite a suitable villain. Donna Murphy (Jade) is Picard's benevolent Ba'ku love interest.

Of course the topic is well worn. Man has long fantasized over finding a fountain of youth; attaining eternal life; achieving immortality. Much has been written, filmed, and sung about man's yearning desire to live forever. What many fail to realize is that eternal life is already an accomplished reality. And we don't need to travel to distant planets to find it. All we have to do is believe the Word of God that has been given to us. Eternal life is part of God's master plan for all who believe. All He asks in return is that we love Him and keep His commandments, one of them being to hold forth His Word so all may come unto a knowledge of the truth.

We should borrow a page from the captured Jean-Luc Picard, who said when asked if he was pleading for his life, "I'm not pleading for my life. I'm pleading for yours." We don't speak the Word for our salvation... We're already saved. We speak the Word so others may have the choice to believe unto salvation. We pray they do, for there will be a day... a day when Jesus Christ returns... when all born again believers will rise and boldly go where only one man has gone before. And there will be no more sequels - because that story will go on forever.
