America's Sweethearts

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 20, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Idolatry; celebrity; hypocrisy; discernment; false images

Author-verified transcription

Meg and Dennis. Tom and Nicole. Julia and Benjamin. What is it about high profile celebrity couples and their marriages, separations, possible reconciliation, and/or divorces that the public and media find so fascinating? I don't know if I have the answer to that perplexing question. That fascination serves as the basis for the scenario at the heart of America's Sweethearts.

Eddie (John Cusack, Being John Malkovich) and Gwen (Catherine Zeta-Jones, Traffic) are the title characters. They are popular celebrities who have appeared together in nine romantic comedies (six of them grossing over $100 million), and their married, fairy-tale life has captured the fancy of movie-going audiences worldwide.

When Gwen leaves Eddie for "the Spaniard" (Hank Azaria, Godzilla), America's collective heart is broken. So is the financial hope the studio has placed upon the newest Eddie & Gwen picture, "Time Over Time," which the eccentric director (Christopher Walken, The Opportunists) is holding hostage.

It is up to studio publicist Lee Philips (Billy Crystal, Analyze This) to arrange for a press junket that will somehow make the press forget the fact that they aren't being shown the film. With the help of Kiki (Julia Roberts, The Mexican), Gwen's sister and personal assistant, Lee manipulates both the press and the stars in order to promote the film no one has seen.

America's Sweethearts, while taking jabs at the often duplicitous nature of movie making, does not go for the jugular. Instead it is content to set its aim upon the funny bone and, for the most part, hits it squarely.

There are enough genuine laughs and recognizable character types that make America's Sweethearts an enjoyable diversion.

Catherine Zeta-Jones is a hoot as the selfish, demanding diva whose sweet and sexy onscreen persona masks a cold and narcissistic attitude. The always likable John Cusack has some fun with his borderline neurotic, obsessive, and emotionally confused character.

Julia Roberts keeps a low profile in this film as the put-upon "gofer" for Gwen. Donning a "fat suit" for part of the film, Ms. Roberts takes a turn out of the ingenue spotlight and basks in the much dimmer light of a supporting player. She may still command top billing, but she doesn't have to "carry" this picture.

The supporting characters each contribute special moments to the film: Alan Arkin as a new age guru "helping" Eddie get over the loss of Gwen; Christopher Walken as a cuckoo director who withholds a multimillion dollar production from being seen by anyone at the studio.; Hank Azaria as "the Spaniard" with a ridiculous lisped accent. Stanley Tucci as the studio head trying to salvage what appears to be an 84 million dollar mistake.

America's Sweethearts is a savvy production which fondly takes more than a few pot shots at Hollywood, celebrities, film criticism, and other such media targets. It boasts a sharp script by Billy Crystal and Peter Tolan (Analyze This), a competent director (Joe Roth, Coupe de Ville), and a talented cast willing to lampoon themselves.

The near obsession that the public (especially the American public) has with celebrities is unhealthy at the very least. Are those in the Hollywood community really the type of "ideal" to whom we, as a people, should be looking as our examples? The very nature of their business requires them to be dissemblers of the truth... to pretend to be something they are not. It is for this reason that the Greek word for stage actor is the word from which we get the English word "hypocrite."

The image and impression we have of the stars we see in films, have next to no substantial reality. To "idolize" them sets us up for disappointment after disappointment. It is foolishness.

Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen. 1 John 5:21 (KJV)
