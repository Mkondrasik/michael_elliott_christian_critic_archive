Author-verified transcription

It would appear that those responsible for the new film A Man Apart never bothered to put it back together. It is an excessively violent, incoherent jumble of a movie.

Vin Diesel (XXX) stars as Sean Vetter, a beefy narcotics cop who is doing all he can to put drug traffickers out of business. After seven years of investigative work, he and his team of ex-gangbangers turned DEA agents manage to capture the head drug lord of the Pacific Coast (Geno Silva, Amistad). One would think that is a good thing.

But the removal of one scum drug lord creates an opening for another to take his place. In this case, it is a mysterious man known only as El Diablo. He ruthlessly and brutally begins to position himself as the king of the West Coast cocaine corridor.

When assassins invade Vetter's home, they kill his wife (Jacqueline Obradors, Tortilla Soup) and leave him wounded, grieving, and thirsting for revenge. The war on drugs has just become something of a personal vendetta for Vetter, one which may cause his own destruction.

What follows is just as predictable as you might think. Lots of explosions, automatic weapons fire, grisly scenes of violence, and a complete disregard for human life set in and around strip clubs in Los Angeles and Mexico. Perhaps if an actor were cast who was capable of carrying some dramatic weight the film would be different. But with Vin Diesel as the headliner, all we need do is to wait for stuff to blow up.

The main problem with A Man Apart, directed by F. Gary Gray (The Negotiator) is that the structure is so disjointed, the audience has no hope to follow who is doing what to whom. There are characters which appear for their sheer quirkiness without any explanation as to how they are tied to the other characters in the film.

Larenz Tate (Biker Boyz) has some good scenes as Vetter's more levelheaded partner. Timothy Olyphant (Go) is amusing as a salon owner cum drug distributor but his antics exist without a framework in which to understand them. Jacqueline Obradors is sympathetic as a devoted wife and Geno Silva is an assured presence as the imprisoned drug lord.

It is said that nature abhors a vacuum. That was certainly the case in the film. As one drug lord is eliminated, another one, worse than the one before, takes his place. As we live our lives, attempting to build them to be pleasing to God, there's a lesson here that is very profitable for us.

Eliminating bad habits developed from our sinful or "old man" nature is only possible if we build positive or godly habits to take their place. Just stopping a bad habit is not enough. Unless we fill the void left by the elimination of unhealthy actions, that bad habit will return with a vengeance.

"When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest; and finding none, he saith, I will return unto my house whence I came out. And when he cometh, he findeth it swept and garnished. Then goeth he, and taketh to him seven other spirits more wicked than himself; and they enter in, and dwell there: and the last state of that man is worse than the first." Luke 11:24-26 (KJV)

Evil can be vanquished and bad habits can be ended. The key is to build good, positive, and desirable habits to take their place. A good place to start would be what we choose to think about.

Finally, brethren, whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report; if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things. Philippians 4:8 (KJV)
