You, Me, and Dupree

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 14, 2006
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Song of Solomon 8:6bProverbs 27:4Proverbs 14:30

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Jealousy; marriage; trust; envy; communication

Author-verified transcription

You Me and Dupree is the kind of film that unfortunately seems to get churned out every so often by the Hollywood machine. It is a one joke storyline to which a few big celebrity names are attached. It is easily pitched - easily made - and all too easily forgotten.

Owen Wilson plays the central character - the Dupree of the title. He's a sad sack loser of a best friend who is thrust into the newlywed life of Carl and Molly Peterson (Matt Dillon and Kate Hudson). Unemployed and unskilled in the social graces, Dupree makes life next to impossible for the love birds and causes a tension that threatens to disturb their wedded bliss.

When Dupree loses his job, his house, and his ride the Petersons open their hearts and home to him. He spends the next 90 minutes abusing their hospitality with behavior usually displayed by animals a few notches down on the food chain.

To make matters worse for Carl, his new father-in-law and boss (Michael Douglas) is not too keen on his daughter's choice for a husband and takes every opportunity to express his utter disdain.

There's a lot of talent on the screen in You Me and Dupree, but you'd only know it by watching the actors' other films. Kate Hudson plays up her cute and coquettish persona to little effect; Matt Dillon simmers and stews as the put upon victim; Owen Wilson mugs laconically as an overgrown frat boy; and Michael Douglas smugly tries to stay above it all. He can't.

The script, by Mike LeSieur, loses focus less than half way through the film. It spends its early minutes establishing Dupree as a major loser who cannot be trusted with even the smallest detail. It then tries to make him the pivotal point in a love triangle that furthers Carl's downward spiral. It doesn't work.

The direction from Anthony and Joe Russo doesn't help to smooth things out. They use a rather straightforward approach that does nothing to mask the flaws in the script.

The humor is based upon the sophomoric actions of Dupree - mostly centered on his sex and bathroom habits. It's nothing we haven't seen and disliked before.

If we were to choose a characteristic from the film as a learning tool perhaps it would be how easily Carl falls into envy or jealousy. These feelings force him into taking action that is both ill-advised and detrimental to his personal goals and interests. Jealousy will do that.

Jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame. Song of Solomon 8:6b (KJV)

Without trust, a marriage cannot and should not survive. The lack of trust will stir the embers of jealousy which, when sparked, have no problem growing into a full blown blaze that will consume every ounce of love and appreciation that once existed in the relationship. It is best to quench that fire before it begins through open communication and faithful devotion to one another.
