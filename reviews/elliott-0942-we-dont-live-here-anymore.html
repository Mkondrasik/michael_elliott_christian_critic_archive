We Don't Live Here Anymore

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 13, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 41 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Genesis 2:24Ephesians 5:28-291 Corinthians 7:3

Sermon / spiritual topic: Marriage; Adultery; Infidelity

Author-verified transcription

Four people, two couples, no morals... this is something I want to watch? Based on a pair of short stories by Andre Dubus, We Don't Live Here Anymore is an adult drama that details the destructive effect an adulterous affair can have on a marriage in trouble. Some things are just obvious.

Jack Linden (Mark Ruffalo) is a college professor on summer hiatus. His best friend Hank (Peter Krause) teaches creative writing at the same college and is using his summer to work on his novel. Their wives are also best friends even though they seem to be quite unlike one another. Terry Linden (Laura Dern) is a bohemian type. Her house is unkempt, her children are a little undisciplined - but she's got a good heart. Hank's wife Edith (Naomi Watts) seems to have it more together: her house is in order; her child is well behaved; she keeps herself well-groomed; and she is pretty much living a lie. She and her husband no longer love one another.

We learn early on that Jack and Edith have begun a consensual affair that both realize is wrong. But that knowledge doesn't stop them. What we don't know is how Hank and Terry will react when they find out. What we do know is that they will find out.

Director John Curran unfolds the story, what little there is, slowly. There's no need to rush since it is obvious how everything is going to shake out in the end. His approach places the focus squarely upon the actors and their work is the only redemptive aspect of the film. The four stars all turn in believable characterizations although none of them are particularly appealing. These are characters that use sex and each other selfishly and without full regard to the consequences of their actions.

Ruffalo is full of brooding intensity as his character wrestles with the internal conflict between forbidden sexual pleasure and familial duties and responsibilities. Dern, as the wronged wife, has the opportunity to lash out in a series of emotional outbursts. Krause plays his character as a Machiavellian manipulator who all but steers his wife into the arms of another man. Watts is more of an enigmatic character - a pawn in this sex game until the film's final scenes.

There's no new ground being broken here. There's no message being communicated that we haven't heard before. There's no up side to adultery. There are no benefits; no rationalizations that make it OK. The temptations of entering into an adulterous affair have existed as long as marriage itself. To succumb to those temptations is a sign of weakness and cowardice.

It requires a level of strength to remain faithful to the vows taken. It requires bravery to remain honest in a relationship and deal with the problems and issues that arise before they blossom into something larger that may cause a rift in the marriage. But that's what marriage is.

The characters in We Don't Live Here Anymore have forgotten the "one flesh" analogy that God uses to define marriage.

Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh. Genesis 2:24 (KJV)

Anyone who cheats on his spouse is cheating on himself as well for they are one flesh. He has robbed himself of a most precious gift - the fully committed relationship between two people forged together by the love of God. Nothing could be worth more.
