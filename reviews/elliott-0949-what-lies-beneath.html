It is said that "hell hath no fury as a woman scorned." Judging by Robert Zemeckis' new film, What Lies Beneath, death apparently doesn't have her beat either.

Michelle Pfeiffer (The Story of Us) stars as Claire Spencer, wife of Norman Spencer (Harrison Ford, Random Hearts), and she's a bit tightly wound. Her back story takes some time to tell, but the film, with a running length of over two hours, apparently doesn't feel the need to be rushing it. Suffice it to say, she sees dead people. More correctly, a dead person. Who that person is and why Claire sees her takes approximately three-quarters of the movie to explain.

There are a lot of red herrings thrown on screen. Claire's only daughter moves out of the house to go to college, leaving Claire distraught over the idea of her "empty nest." Their new next door neighbors are a bit eccentric, possibly homicidal. Norman, an up and coming scientist specializing in genetics must forever live in the shadow of his deceased father who was even more renown in the field. Claire's friend Jody (Diana Scarwid, Mommie Dearest) seems to know just a little bit too much about the occult. The "ghost" appears to be somehow connected to the house and the Spencers. These plot lines could have led to something deeper. Most of them don't.

It has to be difficult attempting this genre in the aftermath of superior "ghost stories" such as The Sixth Sense, or projects which draw attention to the secrets of the genre while at the same time exploiting them such as Scream, or just plain old spoofs which make ridiculous fun of the genre such as Scary Movie.

But Robert Zemeckis (Contact) gamely relies on the tried and true conventions that worked for Hitchcock and countless others. The film contains elements seen many times before (i.e. Rear Window, Psycho, Fatal Attraction, even Double Jeopardy.)

What Lies Beneath is a film that would have been more successful 10 years ago. But, at present, the conventions that are used in this film are bordering on cliché. We have the ominous music, the protagonist slowly creeping down the hall (stairs, boat ramp, driveway, etc.) knowing that the attacker would strike but never quite sure when, and the obligatory "jump" scenes, manipulated so the audience will, in unison, react in surprise, resulting in embarrassed laughter and many denials by macho males as they stream out of the theater.

On this level, the film works... but just barely. It suffers from both the overexposure and comedic parodies the genre has experienced by many of the films mentioned earlier. Ms. Pfeiffer can, at times, overplay her hand, becoming more distraught than the situation merits. Mr. Ford has an almost impossible job to do with his character, given his movie persona. His character should be a possible suspect in the misdeeds we are watching but he doesn't quite succeed in making us believe that his character is capable of such underhanded trickery.

The film is not completely without merit. Some scenes are indeed effectively tense and humor quite often is interjected after particularly harrowing events. I particularly liked how the filmmakers would cut from Claire experiencing yet another unexplained occurrence to her session with her psychiatrist where she self-consciously tries to explain what she has seen or heard knowing that it sounds loony even to her.

Claire doesn't just stop at psychoanalysis. She decides to venture into the realm of the paranormal by playing with a Ouija board and later holding a seance by candlelight. Dangerous and ill-advised behavior. Trying to invoke the dead is pure foolishness according to the Word. The real danger actually lies in the spiritual weakness one exhibits if one engages in such practice. The spirit kingdom (speaking of devil spirits) are experts in deception and manipulation. Familiar spirits are standing by to become whomever it is you want them to be. Their goal is to lead you down an unrighteous path. God's exhortation to us is clear:

Regard not them that have familiar spirits, neither seek after wizards, to be defiled by them: I am the LORD your God. Leviticus 19:31 (KJV)

There may be unexplained phenomenon which takes place in this world. But to seek answers using methods of the occult is sheer lunacy.
