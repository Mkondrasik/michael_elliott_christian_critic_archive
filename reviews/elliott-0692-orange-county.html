There comes a time when every generation must pass the torch to the next. That time may have not yet arrived, but Orange County provides a glimmer of hope that when it does, the offspring of Hollywood's elite might be ready.

For Shaun Brumder (Colin Hanks, son of Tom Hanks), growing up in Orange County, California has always meant one thing: Righteous waves, dude. As a surf bum, he spends more time on the beach with his slacker friends than he ever does at home with his dysfunctional family or at school with his incompetent teachers. When a surfing accident claims the life of one of his "crew," Shaun falls into introspection and while in this state happens to read a "Catcher in the Rye" type book which changes his life.

Deciding that he wants to be a writer, Shaun throws himself into his new career choice with both feet. Rededicating himself to his school work, he manages to develop a transcript which would be the envy of any college bound student. He applies to Stanford University to study under the author of the book which gave him the epiphany but when his guidance counselor forwards the wrong file, he is rejected and his dreams of escaping the Orange County life which he has come to despise seem dashed.

With the help of his girlfriend (Schuyler Fisk, daughter of Sissy Spacek), Shaun sets out to correct the transcript error and convince the powers that be that he belongs in Stanford. His efforts are continually and humorously thwarted by the unusual characters to which he is unfortunately related.

Director Jake Kasdan (son of director Lawrence Kasdan) helms the project and, for the most part, avoids the mediocrity and inanity which accompanies most "teen pictures." There's actually a bit of substance upon which the comedy hangs.

Hanks and Fisk are likable enough and as the "normal" characters, they center the film allowing the supporting cast to explore the comedic fringes of eccentricity. Jack Black, fresh from his romantic lead in Shallow Hal, returns to the zany, offbeat wackiness for which he is well known. As Shaun's zoned out older brother, he spends much of the film parading around in his underwear in a semiconscious state. Though his performance often goes "over the top," he somehow manages to make it work for him.

Catherine O'Hara (Best of Show) and John Lithgow (3rd Rock From The Sun) are quite amusing as Shaun's drunken mother and materialistic father. The fact that they are divorced and remarried (he to a bikini-clad twenty-year-old and she to a wealthy invalid), only adds to Shaun's confusing home life.

Shaun's eagerness to leave Orange County was certainly understandable. Though he might have changed his outlook on life, as well as his goals and aspirations, those around him did not. He believed that only by separating himself from his roots would he be truly able to establish himself in his "new" life. It reminds me of when Jesus returned to teach in Nazareth. The townspeople there remembered him as a child, the son of a carpenter, and had difficulty accepting him as one with spiritual authority.

Then Jesus said to them, "Prophets are not without honor, except in their hometown, and among their own kin, and in their own house." Mark 6:4 (NRSV)

We all, to some extent, have to deal with something like this. Each of us had a life prior to making Jesus our Lord. That decision has changed each one of us... some more than others, depending on what we were involved with before. We shouldn't be surprised when those from our past express disbelief when faced with our "new" selves. What Shaun had to come to realize (and what we in turn must also recognize) is that our change or growth as individuals is not dependent upon how other people react to that change. We can be whatever we choose to be no matter where we are because we carry the power of Christ inside us.

Phil 4:13 I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me. Philippians 4:13 (KJV)
