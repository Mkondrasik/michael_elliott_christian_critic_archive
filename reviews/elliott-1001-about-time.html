About Time

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 1, 2013
Rating
3 stars
Running time
2 hr: 3 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Luke 12:22-26Philippians 4:61 Peter 5:7

Sermon / spiritual topic: Worry; Anxiety; Trusting God; Living in the Present

Author-verified transcription

We have all had those awkward moments where we wish we could immediately go back in time and undo something we had said or done. In About Time, Tim (Domhnall Gleeson) discovers he has the power to do exactly that. He uses this ability to shape his life to be exactly the way he wants it to be. Well, almost.

It is a trait handed down from the men in his family and once he learns the secret from his father (Bill Nighy), the shy and awkward Tim immediately puts it to use. But instead of using his newfound talent to build fame and fortune, Tim opts to chase after the one thing more important to him: Love.

His nervousness with the opposite sex had always put him at a disadvantage in the dating game. Now, he has an unlimited amount of do-overs at his disposal. He uses them to woo and win a charming young woman (Rachel McAdams). But life is not always easy and some things can’t be fixed even by going back in time.

Writer/Director Richard Curtis and an ensemble cast convey a sweet tenderness to this romantic comedy that washes over us and leaves us smiling in its wake. All the characters are likable and have a genuine affection for one another. There is no “villain” in this film, unless we consider life itself, with its challenges and obstacles, to be the antagonist. Or else, perhaps it is the internal battle that Tim faces; all the insecurities and fears that he must overcome to achieve his heart’s desire that provides the film with its dramatic conflict.

Either way, this light and sentimental film weaves a tapestry of love that unites the family and infects the audience with its feel-good aura.

At one point, Tim’s father encourages him to live a day and then go back and relive the same day without changing anything. Of course, it does change because Tim’s perspective has changed. Already knowing the outcome of each encounter or situation eliminates the stresses, anxieties and fears Tim had felt the first time around. Tim experiences a new appreciation for a day he has already lived.

We need not travel in time to gain that same appreciation. In the grand scheme of things, God has already provided us with the outcome that will one day be a reality. All the rest is simply details. He encourages us to live each day with the knowledge he has provided us, free from doubt, worry or fear.

“Then Jesus said to his disciples: “Therefore I tell you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat; or about your body, what you will wear. For life is more than food, and the body more than clothes. Consider the ravens: They do not sow or reap, they have no storeroom or barn; yet God feeds them. And how much more valuable you are than birds! Who of you by worrying can add a single hour to your life. Since you cannot do this very little thing, why do you worry about the rest?” Luke 12:22-26 (NIV)

Unlike Tim, we don’t have the opportunity to re-do a moment of time. But we can learn to appreciate each moment as it arrives.
