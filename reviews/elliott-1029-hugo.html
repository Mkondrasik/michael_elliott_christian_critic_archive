Author-verified transcription

Martin Scorsese delivers a timeless film that tells an engaging story of a young boy’s search for place and purpose while, at the same time, celebrating the art of making movies. It is all wrapped up in a neat and tidy cinematic package called Hugo.

Hugo (Asa Butterfield) is a young orphan who lives in a train station of Paris circa 1930. It is his unofficial job to keep the clocks running while remaining undetected. In his spare time he tinkers with a mechanical man that is the one of the few physical possessions his deceased father left him.

That brings him into conflict with the old man who runs the toy kiosk at the station. Hugo has been known to “borrow” items from the store to help him work on his mechanical man. His actions have not gone unnoticed. What Hugo did not know was that the old man, Georges Melies, was a notable figure, but long forgotten, in film history. It is a fact that has escaped not only everyone in Paris, but also most of the rest of the civilized world.

This is a sweet and simple tale, told by an experienced storyteller, containing spectacular visuals and a likable cast. Scorsese pays tribute to early filmmakers who paved the way for artists and visionaries to create works of imaginative wonder. Hugo, in so many ways, is the legacy of what has come before it.

In the film, Hugo states that the saddest thing to him is a broken machine. It is because the machine was built for a purpose but, being broken, it is unable to fulfill that purpose. These are wise words from such a young person. People, like machines, were also made for a purpose. Just as the maker of a machine has a purpose in mind for it, our Creator designed and built us for a specific reason.

“All has been heard; the end of the matter is: Fear God [revere and worship Him, knowing that He is] and keep His commandments, for this is the whole of man [the full, original purpose of his creation, the object of God's providence, the root of character, the foundation of all happiness, the adjustment to all inharmonious circumstances and conditions under the sun] and the whole [duty] for every man.” Ecclesiastes 12:13 (Amplified)

Our purpose, as living beings, is to know and love God and to keep His commandments. But for reasons beyond our control, we are born into this world spiritually broken… unable to fulfill God’s original purpose for us. But thanks to the sacrifice of Jesus Christ who died to reunite us with our heavenly Father, we have been given the opportunity to achieve all that God desires for us. We can fulfill our purpose. We can know and love God. We can keep His commandments. And we can be blessed doing so.
