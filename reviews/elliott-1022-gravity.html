Gravity

Author-verified transcription

Utilizing an extremely high level of technical wizardry, writer/director Alfonso Cuaron has crafted the most exciting, nail-biting suspense thriller to come along in quite some time. Gravity tells a simple story – the story of a desperate struggle for survival… but it sets this story in the unforgiving environment of deep space. That the audience is so readily drawn into the tale it becomes easy for them to forget that large sections of the film were digitally created (and not actually filmed in space) is simply a testament to Cuaron’s skill and mastery.

The stage is set early on, with jaw-dropping visuals of our blue planet serving as a spectacular backdrop for the members of a shuttle crew who are about to realize a spaceman’s worst nightmare. A miscalculation with a non-working satellite has caused a debris field to start hurtling their way. When it arrives, their ride home is rendered useless.

Surviving the initial impact is Mission Commander Kowalski (George Clooney) and Mission Specialist Ryan Stone (Sandra Bullock). Adrift in space, with oxygen running low, options running out, and no one coming to their aid, the gravity of their situation is mind-numbingly clear.

While George Clooney provides solid support as a seasoned astronaut, demonstrating both confidence and conviviality, this is Sandra Bullock’s film to carry. Her work is remarkably effective while being at the same time physically grueling. Both she and Cuaron are well deserving of the accolades they have received for their contributions to this film.

The film, though it takes place in a unique setting that few of us will ever experience, does deal with a subject universally shared by man. The film asks us to consider the choices we make in the wake of adversity. It has often been said that, in life, we can’t always control what happens to us. What we can always control is our response to what happens.

Stone has a fundamental decision to make in this film. Does she continue on, struggling against massive odds in a harsh and deadly environment for the slim hope that she may return home? And if so, why? We don’t necessarily have to be drifting in space to be able to relate to her plight.

Many of us have found ourselves drifting, in a sense, through life here on earth. Beset by problems, troubles, and tragedies, we can begin to question if life is worth the pain or the difficulties we are experiencing – especially if we don’t immediately see a way out of our current situation.

How we respond at such a time depends to a large extent on how we view life itself. It is merely a happy accident or is it a purposeful design? If the latter, then recognizing our purpose within that design helps us to withstand the adversities we may encounter on the way to fulfilling that purpose. Of course, knowing that the One who purposed our lives is also willing and able to help us, gives us the hope that can strengthen our resolve.

“Blessed is the one who perseveres under trial because, having stood the test, that person will receive the crown of life that the Lord has promised to those who love him.” James 1:12 (NIV)
