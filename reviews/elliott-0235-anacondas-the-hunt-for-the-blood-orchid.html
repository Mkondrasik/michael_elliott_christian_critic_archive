Anacondas: The Hunt for the Blood Orchid

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 27, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Timothy 6:10Proverbs 1:10-19Luke 9:25

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love of money; greed; temptation; priorities; consequences

Author-verified transcription

If one humongous snake equals a decent box office draw in 1997 then, according to Hollywood logic, a whole bunch of humongous snakes will equal a greater box office draw in 2004. That's what the makers of Anacondas: The Hunt for the Blood Orchid believe. And far too often, they are proven to be correct which is why we keep getting films such as these.

In Anacondas, a team of scientists have just a few days to find a rare orchid that only blooms briefly once every seven years. The orchid contains the key ingredient to an abnormally long life. Unfortunately, the orchid can only be found in Borneo which also happens to be home to the biggest man-eating snakes this side of law school.

Because their scientific discovery is bound to make them all billionaires, the team ignores the dangers and hires the only riverboat captain foolish (or brave?) enough to take them upriver during the monsoon season in search of the elusive flower. When the boat (ironically named Bloody Mary) sinks, the captain's ominous warning about the jungle takes on new meaning. "Everything gets eaten out here." Just call them snake bait.

To be fair, if someone is interested in seeing a movie about gigantic snakes that are capable of swallowing a man whole I suppose they could do worse than Anacondas. It pretty much goes by the movie predator's handbook. A group of people - isolated in a hostile environment - trying to make their way to safety - getting picked off one by one. It doesn't help that in the group is one individual so blinded by the prospect of being rich that he is willing to sacrifice every other member of the expedition to the belly of the beast.

Director Dwight Little keeps the action moving along using a script written by a committee bent upon including every jungle danger known to cinema: In addition to the obviously CGI-generated titular predator, the expedition must also contend with white water, poisonous spiders, headhunters, and, of course, leeches.

The film uses a largely unknown cast that screams nicely and appears to be easily digestible. It also delivers a number of snake attack sequences that had the audience twittering in nervous laughter afterwards. They were silly, exaggerated scenes to be sure but moderately effective. Overall, it's a B-movie with a B-movie cast with no aspirations to be anything but that.

Johnny Messner, as the ex-Special Forces riverboat captain growls his lines with a macho gruffness. KaDee Strickland and Salli Richardson-Whitfield provide the PG-13 eye candy. Eugene Byrd brings some comic relief - albeit in a role Stepin Fetchit might have been embarrassed to play.

Of course, the reason why these college educated characters are in the middle of a jungle being chased by freakishly large reptiles in the first place has to do with money. Untold wealth can often persuade even the most intelligent of men to act stupidly.

For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows. 1 Timothy 6:10 (KJV)
