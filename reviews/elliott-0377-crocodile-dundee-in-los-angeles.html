Crocodile Dundee in Los Angeles

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 20, 2001
Rating
2 stars

Author-verified transcription

Crocodile Dundee in Los Angeles is everything one might expect from a 3rd installment of a popular film series. It is hardly an embarrassment for anyone involved, but it is not quite something to get excited about either.

Paul Hogan (Lightning Jack) returns as the leathery and legendary crocodile hunter, Mick Dundee. As in the original film, Mick is taken from his outback surroundings and placed in a much stranger environment... this time, Hollywood, California.

When his live-in girlfriend and mother of his child, Sue Charleton (Linda Kozlowski, Almost An Angel), flies to LA to temporarily take over the reins of her daddy's west coast newspaper, Mick and his young son Mikey (Serge Cockburn in his film debut) go along for the ride. Mick figures it is time that Mikey gets a taste of the big city.

Sue begins investigating a small film studio, the actions of which she deems suspicious. Of course, she is unable to prove any of her suspicions until Mick goes "undercover" as an extra on their latest project, "Lethal Agent 3." What follows is entirely predictable and only occasionally amusing.

It has been fifteen years since Hogan unleashed his Crocodile Dundee character and it appears that time hasn't changed him much. Or perhaps it has, because he doesn't seem to be as funny this time around. Then again, it may be because it all seems so familiar... Aussie outdoorsman in NY; Aussie outdoorsman in LA... Gets mugged in the original; gets mugged in the sequel... Can't figure out a bidet in the original; gets confused by a Jacuzzi tub in the sequel... The comparisons continue ad infinitum.

Paul Hogan deadpans his way through the slight but PG-acceptable script with a confidence born of one who has his character down pat. A lot of his naïveté, however, comes across as artificial. For example, when asked by a woman if he might be "gay," Mick replies, "Yeah, I'm happy most of the time." It's a bad joke that ran its course decades earlier.

Linda Kozlowski, who married Paul Hogan after starring with him in the original film, has little to do here. She offers few expressions and even less emotion in her role as the ex-journalist/mother who returns from her self-imposed outback exile to take the reins of a major paper.

Jere Burns (Dear John) plays Arnan Rothman, a stereotypically bad and arrogant villain who is foiled by the exploits of our hero from down under. Jonathan Banks (Flipper) is cast as Rothman's Eastern European henchman.

Director Simon Wincer, who last worked with Paul Hogan on the set of 1994's Lightning Jack, handles the picture like it was a paint by numbers kit.

He doesn't stray from what has been used in the past and by staying well within the lines of familiar Crocodile Dundee material, produces nothing that is of much interest. This is pure, unadulterated Hollywood sequel, B-movie quality at best. Been there... filmed that.

Yet, there is something about the Crocodile Dundee character that strikes a chord with people. His basic goodness, sense of fair play and no-pretense, confident manner is very appealing. He is what he is. His self-assured self-awareness in the midst of every situation is the very thing that draws people to the character. Perhaps that is an evangelical lesson for us.

For our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance; as ye know what manner of men we were among you for your sake. 1 Thessalonians 1:5 (KJV)

The godly qualities that we demonstrate in the course of our daily lives will often attract people long before we ever utter a word. If people are to hear and believe the truth that we have to share, they should also be able to see the light of that truth made manifest by how we conduct ourselves at all times. What manner of men do we determine ourselves to be?
