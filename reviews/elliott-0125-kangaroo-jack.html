Kangaroo Jack

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 17, 2003
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Friendship; accountability; encouragement; choosing friends wisely

Author-verified transcription

December is a giddy kind of month for film critics. As studios release and rerelease their best for award consideration, critics bask in the glow of one superb screening after another. And then January arrives and it’s like being hit in the face with a cold fish. Say hello to Kangaroo Jack.

Not that it’s a terrible film... but it pales in comparison to the award worthy offerings of weeks past. Come next December, this is not a picture we’ll be revisiting.

Jerry O’Connell (Buying the Cow), stars as Charlie, a Brooklyn hair stylist who has a mobster as his stepfather. When Louis (Anthony Anderson, Barbershop), his troublemaking best friend accidentally leads the police to the mob’s stolen goods warehouse, the two pals are given one last chance to redeem themselves in stepdad’s eyes.

All they have to do is deliver an envelope to a mysterious Mr. Smith. That Mr. Smith is in Australia and the envelope contains $50,000 is only a small concern. They will have much more than that to worry about after a CGI-enhanced kangaroo hops off with the cash.

This is a silly, silly story which has two bumbling fools chasing a wily kangaroo while even bigger, more dangerous fools with guns chase them. And let’s not forget the romance between our hairdresser and the beautiful game warden (Estella Warren, Driven) which occurs in the middle of the frenzied chase.

Kangaroo Jack suffers from a major marketing dilemma. Everything, from the title to the fake kangaroo, cries out for a children’s audience. Everything that is except the sexual innuendoes and bathroom humor. With content inappropriate for youngsters and a story too inane for grownups, the filmmakers seem to be targeting immature adults. Some may argue that this is a wide demographic but I prefer to have a more optimistic view of my fellow man.

Director David McNally (Coyote Ugly) tries to make this into a passible evening’s entertainment, and for some may reach a certain level of success... if one doesn’t set one’s expectations too high. Personally, I would have cut the camel flatulence and the fecal matter jokes but to each his own.

He’s clearly not working with the cream of the Hollywood talent pool. O’Connell is blandly affable, Anderson resorts to broad shtick, and Warren is little more than walking scenery. I must say that having Christopher Walken (Catch Me If You Can) in a small part certainly bumps up the credibility needle a notch or two. And he is funny here as a low class thug with a million dollar vocabulary.

Friendship seems to be the underlying theme of the film though a muddled kind of logic is used. Even though Louis continually gets Charlie in trouble with his hair-brained schemes, Charlie asserts that it is for this reason they’re friends... because being friends with Louis makes for some interesting stories.

Friendship should be based on something far deeper. Interesting story or not, a “friend” who continually draws us into illegal or immoral practices is no friend at all. Job was all too familiar with the kind of faithless friend that Louis was turning out to be. But instead of blindly going along with his friends’ foolishness, Job was able to stand up against them and reprove them.

Yea, ye overwhelm the fatherless, and ye dig a pit for your friend. Job 6:27 (KJV)

A true friend is one who will strengthen, support, and encourage us toward worthwhile endeavors and righteous actions. Just as we should be doing for him or her. Anyone who tries to persuade us from doing what we know to be right is no friend at all.
