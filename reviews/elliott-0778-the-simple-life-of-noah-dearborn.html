Author-verified transcription

Finding a truly memorable character in films is rarer than one might think. The ones that do happen along should be treasured and enjoyed, especially those that come from unexpected sources. The Simple Life of Noah Dearborn, a 1999 made for TV movie which aired on CBS in May, contains one such character. With Trimark Pictures releasing the video this month, we are granted a second opportunity not to miss this sweet and affecting gem of a story which is told with such genuine care.

Noah Dearborn (Sidney Poitier, The Jackal) is something of a "carpenter god" and a small town secret. Insulated from the modern world and its ways, he remains intensely private, supremely focused, fervently passionate regarding his work, and admirably moral in the way he chooses to live life and interact with people. In short, Noah is a refreshing breath of clean good air in a world polluted by the foulness of cynicism, greed, and apathy. In his ninety-first year, he still has the health, vigor, and appearance of a man in his fifties, something he attributes to the discipline of his life and the love he has for his work.

When a land developer (George Newburn, Friends & Lovers) wants Noah's property for the site of a proposed shopping mall, he chooses to interpret Noah's refusal of his generous offer as a sign of senility and looks to have him committed, an act that will award his property to the care of the state.

It is up to visiting psychologist Valerie Crane (Mary-Louise Parker, Goodbye Lover) to protect Noah against the determined forces of "progress" and the self-interests which have placed their desires above Noah's rights.

Director Gregg Champion (The Cowboy Way) maintains an honest, small-town feel about the production which enhances the charm and old-fashioned values which the film exhibits.

Sidney Poitier's presence in the film is strongly felt. As is said about Noah, it is as if everything revolves around him. His silent strength, emotional control, and depth of character serves the film and its message beautifully. The pearls of wisdom given to his character ("What good is wanting to do something without going ahead and doing it?") Are delivered with such a simple conviction, they might as well have been carved in stone and handed down from the mount.

Dianne Wiest (Practical Magic), in a role which garnered for her an Emmy nomination, lends her support as Sarah McClellan, a townswoman who has long been aware of Noah's special qualities.

Mary-Louise Parker is also on hand as the girlfriend of the land developer who is called upon to use her position of psychologist to help her beau, only to be drawn to Noah and his simple life and outlook. She brings an appropriate genuineness and softness which fits well with this family oriented film.

It is undeniable that the soft-spoken Noah Dearborn has an impact on the people with whom he comes into contact.

It is an impact that comes not from a boisterous, larger than life persona, nor from a verbal eloquence. Noah's impact is the simple life he leads with such conviction and faithfulness. His "witness" is heard loudly, without his ever having to speak a word. His actions tell his story.

Just the work ethic he demonstrates by the care and precision he puts into his craft is a glaring condemnation of the shoddy workmanship most of us are accustomed to seeing in our modern world of "conveniences." Noah's approach to life is a reminder to us that when you love what you do, what you do reflects that love.

When we love God, we show our love by keeping His commandments. Here's a commandment that is wonderfully appropriate to the film:

Study to show thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth. 2 Timothy 2:15 [KJV]

The workmen who need not be ashamed are the ones who apply the diligent effort needed to ensure their work is being done properly, meeting the standards set by those who have commissioned them. As Christians, only until we apply this truth to our lives and walk according to God's Will do we truly shine as His lights in this world. It is a simplistic concept, but as the movie shows, simple oftentimes is best.
