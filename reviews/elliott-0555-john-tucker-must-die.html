Author-verified transcription

John Tucker Must Die. The rest of us only have to suffer through it. It is a sex-minded teen comedy about wronged girls who band together to destroy the man/boy who cheated on them. In the process they learn… well, nothing much.

Kate (Brittany Snow) is the perpetual “new girl in school.” She has to be because she’s forced to change towns every time a guy skips out on her tramp of a mom (Jenny McCarthy). From the looks of things, they may as well keep a moving van parked in their driveway.

When she arrives at her latest school, she quickly picks up on the fact that the most popular guy, John Tucker (Jesse Metcalf), is dating three different girls and lying to all three of them. When his “playa” ways eventually come to light, Kate helps the revenge-minded trio figure out a way to give Tucker a taste of his own medicine. They set out to first emasculate him and then to break his heart using for bait the cute but inexperienced Kate.

The emasculation doesn’t work as planned. Tucker’s popularity is so great that any embarrassing situation they put him in is turned to his advantage. They have better success with Plan B… that is until Kate starts to grow a conscience. It’s too little too late to matter – but this film isn’t really concerned about delivering a moral. It is simply looking for laughs in all the same places.

John Tucker Must Die doesn’t have the social awareness or “hip factor” of a Mean Girls. It is content to simply be a frivolous film awash in sexual references and innuendos.

The one saving grace - small as it may be - is the fact that the film contains a likable cast. Attractive, funny, non-threatening (unless viewed as role models) – they make the most of what they are given even though they aren’t given much of anything.

Jesse Metcalf plays the object of everyone’s ire/desire. As Tucker, he’s arrogant, confident, manipulative, athletic, and totally one-dimensional. His girlfriends have the same problem but since there are so many of them blended together they give the appearance of having depth.

Ashanti plays Heather, the cocky, brash head cheerleader. Sophia Bush plays Beth, the cocky, brash, tree-hugging activist. Arielle Kebbel plays Carrie, the cocky, brash extra-curricular joiner. Playing characters that are pretty much cut from the same cloth, they don’t offer much in terms of development but they have a nice energy and are easy to watch.

The same can be said of Brittany Snow who, as the central figure in the film, has more to do and more opportunity to grow as a character. She does a decent job in portraying a character as she goes from a shy and unpopular nobody to a cocky and brash Miss Somebody - but she too is limited by the sophomoric script by Jeff Lowell.

The character of John Tucker does illustrate an oddity we can often see within our own society. Tucker, as blatantly egotistical and self-serving as he may be, remains an object of adulation for those in his school.

There exists a human tendency to respect success – regardless of how that success was achieved or maintained. We have “role models” operating in our world today whose behavior leaves much to be desired. God suggests that there are better role models to be found.

Let not thine heart envy sinners: but be thou in the fear (respect) of the LORD all the day long. Proverbs 23:17 (KJV)

This world is filled with John Tuckers. The way to deal with them is not to try to beat them at their own game. It is simply to ignore them and let them play by themselves.
