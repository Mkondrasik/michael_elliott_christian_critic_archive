The Core

Author-verified transcription

I once heard an old theatrical "urban legend" about how the original intent behind Arsenic and Old Lace was to produce a play of intrigue and suspense. When performing it to test audiences, it was discovered that instead of shocks, it was generating laughter. The playwright chose to rework the play and eventually turned it into one of the most successful comedies of the 20th century.

I don't know whether that story is true or not, but perhaps the people responsible for The Core might consider doing the same thing. What they have put up on the screen is the most unintentionally funny film that I've seen in years. The problem is that we're laughing at it instead of with it.

"Armeggedon meets Journey to the Center of the Earth" could have been the "pitch" used to sell this film to the studio. In the film, it appears that the earth's core has stopped spinning which means that the electromagnetic field which protects our planet from all sorts of natural disasters will very soon cease to exist. Within a year, all life will come to an end. The planet's only hope is a ragtag team of quirky individuals who volunteer to burrow through rock and molten rock to reach the core of the earth and restart the planet using nuclear explosives.

Aaron Eckhardt (Erin Brockovich) stars as Dr. Josh Keyes, a wise-cracking professor who first discovers the coming planetary destruction. Fortunately, he's also developed a method to see through dense matter. A hermit scientist (Delroy Lindo, The One) has built Virgil, a machine that can tunnel through rock. By putting the two inventions together, adding a nuclear scientist (Tcheky Karyo, The Patriot) to deploy the weapons, and training a couple astronauts (Hilary Swank, Insomnia and Bruce Greenwood, Thirteen Days) on how to travel in the opposite direction, a save-the-planet rescue mission is launched (or should I say plunged?)

Also on board is a scientist (Stanley Tucci, Maid in Manhattan) whose ego is only surpassed by the lengths he will go to remain in the spotlight. Remaining on the surface is a teenaged computer hacker (DJ Qualls, The New Guy) whose task is to keep the earth's population from realizing that their planet is doomed. He does this, despite nature's destruction of Rome's Collesium and San Francisco's Golden Gate Bridge, by controlling the flow of information on the Internet. Right. Now if only he could stop the spam from reaching my e-mail.

What makes this big budget action picture so laughable, aside from its ludricrous premise, is the unapologetic way it employs the cliches of the genre. This is a film that the audience can script as they incredulously watch the filmmakers take the story in directions even they wouldn't dare go.

The Core also contains what must be the most ill-timed sequence in movie history. The disruption in the earth's electromagnetic field causes a problem with the re-entry of a returning space shuttle. While the scene ends happily, it can't help but put us in mind of recent tragic events involving the US space program. The real life tragedy therefore becomes a point of reference which continually comes to our mind as we watch this disaster-riddled film.

What is impressive is the film's explanation of the earth's design. We know that God formed this planet for the expressed purpose of holding life. Seeing the intricacy and complexity of that design - especially how God brought order out of chaos - only increases our appreciation and awe for what He has done.

For thus saith the LORD that created the heavens; God himself that formed the earth and made it; he hath established it, he created it not in vain, he formed it to be inhabited: I am the LORD; and there is none else. Isaiah 45:18 (KJV)
