Taxi

Author-verified transcription

Luc Besson's 1988 screenplay of the French comedy Taxi, proved so successful that it spawned two sequels. We should hope that the same will not prove true for the new American remake of the same name.

Queen Latifah stars as a daredevil bicycle messenger who dreams of one day becoming a daredevil NYC cabdriver. It is the next step on the road of one day becoming a NASCAR driver. Having ridden in many NY cabs, I can say that this about the most realistic element to the film.

Jimmy Fallon is the most inept cop in the New York Police Department. Among his many deficiencies, he may be the worst driver since Mr. Magoo. After his most recent vehicular debacle, his sexy lieutenant and ex-girlfriend (Jennifer Esposito) takes his license away and forces him to walk a beat.

When a band of beautiful swimsuit models (led by Gisele Bundchen) begin a crime spree, Fallon commandeers Latifah's cab and together they set out in hot pursuit. The more they try to catch the bad girls, the more trouble they get into. The more trouble they get into, the harder they try to catch the bad girls. It's a vicious comedic cycle and we're caught in the middle of it.

There's plenty of high speed action as Latifah's supercharged funny cab and Gisele's modified BMW 760 use New York streets as their own personal Grand Prix track.

To say that Queen Latifah rises above the banality of this project would be doing her an injustice. She wades right through it but amazingly somehow remains untouched and unfazed by the experience. She has what used to be known as "star power" and it has never served her better. She shines even though everything around her is tarnished beyond salvage.

That would include her costar, Jimmy Fallon. Not as funny as he would like to think he is, his character of Washburn is more annoying than anything else. Whether he's adopting a ridiculous Halloween costume to go undercover or overplaying his ineptitude and lack of "motor skills," he manages to throw the film's tone completely out of balance. He doesn't seem to have settled on what kind of film he's in: farce, crime, comedy, spoof... and so he plays all of them at various times... none to great success.

Jennifer Esposito is unfortunately miscast as Fallon's boss. Their scenes together simply don't work. Ann-Margaret has some amusing moments as Fallon's ditzy and alcoholic mother but there's no place to go with the character.

For people who think that 2 Fast 2 Furious was a documentary and for anyone who owns a calendar featuring hot women with hot cars - this film might be a fun and entertaining diversion. The rest of us might consider renting the original French film.

Latifah tries to help Fallon's character improve his driving by encouraging him to loosen up. "You're trying too hard," she explains. It is difficult to succeed at anything if we are tense, anxious and troubled in our heart. It has been said that the "greatest cargoes in life come in over quiet seas." An ocean liner carrying precious cargo will not try to cross treacherous and unstable waters. If we take the time to quiet our souls so we might reach a state of peace and tranquility before we take action, our rate of success will increase dramatically.

Better is an handful with quietness, than both the hands full with travail and vexation of spirit. Ecclesiastes 4:6 (KJV)
