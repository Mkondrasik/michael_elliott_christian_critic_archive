Reign of Fire

Author-verified transcription

Dragons rule the skies and scorch the earth in the latest Touchstone Pictures' release, Reign of Fire. This time, however, the dragon infestation is not one for medieval kings and their valiant knights to vanquish. These dragons begin their destruction in present day London and by the year 2020, they have pretty much turned the planet to ash.

No one really knows how many humans have survived. Communications are spotty and travel is next to impossible. Those that remain alive do so by hiding in small, heavily fortified communities. Quinn (Christian Bale, American Psycho) is the leader of one such group and keeps his people protected by teaching them to run and hide whenever these flying, fire-breathing creatures appear. His theory is that once their food supply is depleted, the dragons will eventually starve themselves to death. All humans have to do is to wait them out.

Into his sheltered world comes an opposing viewpoint. Van Zan (Matthew McConaughey, The Wedding Planner), a hairless, tattooed, cigar-chomping American freedom fighter, brings his armored militia to the gates of Quinn's isolated castle. Van Zan is not one to wait for the dragons to die off. Not when he can help the process along.

Van Zan's aggressive "dragon-slaying" tactics run at odds with Quinn's passively protective mode. It isn't long before these two vie for leadership of the community. Of course, whoever wins will most likely shape the future of human civilization... That is, if human civilization will have a future.

Director Rob Bowman (The X-Files) uses the dragons much like Spielberg did the shark in Jaws. Knowing that the suggestion is more frightening than the actual depiction, Bowman, for much of the film, doesn't give us a real good look at his CGI-manufactured beasts, focusing instead on the actions and reactions of the humans on the ground.

Those reactions fall neatly into two categories: Those who run from certain death and those who run toward it in an attempt to defeat it. Quinn's reluctance to face and fight the monstrous creatures may stem from the fact that, as a young lad, he was present when the first one awakened. As played by Bale, being the sole survivor of that incident, Quinn has seen first hand the destructive power of the dragons and considers them as virtually invincible.

Van Zan knows better. Using a combination of heavy artillery and human bait, Van Zan knows how to kill the creatures. It is just that at the rate he's going, he'll run out of soldiers long before he'll run out of dragons to slay. McConaughey departs from his typical leading man persona to tackle this extremely physical and brutal character.

Providing support is Gerard Butler (Dracula 2000) as Creedy, Quinn's best friend and confidante. His easy going manner and quick wit account for most of the humor in the film. Also on board is Izabella Scorupco (Vertical Limit) as Alex, one of Van Zan's "soldiers" and an accomplished helicopter pilot.

There are numerous biblical references sprinkled throughout the script: Alex's paratroopers who do battle against the dragons are called "archangels;" graffiti on an exit sign near the dragon's lair indicates "666" which is the number of the beast according to Revelation 13:18. The references should come as no surprise. Dragons have long been associated with the devil and are mentioned in both the Old and New Testaments in this context.

And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him. Revelation 12:9 (KJV)

By comparing the dragons' behavior in this film with what we know of the nature of our spiritual adversary, we can draw many parallels. The dragons feed on death or ashes -- the devil holds the power of death. Both the dragons and the devil instill fear into the hearts of their victims.

But best of all, the film shows that humans can be victorious over the dragons just as we can claim victory over the devil. As we resist, he must flee.
