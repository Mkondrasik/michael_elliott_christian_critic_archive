Keeping The Faith

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 14, 2000
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Psalms 61:82 Corinthians 4:16Romans 12:2

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Commitment; faithfulness; renewing the mind; spiritual discipline

Author-verified transcription

For all the jokes that begin with “A rabbi and a priest (walk into a bar, are playing golf, have died and gone to heaven...) it is surprising that the same comedic premise hasn’t been fully capitalized by the film industry. That is, until now. KEEPING THE FAITH humorously explores what it might be like if a priest and a rabbi were not only best friends, but romantic rivals.

Edward Norton (Fight Club) and Ben Stiller (Mystery Men) star as Father Brian Finn and Rabbi Jake Schram, boyhood pals who maintain their close friendship as they grow up and follow their different religious paths. Both being assigned to NYC, they share an irreverent manner designed to “shake up” their respective congregations in order to minister to them. With sermons which more closely resemble standup comedy routines, they are nicknamed “The God Squad” by the young people of their neighborhood. Their dream is to build an interfaith community center (complete with karaoke machine) where Jews and Roman Catholics can intermingle in peace and harmony amidst really bad pop music.

Enter Anna Reilly (Jenna Elfman, EdTV), the tomboy of their memories who has grown to become the girl of their dreams. As children they were inseparable. Now a high-powered corporate executive in town on some business venture, she decides to look the boys up and in so doing begins a crisis of faith for both.

For Father Brian, her presence leads him to doubt his commitment to his calling, not to mention to his vow of chastity. For Rabbi Jake, who is free to marry, it is the fact that Anna Reilly is not the sort of name belonging to the nice Jewish girl his people (especially his stereotypical Jewish mother) will accept.

Edward Norton, in his directorial debut, wisely keeps the focus away from the diametrically opposed doctrine of the two religions and concentrates instead on the similarities of the men who stay friends despite it. Mr. Norton proves himself to be as confident a director as he is an actor. The comic touches laced throughout the film are well timed and quite amusing.

Especially funny are the scenes depicting the two young spiritual leaders learning the rituals of their respective religions. Rabbi Jake faints while attending his first bris, and Father Brian gives new meaning to the phrase “holy smoke” by setting his vestment on fire during a mishap with a wayward censer. Still, the two learn the “ropes” and develop strong followings through their dynamically hip and populist approach.

Mr. Norton is given an opportunity to play light comedy (complete with pratfalls and sight gags) which he performs with the same impressive adroitness found in his dramatic work. Mr. Stiller is well cast as the single rabbi whom every Jewish mother in town wants as a son-in-law. And as the goy next door, Ms. Elfman is attractive, amusing, and fully believable.

In addition, Anne Bancroft (G.I. Jane) has many nice moments as Rabbi Jake’s mother and Ken Leung (Rush Hour) steals a scene or two as Don, an enthusiastic karaoke salesman.

Obviously, the love triangle at the heart of the picture is an unusual one. And it is one that rocks the central characters’ core beliefs. A spiritual truth is spoken to Father Brian as he seeks counsel to understand what his feelings for Anna might mean to his religious commitment. Father Havel, played by Milos Forman (who directed Edward Norton in The People vs. Larry Flint), reminds Brian that: “Commitment is a choice you keep making again and again.”

Life is a continual process to which there are no shortcuts. The world is full of temptations and a backwards collar or perfect attendance at Sunday School does not release us from our obligation to renew our minds daily to the commitments that we have made to God.

So will I sing praise unto thy name for ever, that I may daily perform my vows. Psalms 61:8 (KJV)

Whether a priest, rabbi, or simply a disciple, as children of God our role is to renew our minds daily to live according to the commitment we’ve made in our heart to serve Him.
