Robots

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 27, 2005
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 31 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Inner Worth; Need; Action

Author-verified transcription

Pixar has set the bar for computer animated films so high that its competitors have no choice but to limbo under it. Blue Sky (the folks that gave us Ice Age) continues to excel technically but, much like the characters in its latest project, it lacks the narrative soul needed to do justice to its 3D artwork.

Robots is a wild ride in a world entirely peopled by machines. Rodney Copperbottom (voiced by Ewan MacGregor) is a young "bot" from Rivet Town who dreams of being an inventor. Son of a lowly dishwasher, Rodney may be comprised of hand-me-down parts but he believes in the motto of his hero, Big Weld (voiced by Mel Brooks). "You can shine no matter what you're made of."

Rodney leaves Rivet Town with his Wonderbot invention in hand to pay a visit to Robot City and Big Weld. What he finds there is as disappointing and disheartening as discovering the early signs of rust.

Big Weld is no where to be seen. Robot Town is now being run by Ratchet (Greg Kinnear), a shiny corporate-minded bot who eliminates the spare parts policy of the society in favor of more expensive and profitable upgrades. Robots like Copperbottom's parents, who can't afford the upgrades are labeled "outmodes" and will be destined for the scrap heap. Rodney and his new "outmoded" friends must band together and try to stop Ratchet and his evil (Manchurian Candidate-like) mother Madame Gasket (voiced by Jim Broadbent).

A lot of stars add their vocal talents to the project but with only one exception none are particularly noteworthy. The standout is the human cartoon Robin Williams in yet another outrageous animated characterization. He plays Fender, a robot with more than one loose screw who befriends Rodney upon his arrival in Robot City. Other names in the cast include Halle Berry as Rodney's love interest; Jennifer Coolidge as a big bottomed "bot"; Amanda Bynes and Drew Carey as two of the "outmodes" who are on Rodney's side; and Stanley Tucci as Rodney's understanding father.

The jokes and sight gags come fast and furious... too fast to register them all with a single viewing. And yet there isn't much else that compels one to sit through the film for a second time. There are a number of clever moments but too often the film relies on the kind of bathroom humor that requires no wit or intelligence.

There are two adages coined in the film that can be equated to scriptural truths. The first - "You can shine no matter what you're made of" - is the motto of Robot Town and a reminder that greatness is not dependent upon outward appearances. The same is true regarding God's creation. The best part of us is what we carry inside.

But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price. 1 Peter 3:4 (KJV)

The second statement is one that spurs Rodney to invent: "See a need - fill a need." The exhortation is that if a deficiency of any kind exists, instead of complaining about it or suffering through it, we should take action to address it and solve the problem.

God promises us that he will meet all our needs. As we identify what those needs are we should find the promise in God's Word that addresses the need and believe that he will honor His promise to us.

But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus. Philippians 4:19 (KJV)
