Author-verified transcription

So, where does the proverbial 2,000 pound gorilla sleep? Anywhere Charlize Theron tells him. That is apparently the new punch line to the old joke, according to the makers of Mighty Joe Young.

Mighty Joe Young is Disney's latest live action feature film directed towards a younger audience. A remake of the 1949 classic starring Terry Moore (who appears here in a short cameo), this 1998 version features improved digitized graphics and superior special effects.

Dr. Ruth Young (Linda Purl, Natural Causes), noted anthropologist, and her young daughter, Jill are in the dark continent of Africa studying a band of gorillas. One in particular, nicknamed Joe, appears to be growing at an incredible rate.

One night, a group of poachers infiltrates the camp. In trying to protect the gorillas, Dr. Young is shot and killed. So is Joe's mother, after which Joe bites the thumb and forefinger off the hand of the evil poacher. With both Jill and Joe now orphaned, Jill keeps a promise to her mother that she will always take care of him.

We jump twelve years into the future to see what an enormous promise that has become. No longer a cute, slightly oversized monkey, Joe is now as big as a truck and three times harder to control... For everyone, that is, except Jill. There must have been a lot of bonding going on during those 12 years because this ape turns into a pussycat whenever he's around her.

Not that he's got bad taste. Charlize Theron (Celebrity) is one fine looking primate. And as an actress, she certainly carries herself better in her monkey movie than did Jessica Lange some 22 years ago in the remake of King Kong.

Joe isn't the only one to notice that Jill is special. Gregg O'Hara (Bill Paxton, Titanic) is a field operative for an animal preserve who discovers Joe and Jill playing hide and seek in the forest. Joe hides by closing his eyes, figuring what he can't see can't see him.) Gregg is awed by both of them. With poachers hot on the trail, he convinces her that Joe would be safer in Los Angeles. (Obviously he hasn't driven the freeways there).

An animal the size of Joe doesn't stay a secret for long. The news of a giant gorilla reaches even Africa, where the now three-fingered poacher (Rade Serbedzijia, Polish Wedding) plots his vengeance on the monster that maimed him.

For such an implausible movie, Mighty Joe Young is still entertaining enough to appeal to its younger audiences. Director Ron Underwood (Heart and Souls) keeps the action flowing smoothly and the special effects he uses to present the illusion of a fifteen foot gorilla are quite effective.

It is a good thing, because Joe is the star of this film. While Miss Theron and Mr. Paxton do admirable work, it is tough to steal focus from the "big guy," which is Jill's playful nickname for Joe.

Watching how servile the gorilla became in the presence of his childhood human “friend” should have reminded us of the order of the animal kingdom which God ordained:

And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth. Genesis 1:28 [KJV]

Sometimes, in our politically correct society, we can lose sight of God's ordered universe. The extreme members of the misnamed “animal rights” movement tend to forget that God commanded man to have dominion over the animal kingdom... that He made them to bless and benefit us.

To demand “rights” for animals... rights which God Himself did not bestow, is simply an exercise in human arrogance.
