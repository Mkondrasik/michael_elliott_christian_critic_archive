Author-verified transcription

One of the most popular characters ever to have wagged an animated tail gets the big screen CGI treatment as Scooby Doo comes to theaters nationwide. Making the transition from Saturday morning cartoon to live action feature film is not always easy (nor successful) and even despite a game cast, this Scooby is more "don't" than "doo."

All of the familiar characters of Mystery Inc. are on board: Fred (Freddie Prinze Jr., Summer Catch), the good-looking but often vapid "leader" of the group; Velma (Linda Cardellini, Legally Blonde), the plain Jane in the turtleneck sweater who is the true brains of the operation; Daphne (Sarah Michelle Gellar, Buffy the Vampire Slayer), miniskirt-wearing babe whose main contribution lies in getting the bad guy to kidnap her; and Shaggy (Matthew Lillard, Thirteen Ghosts), who burns more brain cells in figuring out what to eat next than in solving any mystery. Then of course there's Scooby himself, a Great Dane with a great appetite, a great speech impediment, and a great fear of all things spooky.

After splitting up in the first few minutes of the film, the members of Mystery Inc. find themselves drawn back together when they receive a mysterious invitation to Spooky Island, a popular spring break retreat for rambunctious college students. The thing is, those same students are not so rambunctious when they leave and that has got the theme park mogul, Mondavarious (Rowan Atkinson, Rat Race) worried about some kind of curse being cast upon his guests.

Of course, things are never what they appear and our wacky pack of heroes fumble their way through the clues until they face a few monsters, uncover a few secrets, and solve the great mystery.

While it is tough to base a three dimensional characterization upon a drawn cartoon, Matthew Lillard pretty much nails the persona of Shaggy. Lanky, facially expressive, vocally raspy, Lillard gives dimension to the Shagster while respecting his character's animated roots. Linda Cardellini also excels in her performance as the under appreciated Velma. Freddie Prinze Jr. and Sarah Michelle Gellar don't distract from the proceedings but also don't bring anything particularly special to the table.

Director Raja Gosnell (Never Been Kissed) keeps things light and frivolous, although it would appear that the target audience he is interested in attracting are those who have grown up with Scooby rather than today's youngsters. I can't imagine any producer or director purposely including as much cleavage as fills this screen with the intent of attracting the under ten set.

There is a considerable amount of adult "eye candy" on display for a film that is based on a children's cartoon. But then there is also an elongated scene involving a gas emission contest between Shaggy and Scooby. Such a combination indicates that the filmmakers are looking to appeal to a sexually aware but somewhat immature audience. The filmmakers have also included a few not-so-subtle drug references, presumably as an inside joke to fans of the TV show which some viewers have long claimed contains subliminal drug-friendly messages.

What may disappoint many long time fans is the filmmakers' departure from the series' original concept where all supernatural mysteries have a logical and natural explanation such as an old guy in a plastic mask. Not so with the film. Protoplasm flies freely, monsters wreak havoc, and logic has precious little to do with the story.

Early on in the film, the clue gathering team of Mystery Inc. disbands because each individual selfishly put themselves and their own personal goals ahead of the group. Their past successes and all the experience they gained went for naught because they failed to operate a simple biblical principle:

"If I have the gift of prophecy and can fathom all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have a faith that can move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing." 1 Corinthians 13:2 (NIV)
