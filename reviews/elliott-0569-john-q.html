Author-verified transcription

Health care is, admittedly, a hot button issue. The extensive red tape, the exorbitantly high costs, and the life or death stakes which are a part of it are the essential ingredients which add up to the stuff of high drama… or, in the case of John Q, higher than high melodrama.

We first meet John Q. Archibald (Denzel Washington, Training Day) as his wife’s car is being repossessed. A factory worker whose hours have been cut due to slow economic times, he is one of millions of Americans who survive this life one paycheck at a time. He is also a loving husband and father, the latter of which is about to be tested beyond all reason.

When his son (Daniel E. Smith, in his film debut) collapses at a ball game and is diagnosed with an irreparable heart ailment, John enters a foreign world called HMO that is low on compassion and high on rules or regulations.

Because his HMO doesn’t cover the catastrophic care of a heart transplant, which is the only procedure that will save his son, John must either come up with a quarter of a million dollars or take his son home to die. Neither option being acceptable to him, John invents a third: he will hold the hospital hostage until his son is placed on the recipient list for available hearts.

Denzel Washington gives a heartfelt performance of a father pushed to the brink and driven to extreme measures to save his only son. Even with the heavy-handed “preachiness” of the script and its one-sided moralizing, Washington’s portrayal is never anything less than honest and genuine.

The same cannot be said for his supporting cast, particularly the “bad guys” of the film. Those would be the characters presenting the medical industry’s position. Anne Heche (Six Days, Seven Nights), as the hospital administrator is as heartless as a donor after the procedure. Her cold and unsympathetic manner is so inhumanly abhorrent that it reads as unbelievable. A softer approach would have done more to expose the true difficulties in balancing costs vs. universal care.

James Woods (Any Given Sunday) is the cardiac specialist whose talent could save young Mikey if the price is right. Ray Liotta (Blow) is a publicity seeking police chief who bungles the negotiations out of a desire to end the standoff quickly. Robert Duvall is a veteran police hostage negotiator who doesn’t have much to do except be a voice of reason in an unreasonable situation.

Director Nick Cassavetes (She’s So Lovely) and screenwriter James Kearns stack the deck so obviously in favor of the common man who is “doing battle” against the faceless entity known as the evil HMO that they actually weaken the points being made. To honestly address the flaws inherent in how medical aid is made available to American workers, a more balanced or fair portrayal of both sides will be needed.

What does shine through the all too obvious propagandizing from Cassavetes and crew is the love that this father has for his son. His actions, though illegal and extreme, are motivated by a single overwhelming truth… he will do whatever he must so that his son will live.

God’s fatherly love towards us is no less intense. And no matter what situation we face, he will do whatever he must, within the confines of his own righteousness, to provide for us a solution.

Our responsibility is to act according to His Word in every situation, trusting in Him and staying peaceful so that we might hear His still, small voice if and when it comes.
