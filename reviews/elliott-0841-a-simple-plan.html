A Simple Plan

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 22, 1999
Rating
2 stars
Running time
2 Hr: 1 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Corinthians 6:14-7:11 Timothy 6:10Proverbs 1:15-19

Sermon / spiritual topic: Temptation; Greed; Love of Money

Author-verified transcription

Simple minds make simple plans. The trouble is, simple plans have a bad way of getting complicated... real fast. When Hank, Jacob and Lou find a bag containing four and one half million dollars in the woods, their simple plan looks foolproof. Sit on the money, see if anyone is looking for it, if they are - burn it. If they aren't - then it must be okay to keep. That decision, once made, sets in motion a series of catastrophic events that threaten the trio with increasingly severe consequences.

A Simple Plan, on one level, is actually painful to watch. We feel for these characters who, driven by the lust of money, start on a downward moral spiral that, once begun, looks impossible to stop.

Scott Smith, adapting the screenplay from his own novel, is not merely content to ask a moral question, he shows us the result of answering that question incorrectly.

Bill Paxton (Twister) plays the central character, Hank. He's the one who appears to be smart enough to know better. If he isn't, at least he is married to a bright and attractive woman (Bridget Fonda, Point of No Return) who should be able to keep her man honest. When speaking theoretically, both of these decent people insist on turning the money over to the authorities. To do anything else would be stealing. But when faced with the reality of a coffee table covered with bundles of hundred dollar bills, theory goes flying out the window and greed comes rushing through the door.

Billy Bob Thornton (Slingblade) plays Jacob, Hank's mentally slower (and somewhat socially deficient) brother. If Mr. Thornton does not receive an Academy Award nomination for his work in this movie, there is something seriously wrong with the process. It is truly a remarkable performance.

Director Sam Raimi (The Quick and the Dead) lets the movie plod along at a slow and methodical pace which serves to emphasize the tragedy as we watch, in excruciating detail, their "simple plan" unravel.

Spiritually, it is easy to see that everything going wrong in these characters' lives can be traced back to the moment when they gave in to the temptation of keeping money that didn't belong to them. That one decision opened a door that should have stayed closed.

Each of us face temptation every day. We must remember that it never comes from God. The ability to resist temptation is of God but the temptation itself is always from the devil.

If we choose (and it is always our choice) to give in to temptation, it means that we give the devil access to our lives. Look at what happens to Hank, Jacob, and Lou: Because of their desire for this ill-gotten wealth, they condone or excuse lying, stealing, extortion, murder, and suicide. The biblical truth that "the love of money is the root of all evil" is once again vividly depicted in this film.

Jacob, near the end of the movie raises a pointed question of Hank when he asks, "Do you ever feel evil?" In the context of the actions which these characters have taken, it is a chilling question. Evil is a spiritual reality that can have a physical presence. It is not something that any of us ever want to feel. If evil is within us, the power of God is not. They cannot coexist. God is all light. Evil is pure darkness. Where there is light, there is no darkness and where there is darkness, light does not exist.

The simple plan of Hank, Lou, and Jacob failed miserably and had devastating results because it was based on an ungodly desire. The moral questions that arise in life do not have to be difficult ones to answer. God has already provided all the answers for us. All we have to do is follow His plan. It may not always be easy, but it couldn't be simpler.
