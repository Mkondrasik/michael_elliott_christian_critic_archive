Author-verified transcription

A no name cast performs a script by two no name screenwriters for a film by a no name director. After seeing The In Crowd, I would hope for their sakes that they all remain as anonymous. This is not a picture for which one should seek credit.

Adrien Williams (Lori Heuring, The Newton Boys) manages to get released from the mental institution which has been holding her. Her release is conditional upon the usual terms: She cannot leave the county and she has to keep her job at the posh country club where she rubs elbows with the wealthy and snooty patrons.

The wealthiest and snootiest of these is Brittany Foster (Susan Ward, All My Children), the queen of "the royal swine" who, for reasons undisclosed, takes Adrien under her well-appointed wing and introduces her to the lifestyles of the rich and fatuous. All well and good until it becomes apparent that Brittany has a secret motivation for being nice to Adrien... so secret in fact that the movie never expresses it.

It must be however, a secret to die for because a few of these affluent and hot hard bodies will turn cold by the end of the film. At least, I think so. The lighting is so poor during the action sequences that it is sometimes difficult to tell exactly what is going on.

Ms. Ward is pretty to look at but should know that lip gloss does not a character make. Ms. Heuring fares only slightly better, giving us plenty of attitude to make up for the lack of character depth.

Director Mary Lambert, who helmed both of the Pet Sementary films, gets lost in this "crowd," which has more in common with the TV series Baywatch than any kind of film mystery/thriller.

The script is beyond weak, the acting mediocre, and the direction near laughable. It is the actors' good looks that are used to try to carry the picture, and it is that high gloss look which is featured prominently in the film's marketing strategy. So what can we learn from this attractively packaged mess that will benefit us in the long run?

Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye are like unto whited sepulchres, which indeed appear beautiful outward, but are within full of dead men's bones, and of all uncleanness. Matthew 23:27 (KJV)

Or to quote a secular phrase: "Don't judge a book by its cover." The rich and cliquish "in-crowd" gave the appearance of being successful and happy, but their lives prove to be rather empty and worthless when a closer look is taken. So it is with the film itself. A cast full of tanned and sculpted bodies cannot long disguise the fact that there is nothing "in" The In Crowd. It is all superficial show.
