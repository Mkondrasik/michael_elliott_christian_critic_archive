The fellowship of the ring has finally come to a close and this much can be said: Not only is this epic film the leading contender for best picture of the year, director Peter Jackson may very well have just completed the best movie trilogy ever made.

Clocking in at a healthy 3 hours and 30 minutes, The Return of the King more than delivers on the storyline begun two years ago. None of the major characters get shortchanged.

Hobbits Frodo (Elijah Wood) and Sam (Sean Astin) continue their journey to Mount Doom to finally rid the world of the evils contained in one ring. Leading them is the schizophrenic creature Smeagol/Gollum (Andy Serkis), who has been forever affected by the time he spent bearing the ring.

Meanwhile, the battle for Middle Earth continues with the human Aragorn (Viggo Mortensen) finally accepting the responsibility and rightful position as king of Gondor. He and the wizard Gandalf (Ian McKellen) feverishly work to amass a fighting force able to stand against Sauron’s nightmarish army. Of course, fellow fellowship members Gimli (John Rhys-Davies) and Legolas (Orlando Bloom) are ever close at hand.

Part of that force will have to come from the army of Rohan, led by human King Theodon (Bernard Hill). Disguised within his ranks are his daughter Eowyn (Miranda Otto) and the diminutive hobbit Merry (Dominic Monaghan) who refuse to stay on the sidelines while their friends and loved ones enter the battle.

The final battle that will decide the fate of Middle-Earth will be held at the capital city of Minas Tirith; a city led by the grieving and mentally unstable human Denethor, to whom Pippin (Billy Boyd) pledges his service in honor of the sacrifice made by Denethor’s son Boromir (Sean Bean). Hopelessly outnumbered, the best that Aragon’s army can do is to give Frodo enough time to complete his task. But will he? Or will the temptation of the ring, which grows heavier with every step, prove to be too great for the small hobbit?

The triumphs of the cast, which perform brilliantly, are presented to us on a silver platter. The production design is magnificent; realizing not only Peter Jackson’s vision but (I would venture to say) J.R.R. Tolkien’s as well. It is a story well told on every level.

Though Tolkien himself often said that The Lord of the Rings should not be taken as a Christian allegory, it is impossible not to recognize that it is rife with Christian influences and principles. The lessons learned by these characters and the qualities they manifest are the same ones taught throughout God’s Word and which we can see reflected in the example of believers who have gone before us.

We see in Gollum the ultimate end of a continual association with evil and we see it even more clearly because of the influence the ring is beginning to have on Frodo. In Sam, we see that friendship and fellowship can be as strong a bond as any physical tie. In Aragorn, we recognize the leadership that is needed to inspire men to action and the sense of duty which commands us to pick up the gauntlet of responsibility when it is thrown our way. And, of course, the idea of service and sacrifice for a purpose greater than one’s own self is evident in just about every major character.

The battle between good and evil is not confined to the fantasy world of Middle Earth. It rages today and thrusts the question upon us. How shall we respond? What is our duty and responsibility as we face the evils of today’s world? Can we muster the courage of hobbits? Can we rally together to withstand the storms that are marshaled against all that is good and pure within us? May our fellowship with those who will stand with us be as rich and rewarding as the fellowship of the ring.
