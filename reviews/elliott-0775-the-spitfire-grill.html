Author-verified transcription

Small towns can be the most intimidating and unforgiving places in the world. They can also be the warmest and most loving. Just ask Percy Talbott (Alison Elliott, The Wings of the Dove), a young ex-con who has chosen the tiny community of Gilead, Maine as the place where she'll try to start over... if the town will let her.

Tried and convicted of manslaughter, the plucky young girl has served her 5 years of incarceration and now decides to insert herself into the very lifestream of Gilead by landing a job at the Spitfire Grill which is the town's central meeting place and only eating establishment for miles around. The grill is owned and operated by the feisty though physically challenged Hannah Ferguson (Ellen Burstyn, Divine Secrets of the Ya Ya Sisterhood) who has reluctantly agreed to hire Percy, despite her mysterious past.

Percy's direct manner and unusually poetic way of describing Gilead and its residents have a polarizing effect on people. Shelby, a timid housewife, (Marcia Gay Harden, Space Cowboys) grows stronger as a result of her association with Percy and becomes one of her staunchest supporters. Shelby's husband, Nahum (Will Patton, Remember The Titans) grows increasingly suspicious and appears almost threatened by Percy's influence. Even the mystery man who lives in the Maine woods seems drawn to the aura that surrounds this young girl with a checkered past and uncertain future.

Writer/director Lee David Zlotoff (Age of Treason) opens his tale of second chances and redemption strongly, allowing it to build solidly through three quarters of the film. A few missteps at the end of the film (the events which trigger the ultimate conflict are a bit forced) somewhat lessen the final impact, but even so, the film does leave a positive impression.

Alison Elliott is marvelous as Percy. She gives her character a quiet strength and an appreciation for life which is remarkable. All the more so when Percy's full story is eventually revealed.

God is a master at giving people second chances. It is something that man still needs to practice. It seems we are too quickly willing to judge, condemn, and cast away people who only need the opportunity to start afresh. The odd thing is that there isn't one of us who hasn't received a second chance at some point in our lives. One would think we'd be more magnanimous in granting them to others.

"Therefore, as God's chosen people, holy and dearly loved, clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience. Bear with each other and forgive whatever grievances you may have against one another. Forgive as the Lord forgave you." Colossians 3:12-13 (NIV)
