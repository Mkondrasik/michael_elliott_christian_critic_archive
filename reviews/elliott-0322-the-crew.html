The Crew

Sermon / spiritual topic: Aging; respect for elders; usefulness; faith

Author-verified transcription

As the graying of Hollywood continues, I suppose we should prepare ourselves for more films featuring the famous but wrinkled faces of the senior citizen chapter of the Screen Actors Guild. Earlier this year we had the Geritol-chugging astronauts of Space Cowboys. Now we are treated to the grandfatherly goodfellas of The Crew.

For Bobby Bartellemeo and his friends, their only dream was to become "wise guys" and work on the same gangster crew. In 1968, they were living the "high life" of successful NJ mobsters, figuring that the good times would last forever.

Thirty two years later finds them rocking on the porch of their retirement home in Miami FL, watching the entire world pass them by. When South Beach becomes the newest hot spot for young affluent hardbodies, they find themselves threatened with eviction from their now desirable ocean front apartment.

Their plan to eliminate this threat initiates a chain of events that puts our old boys in danger, while at the same time infusing them with the invigorating memories of their carefree mobster days.

The Crew is comprised of Richard Dreyfuss (Krippendorf's Tribe) as Bobby who narrates this story; Burt Reynolds as the quick-tempered muscleman who gets his best ideas after near death experiences; Dan Hedaya (Dick) as Mike the Brick, not the brightest bulb in the pack, who keeps in touch with all the "wise guys" from the old neighborhood via Christmas card exchanges; and Seymour Cassel (Temps) as Tony "The Mouth" Donato, the ladies man who only talks after he has sex. He's silent for most of the picture.

In addition to the slapstick antics of these white-haired wise guys, there are subplots involving a cartoon of a Colombian drug lord (Miguel Sandoval, Get Shorty), a buxom stripper named Ferris (Jennifer Tilly, Seed of Chucky), a wealthy and understanding hostage (Lainie Kazan, The Big Hit), and a police detective named Olivia (Carrie-Anne Moss, The Matrix) who is trying to make sense of the unusual crime spree taking place on her turf.

Thfe Crew is just one more piece of cinematic fluff released in the throwaway month of August. Be advised, with its many sexual references and use of off-color or inappropriate language, it may offend some that were expecting more of a family picture.

The film may have been more effective had the producers chosen actors who were about a decade older. The four central actors simply appeared too young for the roles in which they were cast, thus the humor which was founded upon their advanced ages did not ring true. On the other hand, the supporting cast provided some brief moments of comedy. Notably, Mr. Sandoval and Ms. Kazan, both found plenty of laughs by overplaying their stereotypical characters.

A less successful stereotype implied in the movie was in the hopeless resignation that many seniors might feel regarding the rest of their life. One of the mobsters, at a point of frustration, shouts out, "We'd be better off dead!"

Life is designed to be lived right until the end. Age shouldn't restrict or dampen our desire to experience or contribute to life as long as we retain the physical ability. Moses was eighty years old when he led the children of Israel out of captivity. Abraham became the "father of all them that believe" when he chose to believe God to produce an offspring at the wise old age of ninety-nine.

The hoary (gray) head is a crown of glory, if it be found in the way of righteousness. Proverbs 16:31 (KJV)

The resignation of the old is often exasperated by the disrespect or dismissal younger generations show to them by acting or speaking without thinking or reflecting on what those elders have accomplished or how they have lived. It is a mistake oft repeated by the arrogance of youth.
