Without a Paddle

Sermon / spiritual topic: Love of Money; Need

Author-verified transcription

Take The Big Chill; add one portion of Deliverance; then remove all evidence of talent, wit, or excitement. What we're left with will be called Without a Paddle. I can say this much: at least they appropriately named their movie... it really is up the proverbial creek with no means to come back down.

Boyhood chums Tom (Dax Shepard), Jerry (Matthew Lillard) and Dan (Seth Green) reunite at their buddy's funeral. While they are there, they visit their old clubhouse where they used to play. Lo and behold, they find a treasure map which their deceased friend has apparently left for them. It is a map that assume will lead them straight to the treasure of the infamous bank robber DB Cooper who parachuted from his escape plane never to have been seen again.

With nary a second thought, these three 30-somethings pack their bags and head off to the northwest in search of lost treasure. What they find is a heap of trouble.

After a brief encounter with an amorous grizzly bear, they cross paths with a pair of pot-growing hillbillies who don't take much of a liking to them. While on the run from them, they encounter a pair of sexy environmental tree huggers who have taken up residence among the redwoods.

Their experience in the woods doesn't amount to too much and, should you decide to give the film a miss, you won't be missing anything of consequence.

Like the gents in City Slickers, the film's central characters are experiencing a bit of a mid-life crisis and use this treasure hunt as a means to shake up their complacency. Seth Green is a diminutive doctor who is, at first, reluctant to join them. Matthew Lillard plays a man who fears commitment and Dax Shepard is a slacker who seems to have nothing much going for him.

Much of the film seems to revolve around Dax and Matthew forcing Seth into going along with one stupid plan after another. Of course, they always manage to backfire and cause Seth some degree of pain or discomfort. After a few minutes of it, the nonsense gets old... and boring. Even Burt Reynolds, who, as a heavily bearded reclusive mountain man, cannot save this piece of tripe from its inevitable self-destruction.

The film ends, none too quickly, with an example of how the value of money or material wealth is relative to one's needs. Money is a tool, nothing more. Accumulating vast stockpiles of cash will not help you if you cannot buy what you most need.

But Peter said unto him, Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money. Acts 8:20 (KJV)

In the film, the money everyone was chasing turned out to be good for only one thing and even then it did not fully meet the needs of the one who had it. There is a far greater wealth to be found. One of God's promises is that no one who trusts in him shall ever be left wanting. It is impossible to be richer than a man or woman who has no need. With God's riches, not only are all needs met, he provides more than we could ever expect.
