Author-verified transcription

Brokeback Mountain is a film that reflects truth while, at the same time, it confuses it. Based on a short story by E. Annie Proulx, it tells the tale of a 20 year long relationship between two cowboys - a sexual/romantic relationship that they tried to keep hidden from the rest of the world.

Jack Twist (Jake Gillenhaal) and Ennis Del Mar (Heath Ledger) met as young, broke cowboys looking for work. They were hired by a rancher to tend sheep upon Brokeback Mountain during a long dormant season. During that season, these two lonely cowboys eventually gave way to the temptation that promised them some relief to the emotional emptiness they felt.

During the next two decades they continued to keep up appearances - marrying women, having children, and publicly denying their sexual attraction to each other; all the while sneaking off together on "fishing trips" to satisfy a hunger that could hardly admit they felt.

Artistically, the film is a marvel. The cinematography is superb, the score is excellent, the casting sublime. It is the message that I have difficulty with.. but more on that later.

Director Ang Lee (The Hulk) shows considerable restraint in dealing with a subject matter that is sure to be controversial given the Hollywood talent associated with it. He delicately shows a taboo relationship between two individuals who were all too aware of the costs of "coming out."

Heath Ledger is brilliant in this film. He grunts and mumbles his lines as an inarticulate ranch hand but still manages to communicate volumes. With three films (The Brothers Grimm, Brokeback Mountain, Casanova released this year that showcase different aspects of his talent, he is sure to climb his way to the A-list in no time at al.

Jake Gillenhaal shares the screen with him and holds his own. His western twang is a bit inconsistent but he reaches an emotional truth that keeps his character in place.

The problem I have with Brokeback Mountain is the implication that is being communicated to the audience - namely that had they'd been in a more tolerant society, Jack and Ennis could have found happiness in a love relationship with each other.

Lee's film reflects God's truth by showing homosexuality as a choice that is made, a temptation that is faced and the shame/denial and pain that is often associated with it. All of that is true and continues to occur in today's society. Where the movie obscures the truth is in the "what might have been" message that is clearly being communicated.

What might have been holds no sway when it comes to truth. Experiential knowledge does not define truth. The sole source of truth comes from the sole source of everything else.. God. And His Word is clear on the matter of same gender sexual relations.

And likewise also the men, leaving the natural use of the woman, burned in their lust one toward another; men with men working that which is unseemly, and receiving in themselves that recompense of their error which was meet. Romans 1:27 (KJV)

Our culture is so sex-obsessed that some confuse love with sex. They are not the same. God's love, which we are to manifest, can be shared with all genders. Jesus loved Lazarus and we know that he never sinned. It is available for men to share camaraderie; closeness; even love.. without turning it into something God did not design it to be.
