Walking Tall

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 2, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Associations; separation; like-mindedness; spiritual contamination; unity

Author-verified transcription

Walking Tall isn't so much a remake of the 1973 film starring Joe Don Baker as it is an excuse to give The Rock another starring role in a simple smash 'em up adventure. Smash things up he does... and to be honest, he looks good while doing it. But there's not much more to the picture.

Chris Vaughn (The Rock), a former Special Forces soldier, returns to his home town in Washington (moved from Tennessee in the earlier version) only to find that it has fallen into corruption and vice at the hands of local casino owner and drug pusher Jay Hamilton, Jr. (Neal McDonough). Figuring that the town needs some cleaning up, Vaughn runs for sheriff, fires all the deputies, and becomes a one man terror force, wielding a fence post as his weapon of choice.

Actually, he does find a little help from his old buddy Ray (Johnny Knoxville), a convicted felon who becomes his deputy and his old girlfriend Michelle (Kristen Wilson), now a stripper in the casino who becomes his new scantily clad girlfriend.

Of course Hamilton doesn't much care for Vaughn's new vision for his town and pulls out the big guns to stop him once and for all. Vaughn does the Rambo bit and in less than 90 minutes it's all over but the credits. And that's about all there is to it.

The Rock, aka Dwayne Johnson, does have a strong screen presence that works to his advantage here. He's the only thing in the film able to transcend the weak material provided for in the script. There are some laughs to be had but I would think the majority of them were unintentional.

Most of the potentially offensive material has been removed from the film in order to receive the PG-13 rating from the MPAA. In other words, there's plenty of violence but nothing too graphic. There's lots of sexual content but everybody keeps their clothes on. Pretty much everything is watered down, including the level of intelligence to be found in the story itself.

This is not the kind of film one would generally turn to in order to find some deep meaningful lesson on life... But that's not to say it cannot be found. Vaughn actually demonstrates a basic principle as he goes about trying to change things for the better.

The first thing he does is to remove all contamination from his presence. By firing all the deputies who were tainted by the previous administration, he was able to operate unhindered by those who might otherwise have been working at cross purposes with him.

We are exhorted to do the same, especially in a spiritual context.

Now we command you, brethren, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye withdraw yourselves from every brother that walketh disorderly, and not after the tradition which he received of us. 2 Thessalonians 3:6 (KJV)

The old adage that a chain is only as strong as its weakest link holds true. The people with whom we associate will affect how well we accomplish the goals we set for ourselves. Aligning ourselves with others who agree with our goals and will work together in like-minded unity with us will serve to make us stronger.
