Author-verified transcription

At the end of Jersey Girl, director Kevin Smith dedicates the film to his father who passed away last year. What better tribute to one’s father than a story about a man, his child, and the bond that forms between them?

Ben Affleck stars as Ollie Trinke, a successful NY based publicity agent who is riding high and sitting pretty. He’s at the top of his game in a job where he excels. Into his seemingly perfect life he brings Gertrude (Jennifer Lopez) and his life becomes even more perfect. And then the rug is pulled out from under him.

Ollie suddenly and unexpectedly becomes a grief-stricken single parent saddled with the unfamiliar responsibility of raising a daughter. Furthermore, due to his grief and the added pressures of fatherhood he has a very public emotional meltdown which makes him a pariah in the industry which he loves.

Forced from NYC and an apartment he can no longer afford, he and his baby girl move to New Jersey and into the home of his father (George Carlin). Seven years later, he’s still there and working for the borough where he lives - driving a street sweeper. He still dreams of the life he once had and envisions himself back on top again. But as a new friend kindly puts it, “Sometimes you have to forget who you thought you were and just accept who you are.” The film is about Ollie trying to figure out whether that is something that he can do.

Ben Affleck cinematically redeems himself after the embarrassment that was known as Gigli. His work here is soulful and heartfelt. This is a relationship drama/comedy and the relationships on display are rich and rewarding.

The young Raquel Castro makes her feature film debut as the seven year old Gertie and she is a sweet and precious little scene stealer. Ben loosens up a bit when he’s in her presence and seems to actually enjoy their scenes together. Liv Tyler is radiant as a rather forward video store clerk who brings the possibility of romance back into Ollie’s life. There’s great chemistry between Affleck and Tyler. Though their dialogue is largely dealing with sexual topics (she’s doing a case study on the renting habits of pornography watchers), it is the nonverbal exchanges that move their relationship forward.

The most subtle relationship and perhaps the most poignant is the one between Affleck and George Carlin who plays Ollie’s blue collar dad. Carlin underplays to great effect in this role and the result is a deeply touching testament to the love of a father and the importance of family.

Kevin Smith did not completely abandon his brand of controversial and irreverent humor but it is somewhat muted in relation to his earlier films. Still, there is language use, frankly spoken sexual references and thematic content that some may find objectionable. But these elements are on the periphery. They are not at the heart of the film.

What this film is about is learning what one’s priorities are and living life according to them. Ollie thought he had life figured out but he set his vision too low. He focused on career as the measuring tape for his sense of self-worth and value. There is a far greater yardstick that can be used. Ask any parent.

Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward. As arrows are in the hand of a mighty man; so are children of the youth. Happy is the man that hath his quiver full of them. Psalms 127:3-5a (KJV)

There are many accomplishments that a man can point to as he looks back upon his life. None will be greater than the raising of a child.
