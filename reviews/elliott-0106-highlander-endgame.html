Highlander: Endgame

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 1, 2000
Rating
½ star
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Revelation 21:4Isaiah 25:82 Timothy 1:9-10

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Eternal life; hope; death; living by God’s Word

Author-verified transcription

Would that the application of the phrase “Endgame” in Highlander: Endgame be a sign that this game is over and the series will be finally put to rest. Any sequel so diluted from the heart of the original work need not be continually repeated.

For readers unfamiliar with the original Highlander film, the story is based upon the belief that there are “immortals” among us. These individuals cannot die (or rather they can’t stay dead.) unless their heads are severed from their bodies. They spend their eternity searching for other immortals in order to decapitate them before losing their own heads.

The benefit to this action is the “quickening,” the process by which the life essence of the decapitated immortal is transferred (via electric bolts and amidst shattering glass) to whichever immortal who removed the head. Thus, the more heads removed, the stronger that immortal is.

Of course, this is all background information. The movie hasn’t yet started.

Highlander: Endgame gives us two heroes for the price of one. Christopher Lambert (Highlander) stars as Conner MacLeod, the protagonist in the first three films. Adrian Paul (TV’s Highlander) costars as Duncan MacLeod, the protagonist in the TV series. Together, they must stand against a growing threat in the world of immortals: Kell (Bruce Payne, Passenger 57), a figure which has held a vengeful spirit since the mid 16th century, has been building an alliance (similar to the island shenanigans on Survivor) with other immortals to rid themselves of the strong independent swordsman type, keeping a particular vengeful focus upon Conner MacLeod.

Mr. Lambert’s character has become a bit dour as a result of having so many loved ones die on him throughout the centuries. He removes himself from the eternal contest by hiding himself with a band of gun toting monks who provide sanctuary for disillusioned immortals. When that sanctuary is attacked by Kell and the gang, Mr. Paul’s character refuses to believe that Conner has lost his head and decides to set out in search of him.

Duncan MacLeod must also deal with the appearance of Faith (Lisa Barbuscia, Almost Heroes), an ex-wife and immortal who has joined forces with Kell.

Director Douglas Aarniokoski takes the reins of a feature film for the first time and has a difficult time keeping it under control. The acting, particularly from Kell and his Clockwork Orangish band of immortal thugs, is so over the top as to be laughable. The story, screenplay by Joel Soisson (Prophecy III), doesn’t make much sense and serves only to set up the many extended sequences of swordplay and martial arts action.

While religious symbolism or icons can be seen sprinkled throughout the film, there is little of true spiritual significance to be found in Highlander: Endgame.

A born again Christian is granted eternal life and can live with the assurance that his eternity will not be spent wielding swords in a valiant attempt to keep his head on his shoulders. Our eternity has been designed by God for us to live as one with Him. Death, pain, and destruction will quickly become things of the past and forgotten.

And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. Revelation 21:4 (KJV)

Until this promised hope becomes a reality, we do have a struggle with which we contend daily. It is not to keep our heads on our shoulders but rather to keep our heads in the Word of God. To live by its standards, sharing its truths with all who have ears to hear.
