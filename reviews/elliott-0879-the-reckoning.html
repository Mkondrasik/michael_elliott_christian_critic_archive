Author-verified transcription

I will admit to having a bit of a soft spot in my heart for The Reckoning because, within its tale, I recognize a principle which I used to build the Movie Parables concept and launch my site - ChristianCritic.com.

In 1380, Nicholas (Paul Bettany), an English priest, succumbs to the weaknesses of the flesh and has an adulterous liaison with one of his parishioners. He flees his village and disguises himself to avoid retribution. Meeting up with a troupe of traveling players, he joins them as they travel to a neighboring village to perform. A downed bridge forces them to take a detour and they end up in a small town which has just sentenced a deaf mute woman (Elvira Minguez) to hang, finding her guilty of murdering a small boy.

Needing additional funds to meet their expenses, they decide to perform their prepared Adam and Eve play. It isn't well received. The troupe leader (Willem Dafoe) decides to forgo the Bible stories they have always performed and, in a drastic departure from tradition, writes his own play about the death of the young boy from the village. In writing and researching the story, they become convinced that the accused woman is innocent. This raises a moral dilemma in the heart of the disguised priest.

I found this film to be very enjoyable. The identity of the bad guy is obvious from the start but our focus is not really upon him. We are too interested in seeing how these theatrical players will react to the moral question that faces them.

Paul Bettany, as the conflicted and troubled priest straddles the moral line with adeptness. His character knows right from wrong but he also proves that the flesh is weak.

Willem Dafoe plays the leader of the acting troupe as a visionary. He has long desired to break out of the biblical stories which his troupe has always played in order to tell "modern tales" which can also represent God's teachings. Little did he know the impact his direction would have.

Not every member of the troupe is in agreement with the new direction. They argue that the meaning behind the Bible stories is universally understood, having been revealed by God. To play a tale without God's guidance or revelation strikes some as being foolish. The priest reminds them that God is everywhere present - even in these new tales. It is just up to each individual to search for Him.

This is the same premise I took when I started Movie Parables. God must be present in any story having to do with life because He created life. It cannot exist without Him. It is up to us to recognize the evidence of Him in the movies we watch. All we need do is consciously look for Him.

But if from thence thou shalt seek the LORD thy God, thou shalt find him, if thou seek him with all thy heart and with all thy soul. Deuteronomy 4:29 (KJV)
