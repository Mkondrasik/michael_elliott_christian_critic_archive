Antwone Fisher

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 10, 2003
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Romans 12:2

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Renewing the mind; healing; overcoming the past; transformation

Author-verified transcription

What happens to a man who, as a boy, grows up feeling unwanted and unloved? The answer will depend a lot on the man. Antwone Fisher is the story of one such man and the path he took to restore himself to mental and emotional wholeness.

We meet Navy Seaman Fisher (Derek Luke in his film debut) on board his ship and within minutes he’s fighting with one of his shipmates over an innocuous comment. Ordered to undergo psychiatric evaluation, he meets Dr. Jerome Davenport (Denzel Washington, The Hurricane) who begins to slowly build a relationship with the young man.

Flashbacks take us through Fisher’s difficult and heartbreaking childhood which include being born inside a prison, abandoned by his mother, never knowing his father, and being raised by an abusive stepmother and stepsister.

With that kind of history, interpersonal relationships were hard for Fisher to form, even though he craved them with a passion. It takes Davenport and a very patient and beautiful navy clerk named Cheryl Smolley (Joy Bryant, Showtime) to help him break free of his emotional isolationism.

Fisher is not alone in needing help. Davenport has built a few emotional walls of his own which need tearing down and Fisher unknowingly becomes part of the doctor’s much needed healing.

This is Denzel Washington’s first directorial attempt. Many of the same qualities which we appreciate in his acting we see in his directorial approach. There’s an undercurrent of intelligence and humor which helps ground the film and a considerable amount of restraint in dealing with sensitive matters. Washington is not looking to exploit the tragic childhood of Fisher but rather to help us to understand who he is by letting us know what he has come through.

This movie, based upon the life of Antwone Fisher, was written by none other than Antwone Fisher himself, who, after his naval career, found work as a security guard for Sony Pictures Studios. Thus, we have a film from a first time director, working from a script by an unproven writer, starring an actor who had never before been in a feature film. It is a brave and bold combination that I’m happy to say works wonderfully.

In addition to Washington’s contribution, whose experience in front of the camera must have been a great preparation for his new role behind the lens, the film’s success can be attributed to a stunning debut performance by young Derek Luke. An angry, emotionally unstable introvert is not an easy person to like. Yet Luke makes it impossible for us not to like Fisher. His vulnerability shines through his sullen attitude and sometimes aggressive behavior to reach our hearts.

Too often it seems that we are willing to accept or excuse anti-social behavior from people who have had to live through hard times. The message we receive from Antwone Fisher is just the opposite. Despite his terrible boyhood, Antwone grew up to have a kind and gentle soul. True, he was a troubled man but with some support, patient understanding and love, he managed to put those troubles behind him.

Oliver Wendell Holmes once wrote: “I find the great thing in this world is not so much where we stand, as in what direction we are moving: To reach the port of heaven, we must sail sometimes with the wind and sometimes against it, but we must sail, and not drift, or lie at anchor.”

The point is that all of us shall live through difficult days, perhaps none as severe as Fisher, but we will have our own slate of problems. Those problems, whatever they may be, do not have to defeat us nor make us become less than our greatest potential. We can sail past them. We need not be transformed by outside influences. Our transformation is to come from within.

And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God. Romans 12:2 (KJV)
