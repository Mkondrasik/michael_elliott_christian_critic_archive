Author-verified transcription

The questionable ethics of publicity agents and the enormous power that gossip columnists wield are on eviscerating display in the 1957 film noir classic Sweet Smell of Success.

Sidney Falco (Tony Curtis, Some Like It Hot) is a press hungry publicity agent who shows himself all too willing to resort to dirty tricks in order to place his clients' names into the gossip columns which fill the newsprint of New York tabloids. One of his primary forums is the Eyes on Broadway column of J.J. Hunsucker (Burt Lancaster, From Here to Eternity), a powerful figure among the celebrity set and someone who can and often does make or break careers without a second thought.

When Hunsucker freezes Falco out of his column, it is because he wants something in return. Hunsucker's sister (Susan Harrison in her film debut), whom he adores, is keeping company with a talented and principled jazz musician (Martin Milner, Route 66). A concerned and threatened Hunsucker wants the relationship ended and figures that Falco is just the kind of unscrupulous guy to do it.

Lying, cheating, and scheming is all part of the perverse game in the dog-eat-dog world that Falco and Hunsucker inhabit. The only variable that exists is how clever they can be while doing it. With screenwriters Ernest Lehman (North by Northwest) and Clifford Odets (Golden Boy) handling the dialogue, there are none cleverer. The wit which springs from the lips of these characters is razor sharp and delivered with the precision of a smart bomb. Especially proficient is Hunsucker, the character reputedly based upon the influential columnist Walter Winchell. For Hunsucker, words are his weapons of choice and people are merely targets.

Burt Lancaster is perfectly cast. Physically intimidating, he casts an aura about him that suggests that he is far superior to everyone else who hovers around him. While dishing out the dirt, Hunsucker fully expects to find everyone else groveling in it... and rarely is he proven wrong.

Tony Curtis plays Sidney Falco as a man being lured by the trappings of fame. He still recognizes the moral road but is choosing to walk a different path in order to achieve the success he desires. It is arguably the finest performance of Curtis's career.

Falco is contrasted by the character of Steve Dallas, the musician who is in love with Hunsucker's sister. A highly moral man, who refuses to be cowed by the likes of Falco and Hunsucker, Dallas demonstrates an integrity which is both admirable and dangerous as it sets him at odds with men who are not bound to the same code of conduct.

Beautifully shot in black and white by noted cinematographer James Wong Howe (Hud), Sweet Smell of Success was not initially embraced by audiences during its first release. It has since become more successful, being recognized as a brilliant, if bitter, morality tale. We should take note of whose desires are fulfilled at the end of the film and whose are disappointed. Such is lesson God would have us remember. A walk of integrity is not always performed in comfortable shoes. However, God promises that when we will reach our destination, and that we will not be disappointed in our expectations.
