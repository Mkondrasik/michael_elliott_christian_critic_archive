It Runs In The Family

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 25, 2003
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 2:19Galatians 4:4-7John 1:12

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Family; God as Father; sonship; love; spiritual family

Author-verified transcription

Sometimes when we go into a movie expecting to see a disaster, the fact that it isn’t leaves us so relieved that we actually enjoy what might otherwise be a rather mediocre film.

It Runs In The Family is as apt as a title can get. It is led by post-stroke patriarch Kirk Douglas (Spartacus) and features his wife Diana (TV’s The Paper Chase), his son Michael (Wall Street), and his grandson Cameron (Mr. Nice Guy). With three generations of the Douglas family represented, it could have been an ego-filled family affair from hell. It turns out to be a rather touching and sentimental portrait of an American family just trying to get by in life with their love for each other intact.

Michael Douglas plays Alex Gromberg, a high-profile lawyer who doesn’t much like his job; loves his wife (Bernadette Peters, The Jerk) but is beginning to feel the stirrings of lustful temptations; and has little connection with either his sons or his father.

Kirk Douglas is Mitchell Gromberg, a stubborn cuss of a man; recuperating from a stroke which may have slowed his speech but not his opinionated mind.

Cameron Douglas is Asher Gromberg, a pot-growing slacker with a bit of charm but no plan as to what he wants to do after he graduates, which at this point doesn’t look that likely. The bright spot on his horizon is an attractive student named Peg (Michelle Monaghan, Unfaithful) who may be willing to have a relationship with him. The question remains: Will she pull him up or will he pull her down?

The youngest of the Grombergs is Eli (Rory Culkin, You Can Count On Me) who, at 11 years old, is weird in an indescribably way, a point underscored by the other characters who can't quite put a finder on what's so odd about him. His big crisis during the film has to do with his feelings towards the bad girl of his sixth grade class - a runaway with a nose ring. (The funniest line in the film - Asher: “What’s wrong with a nose ring” Eli: “It’s in her nose!”)

This dysfunctional family somehow manages to get beneath our skin so that we actually care about what happens to them. It is clear that they do love one another but the communication of that love needs a lot of work.

Director Fred Schepisi (Roxanne) takes his time developing the characters and their relationships with each other. It is a slow moving film but one that is rich in relationships and an honest family dynamic. The Grombergs may not be the Bradys or Ozzie and Harriet but who among us is?

Their family, as odd and rife with problems as it is, is still bound together by love. It is a structure designed by God and to show how much importance He places upon it, it is this family relationship that He desires to have with us.

"Yet to all who did receive him, to those who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God." John 1:12 (NIV)

It is within this family relationship, with God as our Father, that we are able to grow and develop as spiritual men and women. We may fall in and out of favor due to our behavior, but we cannot lose His love. The love of a father is not something that is earned, it just is. He has called us to be His sons. That spiritual seed that God has planted within us cannot be removed. We are sons of God today and will remain sons of God forever.
