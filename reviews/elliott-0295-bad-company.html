Bad Company

Author-verified transcription

Note to readers: Never sit in the front row of a Joel Schumacher/Jerry Bruckheimer film. I speak from experience. Arriving with seconds to spare for a screening of the Schumacher-directed, Bruckheimer-produced film Bad Company, I took the first available seat I could see and spent the next 111 minutes looking up the suddenly cavernous nostrils of Anthony Hopkins and counting the no longer microscopic pores on the neck of Chris Rock. To say that these guys like to use close-ups is to say that a dictionary likes to define words. But I digress... there is a movie to review here.

When the CIA partner of senior agent Gaylord Oakes (Anthony Hopkins, Hannibal) suddenly dies in the middle of a sensitive undercover operation, the future stability of the entire world becomes questionable. Fortunately, the dead CIA guy has a secret twin brother that even he didn't know existed.

Jake Hayes (Chris Rock, Down to Earth) is a street smart but small time hustler. Playing chess in the park for twenty dollar bets; scalping tickets to the hot sporting events; and working part time as a DJ in a dance club, Jake is still barely scratching by, a fact that does not go unnoticed by his disillusioned girlfriend (Kerry Washington, Save the Last Dance). And this is the guy that the CIA needs to save the free world? Of course it is.

There's a nuclear bomb on the market which Jake's twin was negotiating to purchase for the CIA as part of an undercover sting operation. Now, Jake is going to have to pretend to be the highly sophisticated and highly trained twin he never knew he had in order to give the CIA the time it needs to catch the bad guys and defuse the bomb.

This is an average summer release that offers a momentary escape from the summer heat and the sedentary doldrums that set in at this time of year. It is not without merit but neither is it without flaws.

Anthony Hopkins is the seasoned, unflappable pro; Chris Rock, the unseasoned amateur who is seen flapping all over the place. There is no more depth to the character development than that. These are cardboard cutout caricatures that serve no other purpose than to stand in the foreground of whatever explosions or automatic weapons fire might be taking place behind them.

That the dialogue is stilted and the editing less than smooth will matter little to audiences looking for loud, mindlessly entertaining films. Bad Company will deliver what it is they seek.

Those who might be looking for something a bit more redeeming may want to consider the film in the following light. Against Oakes' better judgment, the CIA decides that it would be better not to tell Jake what is really at stake and what risks he'll be taking. It is need to know information and they figure he doesn't need to know. It is a decision that almost costs them the mission.

It is impossible to prepare for a task without the knowledge of what the task will entail. Without preparation, success is never assured.

A wise man is strong; yea, a man of knowledge increaseth strength. Proverbs 24:5 (KJV)

For a believer, it is never better not knowing what evil may be lurking around the corner. When armed with knowledge, the prayers and action of a believing man or woman will be far more effective than the first impulse reactions to a surprise attack.
