The historical drama, Elizabeth, opens with a scene that should make a tremendous impression upon the heart of any true Christian:

Three people, having been shaved and bound, are burned alive in a public square by the religious leaders of their time. The reason: They chose to worship God rather than the false doctrines of men. Labeled heretics by the Roman Catholic authorities in England of 1555, they went to their deaths rather than denounce their Protestant faith. The image is one that will not be soon forgotten by this reviewer.

We should all remember that such atrocities did indeed take place. In those days, the "name of God" was invoked and used to cover a multitude of sins. It is not altogether inconceivable for those days to return. In fact, if the Christian world continues on its present course of spiritual complacency, we can be assured that such persecutions will once again be commonplace.

It was in such a spiritual climate that Elizabeth I began her reign as Queen of England. A daughter of King Henry VIII and the "whore Anne Boleyn", Elizabeth was not a popular choice to gain the crown. She was young. She was unmarried. She was inexperienced. Worst of all, she was a Protestant in a Roman Catholic world.

To make matters worse, her country, severely divided along these denominational lines, was being threatened by other nations. Unable to get the support she needed from the church in order to muster a proper army to defend her monarchy, she realized that the only way to heal the rift that existed was to form a unified Church of England. This feat would prove to be easier to conceptualize than achieve as she had to gain the acceptance of those whose allegiances leaned more to Rome than England.

Cate Blanchett (Oscar and Lucinda) gives a remarkable performance as Elizabeth. Miss Blanchett allows us to see Elizabeth as both the young, winsome girl that she is, as well as the austere, powerful queen that she will become. She is well deserving of every accolade that will surely come her way.

Geoffrey Rush as Sir Francis Walsingham, Elizabeth's primary protector and political advisor, is every bit her equal on screen. Mr. Rush is building quite an impressive and varied resume for himself, having already won an Academy Award for his work in Shine as well as receiving favorable notices for his roles in Les Miserables and Shakespeare in Love.

Joseph Fiennes (Shakespeare in Love) is appropriately "moonish" as Lord Robert Dudley, Elizabeth's true love who finds that loving a queen is not quite the same as loving a woman.

Christopher Eccleston (A Price Above Rubies) as Duke of Norfolk, cuts an imposing figure as he first seeks to prevent Elizabeth from gaining the throne, then plots to overthrow her and win the crown for himself.

Kathy Burke (Dancing at Lughnasa) is very effective as the dying Roman Catholic Queen Mary Tudor who is the ruling monarch at the beginning of the film.

There is no denying that denominational religion has long been used for devilish purposes. Wars, crusades, persecutions, purgings.. al performed by religious fanatics who claim to have God's blessings on their side. It has been a successful device of our adversary. By fueling the hatred and distrust that burns in the heart of man, he can blind us to the light and love of the one true God.

It has never been God's will for His church to be divided. But unification is only possible if men will set aside their egos, their passion for power or prestige, their greed, and their love for tradition in order to become submissive and humble to God's Word which is the only source of truth and should be the only doctrine for man to follow.

Any religious organization unable or unwilling to humble itself to God's Word becomes an emissary for the devil. Any person following such an organization is being led away from God into a wilderness of spiritual darkness. Roman Catholicism, for all of its vast resources, and its millions of followers, is a spiritual wasteland if it elevates its own church history above the scriptures.

Becoming a likeminded Christian does not mean compromising the Word of God to form alliances with those who choose not to obey its truths. In our day there remains a great deal of ecumenical treachery.

As Christians, we must stand firm on the truth of the scriptures... even if it means enduring the flames of religious persecution.
