Cherish

Author-verified transcription

Cherish is all over the map genre-wise. And while it seems to be a deliberate directorial choice, it mars what is otherwise a fun and quirky film. Part romantic-comedy, part mystery, part music video, part Elizabethan drama... (OK, so maybe they didn’t go as far as that last one) Cherish switches moods and styles so frequently that it keeps us somewhat off balance.

Zoe (Robin Tunney, Vertical Limit) is a dorky computer programmer who finds escape from life’s tedium by listening to the pop music of the 70s and 80s. She’s not alone, for somewhere in the city is a stalker who shares her music obsession and has become fixated upon her.

When he finally acts and accosts Zoe as she’s leaving a nightclub, he forces her car to go out of control and hit a bike patrolman, killing him. Of course nobody believes Zoe’s story and so she is accused of DUI and manslaughter.

While awaiting trial, which has been delayed by her lawyer (Nora Dunn) in an attempt to defuse the emotional outcry of public opinion against her client, Zoe enters into an electronic bracelet program which is essentially a house arrest. Donning an anklet which trips an alarm if she travels outside of the confines of the apartment, she is completely isolated from the outside world except for sporadic maintenance visits from the bracelet technician (Tim Blake Nelson) and a developing friendship with her downstairs neighbor - a homosexual, wheelchair-bound, Jewish dwarf (Ricardo Gil).

As Zoe’s isolation continues, she is miraculously transformed from that dorky programmer to a resourceful, beautiful, unjustly imprisoned girl. It is a fact that does not go unnoticed by the technician. With only twenty-four hours remaining until her trial, she realizes her only chance of freedom is to track down her stalker and find the evidence which places him at the scene of the accident. If it only wasn’t for that pesky bracelet...

While there are a great many flaws to this film, the performances of Tunney and Blake are well worth seeing. Tunney, once she sheds her early, annoying dot-commer personality, is quite appealing and shows both an inner strength and vulnerability. Blake gives a nicely crafted and subtle performance as a lonely correctional officer who becomes smitten with this most unusual and vivacious individual.

Another element of interest is the soundtrack containing many familiar tunes which play such an important part in Zoe’s life. Music has long been used as a calming influence, having the ability to alter moods and soothe a troubled heart... even before AM radio was invented.
