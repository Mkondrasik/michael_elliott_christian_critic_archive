Author-verified transcription

Moving in with a house full of cheerleaders may sound like a dream come true for most men but Texas Ranger Roland Sharp (Tommy Lee Jones) sees it as a nightmare. In actuality, Man of the House, the lightweight action comedy that tells the story, is not the stuff of bad dreams but it is sleep-inducing as it takes a one-joke premise and tries to stretch it to fit a 90 minute film.

When the chief witness needed to testify against a criminal kingpin is brutally gunned down, the only hope for the state's case lies with the five Texan cheerleaders who saw the killing. In order to protect them against the cold-blooded bad guys, the stoic, emotionally dead Sharp moves in with the scantily clad and perpetually perky pom-pommers. If you don't know the rest of the story, you don't get out much.

By film's end, Sharp's rough edges have been polished smooth and the girls have proved that their cheerleading skills can sometimes save the day. There are some superfluous subplots involving Cedric the Entertainer as an ex-con/ex-cheerleader turned preacher and Anne Archer as a college professor more than willing to give Sharp some private tutoring.

But the crux of the film lies in the relationship between Sharp and the girls. We see them giving him a facial, resisting the conservative dress code he has forced upon them, and teaching him how to relate with the teenage daughter he's ignored most of his life.

The normally reliable Tommy Lee Jones, who also is credited as being the executive producer of this film, is unable to lift the material above its rather pedestrian nature. Uninspired, unoriginal and bland pretty much sums up the experience. It's difficult to know who is more embarrassed here - Jones or the character he plays.

The girls are not required to do much more than look good in their belly shirts and cheerleading outfits. While the screenwriters tried to give them unique personalities, the general tendency is to think of them as one big bubble-brained entity rather than as individuals.

Christina Milian plays Anne, the determined and plucky squad captain with a flat stomach. Paula Garces is Teresa, the flat-stomached Hispanic with a boyfriend we never see. Monica Keena is Evie, the serious student with a flat stomach who is hyper-nervous of anything that might affect her scholarship. Kelli Garner plays Barb who, along with her flat stomach, has a G-rated crush on Sharp. Vanessa Ferlito rounds out the quintet as Heather, the street smart, flat-stomached independent gal. All together they make up what Hollywood deems to be a typical male fantasy.

The one thing they have in common is the devotion they have to what they believe is their calling - cheerleading. They see it as a gift - to be able to generate positive energy even (or especially) when their team is losing badly. In that respect, we are all called to be cheerleaders of a kind. Our attitude should always remain upbeat and confident no matter what outward circumstances we may be facing.

Great is my boldness of speech toward you, great is my glorying of you: I am filled with comfort, I am exceeding joyful in all our tribulation. 2 Corinthians 7:4 (KJV)

Paul was able to stay joyful even in the midst of tribulation because of his unwavering belief that God remained at his side throughout all. Knowing that He is always present in our lives gives us the strength to remain cheerful and enables us to lead others to the joy that need never fade.
