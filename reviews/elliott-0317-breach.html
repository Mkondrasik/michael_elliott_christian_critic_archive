Breach

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 16, 2007
Rating
2½ stars

1 hr: 50 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Deception

Author-verified transcription

The worst breach in the history of US intelligence is given the movie treatment in a new, appropriately-titled, film, Breach. The end result is a slow-moving but intelligent and tense drama that is solidly anchored by a brilliant performance.

Robert Hanssen (Chris Cooper) is an FBI operative nearing the end of a long and successful career as one of our nation's top espionage experts. Eric O'Neil (Ryan Phillippe) is a young man just starting out who has ambitions to one day become an FBI field agent.

When Eric is abruptly reassigned to be Hanssen's clerk, he is asked by his superiors to keep an eye out for any improprieties. Little did he know how deep those improprieties would run.

What Eric was not initially told was the fact that Hanssen is a traitor - selling or forwarding classified military secrets to foreign governments. He apparently had been doing it for years, compromising this nation's security and putting many of our assets at mortal risk.

O'Neil's assignment was nothing more than a dangerous game of cat and mouse with a man who had proven to be extremely adept at keeping his own secrets while simultaneously exposing the secrets of others.

Chris Cooper gives an award-worthy performance as Hanssen. As complex as he was contradictory, Hanssen was devoutly religious and a devoted family man. He also traded secrets which led to the deaths of others. Cooper brings this flawed man to life and while we never really understand the reasons for why he did what he did, we do know that we are watching a fully realized personification.

Acting opposite him is Ryan Phillippe as a young man out of his depth, trying to navigate blindfolded through uncharted waters. He does an adequate job but could have benefited from providing a larger arc over which his character could travel. Perhaps partly because of the detailed characterization of Chris Cooper with which he shared the screen, Phillippe's work appeared to lack the nuances it needed to match against his co-star.

Laura Linney adds a single-layered gravitas to the film as O'Neil's handler. Kathleen Quinlan plays one of the film's more likeable and lighter characters, Hanssen's wife Bonnie.

So why did Hanssen betray the country he was sworn to protect? We aren't given the complete answer to this question. Perhaps it isn't known. Perhaps Hanssen himself doesn't fully understand it. It might have been ego, a skewed sense of purpose, or simple greed. Whatever the motivation, Hanssen had to delude himself into thinking that his actions were somehow reasonable.

Self-deception is a common theme in the scriptures because it is a common malady suffered by man. When we lose sight of a fixed standard of right or wrong and instead follow our own narrowly conceived, selfish desires, we are able to rationalize anything. It doesn't make our actions right; neither does it make them truth. It simply makes them happen and then leaves us to face the consequences.

Let no man deceive himself. If any man among you seemeth to be wise in this world, let him become a fool, that he may be wise. 1 Corinthians 3:18 (KJV)

To become a "fool" simply means to humble ourselves to a higher authority of wisdom. It is not foolish to measure our thoughts and actions according to an all-knowing, all-loving source outside of ourselves to which we can pledge allegiance and obedience. Hanssen became a fool because he led himself to believe that he was the wisest of all.
