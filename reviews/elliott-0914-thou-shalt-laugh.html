Thou Shalt Laugh

Sermon / spiritual topic: Laughter; Humor

Author-verified transcription

God does have a sense of humor. It's only His followers who sometimes take themselves too seriously. But you couldn't prove it by the collection of Christian stand-up comedians assembled in the new DVD Thou Shalt Laugh.

Hosted by Patricia Heaton (Everybody Loves Raymond), the film features seven Christian comedians and, as its title suggests, funny is virtually guaranteed.

It's a bit of a disservice to label the DVD as a "Christian" comedy film because, to be honest, this is not an "in-your-face" evangelical experience. In fact, the religion or religious beliefs of its participants are hardly mentioned.

The themes or riffs given by these comics are the same ones used by comedians everywhere. Observations about marriage, kids, dating, race relations, language, weight loss, differences in ethnicities, and more are made with a comic's typical slanted perspective and given with a single aim in mind... to make us laugh. And here they succeed.

It is just that these comedians manage to be funny without resorting to profanity or obscenities. They make us laugh the old-fashioned way, by holding our face to the mirror of life and letting us see some of the absurdities there.

Thor Ramsey opens the show with an energetic routine that bounces all over the place: toy manufacturers, the weather in Bismark N.D., Christian marketing techniques, greeting cards, and snorers all get a bit pierced by his comic barbs. Michael Jr., offers the show's black man's perspective and has a little fun with wordplay.

Jeff Allen, the oldest comedian on stage, has teenagers and therefore no lack of a comedy source. His routine on married life and the experiences of living with older children is a high point to the show.

Theresa Roberts Logan is the sole female of the group and spends most of her minutes dealing with the pains of weight loss/gain. Joby Saad uses a gimmick to present his material. Billing himself as "the village idiot," he wears a goofy pointed wool cap and adopts a slow but self-aware mentality.

Gilbert Esquivel adds a little ethnicity to the program from a "Messican's" point of view (which is different from a Mexican's). Ventriloquist Taylor Mason closes the show on a high note with a hysterical audience participation bit.

The wonderful comedic actress Patricia Heaton, as host, is a bit out of her element here but her involvement is relatively small. All in all, the show offers plenty of laughs and offers them in a non-offensive way. Kudos to all involved with the project.

Cheap laughs are relatively easy to get. Relying on shock factor or titillation can evoke a kind of laugh but it isn't very rewarding for either the performer or the audience. The laughs generated from the performers of Thou Shalt Laugh are richly deserved because they are not wrenched out of us but are rather reflective of things we already know, shown to us in a different, funnier light. It is a refreshing tonic in the world of comedy.

Thou Shalt Laugh is representative of the best kind of Christian witness as it does not stoically preach to us on how we should live a good life. It instead simply shows us how much fun we can have when we do.

(Gen 21:6) And Sarah said,' God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me.' (KJV)
