Author-verified transcription

Life is not fair. At least the life of an artist, as depicted in the new film, Incognito. Why else would a painting with Rembrandt's signature be worth millions while a similar painting with a different signature be worth nothing? That's not art - that's autographs. So says Harry Donovan (Jason Patric, Speed 2: Cruise Control), a gifted artist and undoubtedly the most talented art forger in the world.

Harry usually keeps a low profile, reproducing the works of second or third tier artists instead of the grand masters. Those that are a rung or two down on the ladder of fame are less likely to attract attention or invite deep inspection. But along comes an offer that Harry simply cannot refuse - To paint an "original" Rembrandt. Even though "only Rembrandt can paint a Rembrandt," Harry, driven by the artistic challenge and tempted by the financial reward, sets out to do the impossible.

After he succeeds, those who had contracted his services renege on their offer of payment. Harry takes back his own painting, which ironically labels him an art thief in possession of a rare multi-million dollar work of the Dutch master. If that isn't enough, he's also being accused of murder.

Incognito offers a fascinating look into a world which many have heretofore only taken a cursory glance: the world of art. Framing it within the format of an adventure/thriller, Director John Badham (Nick of Time) adds just enough intrigue, suspense and romance to interest even those who have "dogs playing poker" hanging in their living room.

Mr. Patric seems to be forever threatening to break through that second or third tier of artists into the top echelon of box office draws. He doesn't quite accomplish it with this film. His work is quite competent and his portrayal is sound, but whatever that extra quality is that sets the top ranking actors apart just isn't there... yet.

Irene Jacob (Othello) is lovely as Marieke Van Der Broeck, an art expert who becomes interested in Harry for more than his brush work. Rod Steiger (Shiloh) is a bit over the top as Harry's ailing father. Thomas Lockyer (Cutthroat Island) is appropriately slimy as the double crossing art dealer who hires Harry.

Watching Harry paint his forgery gives us an inkling as to the intricate details that must go into the making of a true piece of art. If something that man makes can be that precise, that detailed, that multi-layered... what about the works of God?

Just one look at the human body which God made shows us that He is no stranger to precision and detail. But even as miraculous and wonderfully beautiful our bodies are in their design, they are not God's greatest work. For the ultimate in precision and exactitude, God's masterpiece is His Word.

Authored by God, written over a period of time spanning thousands of years, penned by many different men from different educational and environmental backgrounds, the Word of God is perfect. Closer examination only reinforces and clarifies its perfection.

We, to whom God's Word has been committed, must be continually on guard against the "forgers" that are in this world. As with Harry's art in Incognito, a good forgery is oftentimes indistinguishable from the original. Religion continues to be an area where our spiritual enemy endeavors to deceive us. Things that may appear on the surface to be godlike and holy, could very well be a devilish forgery. That is why all sermons, hymns, prayers, opinions, and commentaries must be taken and compared against the perfect truth of the scriptures. That which agrees with the Word, we can accept. That which disagrees with the Word, we should cast out as a lie. As the Scriptures say:

"Prove all things; hold fast that which is good." 1 Thessalonians 5:21 [KJV]

It isn't always easy, but it is always profitable. Life may not be fair, but our heavenly Father is. Genuine truth has power. A forgery may appear to be true, but just when you need its power the most, it will not be there. The most dangerous lie in the world is always the one that most closely resembles the truth.
