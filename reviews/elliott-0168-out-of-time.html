Out of Time

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 3, 2003
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 John 1:9Proverbs 28:13Psalms 32:5

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Confession; forgiveness; mistakes; honesty; consequences

Author-verified transcription

Even a smart and good man can make a stupid decision. And as anyone knows, the problem with one stupid decision is that it can lead to many others. This is the dilemma facing Matt Lee Whitlock (Denzel Washington, Antwone Fisher) in Out of Time.

As the chief of police for a sleepy south Florida town, Whitlock doesn't have much to do other than checking the storefronts on Main Street and sneaking a beer or two while on the job. The latter might be his way to dull the pain stemming from his pending divorce. He still loves his wife, Alex (Eva Mendes, 2 Fast 2 Furious), who happens to be a homicide detective.

He also is involved in an extramarital affair with an old high school flame (Sanaa Lathan, Brown Sugar). When she learns of her terminal cancer and the high cost of experimental treatment, she resigns herself to the inevitable and begins planning for her death. She starts by naming Whitlock as beneficiary for a million dollar insurance policy.

Whitlock, who has the compassion of a boy scout but apparently the brains of a trout, decides to let his mistress "borrow" close to a half million dollars in confiscated drug money so she can afford to fly to Europe for the experimental treatment.

When the girl's house is burnt to a crisp along with her and her husband, guess who's looking like the prime suspect? There's also the small matter of the missing half mil from the police vault. Whitlock is in deep trouble and must unravel this mystery before his ex-wife discovers that all the circumstantial evidence points to him.

Out of Time is hampered by an extremely weak script and the only reason that it doesn't get a failing grade from me is because the actors and director manage to overcome (to a small extent) the flaws in the writing.

There's no denying that Denzel Washington is an accomplished film actor. This is nowhere near his best performance but he does bring a lot to the role and he makes us like his character even when he's being dumber than dirt.

While the ladies are acceptable in their respective parts they aren't that exceptional. Dean Cain (Lois & Clark), in a limited appearance as the cuckolded husband, provides a nice sinister edge to the film. The spotlight shines briefly on John Billingsley (High Crimes) as a medical examiner and slovenly friend to Whitlock. His amusing asides give welcome comic relief to distract us from some of the film's problems.

Director Carl Franklin (Devil in a Blue Dress) manages to extract half a decent movie from the scenario. The preliminary events are just too far-fetched to be believed. But once the premise is set and our hero is trapped, the movie's pulse starts beating as he tries to extract himself without letting anyone know of his initial error in judgment. But we must eventually return to the place (in our minds) where the rubber meets the road... Or in this case, where the script smashes against the wall of common sense. Denzel's character had to be insane to risk his career and his freedom to give a woman he barely knows money that did not belong to him.

Of course, he wasn't insane and therein lies the weakness of the script. But we can use that weakness to examine what we should do if we ever make an error in judgment or otherwise stray from doing what we know to be right. Should we act as Chief Whitlock and conceal our error at any cost? Or does God have a better plan to make things right again?

If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us [our] sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. 1 John 1:9 (KJV)

The biggest error, spiritually speaking, that Whitlock made was not in stealing money to try and help a woman in need. It was in trying to cover up his mistake when he was in danger of being discovered.
