Author-verified transcription

Minority Report is a great "what if" movie. What if it were available to look into the future and see a crime being committed? What if the police then took action to stop the person before they committed that crime? Can we say there was a crime? Was there a victim? Should there be punishment? Based upon a short story by the prolific Philip K. Dick (Blade Runner), Minority Report raises these questions and much, much more.

In the year 2054, an experiment is being conducted in Washington DC as Detective John Anderton (Tom Cruise, Vanilla Sky) heads up the new "Ministry of Precrime." Using the recorded "visions" of three psychics (called pre-cognitives or pre-cogs) who are kept wired, drugged, and floating in a solution filled vat, the precrime agents are able to arrest murderers before they actually kill anyone. Since the inception of precrime, the murder rate in DC has dropped by 90%. The system has proven itself to be flawless which is why the government is considering making it the national crime prevention standard.

Before that happens however, Anderton himself is named by the pre-cogs as the next murderer. Refusing to believe himself guilty of a future crime, Anderton runs, looking for evidence that will clear his name now and in the future even though he knows that if he does, the precrime division that he helped build will be proven fallible and rendered useless.

Director Stephen Spielberg (AI: Artificial Intelligence) meticulously paints a dark and gritty picture of the future which is believable because it is a logical extension of the present. From its Mag-lev transportation system to the eye scans which are used as the primary means of identification, Minority Report expertly blends expected technological advancements with their presumed commercial application. While all the high tech gadgetry looks fascinating, Spielberg has us wondering if the loss of personal privacy isn't too costly a price for the added conveniences.

Tom Cruise is at the top of his game as Anderton. Though we see him as being self-assured and competent at his job, Cruise lifts his character's professional mask to show us the emotional wreck of a man. The loss of his only son six years ago followed by the dissolution of his marriage is still deeply felt and Cruise makes us believe it all.

Colin Farrell (Hart's War) plays Det. Ed Witwer, a federal cop who comes to Precrime on the eve of its national referendum, looking to take charge of the unit. Samantha Morton (Sweet and Lowdown) is a picture of innocent vulnerability as Agatha, the strongest of the three precogs within whom may lie the evidence Anderton needs. Lois Smith (Twister) appears briefly to steal a scene as the eccentric scientist whose work with the pre-cogs led to the development of precrime.

Minority Report, while being a thoroughly entertaining thrill ride of a movie, also provides much food for thought, especially along the lines of balancing the concept of predestination with that of free will. As Christians, these are areas to which we should have already given much thought. We know from the scriptures that God's foreknowledge of future events is an eternal reality. We also know that He has given us free will to choose and that our choices will largely shape the kind of future we will have.

To reconcile these seemingly contradictory truths, it is important to recognize that foreknowledge does not imply control. God does not impose His will upon us. He makes His will known and exhorts us to follow it but ultimately it is we who must choose whether to obey it or not. God's foreknowledge is merely the result of His not being bound to the limitations of the linear time which exists in this world.

But, beloved, be not ignorant of this one thing, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. 2 Peter 3:8 (KJV)

God knew that we would choose to accept Jesus as Lord before we did. But it was still our free will choice. Of course, being God, He is infallible in his foreknowledge. In Minority Report, John Anderton is hoping that the pre-cogs do not share that godly characteristic.
