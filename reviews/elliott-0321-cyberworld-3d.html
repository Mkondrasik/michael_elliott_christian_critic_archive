Cyberworld 3D

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 6, 2000
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
44 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Psalms 104:24Jeremiah 14:22Ecclesiastes 7:13

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Creation; man's works versus God's works; awe; technology

Author-verified transcription

For people who enjoy unique film experiences, the Imax Corporation's "Cyberworld 3D" may be the newest and hottest ticket. A collection of animated film shorts that have been reworked and translated into 3D, "Cyberworld 3D" makes the most of cutting edge technology and the 7 story screens of the Imax theaters to produce effects which are at once imaginative, impressive, and fascinating to watch.

To introduce the various segments (which include scenes from Dreamworks' "Antz" and 20th Century Fox Television's "The Simpsons") and provide a narrative flow to the film, the filmmakers have invented Phig, a computer generated hostess (voice by Jenna Elfman, "Keeping the Faith") who runs the cavernous Galleria Animatica. As she leads us from exhibit to exhibit, she discovers there are code-eating bugs (known as Cyber Critters) in her program which she must find and exterminate before her entire cyberworld disintegrates.

Some of the segments are indeed familiar, but we've never seen them like this. With the assistance of Imax's futuristic 3D headgear, we are brought up close and personal with the action which appears on screen, off screen, and literally all around us.

The most successful segments are also the most familiar. In "Antz," we are reminded of how witty and rich the dialogue of that film could be as we watch Z pitch woo and impress Princess Bala with a few fancy dance moves. Instead of detracting from the scene, the 3D effect served to compliment the outstanding computer artwork already evident in the film.

To be honest, the film is not much more than a showcase for the advanced technology being displayed. The added storyline of Phig and the Cyber Critters and the stories of the individual segments are rather shallow and insubstantial. But the visual effects are so magnificent, that we barely take notice. 3D technology has come a long way since Vincent Price's "House of Wax" and "Cyberworld 3D" will surely astound and please old and young alike.

In many cities, "Cyberworld 3D" is playing as a double feature with a 2D production from Discovery Channel Pictures. "Africa's Elephant Kingdom" follows a herd of African elephants as they travel across the Kenyan plains in the flatlands of the White Mountain.

On the huge Imax screen, we can certainly gain a massive appreciation for the size and strength of these creatures. What is surprising is their gentleness and docility they show to one another. Filmed primarily in the Amboseli National Park in Kenya, director Michael Caulfield and crew stayed with this particular "family" of elephants for over six months and indeed managed to obtain footage that is quite remarkable.

Less remarkable is the narration by Avery Brooks ("Star Trek: Deep Space Nine.") Perhaps in order to avoid the appearance of a dry documentary, the filmmakers have Mr. Brooks deliver the narration in the first person, as an old and wisened bull elephant. Because of this, some of the narration gets a bit silly, filled with "new age" sentiment as the elephants are attributed with attributes and motivations which are human.

Still, the visual beauty of the cinematography and the close look of these magnificent animals which the film presents to us make "Africa's Elephant Kingdom" worth a visit.

Seeing these two films in tandem was also an interesting experience for they provide us with a valuable perspective. Man's technical achievement and imagination does shine incredibly in the animated fantasy that is "Cyberworld 3D." However, his works still pales in comparison to the complexity, power, and magnitude seen in all of God's creation which is on display in "Elephant Kingdom."

Proof once again that nothing man makes on his own can ever come close to measuring up to the awesome and glorious works of God.
