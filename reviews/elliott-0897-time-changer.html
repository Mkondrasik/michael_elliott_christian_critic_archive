Time Changer

Author-verified transcription

When is being good not good enough? That's the question which drives Time Changer, a new Christian production, unabashedly evangelical, which has seamlessly woven its proselytizing into its inventive story line.

The year is 1890 and bible professor Dr. Russell Carlisle (D. David Morin, The Moment After) has written a book on morality and our changing times. Though admittedly well-researched and well-written, one of his colleagues, Dr. Norris Anderson (Gavin MacLeod, The Love Boat) takes issue with an integral aspect of his work and withholds a crucial recommendation. In his book, Carlisle asserts that one can and should teach or practice morality without preaching or claiming the authority of Christ as the name of Jesus Christ can be a stumbling block to some.

Anderson believes that such an approach is actually dangerous to the faith. As he says, "The devil has nothing against morality. He's against Jesus Christ." To prove his point, Anderson offers to send Carlisle on a journey. Using a time machine which his inventor father developed, Anderson transports the disbelieving Carlisle 100 years into the future so he can see with his own eyes the resulting fruit that his teachings would bring.

What Carlisle experiences in our time is not the apocalyptic doom and gloom that we might envision, but something far more insidious. He sees a general malaise or apathy when it comes to matters of faith. Children are disrespectful to their elders; people have become complacent, even bored by the teachings from their pastors through which they dutifully sit without purpose. He is shocked by the divorce rate, by the lack of reverence given the Lord, by the content of movies and the audience's acceptance of that content.

The point is made that although most people of today are by and large "good, moral people," because their morality is not based upon the standard set by God and taught by Jesus Christ, they have drifted away from the true power and have not found the eternal life and rich rewards promised to all who believe.

A heavily bearded D. David Morin benefits from having a bit of an anachronistic look to him which serves him well in this role. The script too often calls for him to be befuddled or bewildered by the technological and social changes between the two time periods and so there are places where the film feels repetitive. On the whole however, he portrays a likable, sympathetic character whom we don't mind spending some time with... even a century, give or take a decade.

The supporting cast is peopled with a number of familiar faces: Gavin MacLeod as the dissenting professor; Hal Linden (Barney Miller) as the head of the 19th century school board; Paul Rodriguez (Blood Work) as a good hearted laundromat employee; and Jennifer O'Neill (Summer of 42) as a born again librarian who helps Russell put things into spiritual perspective.

Time Changer is so "in your face" Christian, it is unlikely that unbelievers will take its message to heart. Where it might prove effective is in waking up members of a church who may have grown complacent in their faith or who may have begun to "secularize" their morality. Being a good person is admirable, but being a Christian is much more than that.

Knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Jesus Christ, that we might be justified by the faith of Christ, and not by the works of the law: for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified. Galatians 2:16 (KJV)

We cannot separate the teachings from the teacher. Jesus Christ is the key to unlock the truths of the scriptures. Without him, the book loses its power and its way.

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. John 14:6 (KJV)
