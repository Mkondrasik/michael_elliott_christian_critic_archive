August: Osage County

Author-verified transcription

Oklahoma can be unbearable in the hot, sweltering days of August. So can the Weston family but they can’t really use the weather as an excuse. It would appear that they are unbearable all twelve months out of the year. Based on the stage play by Tracy Letts who adapted his work for the screenplay, August: Osage County presents a dysfunctional family in all its ignominious glory using a large cast of Hollywood A-listers at the top of their game.

Leading the cast, (nay the entire acting community), is the incomparable Meryl Streep who brilliantly portrays Violet Weston, a drug-addicted, cigarette smoking, mouth cancer victim whose judgmental and highly opinionated rants belie her own emotional needs. When her alcoholic husband played by Sam Shepard disappears, the Weston family come back to the roost to offer their… well, whatever it is, it can’t be called support.

Julia Roberts is the eldest, a somber and controlling woman who is losing control of her marriage to Ewan McGregor. They arrive with their 14-year-old daughter in tow played by Abigail Breslin. Younger sister Juliette Lewis breezes into town with her current lover and indiscriminate skirt-chaser Dermot Mulroney. Ivy, another sister played by Julianne Nicholson, has not yet escaped from the uncomfortably oppressive homestead and awaits this family reunion filled with resentment and secrets to be revealed.

Adding to the tension and drama are Margo Martindale as Violet’s overbearing sister with Chris Cooper as her long-suffering husband. Benedict Cumberbatch is on hand as their adult son and constant embarrassment.

All of the actors in this film give first rate, award-worthy performances and to watch their impressive work can be likened to an enrollment in an advanced film-acting workshop. That does not make the movie enjoyable or even a particularly pleasant viewing experience. Inconceivably billed as a comedy, this film is as depressing as it gets – through no fault of the actors. The characters they portray are just mean-spirited, embittered personalities possessing little to no grace for one another.

Grace, as used in the scriptures, can be defined as God’s unmerited favor. We have done nothing to earn God’s love yet He gives it to us freely. We have done nothing to deserve His attention or concern and yet He waits patiently for us to turn to Him or call for Him. And without hesitation, He accepts us for who we are and for where we are because He sees us as only He can – as the men and women we could grow to become.

Grace can be a rare quality in man but it is available for us to cultivate and embrace it. It is a choice we make. To walk in grace is to walk in love. It is to live in a judgment-free zone, seeing the best in people even when they are not at their best. Adopting an attitude of grace towards others takes us out of ourselves. It forces us to be unselfish. How different a dynamic would have existed within the Weston family had they learned or even attempted to build grace in their lives?

“Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.” Ephesians 4:29 (KJV)
