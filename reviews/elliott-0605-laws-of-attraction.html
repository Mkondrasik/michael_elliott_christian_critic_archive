Author-verified transcription

Jesus Christ explained divorce almost 2,000 years ago. When asked by the Pharisees if it was according to God’s law, he reminded them of what Moses had taught. He then further taught them that divorce was a result of the hardness of men’s hearts... meaning, it was not God’s primary will that married couples divorce but due to our own failings and shortcomings it became necessary for there to be a provision in God’s law for it.

In Laws of Attraction, divorce attorney Daniel Rafferty (Pierce Brosnan) makes a similar argument. He observes that in a divorce people find a passion for a fight they didn’t know they wanted. Where was that passion, he wonders, when it was time to fight for the marriage?

He is one of the best divorce lawyers in New York City, handling only the most lucrative cases. His equal, though with a dramatically different approach to law, is Audrey Woods (Julianne Moore). Where she is a by the book professional (masking a deep insecurity), he is a by the seat of his pants charmer (masking a deep romanticism). Both win their share of cases at the expense of the other and, in so doing, come to learn that the old adage - “opposites attract” - remains true.

While in Ireland researching claims for their latest high profile case - this one involving a rock star (Michael Sheen) and his fashion designer wife (Parker Posey) - they awaken after a night’s revelry to the realization that, while inebriated, they got married.

Typically, they respond to this news in different ways: he’s delighted; she’s mortified. But in order not to become the laughingstock of their legal circles, they agree to present the facade of a happy marriage until after the completion of their big case.

Romantic comedies may be the most difficult genre to do well but director Peter Howitt hits the mark with a deft hand and a light touch. Reminiscent of the personality driven comedies of the 40s and 50s (notably Adam’s Rib which starred Spencer Tracy and Katherine Hepburn as married attorneys who opposed each other in court), Laws of Attraction succeeds for no other reason than the fact that we like the two stars and the characters they portray. The chemistry works.

For me, Pierce Brosnan is at his best when he drops the hard edges he often adopts for his characters and just uses his charm and his comic timing to win us over. These qualities are on full display here as he presents Daniel Radcliffe as an impish romantic employed in as unromantic a job as there is.

Julianne Moore matches well with him as an accomplished but insecure attorney who finds comfort in junk food and conversations with her botox injected mother (Frances Farmer). Her conflict in being simultaneously attracted and infuriated by Radcliffe is quite amusing.

The film is rated PG-13 and there is a fair amount of sexuality in the film although nothing graphic is seen. There are also a few scenes depicting alcohol abuse which is used to help the characters drop their inhibitions and move their relationship further along its way.

While parental caution should be advised, there is a decent and wholesome message that the film delivers. Namely, that a divorce is not worth fighting over but a marriage is definitely worth fighting for.

It takes a lifetime to build a marriage - brick by brick, day by day, year by year. It only takes a relatively short amount of time to destroy it.

Destroying something is easy. Building something to last can be hard. At the end of our lives, as we look back upon all that we’ve done, it won’t be a divorce that gives us a sense of accomplishment and purpose. It won’t be a divorce that will provide us with comfort and strength. A marriage can do all that and more.

Redirecting our energies into our marriages and relationships with the goal of fighting for them to succeed will provide us with a far greater reward than negatively picking at them until they finally fall apart.
