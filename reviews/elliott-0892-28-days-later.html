Author-verified transcription

People going to 28 Days Later expecting to see Sandra Bullock in a sequel to her 2002 film, 28 Days, are in for a rude surprise. Directed by Danny Boyle (Trainspotting), 28 Days Later is a horrifically violent, apocalyptic treatment on rage and human survival in the face of destructive chaos.

When a trio of PETA-type activists break into a laboratory with the intention of freeing the animals that are caged there, they unwittingly release a highly contagious, fast-acting virus which turns anyone infected into mindless, rage-filled monsters.

Within 28 days of the first infection, all of London has been abandoned, left to the ravages of these zombie-like creatures who can no longer be called human. Jimmy (Cillian Murphy, Disco Pigs), a bicycle courier, awakens in an empty hospital, having finally recovered from a car accident which occurred before the infection became epidemic.

Jimmy wanders the deserted streets of London, encounters the first of many murderous beings, and meets up with a handful of other uninfected survivors who teach him the new rules of living in an infected world. Rule #1: Don't hesitate to kill anyone who shows signs of being infected with rage.

Eventually, mere survival without hope of a better life seems rather pointless and so a quartet of survivors, including Jimmy, set off in search of a safe zone protected by remnants of the military. But in this strange new world, is anyone or any place really safe?

Boyle has developed a reputation for making quality, thought-provoking films on a shoestring budget and 28 Days Later is no exception. Costing a mere 15 million dollars, low by industry standards, 28 Days Later does not show the signs of budgetary restraints. The story is compelling, the characters are interesting, and extra (i.e. costly) special effects would have only detracted from the eerie mood Boyle manages to evoke.

This is not a film for the squeamish or skittish. An extremely creepy mood prevails over the production and there are enough "jump scenes" to embarrass anyone who lets his attention wander.

Cillian Murphy, plays the young man awakening to this nightmare and learning how to cope in midst of it. He moves from incredulity to resignation to a form of rage born from righteous anger with such believability we can't help but share in his emotions.

Naomi Harris (Living in Hope) plays Selena who helps Jim to learn how to stay alive. What she doesn't share (because she herself doesn't know) is why they should bother. That lesson is provided by a father (Brendan Gleeson, Gangs of New York) and his young daughter (Megan Burns, Liam). Even in the midst of this insanity, they are able to maintain a close and loving relationship, suggesting to Jim and Selena that all hope of humanity is not yet lost.

What sets 28 Days Later apart from the typical zombie/horror B-movie fare is its examination of human nature. In this kill or be killed situation, what it is that delineates between the infected and those trying to stay uninfected? Is there room in the hearts of men who are desperate to survive for such qualities as compassion, love, or a sense of obligation towards one's fellow man?

In this horror film, we can see that what enables us to elevate ourselves above the rest of the animal kingdom is our ability to reason. We are not mindless beings driven solely by instinct or emotion. Emotion without reason... that is what rage is. It is unreasonable, irrational, and uncontrollable. It is also godless.

For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God. James 1:20 (KJV)
