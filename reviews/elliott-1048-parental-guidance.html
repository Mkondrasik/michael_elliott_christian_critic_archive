Author-verified transcription

Parenthood is or at least can be a rich and rewarding experience. For those of you currently starting out on this adventure… just wait. Grandparenthood is so much better. Parental Guidance is a simple and sweet movie that is tailor made for this demographic.

Artie Decker (Billy Crystal) is a minor league baseball announcer who finds himself in early retirement after the young executive now running the club fires him. As it happens, his only daughter (Marisa Tomei) wants to accompany her husband on a business trip and needs someone to watch her kids. Artie’s wife, Diane (Bette Midler), is beside herself with joy and Artie, to his dismay, no longer has an excuse not to make himself available to them. It appears that he and those kids don’t really understand one another.

The film is a contrast in parenting styles. The younger generation’s “new-age,” feel good method of reasoning with their children is completely lost on their parents who, quite frankly, don’t seem to have much of a style at all. They just do the best they can in any situation and make things up as they go along.

And these kids do have some issues. The youngest is an imaginative, over-excited lad with an imaginary kangaroo friend. The middle child is an awkwardly shy stutterer and bully victim. The eldest is an extreme over achiever with a pending recital she is sure will shape her entire future. And while everything Artie and Diane try to do seems to make matters worse for these kids, at the same time, because they are there and involved with their grandchildren, things somehow seem to work out for the best.

What is clear to anyone who takes parenting seriously (and shouldn’t we all?) is that children need boundaries. They need to have someone in authority lovingly set those boundaries; lovingly explain why the boundaries are important; and lovingly enforce the rule that those boundaries be respected and met. Children need to receive loving reminders (oftentimes sharply worded) whenever they try to cross over those boundaries. As Artie declares in frustration to a auditorium filled with a shocked audience as he prepares to spank one of his grandchildren, “Everyone seems to want these kids to ‘use their words’ but the one word they never seem to hear is ‘no!’”

“Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.” Proverbs 22:6 (KJV)

Part of training is providing the discipline that encourages a child to take a proper or desired action. An undisciplined child is usually one who has not been “trained up in the way he should go.”

Raising a child can be a terrifying and humbling experience because they don’t come with a clear set of instruction manuals. But with God’s help and the divine guidance found in His Word, we can provide our kids with a strong basic understanding upon which to build their lives. As we continue to teach our children the principles of truth that they can then use to frame their doings, we are providing them with the best possible foundation upon which to start their lives. It is a foundation that they will continue to stand upon when it is time for them to lay it for their own children.
