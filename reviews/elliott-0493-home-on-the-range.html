Author-verified transcription

Sometimes, in the old West, when there was no hero to be found, heroes had to be made. In Walt Disney Pictures' newest hand drawn animated film, Home on the Range, the only ones available to save the day for a poor aging farmer are a trio of dairy cows. Mooove over John Wayne, these ladies are udderly serious.

When the farm where Maggie (Roseanne Barr), a prize winning show cow (three time recipient of the Golden Udder Award), is sold at auction, the former owner takes her to a dairy farm named A Patch of Heaven, where the proprietor Pearl (Carole Cook) agrees to take her in. Unfortunately, it appears that the same fate may await Maggie's new home. Unless Pearl can come up with $750, she will lose her farm and everything on it.

When Maggie learns that there's a reward of $750 for the capture of the cattle rustler Alameda Slim (Randy Quaid) her mind is set. She will capture the bad guy, receive the reward, and save the farm. And she won't have to do it alone. The matronly bovine of the barnyard, Mrs. Calloway (Judi Dench) and a tone-deaf, slightly scatterbrained cow named Grace (Jennifer Tilly) join her for the adventure.

They do have competition for the reward. The legendary bounty hunter Rico (Charles Dennis) is after Slim as well and he's partnered up with a horse (Cuba Gooding Jr.) who is beside himself with glee at the thought of actually being the steed of a real live Western hero.

This is broad, Saturday morning cartoon fun that should please the young ones of the family. The secondary characters are ridiculous - a crotchety old goat; a trio of dimwitted henchmen, a rascally one-legged jackrabbit, a trio of piglets awed by Maggie's prestigious appetite, and more.

For the most part, the vocal performances are decent. Randy Quaid is most effective as the yodelling, larger than life bad guy. Cuba Gooding Jr. is also surprisingly funny as the horse with delusions of martial arts grandeur. I didn't find Roseanne Barr, as Maggie the show cow, to be all that notable. Her lazy drawl of a delivery simply isn't sharp or precise enough to communicate the nuances needed to fully bring to life an animated character.

The original songs, written by Alan Menken and Glenn Slater, are performed by k.d. lang, Bonnie Raitt, and Tim McGraw among others. They manage to evoke the western flavor of the film but aren't as memorable as some of the more classic Disney animated features.

All in all, this is a nice, safe feature film for children to enjoy. There are a few adult double entendres, which is the reason for the PG rating. The most potentially offensive of them is featured predominantly in the television and movie trailers used to advertise the film.

I suppose the point of the film is that heroes come in all shapes and sizes. They may not be the bravest or the strongest or the wisest... But they are the ones willing to act when action is required. The scriptures contain many such examples.

And what shall I more say? for the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae; of David also, and Samuel, and of the prophets: who through faith subdued kingdoms, wrought righteousness, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions, quenched the violence of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight, turned to flight the armies of the aliens. Hebrews 11:32-34 (KJV)

The one common element of these "heroes" of the Bible is their faith or believing. Because they trusted in God and believed in His promises, they were able to do many heroic works. God has not changed and His promises are still good. As we believe them and act according to them, we will see the same results.
