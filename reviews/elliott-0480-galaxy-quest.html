Author-verified transcription

"Never give up. Never surrender." This pithy phrase is the trademark tagline of TV's cult action hero Commander Peter Quincy Taggart of the popular sci-fi series Galaxy Quest. Twelve years after the show's cancellation, he's still quoting it, keeping his character alive by attending Galaxy Quest conventions, supermarket openings and other mundane public appearances. No longer acting, he makes his living selling autographs to his adoring if somewhat geeky fans. His fellow cast members are also in tow although, from the looks of things, they aren't too happy about their lot in life.

And then, at one of the sci-fi conventions, a few of the geeky fans dressed up like aliens turn out to be the real thing. Not understanding the concept of pretense and make believe, the aliens (otherwise known as Thermians) have fashioned their entire culture around the broadcast exploits of Taggart and the crew of the SEA Starship Protector. Now, in their hour of need, they have come to solicit Taggart for help in their plight. It seems that their entire race is threatened, facing extinction at the hands of an ugly and menacing foe.

Taggart, thinking this to be just another promotional gig, goes along, with the rest of the crew following. Soon these actors turned cultural icons are involved in the type of epic intergalactic struggle once depicted on their show.

Clearly drawing from Star Trek for its inspiration, Galaxy Quest walks a fine line, delivering both parody and praise. The filmmakers succeed, mainly because even as they are spoofing the "cheesiness" of the 1960's series, they do so with the utmost respect and admiration. Never pandering for a cheap laugh, Galaxy Quest gets us to appreciate the humor even as we fondly remember the target of the joke. The film starts a bit unsteadily but soon finds its audience pleasing groove and settles down to provide an enjoyable evening's entertainment.

Tim Allen (Toy Story 2), in a personification of his Buzz Lightyear character, is a scream as a William Shatner clone. But beneath the caricatured stereotype, Mr. Allen allows a touch of real emotion to come through. Overhearing a couple of "fans" ridiculing his appearance at a convention, his depression and self-loathing is all too uncomfortably real.

Sigourney Weaver (Alien) is the show's designated piece of eye candy, Gwen DeMarco/Lt. Tawny Madison. With v-neck costume and push-up bra, her character has one redundant job on the ship which is used to great comic effect. As the ship's communicator, she must repeat whatever the computer says, as if no one else had heard it.

Alan Rickman (Dogma) is at his acerbic best as Alexander Dane/Dr. Lazarus, the Spock-like character who disdains the celebrity gravy train he is riding while remembering the all too distant time when he used to be a real actor.

Director Dean Parisot (Home Fries), in paying homage to the Star Trek mystique even as he parodies it, makes it obvious that he has great affection for the show. Fans and non-fans alike will appreciate the inside jokes: The 'expendable' crew member, the illogical obstacles placed in the way of our heroes by lazy writers, how the captain manages to appear bare-chested in every episode; even the obsessive fans who have memorized every facet of every episode until they have reached a level of GQ awareness that even surpasses those who work on the show.

The Thermians emulated the actions of the characters in a fictional tv show. It almost resulted in their complete and total destruction. As Christians, we are called to be wise and not follow in the way of false prophets and teachers. Rather, we are to follow the standard kept by Christ and his disciples.

For even hereunto were ye called: because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that ye should follow his steps: 1 Peter 2:21 [KJV]

Following his steps; doing his works; thinking his thoughts. There is an emulation worthy to be carried out. And it is one which will have a lasting impact.. Not only upon our lives but also upon the lives of those whose paths we cross.
