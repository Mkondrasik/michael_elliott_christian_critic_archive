Escape Plan

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 18, 2013
Rating
2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 55 mins
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Hope; Deliverance; Escape from Trials; Trusting God

Author-verified transcription

There’s something old school about Escape Plan. Oh yeah… it must be the stars. Sylvester Stallone and Arnold Schwarzenegger pay homage to their past in this 80s-style prison film. And while these 60-somethings still manage to bring the machismo to the screen I found there to be something unsettling about watching them go through the paces that the genre demands.

Stallone stars as Ray Breslin, the man who wrote the book on high security enclosures… literally. So sure he is of his ability to find the weaknesses in a prison system, he allows himself to be inserted, unannounced, into places where the vilest criminals are housed so he might break out and school the warden and guards as to where their weaknesses lie.

He’s presented with an unusual offer from the highest levels of the government. To break out of a government institution where global “game-changers” are meant to disappear from the rest of humanity. No sooner does he accept the job, he is whisked off to an unclassified, off-the-grid location where even his extraction team can’t find him. This time, if he wants to taste freedom again, he’s going to have to find his own way out.

Stallone is in fine form for 67 though he resorts to a deadpan delivery through most of the film that I guess is meant to represent (confidence, arrogance, indifference… take your pick). Arnold, as an inmate whom Stallone takes into his confidence, seems to relish his role more. He picks over and savors his lines like he’s enjoying a tasty rib roast. Jim Caviezel, as the evil warden following orders from an unnamed cartel, wisely chooses to underplay his role as if he knew he would never be able to chew more scenery that his co-stars combined.

Director Mikael Hafstrom not only doesn’t break any new ground with this film… he tramples over ground oft traveled before. But the genre works if the audience is willing to go along for the ride. If they are, they may find themselves enjoying it more than they expected.

The idea of an escape-proof situation is a frightening one – if one is in the position of needing an escape route. Ray Breslin attuned his senses to finding the weakness in a situation or pattern that he could exploit in order to gain his freedom. In a spiritual sense, God wants us to do the same.

In life we may often find ourselves at a crossroads or even at a dead end. When all hope appears lost, God wants us to know that He can provide a way to escape the situation even if we can’t see it.

“For no temptation (no trial regarded as enticing to sin), [no matter how it comes or where it leads] has overtaken you and laid hold on you that is not common to man [that is, no temptation or trial has come to you that is beyond human resistance and that is not adjusted and adapted and belonging to human experience, and such as man can bear]. But God is faithful [to His Word and to His compassionate nature], and He [can be trusted] not to let you be tempted and tried and assayed beyond your ability and strength of resistance and power to endure, but with the temptation He will [always] also provide the way out (the means of escape to a landing place), that you may be capable and strong and powerful to bear up under it patiently.” 1 Corinthians 10:13 (Amplified)

The key for us, as it was for Ray in the film, not to look at any situation as hopeless. God’s promises extend beyond our own understanding and can deliver us from any “prisons” we may think are escape-proof.
