The Avengers

Author-verified transcription

Patrick Macnee, who starred in the television series, The Avengers with Diana Rigg, also appears in the new film version, but he cleverly keeps his face well disguised. I can't blame him. I wouldn't want to be associated with this turkey either.

What is it with all the recent attempts to remake the "classic" TV shows from our baby boomer youth? What exactly are the film makers of today trying to recapture? Whatever it is, it is not making its way onto the screen.

If you didn't know, The Avengers are British, James Bond-like secret agents. They are unflappable in the face of danger, especially when they are being "flapped" by those who oppose them.

Top agents John Steed (Ralph Fiennes, The English Patient) and Mrs. Emma Peel (Uma Thurman, Pulp Fiction) are paired together in order to halt a serious infraction of British etiquette. It has come to their attention that Sir August de Wynter (get the seasonal joke?) has somehow taken control of the weather. He plans to hold the world hostage by making them pay for good climate. (The thought had to cross my mind... how would the British know what good climate is?)

Of course, it is a ridiculous premise and, surprisingly enough, not all that original. However, the television series could take a ridiculous story line and make it both intriguing and fun from the sheer power of the suggested relationship between Mr. Steed and Mrs. Peel.

In this film version, their relationship is more than suggested... the script makes it downright obvious. It is the actors that don't make it believable. For whatever reason, Mr. Fiennes and Miss Thurman don't "click" as a couple. They appear bored and uninvolved.

As bland as they are, they shine brilliantly compared to the real disappointment of the film. Sean Connery (The Rock) has never before failed to raise the quality level of any film in which he has appeared. Just his presence in a scene will elevate the movie in which that scene appears to a higher level of "watchability". Not this time. As the weather-stealing Sir August de Wynter, Mr. Connery may blow and bluster, but he never delivers with the impact of a storm.

The idea of an enemy controlling the environment is not as far-fetched as it may first appear. We are not referring to a physical enemy of course, that is still ridiculous. But spiritually, we have to remember that we live in the midst of our enemy's realm. He is called "the prince of the power of the air." He is called "the god of this world." If he were not in the picture, every day would reflect the perfection of God's order. Because he does exist and has dominion of the natural world, we experience nature's devastations.

Next time we hear someone referring to an earthquake, hurricane or flood as being "an act of God", we should ask ourselves which god is responsible for the act... The one true God Who is all light and in Whom is no darkness at all? Or the god of this world who comes not but for to steal, kill, and destroy?

One day, we will live in the midst of perfection. But until that day comes, we must face the imperfections of this world... whether they be natural disasters brought on by our spiritual enemy, or man-made disasters brought on by film makers who really ought to know how to do better.
