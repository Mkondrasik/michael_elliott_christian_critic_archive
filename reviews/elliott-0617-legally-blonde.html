Author-verified transcription

Did you hear the one about the blonde who went to law school? She studied for her bar exam by memorizing which drinks come with little umbrellas.

We’ve all heard them before. Dumb blonde jokes featuring flaxen haired beauties whose bust size always exceed their IQs. Legally Blonde capitalizes on the image and plays it up for laughs by inserting a sorority girl beauty queen into the highly competitive world known as Harvard Law School.

Elle Woods (Reese Witherspoon, Election), a 4.0 college student (she’s majoring in fashion management) is devastated when her boyfriend Warner (Matthew Davis, Pearl Harbor) dumps her. Citing his political aspirations, he tells Elle that she would not be good for his career path. (“I need a Jackie, not a Marilyn.”)

Elle’s perfect solution: Get accepted into Harvard Law School and prove to Warner and the rest of us that there is more to her than her Barbie doll features.

Thanks to a bikini clad video essay, Elle is able to pack up her heart-shaped notebooks and fuzzy pink pens and, followed by a moving truck filled with other stereotypically blonde accessories, begin her matriculation at the most prestigious law school in the country.

Directed by Robert Luketic in his feature film debut, Legally Blonde begins rather slowly, showing Elle in her sorority environment, being dumped, and feeling miserable afterwards. But once it moves to the halls of Harvard, the contrast between Elle and just about everyone else there become fodder for the jokes and humorous situations that follow.

Of course, this highly implausible story based on a novel by Amanda Brown will eventually put first year law student Elle in charge of an important murder trial. But no matter how thin the credibility of the film is stretched, Reese Witherspoon is so consistently perky and genuinely funny that we don’t have to buy into the story... we can just enjoy it for what it is.

She plays the “dumb blonde” persona not as one who is intellectually inferior but rather as one who is simply motivated by other factors. Her cheerful optimism is contagious and she has the audience rooting for her character as she works to establish herself as a serious law student.

The other cast members are quite supportive. They include Luke Wilson (My Dog Skip) as an associate lawyer; Selma Blair (Cruel Intentions) as Elle’s competition for Warner; and Jennifer Coolidge (Best of Show) as a ditzy beautician who befriends Elle during a manicure.

Elle’s dilemma in Legally Blonde is one of which many of us have, at one time or another, experienced. People will often judge us on appearance or by some other superficial means, without giving us an opportunity to prove that there is more to us than meets the eye. This is not a new phenomenon. The first century believers, even Jesus Christ himself, had detractors not because of what he said or did, but for silly superficial reasons.

Is not this the carpenter’s son? Matthew 13:55 (KJV)

Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth? John 1:46 (KJV)

God’s truth, too, will often result in people labeling us as being “close-minded,” or “intolerant” and give us labels such as “Jesus freaks,” or “religious fanatics.” Many will be quick to judge us before they have an opportunity to know who we are, and more importantly who we are in Christ.

The thing to remember is that their perception of us does not change who we really are. As we stay faithful to ourselves and our beliefs, we can change the perception of those around us.
