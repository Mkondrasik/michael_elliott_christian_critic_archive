Aeon Flux

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 2, 2005
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 3:13 Revelation 21:1-4 2 Peter 3:13

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Paradise/Creation; human imperfection; heaven; God’s creation

Author-verified transcription

Paramount Pictures made the decision to open Aeon Flux, the only movie to be released wide on the weekend after Thanksgiving, without first screening it for the press. It certainly isn’t the first time this has occurred but what makes it unusual is the fact that such an action is typically reserved for films not expected to garner much critical acclaim. With Aeon Flux boasting a pair of Oscar-winning actors in its cast, we thought hopes for the film would have been set a bit higher.

Set in the distant future, eight generations after 99% of the world’s population has been destroyed by a plague-like virus; the remaining humans live in what appears to be a closeted Utopian society. But unrest is brewing behind the walls that protect them from the wilderness.

A band of rebels called Monicans are unhappy with the ruling Goodchild regime, so called for the man who developed the vaccine that kept their ancestors alive during the plague. They plan to unseat the current administration through assassination. A highly trained killer named Aeon Flux (Charlize Theron) is being sent into a heavily guarded complex where the society’s leader Trevor Goodchild (Marton Csokas) is planning a speech.

When her opportunity arises, she hesitates. Something she doesn’t quite understand is troubling her and she decides, instead of killing Goodchild, to work with him to figure out what it is.

Aeon Flux began as a series of short animated segments which ran on MTV’s “Liquid Television.” Never having seen them, I have no way of knowing how faithful director Karyn Kusama (Girlfight) and screenwriters Phil Hay and Matt Manfredi have been to Peter Chung’s initial vision. What I can say is that they have constructed a piece of sci-fi fiction that is mildly entertaining and somewhat thought provoking.

Charlize Theron, taking a break from the “serious actress” mode she adopted in Monster and North Country, dons the latex and shows that she can hold her own with the best of the cinematic super heroes. She does show a bit more skin than any Oscar winning actress should have to show but she doesn’t embarrass herself with a lackluster performance. The same cannot be said for Oscar winner Frances McDormand who, in the small role of the rebel leader, looks a little like Carol Burnett’s exaggerated version of Norma Desmond of Sunset Boulevard.

All in all, however, Aeon Flux is a moderately passable sci-fi adventure. The film is hardly the train wreck that many predicted it would be. What is most disappointing is the knowledge that it could have been more.

Aeon suspects that all is not perfect in the artificially created paradise she calls home. It’s hardly surprising. Man has often tried to build heaven on earth and has always fallen well short of the goal. We are flawed and fallible and therefore limited in what we can produce.

Every man’s work shall be made manifest: for the day shall declare it, because it shall be revealed by fire; and the fire shall try every man’s work of what sort it is. 1 Corinthians 3:13 (KJV)

Fortunately for us, the paradise that awaits us is not one that we had a hand in building although it was built with us in mind. God, who is infallible, has prepared a permanent home for us beyond anything our minds could comprehend; certainly far beyond anything our hands could fashion.
