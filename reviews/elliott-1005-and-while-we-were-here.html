Author-verified transcription

It is so hard to make melancholy dramatically interesting. In And While We Were Here, writer/director Kat Coiro tries to give it a go. And it doesn’t go anywhere.

Jane (Kate Bosworth) is the bored and noticeably unhappy wife of Leonard (Jamie Blackley), a concert violist. He has brought her to Italy on one of his concert tours. While there, she hopes to get inspiration to work on a book she wants to write about her grandmother’s life and times which spanned both World Wars.

From the onset it is obvious that a major disconnect has occurred between the two central characters. They barely relate to each other, let alone talk. When Jane decides to explore the nearby sights on the island of Ischia, she bumps into a young 19 year old (Iddo Goldberg) who reawakens in her a joie de vivre that she had long ago forgotten.

The major problem with the film is that it picks up Jane and Leonard as they are when the film begins. There’s nothing that shows what their relationship once was and the relationship they now share can hardly be defined as a relationship. Thus Jane comes across and cold, distant and selfish while Leonard comes off as an insensitive bore.

Neither is really the truth. We learn that due to a tragedy that has deeply affected them, they have adopted these behaviors as a kind of defense mechanism to get them through their days. It is a lonely path they have decided to travel – one that most certainly will result in their arriving at different destinations.

We can’t always control what happens to us during the course of our lives. But it is available to control how we react to whatever happens. And it is important to remember that we don't have to go through anything alone.

Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep. Romans 12:5 (KJV)
