The Beach

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 2, 2000
Rating
2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 59 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Numbers 32:11Jeremiah 23:241 John 5:4

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Paradise; obedience; the heart; promised land

Author-verified transcription

Beware of that which seems too good to be true... because it may be neither. The Beach (which is also neither good nor true) is a movie about the quest for one man's idea of paradise.

Richard (Leonardo DiCaprio, Titanic) is a typical American tourist. Thousands of miles from home, looking for the offbeat and unusual in Thailand, he instead finds others who, like him, manage to settle for the mundane and ordinary until it is time to go home.

That is, until a map falls into his hands. Given to him by a crazed Brit (Robert Carlyle, Angela's Ashes) the day before his suicide, this map supposedly leads the way to a secret island paradise once thought to be only an urban myth. Sharing his map with Francoise and Etienne, a pair of adventurous French travelers played by Virginie Ledoyen (A Soldier's Daughter Never Cries) and Guillaume Canet (La Fidelite), Richard and friends set out in search of this promised land of Utopia where the beach is pristine and marijuana grows like a weed.

The island they find is indeed beautiful as well as being home to a hippie-like commune led by the young matriarch, Sal (Tilda Swinton, The War Zone), who closely guards their isolation and seclusion to protect their idyllic life. Such necessary secrecy is what eventually brings trouble to paradise.

We come to find that the island is shared by a heavily armed band of local farmers involved in the cultivation of a certain illegal (albeit profitable) crop. These farmers allow the commune to remain on their side of the island as long as no one else learns of their existence. At first, this uneasy alliance which requires a complete separation from the "outside world" is perfectly agreeable to these young escapists. However, as time passes, they are forced to make one moral compromise after another in order to keep their island life a secret.

Director Danny Boyle and screenwriter John Hodge, who last collaborated on A Life Less Ordinary, team up again in this weak adaptation of Alex Garland's novel. What could have been a thought-provoking action/drama suffers terribly due to an overly melodramatic treatment featuring America's Titanic heartthrob in the central role.

While Mr. DiCaprio's "bankability" should emerge intact, it is in no thanks to this vehicle. His character is likable enough, but the psychological journey we are asked to believe he takes is merely suggested rather than actually depicted. His naturalness and affability serve him well to a point, but when the script takes a darker turn, he doesn't make the transition effectively.

The filmmakers chose to keep the focus of their story on Richard, relegating the other members of the commune to the human equivalent of background scenery. They are there, but hardly worth a mention. If some of these characters had been fleshed out or given a bit more substance, the film may have been able to evoke something more out of its audience than unintentional laughter.

The idea of finding a physical paradise may be very tempting for some. And certainly, God promises a paradise for us in the future. But in this world, as the characters in the film discover, a place is only a place. The key to living the "idyllic" life today is not found in one's external surroundings, but rather in one's internal reality.

Even when leading His people out of bondage, God did not permit an entire generation to enter into "The Promised Land" because of the hardness of their hearts.

Surely none of the men that came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, shall see the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob; because they have not wholly followed me: Numbers 32:11 [KJV]

God's abundance is available today to men and women willing to wholly follow Him by wholly following His Word. The physical paradise will have to wait on the Lord. Our promised land of today can be found in the day to day victories which we have the right to claim in his name.
