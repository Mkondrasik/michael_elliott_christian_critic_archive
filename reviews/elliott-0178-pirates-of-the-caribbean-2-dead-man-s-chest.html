Pirates of the Caribbean 2: Dead Man's Chest

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 7, 2006
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Temptation

Author-verified transcription

The ride continues. The theme park attraction turned major motion picture now has a sequel. Pirates of the Caribbean 2: Dead Man's Chest contains all the action and adventure of the original - and more. Swordfights, sea monsters, cannibals, and buried treasure... it's all so piratey.

Central to the film (and the film's success) is Johnny Depp as Captain Jack Sparrow, the excessively flamboyant leader of the pirate ship, The Black Pearl. Sparrow is on the run from just about everyone. Lord Beckett (Tom Hollander) wants Sparrow's mystical compass and so has imprisoned Will Turner (Orlando Bloom) and Elizabeth Swann (Keira Knightley), interrupting their wedding day in the process.

To purchase their freedom, Will must find Captain Jack and convince him to part with this prized possession. Jack has other problems. He is under a supernatural debt to none other than legendary (and deceased) Davy Jones and must avoid running into him and his ghost ship, The Flying Dutchman, or else be doomed to an eternity of servitude.

The only way out for Jack is to find and steal the source of Davy Jones' supernatural power. To do that, he's going to need the help of the ever trusting Will and Elizabeth.

Depp, reprising his Academy Award-nominated role, seems even more comfortable in the dirt-encrusted skin of the roguish pirate than he did the first time around. He mugs and prances about the screen with a gleeful energy.

Bloom and Knightley also return with successful repeat performances. Knightley is even given an opportunity to show off her athleticism with a couple of dueling sequences. But it is the supporting players who have the meatier roles. Bill Nighy in particular, plays Davy Jones and is unidentifiable behind pounds of prosthetic octopus-like make-up but is a powerful presence nonetheless.

The special effects and stunts are impressive and entertaining although they may be too intense for a family's youngest viewers. The extended fight scene atop a runaway mill wheel between Capt Jack, Will Turner and James Norrington (Jack Davenport) is especially notable.

The film's weakness lies in its script by Ted Elliott and Terry Rossio. At 2 ½ hours, the film is about 50 minutes too long and filled with non-essential padding. It is also filled with an abundance of characters and storylines which, over time, gets muddled and lost. For a fantasy summer blockbuster, it requires its audience to stay connected and involved. It also ends on an unresolved note which, though promising a third installment to this new Disney franchise, does not leave one with a satisfying sense of completion.

Captain Jack's troubles stem from a Faustian bargain he struck with Davy Jones. He, in essence, sold his soul in order to obtain what he most wanted - his ship. As in all cases when we deal with the devil to obtain something -- even when we get it, it isn't what we really want. In Jack's case, he got his ship and almost immediately lost it through his crew's mutiny. Now just when he's got it back, it is time to pay his debt. It was a no win situation from the very beginning.

Jesus Christ died so that we do not have to live in debt to our spiritual enemy. He does not have anything we need and cannot offer us anything we want. Our only response to him when he tries to entice us with temptation and empty promises should be the same one Jesus Christ had when he was tempted: "Get the behind me Satan, for It is written...."
