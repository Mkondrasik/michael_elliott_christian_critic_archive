Daredevil

Author-verified transcription

All the A-list super heroes must be otherwise engaged. Filling in this week will be Daredevil, a lesser known Marvel character who does enjoy some popularity. He's a crime fighter who, reportedly, is without fear. There is allegedly a great deal of Roman Catholic symbolism evident on screen. If there is, much of it escaped me. Any biblical standard which might have been seen was overshadowed by the vigilante aspect of the title character.

Hell's Kitchen is a dangerous place to grow up. Just ask Matt Murdock (Ben Affleck, Pearl Harbor) who, as a young boy, was blinded after running into some toxic waste in one of the neighborhood alleys. On a positive note, his remaining four senses were mysteriously enhanced, giving him a radar-like ability which enables him to see without seeing.

Now an adult, he serves as a mild-mannered blind attorney by day. But at night, Matt becomes Daredevil, a leather-clad, masked vigilante. His goal is to bring justice to an unjust world, even if he has to break every law in the book to do it.

Hell's Kitchen is run by the crime boss, Kingpin (Michael Clarke Duncan, The Green Mile) whose main henchman is a bald, tattooed, and severely disturbed killer named Bullseye (Colin Farrell, The Recruit).

When Daredevil is not beating up on some bad guy, he's busy trying to beat up his latest girlfriend. Electra Natchios (Jennifer Garner, Alias) is a martial arts expert who pits her skills against those of Daredevil and between blows and counterblows, finds herself falling in love.

Yes, it is a ridiculous premise but even a silly story can be redeemed if the production values rise to the challenge. These don't, though it is not for lack of trying.

Writer/director Mark Steven Johnson (Simon Birch) seems almost desperate to outdo Spider-man and so he fills the screen with nonstop action and special effects. There's two main problems: First is that he never really takes the time to let us care about his characters or what happens to them. Secondly, he keeps the camera so close to the action that, while it looks like something cool is happening, it is difficult to tell what it is... much less appreciate it.

Ben Affleck is a rather bland blind super hero and, as his arch-rival, Colin Farrell gives new hope to all overactors everywhere. His characterization is not just over the top, it relocates just where that top might be. (For the record, that wasn't a compliment.)

Jennifer Garner provides little more than eye candy as she and her wonderbra are put through their physical paces. The most effective characterizations are from a pair of supporting players - David Keith (Behind Enemy Lines) as Matt's washed up boxer of a dad and Michael Clarke Duncan as the cigar-chomping Kingpin. They, at least, seem to have established a solid foundation upon which to build an actual character rather than relying solely upon superficiality.

The strongest weapon in Daredevil's arsenal is his lack of fear. Fear is something that affects us all, and always in a negative sense. God constantly reminds us to "fear not" or "be not afraid" for He knows that fear can be a stumbling block to our receiving the fullness of blessings and the deliverance which He desires for us to have. One of the things Jesus Christ came to deliver us from was fear.

And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage. Hebrews 2:15 (KJV)

The hope granted to us and the love bestowed upon us, when we claim and believe it, can completely annihilate any fear that may exist within us. And without fear, what can we not accomplish? That said, we should note that an absence of fear does not give us a license to risk our lives - rather it gives us the freedom to live them more fully.
