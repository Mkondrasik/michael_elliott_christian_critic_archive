Author-verified transcription

Pain & Gain gets it half right. I totally understand the Pain part. After all, I watched the film all the way through to its end. It’s the Gain that I don’t quite understand because I certainly didn’t gain anything from the experience.

Touted as being a “true story,” Pain & Gain shows us a trio of body builders who, having a desire to live the American Dream, decide to steal someone’s else’s dream and claim it as their own. Daniel Lugo (Mark Wahlberg) has spent his life beefing up his muscles instead of his brain. As a result, he’s got a body that landed him a job as a personal trainer at a gym. But he wants more for himself. Too bad he spent no time developing his intellect.

When a rich, obnoxious client (Tony Shalhoub) falls in his lap, Lugo enlists the help of two other muscle-heads in a knuckle-brained scheme. Lugo convinces the steroid shooting (and therefore impotent) Adrian Dorbal (Anthony Mackie) and the cocaine snorting, Jesus-loving Paul Doyle (Dwayne Johnson) into kidnapping the rich guy and taking all his stuff. His plan doesn’t go much further than that… which is why it doesn’t work that well.

Michael Bay, scaling down from his typical multi-million dollar budgeted, high-explosive blockbusters, delivers a relatively modest black comedy about stupid people doing stupid things for stupid reasons. His problem is that there’s just too much stupid floating about the screen and he is unable to harness it or deliver it in a smart or clever way.

Envy can lead people into making foolish and dangerous decisions. We should not spend a moment of our time comparing what we have (or don’t have) to what someone else has (or doesn’t have.) Such comparisons simply build strife, discontent, and envy – all of which feeds off of negative energy that will have a corrosive effect upon our peace and wellbeing.

“A calm and undisturbed mind and heart are the life and health of the body, but envy, jealousy, and wrath are like rottenness of the bones.” Proverbs 14:30 (Amplified)

There is no good thing that comes of envy. While it is human nature to desire to grow and better oneself, as sons of God we have no right or reason to envy any other person. Our heavenly Father promises us that He has given us all things. We have no cause to doubt Him and even less cause to desire anything more than the all things promised us by God Himself.

Envy is nothing more than an acid that eats away at our righteousness. If we harbor envious thoughts in our head, they will eventually be manifested in our actions. Actions that will lead to a dead end where we will find only pain but no gain.
