Sermon / spiritual topic: Motherhood

Author-verified transcription

If only life were as “easy as pie...” That’s the very appropriate tagline of the sweetly simple independent film Waitress.

Jenna (Keri Russell) is an irresistibly cute and vibrant woman trapped in a dead end job, a loveless marriage, lots of unfulfilled dreams and an impossible situation - she’s pregnant with a baby she doesn’t want. She also has one undeniable strength... she’s a pie-making genius. Her pies are described as indescribable, heavenly, other-worldly, and (more mundanely) yummy.

Her life doesn’t engender the same accolades. Her husband, Earl (Jeremy Sisto), gives men everywhere a bad name. He’s ignorant, selfish, abusive, mean-spirited... and those are his most positive qualities. Yet Jenna stays with him because she feels she can do nothing else but make pies.

It’s only when a new married doctor (Nathan Fillion) comes to town that Jenna begins to think that other options may be available to her. Throughout her adulterous tryst with her OB-GYN, she maintains the confidence of her co-workers (Cheryl Hines and Adrienne Shelly), puts up with the ornery owner of the diner (Andy Griffith) and continues to concoct mouth-watering pie creations as delicious as they are ridiculously named (“I Don’t Want This Baby Pie?”).

Director Adrienne Shelly (who was tragically killed before the release of her film) wisely kept the tone of the film as frothy as one of Jenna’s meringues. Whipping up the laughs is a star-making performance by Keri Russell whose Jenna is endearing, approachable, and immensely loveable.

As her contrast, Jeremy Sisto gives a performance that could have easily become a one-note stereotype of a brutish oaf of a man. Instead, he makes Earl a fully-realized personality that will immediately repel audiences.

Nathan Fillion plays the smitten doctor with a charming boyish awkwardness and both Cheryl Hines and Adrienne Shelly provide strong support as the other two-thirds of the waitress team at Joe’s Pie Shop.

The sanctity of marriage does take a bit of a hit in the film as Jenna’s union is viewed as such a grave mistake as to cause empathy when she falls into adultery in order to find some degree of happiness. We should remember as we’re watching this film that one man who fails to grasp or embrace the God-given responsibilities as the loving head of a marriage relationship does not reflect poorly on the institution itself. And it certainly shouldn’t be used as rationalization for an extra-marital affair.

What the film does capture is the life changing effect that takes place as a mother bonds with the child she brings into the world. It may be cliché but oftentimes clichés contain some of the world’s strongest truths. The sacrifices a mother will make on behalf of her child have been documented throughout the ages - including in the scriptures. It was this very bond that Solomon relied upon to make one of his most famous judgments.

And the king said, Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one, and half to the other. Then spake the woman whose the living child was unto the king, for her bowels yearned upon her son, and she said, O my lord, give her the living child, and in no wise slay it. But the other said, Let it be neither mine nor thine, but divide it. Then the king answered and said, Give her the living child, and in no wise slay it: she is the mother thereof. 1 Kings 3:25-27 (KJV)
