Author-verified transcription

Mort Rainey is a successful author whose life has taken a rather unsuccessful turn in Secret Window. Based upon a Steven King story adapted and directed by David Koepp (who wrote Panic Room), the film is a mystery thriller with psychological overtones that entertains while, at the same time, tries to keep us guessing as to the final outcome.

After Mort (Johnny Depp) discovers that his wife (Maria Bello) has been having an affair with another man (Timothy Hutton), he leaves their house and retreats to his cabin in the woods. Six months later, we find him there, spending most of his days in his tattered bathrobe, taking naps on his couch. It is from that position that he is awakened by an insistent knocking at his front door. The knocking is due to a strange Southern man named John Shooter (John Turturro) who insists that Mort has plagiarized one of his stories.

Despite Mort's denials, Shooter becomes insistent and, as time goes by, increasingly threatening. Soon, people and pets start becoming expendable items which are used to make a very specific point designed to sway Mort into meeting Shooter's unusual demands.

Mort employs the services of private detective Ken Karsch (Charles Dutton) to track and protect him from his strange accuser. Though Karsch has difficulty believing that there's a real threat involved, he soon discovers that his initial reaction is false.

David Koepp has taken King's novella which appeared in "Four Past Midnight" and expanded it into a full length feature film. He has added humor, a bit of suspense, some character development... but not enough twists to keep us from guessing who did what to whom. Some audience members may be caught by surprise, but anyone familiar with the genre will see the end coming long before it arrives.

Johnny Depp, in his first film since his successful turn as a pirate, shows enough quirks and eccentricities to entertain us despite a rather obvious story. The humor built into his character helps to make the film watchable but it is not enough to make it notable.

John Turturro plays a stereotypical Southern redneck who would be more than comfortable in the cast of Deliverance. But there just isn't enough substance to his role to make him entirely believable.

The rest of the cast only serves to provide various plot points but adds little to the development of the story itself. Maria Bello plays Mort's wife who is in the midst of a divorce; Timothy Hutton is the man with whom she is currently involved and serves as a possible suspect. Charles Dutton is the skeptical private eye and Len Cariou is the local sheriff who seems oblivious to the events that are taking place under his eye.

Secret Window is not a bad film, but it isn't necessarily a great one. Depp's characterization is worth a look - as is Tuturro's. But overall, the twists of the film are a bit obvious which may reduce the "enjoyment" level of the experience for some.

The lesson we can draw from the film has to do with what we choose to do with our minds. We can dwell on the hurt and pain that we have experienced in the past or we can choose to look towards the future which holds the promise of a life yet to come. The latter choice is far healthier.

Brothers, I do not count myself to have laid hold, but one thing I do, forgetting the things behind, and stretching forward to those things before. Philippians 3:13 (LITV)
