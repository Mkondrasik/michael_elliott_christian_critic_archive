Raise Your Voice

Michael Elliott
Original publication date

Rating
2 stars

1 hr: 37 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Dreams; Perseverance; Vision

Author-verified transcription

Faintly reminiscent of 1980s Fame, Raise Your Voice depicts a young girl's attempts to follow her dreams. Blessed with a great voice, a bubbly personality, and an obvious joy for singing, the only thing 16-year-old Terri Fletcher (Hilary Duff) doesn't have going for her is the fact that she lives in the small and culturally limited town of Flagstaff, Arizona. Apparently, there are few artistic opportunities in Flagstaff.

It is a mere logistical detail that can be easily remedied according to her amazingly supportive older brother (Jason Ritter). He burns a DVD of Terri singing around the house and in the church choir and sends it off to an exclusive school for the performing arts. They are offering an accelerated summer program to a few talented teenagers. The bad news is that the program is located in Los Angeles and Terri's father (David Keith) is convinced that the corrupting influence of LA will be harmful to his daughter. He forbids her to go.

When Terri does get accepted, her mother (Rita Wilson) and her aunt (Rebecca DeMornay) conspire to keep her whereabouts a secret from her overly protective dad which gives her the freedom to attend. But by then a family tragedy for which Terri feels responsible has her so shaken up that she's no longer certain she wants to go. She's not even sure she ever wants to sing again.

Fans of Hilary Duff, accustomed to the lighter fare with which she is usually associated, may be surprised by the heavily emotional themes of Raise Your Voice. Death, tragedy, and guilt take center stage and have a debilitating effect on young Terri.

Hilary shows surprising growth as an actress and handles delicate scenes of emotional complexity with intelligence and care. She is able to believably convey the internal torment and conflicting emotions that often accompany grief and guilt.

Of course, her character does decide to defy her father and go to LA. There she is thrust into an intensely competitive and unfriendly environment. She survives the experience thanks to a few fellow students who befriend her. She also gets support from an unconventional teacher (John Corbett) who sees the talent buried under her emotional turmoil.

Director Sean McNamara, who introduced audiences to a ten-year-old Hilary Duff in Casper Meets Wendy, utilized his television experience to complete this shoot under a very tight 30 day schedule - and it unfortunately shows. Plot developments and character arcs feel rushed and superficial - specifically in relation to the hardnosed attitude of Terri's father and in the developing love connection between Terri and another student Jay (Oliver James).

The film's major theme is the pursuing of one's dreams and letting nothing stand in one's way of achieving them. The first step in accomplishing anything of note is to set a vision for where we want to be. Without a "dream" or "vision," we have nothing to focus upon as we go through life. Terri's dream - to sing professionally - was hampered by both physical and emotional obstacles. In order to be true to her dream she had to find a way to overcome the obstacles which stood in her path.

As Christians we are not only commanded to do the same, we are uniquely equipped to meet and overcome all obstacles. God has given us the vision, the tools to meet that vision, and the strength to persevere until we do. Our success is guaranteed unless we talk ourselves out of it by failing to stay the course. If we quit before we achieve what it is we set out to achieve — what God would have us achieve - we have no one to blame but ourselves.

"Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up." Galatians 6:9 (NIV)
