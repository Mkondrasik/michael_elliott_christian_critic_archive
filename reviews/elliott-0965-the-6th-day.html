Author-verified transcription

For Arnie fans, what could be better? Two Schwarzeneggers for the price of one! (Of course, Arnie detractors might not look upon this as a "double your pleasure" enjoyment.) In any event, The 6th Day superficially deals with the morality question facing tomorrow's world. Human cloning: Boon or abomination?

Set in a future that is "closer than we think," The 6th Day depicts a rather frightening world in which synthetic or virtual reality is fast encroaching upon real life. Helicopter pilot, Adam Gibson (Arnold Schwarzenegger (End of Days) has so far resisted most the "conveniences" of futuristic life. But he is a lone figure trying to single-handedly hold back the onrushing sea of technical progress. His daughter wants a "sym-pal," a horrific looking doll/robot which gives the grotesque appearance of being almost human. His wife wants to go to "Re-Pet" to clone a duplicate of their family dog so their daughter will never have to know that the original died.

The explanation of the film's title comes from Genesis 1:27. "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them." This God did on the sixth day. The future law, according to the film, states that man must not try to do what God accomplished. Human cloning is illegal... A 6th day violation.

But this doesn't stop a group of entrepreneurial scofflaws. Through a complicated and extremely far-fetched series of events, this group mistakenly clones Gibson and releases him into normal society. When Gibson arrives home to see "himself" enjoying his own surprise birthday party, the clone patrol must try to kill him lest their illegal activities become known.

In The 6th Day, Arnie is in his favorite cinematic surroundings. Alone, with only a minimal understanding about what is happening, pitted against a larger, better equipped, better informed opponent. This one has an additional characteristic which, among other things, will drive up the body count of the film. No matter how many times Arnie kills them, they don't stay dead. They just keep cloning.

As action adventures go, The 6th Day stays close to formula. There are no surprises, no revelations. Even a plot twist at the end is something that we could see coming some 30 minutes into the film. But there are plenty of chase scenes, explosions, futuristic gunplay, and opportunities for pithy one liners to be thrown out by Arnie with a self-awareness that is often amusing. "I may be back," he intones to a salesman, playing off his catch phrase from an earlier, more successful time.

Tony Goldwyn (Bounce) is cast as clone czar Drucker, the chief bad guy. He sees cloning as an inevitable reality whose time has come... whether the earth's people are ready for it or not. Robert Duvall (Gone In 60 Seconds) is wasted in yet another film role beneath his abilities. Here, he is Dr. Graham Weir, whose process is used in the cloning process.

Director Roger Spottiswoode (Tomorrow Never Dies), working from a script by Cormac and Marianne Wibberley (Motel Blue) fills the screen with clever futuristic devices such as remote controlled jet-powered helicopters, virtual holographic girlfriends, and automobile autopilots.

Of course, the terminology used in the film is incorrect. Whatever else cloning may be, it is not the "creation" of life. Only God can create. To literally create something, one must bring something out of nothingness. Man simply doesn't have the power or ability to accomplish that. Cloning, even the kind invented for this film, must start with an existing life force and then build from there.

Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created. Revelation 4:11 (KJV)

Man, despite all his claims and braggadocio has never created anything. He can make, form, fashion, manipulate, produce, initiate, instigate, construct, and mold, using elements already in existence. But to create something where once there was nothing? That is and will always be solely God's domain.
