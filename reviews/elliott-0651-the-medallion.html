They say that if you have an infinite amount of monkeys and give them an infinite amount of typewriters and an infinite amount of time, they'll eventually bang out the works of Shakespeare. True or not, it is doubtful that they would do worse than the five screenwriters responsible for The Medallion.

Jackie Chan (Shanghai Knights) stars as Eddie Wang, a high-kicking, fast-moving Hong Kong cop who is working in conjunction with Interpol for reasons not immediately made clear. It has something to do with a young boy, a light-emitting medallion possessing supernatural, life-giving powers, and an internationally known criminal known only as Snakehead (Julian Sands, Timecode).

After thwarting the boy's kidnapping, Wang and the most inept law enforcement personnel seen since the days of Inspector Clousseau allow the kid to get nabbed the very next day. Fortunately Wang avoids the need for any actual police investigative work by accidentally bumping into one of the henchmen getting out of a cab and the chase is, once again, on.

The Medallion should not be mistaken for one of Chan's successful Hollywood franchise pictures (Rush Hour, Shanghai Noon, etc.) But it is certainly trying to look like one. This is actually a Hong Kong production which has been given the Hollywood treatment. With the largest budget ever for a HK film, The Medallion also has been filmed in English, uses British actors, is set part of the time in Ireland, and operates under the advisement of American studio executives who insisted that director Gordon Chan (Beast Cops) shave about 30 minutes from his original cut.

This last part, in hindsight, might have been ill-advised. The film feels choppy, the narrative skips and sputters and, for once, even Chan's amazing athletic abilities fail to captivate.

Claire Forlani (Meet Joe Black) doesn't have much to do as Eddie's love interest, although she does manage to throw down a few impressive martial arts moves of her own. Comic actor Lee Evans (The Ladies Man) as Watson, Eddie's incompetent Interpol partner, plays so broadly... almost clown-like in fact, that at times he appears to be part of a different film altogether. On the other hand, he does provide the most life in the film even if it does seem a bit "sitcomish."

In the most amusing sequence, Watson seems so mesmerized by the miraculous healing properties of the mystical medallion that he can't stop himself from repeatedly poking a "healed" man with a knife just to see it happen again.

A medallion which grants supernatural powers and eternal life doesn't exist, of course. But this film's fantasy can lead us to speculate on what we might our future may look like once we inhabit the new spiritual bodies which God promised would one day be ours.

But our citizenship is in heaven. And we eagerly await a Savior from there, the Lord Jesus Christ, who by the power that enables him to bring everything under his control, will transform our lowly bodies so that they will be like his glorious body. Philippians 3:20-21 (NIV)

We will not know the fullness of this reality until Christ comes for his church and we are either resurrected or changed but there are a few clues sprinkled throughout the scriptures. We know that the life of our new bodies will not be dependent upon breath but rather upon a "quickening spirit." (1 Corinthians 15:45). By studying what Jesus Christ was able to do in his resurrected body, we'll know in part what we'll be able to do in ours for we will be "like him" (1 John 3:2).

We'll know more soon enough. Until that time, we'll just have to muddle through with the limitations of our current bodies keeping ourselves strengthened by the hope of his return.
