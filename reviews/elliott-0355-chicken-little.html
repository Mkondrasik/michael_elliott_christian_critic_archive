Chicken Little

Author-verified transcription

Don't expect big things from Chicken Little. This newest film from Disney is being heavily hyped by the studio as the next step forward in computer digital animation but as any fan of the Pixar films will recognize, Disney is no longer king of the castle when it comes to animated films. They may be making new steps but Pixar is still strides ahead of them.

The Chicken Little story is familiar to most children and is the basis for the 77 minute film which quickly morphs into a sci-fi adventure. The movie starts after the infamous 'the sky is falling' event which has made Chicken Little (voice by Zack Braff) into the town's laughingstock. Even Hollywood is planning a movie with Little as the feathery butt of the joke.

In hopes to redeem himself, especially in the eyes of his single parent, Buck Cluck (Garry Marshall), who was, in his day, a local sports legend, Little decides to join the town's baseball team. Against all odds, he scores the winning run in a championship game and instantly becomes the new town hero. That is, until a piece of the sky hits him on the head again.

He is hesitant to subject himself to the town's ridicule once more so Little decides to keep the truth to himself. He only brings his closest nerdy friends, Abby Mallard aka the Ugly Duckling (Joan Cusack), Runt of the Litter (Steve Zahn), and the non-verbal Fish Out of Water, in on the secret.

They quickly discover that the piece of sky is not sky at all - it is a section of a spaceship which may or may not be preparing an invasion on the planet. A hero is needed and the only one around is Chicken Little.

There's something missing in the voices of the central characters. Zack Braff is not distinctive as the title character. Cute can't really carry a film - even one that lasts slightly more than an hour. Garry Marshall's voice would seem perfect for an animated character but it doesn't work with this one. Buck Cluck turns out to be a pivotal role in this film but Marshall undercooks it.

The secondary characters are a bit more fun. Steve Zahn and Joan Cusack have some good moments as Little's barnyard pals. And the non-verbal Fish Out of Water is the film's scene stealer.

Chicken Little, despite its high tech and polished exterior is still only childish entertainment with little depth. The message woven into the script is a bit too heady for children to grasp and a bit too flawed for parents to accept.

The original moral to the Chicken Little story speaks of the danger of making assumptions. In this Disney version, the whole 'sky is falling' episode is relegated to a barely mentioned back story. In its place is left a cautionary message to parents about the importance of supporting your children even if you think they are wrong. It is a strange moral - especially when you consider the scriptures.

Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him. Proverbs 22:15 (KJV)

Parents should of course support their children in their endeavors. But they should also guide and correct them. To blindly support and encourage a child who, in his foolishness, is taking a wrong turn down a bad path would be the height of bad parenting.
