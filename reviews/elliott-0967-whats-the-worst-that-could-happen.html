Author-verified transcription

What's The Worst That Could Happen? It's not only the title of a new film... it is a question most reviewers ask themselves on the way to the theater. Sometimes we even paraphrase it to say: "How bad can it be?" In the case of this production, things were leaning towards a favorable reply: A pair of proven comedic actors; a large and talented supporting cast; and a moderately successful sitcom director with a handful of feature films under his belt. Who knows? What's the worst that could happen? We soon learn as the answer stares gloomily back at us from the screen.

Even with its somewhat promising ingredients, this comedy is stale and flat, its interesting premise wasted by the all-too-obvious and formulaic treatment of the filmmakers. What's The Worst That Could Happen? ends up being worse than a rhetorical question. It is a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Kevin (Martin Lawrence, Big Momma's House) is a thief. Being the hero, he's obviously a no harm, no foul kind of guy. He's involved with a girl who, even after finding out he's a thief, gives him a family heirloom... a diamond studded ring.

Kevin and his partner Berger (John Leguizamo, Moulin Rouge) decide to rob a business tycoon named Max Fairbanks (Danny DeVito, The Big Kahuna) who just may be a bigger crook (in a white collar kind of way) than the guys who are robbing him. Kevin is caught in the act by Max and, when the police arrive, is forced to relinquish his ring because Max fraudulently claims that it is one of the things that was stolen.

A perverse game of ego and pride is then begun as neither one will give up his claim to the ring even though pursuing it may bring ruin to them and the relationships they have with their women.

It is a silly little story and none of the actors give anything close to a notable performance with the possible exception of William Fichtner (Drowning Mona) as the flamboyant detective Alex Tardio. Even then, the comedic mileage he manages to gain is all his own doing for the character he plays is so extraneous to the plot that there's no reason for him to be in this movie.

Martin Lawrence and Danny DeVito both rely on tried and true shtick but because they are so accomplished as actors, they won't be embarrassed by their efforts. Still, neither of them will be boasting this comedy as being a highlight on his filmography.

The screenplay by Matthew Chapman (Color of Night) is little more than forced gags performed by quirky characters who are neither logical nor rational in the things they do. For instance, Kevin is given a facial tic which serves as a "lie detector" because it goes into hyper-drive whenever he's not telling the truth. As ridiculous as this character trait may be, it is even more ridiculous because it isn't even used as comedy fodder to trap the character into an uncomfortable position. It is brought up on various occasions, but never towards any meaningful purpose and never to garner a laugh.

Even though the film has as much substance as a quarter-inch slice of angel food cake, we can still find something meaningful within its premise. The two main characters are willing to sacrifice all they have built... all they have acquired for themselves to lay claim to an object which has relatively little value. The object (the ring) is never really the issue. It was the damaged pride that the ring represented which lies at the core of these characters' decisions.

Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall. Proverbs 16:18 (KJV)

Neither Max nor Kevin took the time or had the wisdom to analyze what action would be best for their lives. Instead they plunged blindly ahead, fueled by a thirst for revenge and a desire to win in an immoral battle. In so doing, they got themselves into worse trouble than if they had just decided to let the other have the relatively worthless ring.

What precious object in our possession would we not relinquish in order to be able to have peace, joy and happiness abound in our lives?
