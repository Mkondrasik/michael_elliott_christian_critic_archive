Eyes Wide Shut

Sermon / spiritual topic: Jealousy; Marriage; Trust; Infidelity

Author-verified transcription

If you've missed the hype surrounding the late Stanley Kubrick's final film, you obviously have been living with your "Eyes Wide Shut." This sexual odyssey starring two of the more bankable stars of Hollywood has been the subject of tabloid headlines and scandalous rumors for much of 1999. Now that the movie has finally been released to the public, we are able to see that, once again, the hype far overshadows the worth of the film.

Eyes Wide Shut is a nearly 3 hour film that feels like it drags for 3 days. Even with titillating subject matter and pervasive nudity, the film remains cold, detached and thoroughly non-erotic as it limps along its mind-numbingly slow pace.

Tom Cruise (Jerry Maguire) is Dr. William (Bill) Hartford, a highly successful NY physician who is happily married to his beautiful wife, Alice (Nicole Kidman, Practical Magic). They attend a ritzy party thrown by the affluent Victor Ziegler (Sidney Pollack, Tootsie) where both husband and wife independently enjoy meaningless flirtations with total strangers. Afterwards, Alice, in a marijuana influenced diatribe, reveals that she once seriously fantasized having an affair with a handsome naval officer she never met.

This discovery sends Bill reeling off into the night where he encounters a number of sexual temptations including a kindly prostitute, a fourteen year old tramp clad in nothing but her Underoos, and a patient willing to dump her fiancé for him. The evening reaches its climax (pardon the pun) at a deeply secret, costumed orgy where masked participants engage in anonymous sex with multiple partners as unknown voyeurs look on with surprising disinterest.

Computer generated images were placed in front of the copulating couples by the producers after Mr. Kubrick's death (albeit with his alleged approval) in order to receive an "R" rating from the MPAA. They needn't have bothered. This film deserves and should have received an NC-17 rating, with or without the posthumous editing.

Mr. Kubrick's past films have become recognizable cinematic landmarks. 2001: A Space Odyssey, Dr. Strangelove: Or How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bob, and The Shining, among others, continue to appear on numerous "best" lists around the world. Eyes Wide Shut is a disappointing finale to a notable film career.

Clearly, Mr. Kubrick wanted to evoke an intellectual response from his audience instead of an emotional one, hence the snail-like pace of the film. Intellectually the only thought that kept running through my mind was: "What were the Cruises thinking?" Nicole Kidman (Mrs. Tom Cruise) does not fare particularly well. Her drunk and stoned scenes are notably embarrassing, obviously directed to be so. Her nude or seminude scenes are frequent and unnecessary. Mr. Cruise, while being a more accomplished craftsman, still seems hampered by Mr. Kubrick's austere directorial hand.

Giving the final product more credibility than it deserves, I will say that the film is more about jealousy than it is about sex. Miss Kidman's character makes her shocking revelation because she is hurt by her husband's lack of jealousy. Mr. Cruise's character entertains the thoughts of sexual infidelity because, for the first time, he is jealous of his wife's fantasies. This jealousy, which his wife goes out of her way to provoke, nearly consumes him to the point of destruction. As it is, his life is spared, but his relationship with his wife may never be the same.

Jealousy is cruel as the grave: the coals thereof are coals of fire, which hath a most vehement flame. Song of Solomon 8:6b [KJV]

Jealousy is not a sign or indication of love. Rather, it marks a significant absence of trust. Without commitment, marriage is merely a temporary convenience, devoid of any substance. The same can be said of sex. Eyes Wide Shut tries to show the personal devastation that jealousy and infidelity can bring. But then anyone with their eyes wide open would already know that.
