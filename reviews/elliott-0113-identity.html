Identity

Michael Elliott
Original publication date

2½ stars

Sermon / spiritual topic: The mind; spiritual warfare; fear; disciplined thinking; God’s Word

Author-verified transcription

Agatha Christie’s And Then There Were None gets a gory makeover in James Mangold’s Identity. As with the 1945 classic murder mystery, Identity gathers a group of strangers together in an inhospitable (and inescapable) place and then begins killing them off one by one.

Identity, written by Michael Cooney (Jack Frost) is extremely violent and contains images which some may not want to see. There is a solvable mystery to the story (for the more astute members of the audience) and to those looking forward to seeing this film, I would suggest trying to avoid hearing any “spoilers” in order to experience it without knowing the ending in advance.

The movie takes place on a dark and stormy night and any intrepid sojourners who dare to battle the elements are going to pay for their foolishness. After a series of accidents and chance meetings, a small motel in the middle of nowhere serves as an oasis from the tempest. It just isn’t very safe.

Among those seeking haven are an ex-cop turned chauffeur (John Cusack, Max); the has-been actress he’s driving (Rebecca DeMornay, The Hand That Rocks the Cradle); a family of three; a pair of newlyweds; a hooker (Amanda Peet, Changing Lanes); and a cop (Ray Liotta, Blow) transporting his prisoner (Jake Busey, Tomcats). It isn’t long before the bodies begin to pile up and the red herrings start coming out as the survivors try to uncover who among them is a psychotic killer.

Director James Mangold (Girl, Interrupted) carefully weaves his way through the psychologically complex web of a story. Maintaining the tension at an appropriate high level and revealing only just enough to keep us guessing, he is aided by some great character work from his talented cast.

Both John Cusack and Ray Liotta play to their strengths in credible performances. John C. McGinley (Stealing Harvard) has some nice moments as a quirky man who is not especially good under pressure. John Hawkes (Hardball) is a standout as the creepy motel clerk with something to hide. Pruitt Taylor Vince (Mumford) is a large and frightening presence in a parallel story of a mentally disturbed and violent psychiatric patient facing his execution for a killing spree which took place, oddly enough, at a motel.

However, it is through the eyes of John Cusack’s character that the story is told and his “everyman” persona is inviting enough for the audience to immediately connect with him and hang on for the duration of the film.

With a good cast and a psychological twist on a classic premise, Identity has the goods which will please fans of the genre. For those who, like me, don’t much care for tense scenes designed to scare or to see images of death and mutilation, my only suggestion would be to keep one eye on the exit and the other eye firmly closed... at least until the lights come up.

How does one explain a criminally deranged mind? What causes the total breakdown of the moral boundaries that lead to the kind of perversity that we too often see and hear on our network news? Scientists have investigated such things as chemical imbalances, environmental influences, genetic predisposition, as well as other theories without coming any closer to discovering an answer. Sooner or later someone should look at the question from a spiritual perspective.

According to the scriptures, the mind is the spiritual battlefield where good and evil vie for dominion. This is why God commands us to keep His Word in our mind and protect it as we would a treasure. God’s Word, our standard for goodness and godliness, is always able to drive back the evil thoughts which try to find a home in our minds.

For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind. 2 Timothy 1:7 (KJV)

A sound (or disciplined) mind is one that is rooted in the truths of God. With His strength and wisdom, evil thoughts or intents can be halted and repelled.
