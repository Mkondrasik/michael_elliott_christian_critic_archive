Author-verified transcription

In After Earth, a spaceship filled with descendants of Earth citizens who were forced to evacuate the planet some 1,000 years ago find themselves having to crash land on the home planet of their ancestors. For the two survivors, they are about to learn why their forefathers left in the first place.

After ecologically destroying the earth through their own negligence, man sought to find a replacement home in the cosmos. They quickly found that they were not alone. Not only did their global expansion come under attack, the aliens chose to make Earth completely uninhabitable by sending creatures called Ursas who have the ability to sense fear and the power to terminate any life force whose fear they sense.

When Cypher Raige (Will Smith) and his teen aged son Katai (Jaden Smith) are the sole survivors of a crash landing upon the inhospitable earth, Cypher is forced to teach his son the survival techniques needed in this harsh environment if they are to have any chance of an escape.

With the emergency beacon contained in the tail section of the spacecraft some 100 kilometers away and the fact that Cypher’s legs were broken in the crash, there is no choice but to send the untested Katai into the dangerous and deadly wilderness that Earth has become.

Director M. Night Shyamalan keeps the action pumped up although the film suffers at the hands of the father/son team of Will and Jaden Smith. Will, as the fearless Cypher, seems to have trouble expressing any other emotions. This results in a flat and uninteresting performance. Jaden has quite the opposite problem. His character is filled with fear and as such is not particularly appealing as a lead character.

That said, After Earth does allow for an opportunity to discuss fear in some detail from a spiritual perspective. The only defense earthlings have against the Ursas is to feel no fear. When an individual can suppress their fear, the Ursas cannot detect them. As one character states, “Danger is real… Fear is a choice.”

That is a lesson that resounds loudly in the spiritual competition. Fear is a believer’s worst enemy. It is a weakness that our spiritual adversary will exploit and use to our eventual destruction. But we do not have to experience fear. It is a spiritual choice. Fear is nothing more than negative believing. Fear is the belief that God will not save us nor protect us from harm. There is a simple antidote for fear. God explains it for us in 1 John 4:18:

“There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love.” 1 John 4:18 (KJV)

We need not fear anything in this world (or any other). God promises us that He will take care of us. We need not fear poverty, sickness, death, destruction, war, or any other calamity. By recognizing God’s promises to us, we free ourselves of the bondage that fear places us in. Perfect love… God’s love for us… enables us to eliminate fear from our lives and empowers us to act in the face of fearful opposition in order to achieve a victory.
