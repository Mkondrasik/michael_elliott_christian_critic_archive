Author-verified transcription

Instinct is the rarest of all movies... A gripping story that engages the heart while challenging the mind. The movie's philosophical leanings were inspired by the novel Ishmael by Daniel Quinn, but the screenplay by Gerald DiPego (Phenomenon) tells an altogether different story.

Anthony Hopkins (The Edge) plays Ethan Powell, a brilliant professor of anthropology who has been missing for two years while studying mountain gorillas in Rwanda. He is found and subsequently arrested for clubbing to death two of the officials who found him.

Now resolutely silent, he has been brought back to the United States for psychiatric evaluation. His case has been assigned to Theo Caulder (Cuba Gooding Jr., What Dreams May Come), a rising young psychiatrist who sees in this case the opportunity to further his career and gain a possible book deal.

As Theo begins to unlock the mystery of what happened to Powell and find the psychological cause of his violent outbreaks, the case takes on a life-changing significance for him.

The film showcases bravura performances from its two male leads. Mr. Hopkins proves to us again that he is one of the most talented and inventive screen actors of our day. His performance is both raw and controlled, as he keeps his primal energies bottled up, ready to be released at the slightest provocation.

Mr. Gooding's Theo is also bottled up. Suppressing his emotions, he has become expert at the game of career advancement. Under the tutelage of his mentor Ben Hillard (Donald Sutherland, Without Limits), he has positioned himself as one of the most promising new psychiatrists in residency. Slowly, we see Theo's emotional wall which distances him from his patients start to break down as Powell begins to teach him what he has learned in the wilds of Rwanda whilst living amongst the gorillas.

Director Jon Turteltaub (Phenomenon) doesn't miss a beat in the telling of this intellectually suspenseful film. It is a movie of layers which combine together beautifully. In addition to the main story of what happened in Rwanda, there is a fuller exploration of the awful conditions at the prison for the criminally insane where Powell is being held, and the changes that occur there because of Powell and Theo.

To support his leads, Mr. Turteltaub has assembled a very talented cast. Mr. Sutherland gives a wonderfully understated performance as a wise and concerned professor. Maura Tierney (Forces of Nature), is Powell's beautiful but emotionally conflicted daughter Lyn, who becomes a sounding board for Theo as he tries to understand her father. George Dzundza (Crimson Tide) plays a complacent and non-effective psychiatrist assigned to the rundown and oppressive Harmony Bay Correctional Institute. John Ashton (Beverly Hills Cop) plays a sadistic guard whose authority is challenged as Theo becomes less involved with his own expectations of fame and glory and more concerned with the situations and conditions around him.

This is one of the major themes of the movie. Powell calls it the difference between being a "taker" and being a "leaver." By Powell's sharing his story with Theo, Theo learns to reach out beyond himself and his own goals in order to give of himself to those who may be in need. Dr. Hillard may be teaching him how to be a better psychiatrist, but it is Ethan Powell who teaches him how to be a better man.

It is a fundamental biblical principle, this idea of being a "leaver." Jesus Christ himself told his followers, "It is more blessed to give than to receive." This principle is one that should be instinctive to all of us. An instinct is something that is innate, compelling us from within. It is also something that can be either suppressed or nurtured.

Too many of us are taught (wrongly) that it is better to receive than to give. Our culture is continually bombarded with messages of greed and self-aggrandizement. As a result, we are being raised as a community of "takers." It is sad, for it is our instinctive giving nature that lies at the heart of true love. If we don't give, we can't love. And if we can't love, we're not living.
