Author-verified transcription

We can happily remove The Love Bug from the "where are they now?" column. No longer a forgotten 1960s film icon, he has been found, retooled and polished to a high sheen in order to star in the newest Disney family film, Herbie: Fully Loaded. I'm happy to say that it's as if he were never gone.

The film is a fresh, albeit silly, love story about a girl, her car, and the dream they share - the dream to race.

Lindsay Lohan stars as Maggie Peyton, the only female member of a legendary NASCAR family. Father Ray Peyton (Michael Keaton) has handed down the keys to the family business to his son, Ray Jr. (Breckin Meyer) even though it is Maggie who inherited the racing gene.

For a college graduation present, dad drives Maggie to the junk yard and lets her pick out the car of her choice. Her eyes eventually settle upon a beat up VW bug that quickly proves to have a mind (and heart) of its own.

Maggie and Herbie run the gauntlet - from street racing to demolition derbies to the pinnacle of all racing venues: NASCAR. Along the way they meet hundreds upon thousands of new friends and fans. They also meet one very big enemy - NASCAR champion Trip Murphy (Matt Dillon). After being trounced by Herbie in an impromptu street race, the heretofore undefeated Trip vows his revenge and redemption by squashing the bug once and for all.

Director Angela Robinson (D.E.B.S.) has clearly gone to school - both figuratively and literally. She has captured the essence of the earlier Love Bug films (The Love Bug, Herbie Rides Again, Herbie Goes to Monte Carlo, Herbie Goes Bananas) and continues in the rich vein they so successfully mined decades ago.

Lindsay Lohan may get top billing and she does wonderful work here in what will most probably be her last "adolescent" role, but it is the bug itself that makes the film so much fun for old and young alike. The car smiles, frowns, and rolls its headlights while displaying a range of emotions that should be the envy of half the Hollywood stars working today. It is a door-swinging, oil-spurting, horn-honking, all-pistons-firing performance that will be sure to enrapture a whole new generation of Herbie fans.

Lohan has a harder job that most may fail to recognize. After all, the car is just a small package of nuts, bolts, and gears. She must convince us that she believes it to have a soul and personality. It is a testament to her success that we are convinced. Because she believes, we believe and the movie-going experience is that much more pleasurable.

Herbie: Fully Loaded is a true family film in that all ages will find something to appreciate. There is a brief and halfway disguised sexual joke (courtesy of Herbie and his antenna "boinging" reaction to a cute little Beetle) but for the most part, this is a film that will allow parents to kick back, relax and enjoy with their children.

While enjoying the film, families could also learn to appreciate the subtle message in the story. Seeing Herbie in the junk yard, it was easy to dismiss him as a broken down and worthless hunk of metal. But as the movie proves, all he needed was a second chance and someone to believe in him. All of us need a second chance to be all that is in us to be. Thankfully, God believes in us and has provided us with the opportunity to rise from the scrap heap of our lives to claim the victory in the most important race of all.
