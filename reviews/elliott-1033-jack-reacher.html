Jack Reacher

Author-verified transcription

Jack Reacher, the extraordinary military cop turned into an off-the-grid hermit, reaches the big screen in this aptly titled film. Born from the pages of the Lee Child novel, One Shot, Jack Reacher features the titular character played by Tom Cruise who is drawn into a multiple murder investigation where an army sniper appears to be the chief suspect. But is he guilty?

The police and the D.A. (Richard Jenkins) certainly believe it. As a matter of fact, so does Reacher because he knows what terrible crimes the sniper has committed in the past. But when the suspect’s attorney (Rosamund Pike) hires Reacher to look into the details of the case, the perfect crime scene begins to unravel and Reacher draws the inevitable conclusion that there is a conspiracy afoot… and the sniper was set up to be nothing more than the fall guy.

Director Christopher McQuarrie fills the screen with car chases, fight scenes, and gun battles and he is competent in his execution. Where the film suffers is in the initial casting. While some have pointed out that the small-framed Tom Cruise simply does not fit the 6’5” character description so familiar to the fans of Lee Child, he does a serviceable job. He dons his action hero persona and dresses it up in what he probably hopes will be a new franchised cash cow. But while he moves the action along, he doesn’t distinguish Reacher as a unique or unforgettable character. Rosamund Pike just flounders in her role as the lawyer in stress/damsel in distress. She is a far better actress than she demonstrates here and is simply not believable in this particular role. Perhaps it is because the role itself is not written particularly well.

The supporting cast fares much better. Richard Jenkins does fine work as the D.A. (and father of the defense attorney); the always masterful Robert Duvall shows up briefly as a gun shop owner; Werner Herzog and Jai Courtney are effectively sinister as the puppet masters behind the insidious plot and David Oyelowo shows a commanding presence as the lead cop investigating the multiple homicide.

Reacher is on a quest for truth. The clues seem to point to an obvious conclusion but he is not satisfied with where they are leading him. So he makes the decision to dig further. As he does, he is forced to set aside his preconceptions in order to consider the new information he uncovers.

This is a basic tenet of biblical research. Most of us have preconceived ideas regarding the teachings of Jesus Christ or the messages derived from the Old Testament records. But as we study more and learn more about what the Bible says, we come across contradictions or inconsistencies that don’t match what we already know. If the Bible is indeed the recorded Word and Will of God these contradictions must be able to be explained. Many times, the problem is with our own preconceived notions.

“Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation.” 2 Peter 1:20 (KJV)

No one individual can or should interpret the Bible. What someone may think a scripture means is immaterial and has no bearing on the truth. When approaching the scriptures to learn what God has to say about a subject, we must allow the Bible to speak for itself. The interpretation will be there – either in the verse we are reading; or in the context of the verse as it may relate to the verses around it; or sometimes through the truth or knowledge conveyed in other sections of the Bible that may be carried over and applied to other records.

When we find that two separate verses don’t agree with each other on a subject, further study is required that we may root out the reason. It is our lack of understanding that has led to the error. In other words, there’s nothing wrong with the book. The fault, dear readers, is in our understanding of it.

“Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth.” 2 Timothy 2:15 (KJV)
