Author-verified transcription

I did not envy the task that producers Peter and Paul Lalonde (The Omega Code) set for themselves when they accepted the challenge of making a film adaptation of the phenomenal best-selling novel Left Behind written by authors Tim LaHaye and Jerry B. Jenkins. It would have been incredibly difficult for even the most accomplished film studio to satisfy the high expectations of the millions of fans which the Left Behind series has generated.

Disappointment is the first word that comes to mind upon viewing the Left Behind video which, in an unusual marketing approach, has preceded the film’s theatrical release by three months. It is, however, a disappointment born from our desire to see this wonderfully conceived story in which the Christian message of salvation is so beautifully woven within a thrilling adventure tale. The disappointment stems from our high expectations and preconceived vision we’ve developed while reading the book. Upon a second viewing, having had our preconceptions tempered a bit, we can better evaluate the film that was made. Left Behind is not a great movie, but it is a good movie... a good movie with a great message.

Staying relatively faithful to the text of the book, Left Behind tells the story of what happens after Christ comes to gather his church as is prophesied in 1 Thessalonians and 1 Corinthians. The world, consisting of those left behind, finds a portion of its population missing, and erupts in turmoil and confused panic. Among them is airline pilot Rayford Steele (Brad Johnson, Always) and TV correspondent Buck Williams (Kirk Cameron, TV’s Growing Pains).

As Buck tries to get to the bottom of the disappearances, Rayford struggles with the realization that his wife, among those missing, had tried to warn him of this predicted event. Convinced now that she was right, he turns to the Bible and tries to lead his daughter Chloe (Janaya Stephens, The Skulls), who was also left behind, to its truth.

Meanwhile, the world, hurting and desperate, is looking for a “savior.” They find a false one in the persona of Nicholae Carpathia (Gordon Currie, Dog Park), the new Secretary General of the United Nations who positions himself as the leader of the post-rapture world. At first appearing to be benevolent and caring, his true colors will eventually be revealed to those who try to oppose him.

Despite my disappointment at first viewing, there were a number of elements that I appreciated the second time though. Brad Johnson benefited the most from the second viewing. I did not care for his portrayal at first because I felt it to be too contrived... his character’s conversion to Christianity too quickly come. Upon review, I recognized a subtle and admirable strength to his character.

Kirk Cameron as Buck suffers from his youthful appearance although his character’s acceptance of the truth which comes near the end of the film is very moving and believable. Chelsea Noble (Mrs. Kirk Cameron), playing the stewardess Hattie who finds herself in the employ of Carpathia, is not quite able to pull off the intricacies of her character, but Gordon Currie as Carpathia, Clarence Gilyard (Walker: Texas Ranger) as Pastor Bruce Barnes, and Janaya Stephens as Chloe Steel all provide excellent portrayals.

The element which was the most obtrusive was the incidental musical score. Syrupy and melodramatic, it overplayed its dramatic effect to the point that it became distracting to the message of the film. A simpler, subtler score would have been an improvement.

Still, the film presents an opportunity to see, in a dramatized form, many truths which have been set in the scriptures. Rather than be left behind, we have the opportunity now to claim God’s deliverance and escape the wrath that is to come.

And to wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, which delivered us from the wrath to come. 1 Thessalonians 1:10 (KJV)

God, through Christ, has rescued all who believe from the wrath which has yet to come to this world. This film, in addition to depicting a plausible adventure/thriller using Christian beliefs, gives people an opportunity to know that there is a way to escape the evil that is to come. Jesus Christ, is that way... the way, the truth, and the life.
