Doug's First Movie

Author-verified transcription

As animation goes, Doug's First Movie ranks right up there with those old 19 century flip cards. If this is an indication of what our children are watching on television, it is the best excuse yet to pull the plug on that boondoggle.

What is surprising is that it is a product of Walt Disney Pictures which, in the past, has prided itself on its cutting edge animation processes. Doug's First Movie reflects none of the skill or competence normally seen in a Disney animated film. This movie looks more like it was done in the basement by Huey, Louie, and Dewey.

Doug Funnie and his friends, Skeeter Valentine, Patti Mayonnaise, and Beebe Bluff are grade school students with pastel faces and nasally-challenged voices. With just a few weeks left before the big Valentine's Day dance, Doug is still trying to work up the courage to ask Patti to be his date. Skeeter is still trying to prove that there is a monster in Lucky Duck Lake, Beebe is still ruling her home as well as her wealthy but wimpish father, and this reviewer is doing his best to stay awake.

Sitting next to a five year old who continually played with his talking Woody doll (from Toy Story) throughout the movie helped in that endeavor. He was also more interesting to watch than anything taking place on the screen.

Doug's efforts get thwarted when an upperclassman, Guy Graham, becomes interested in Patti. Skeeter's plans change when he discovers that the monster in Lucky Duck Lake is a friendly creature who means no harm. Beebe continues to browbeat her wealthy but wimpish father, and ... oops, my eyelids are getting heavy again... quick, where's that Woody doll?

As a sedative, Doug's First Movie is second to none. But as an entertaining film able to hold the interest of preschoolers or elementary school age children, it leaves much to be desired.

Jim Jenkins and David Campbell, first conceived of Doug and his friends for Nickelodeon TV back in 1991 where it enjoyed a popular five year run. In 1996, the Disney people bought the animated property and moved the series to ABC. The movie is an obvious attempt to capitalize on the series popularity as well as the current trend to bring animated series to the big screen. (Examples being: Rugrats, Beavis and Butthead, and South Park.)

The movie tries to incorporate family values in its story line, but they are sloppily handled and amateurishly drawn. The overall "moral" of the story line is that it is important to "do the right thing" even if it means people won't like us. That's a fine sentiment... but how do we know what the right thing is? The movie never gives an explanation.

The adults in the story don't serve as moral examples for these children. The standard of right vs. wrong (as seen in this film) seems to be more of a nebulous affair that is aligned to how one "feels" rather than to any kind of concrete standard.

The benefit of using the scriptures as our standard for life and living is that God has them graven in stone, figuratively speaking. They don't change. Therefore it is a standard in which we may trust with our whole heart.

Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast... Hebrews 6:19 (KJV)

It is the Word of God that serves as an anchor to our lives, keeping us on a proper course despite the gale-like winds and storm-tossed waves that may rage against us because of our spiritual opponent. Using the immovable truth of the Word as a standard makes decisions less complicated and life a lot simpler.

I'd like to applaud any filmmaker for bringing wholesome family entertainment to the theaters, but Doug's First Movie only gives the illusion of wholesomeness. Its moral has no substance behind it and its message is dulled by its boring execution.

If the title, Doug's First Movie, implies that there are more Doug movies to follow, we should thank Disney for giving us ample warning.
