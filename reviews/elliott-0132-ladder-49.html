Ladder 49

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 1, 2004
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Sacrifice; service; courage; honoring Christ; stewardship of life

Author-verified transcription

Heroes come in all shapes and sizes but after 9/11 none come any larger than those in a firefighter's uniform. Ladder 49 is a dramatic tribute to the men who have always put their lives at risk in order to save those in danger.

The movie focuses upon Jack Morrison (Joaquin Phoenix), a Baltimore fireman trapped inside a burning warehouse. As his fellow firefighters battle the inferno and the structural debris which keeps them separated from him, he reflects upon the highlights of his life.

We trace his early years as an energetic rookie and his first meeting with the men who would become his brothers in a relationship forged by the fires they fought. We follow his relationship with Linda Morrison (Jacinda Barrett), the young woman he would eventually marry and who would give birth to his two children.

We watch as he gains experience and maturity as a fireman. We relive some of the most memorable moments in his career - the rescues; the close calls; the tragedies. By doing so, we get an inside look at the two sides of those who wear the uniform - the station life and camaraderie that exists between men who must trust each other with their very lives; and the home life of a husband and father who must deal with the fears and concerns of his loved ones over the dangers of his work.

Through it all, the question is asked: "What drives a man to run into a burning building when everyone else is scrambling to get out?" That question is never sufficiently answered and perhaps it can never be fully expressed in words. What does communicate is how thankful we should be that such men exist.

Joaquin Phoenix is the movie's center. As Jack, he plays a man who simply loves what he does. To him, there is no greater feeling than to have saved the life of another human being. His humility and humanity rings true and is especially evident as the love for his wife and family begin to conflict with the love he has for the job.

John Travolta provides a strong, fatherly presence as Fire Chief Mike Kennedy, leader of the firehouse and mentor to Jack. Jacinda Barrett represents the "unsung heroes" of the firehouse - the wives who enter their marriages with the full knowledge that they may at any time become widows.

The rest of the cast, which includes Robert Patrick, Morris Chestnut, and Jay Hernandez, form an unselfish ensemble but are given few opportunities to establish a uniquely identifiable character of their own.

Director Jay Russell clearly wanted to pay homage to the bravery of the often faceless men and women who volunteer to put themselves in life-threatening situations to save people they don't even know. He does so with an honest and accurate depiction of one man's experiences as a firefighter.

There certainly can't be anything harder than to offer one's life for another living soul. How do we pay tribute to that kind of courageous sacrifice? As Travolta's character says at one point in the film, referring to a man who lost his life in the line of duty, "It isn't done by mourning his death, but by celebrating his life." He goes on to infer that the man will be honored by how we choose to go on with our lives. Those words have special meaning for us, seeing as our lives were saved eternally because of the sacrificial death of our Lord. We honor his sacrifice and celebrate his life by the way we choose to spend the life that has been bought for us at so dear a price.

For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's. 1 Corinthians 6:20 (KJV)
