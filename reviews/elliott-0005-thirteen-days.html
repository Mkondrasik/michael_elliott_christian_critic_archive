Thirteen Days

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 12, 2001
Rating
3½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 1:5 Romans 13:12 Proverbs 15:22

Translation: NRSV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Not yet indexed

Author-verified transcription

Even when taking into account the vivid imagination of Hollywood screenwriters, rarely is a film more riveting or thrilling than one which depicts actual events.. Especially when those events had the entire world holding its breath for Thirteen Days. The film tells the amazing and gripping tale of the behind the scenes activities during what would come to be known as the "Cuban Missile Crisis."

Told from the vantage point of presidential aide Kenny O'Donnell (Kevin Costner, JFK), who was a part of EXCOMM (the advisory group formed to meet daily with the president to address the dangerously explosive situation, Thirteen Days captures the tension, frustration, and chilling implications of the 1962 event which brought the world to the brink of nuclear war.

When high-altitude photos from a U-2 surveillance plane reveal the presence of Russian missiles with nuclear capabilities being assembled in Cuba, President John F. Kennedy (Bruce Greenwood, Double Jeopardy), Attorney General Robert Kennedy (Steven Culp, TV's JAG), and their group of advisors must wade through the treacherous waters of Cold War diplomacy and military posturing.

Even though the Joint Chiefs of Staff urge the young president to use the military force at his command, the Kennedys know full well that the subsequent reprisals by the Kremlin would escalate matters into a full-fledged war... one which may not end before both sides unleash their nuclear arsenals.

While I can't speak to the historical accuracy of the film, I can say that its dramatic level is simply off the chart. Watching a group of "smart guys" (RFK's description) brainstorming ideas that might prevent World War III from happening is far more exciting than any testosterone driven shoot-em-up action/adventure film.

The credit goes to director Roger Donaldson (No Way Out) who certainly has a feel for the material as well as the ability to keep the action taut and unflagging for the film's 145 minute length. His use of powerful mushroom cloud footage reminds us of the high stakes at risk and his unusual practice to repeatedly switch from black and white to color helps to keep us grounded in the film's historical context.

The screenplay by David Self (The Haunting) is obviously the result of exhaustive research. He has kept to the basic narrative of events, adding fictional albeit plausible scenes and relationships where history is not recorded. Furthermore, by telling the story through the largely unknown O'Connell's eyes, with whom the audience will most closely associate, he has kept the film terrifyingly real.

The casting is superb... from the lookalikes playing Jackie Kennedy and Lyndon Johnson, all the way up the line to the three central characters. Kevin Costner does fine work as O'Connell although he is likely to get critically lambasted (once again) for going with an accent. His extremely thick Bostonian is distracting but we soon get used to it as the other elements of the film take center stage.

Bruce Greenwood as JFK gives a masterful performance. Capturing the essence of the man instead of the icon, his work is clean, assured, and always interesting to watch. Likewise with Steven Culp as RFK. Not only does Culp display both the quick mind and fiery disposition of Robert Kennedy, he shows the insecurities and natural doubts of a good man asked to handle a job, the outcome of which will affect the lives of millions of people on both sides of the globe.

At a crucial point in the political negotiations, O'Connell poignantly tells his wife, "If the sun comes up tomorrow, it's only because of the will of good men. That's all that stands between us and the devil."

Replace the word "good" in that sentence with "godly" and we've got a truthful statement. The light of God manifested from the lives of godly men and women is exactly what hinders the devil's darkness from prevailing over every corner of this earth. Light holds dominance over darkness and it always will.

The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it. John 1:5 (NRSV)

Praise God that the will of good men prevailed over the dark cloud of war during those thirteen days in 1962.
