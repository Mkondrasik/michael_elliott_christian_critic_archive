Blue Streak

Comedian Martin Lawrence (Life) certainly knows how to talk a blue streak. So it should come as no surprise to find the title of his new “cop” picture to be... Blue Streak. While predictably formulaic, the film is also enjoyably entertaining (in a mindless sort of way) due to Mr. Lawrence’s highly-charged and overly energetic exuberance.

In Blue Streak, Mr. Lawrence plays Miles Logan, a high-tech thief with a quick mouth, a good heart, and a rotten taste in partners. As the movie opens, Logan and team are in the midst of a robbery with the target in question being a fourteen million dollar diamond. Just as soon as the diamond is in their grasp, one partner inadvertently draws the attention of the police while the other double-crosses Logan. Fleeing from both the police and his own partner, Logan manages to hide the gem in an air duct of a building under construction just before he is caught.

After serving a two year prison sentence (which apparently had no rehabilitating effects), Logan is released from jail and immediately goes to collect his secreted diamond. Much to his comic dismay, he discovers that the building, which was under construction when he used it as a hiding place, is now serving as the headquarters for the Los Angeles Police Department (LAPD). With their security measures blocking the inner doors, there is only one way to gain access to his fourteen million dollar prize... He must become a cop.

The movie proceeds to follow Logan’s masquerade and infiltration of the LAPD, where he excels at predicting the criminal mind... mainly because of his predilection of thinking like a criminal. It isn’t long before he is promoted to the office of Lead Detective, Burglary Division. Through it all, Logan keeps trying fervently to gain access to his hidden treasure so he can grab it and get out of there before his ruse is discovered.

This is Martin Lawrence’s project from beginning frame to closing credit. Wind him up, turn the camera on, and get out of his way. Whether he is dancing like a mad fool or punching the air around him (Ka-Pow, ka-pow-pow), Mr. Lawrence demands your attention... Not that there is much else on the screen competing against him.

Director Les Mayfield (Flubber) manages to keep the action moving briskly with plenty of car chases, stunts, and sight gags. This is a wise play on his part as it deters any prolonged inspection of the flawed script and story line.

Parents should note that the humor and much of the language used in the film is both urban and profane. The PG-13 rating in this case is appropriate.

Logan continually escapes detection in his masquerade by “being” whatever people want to believe he is. At various times, his fellow cops think him to be working for another precinct, Internal Affairs, the FBI, even another country’s government. In order to gain their acceptance, he played whatever part they cast for him.

The apostle Paul, acted in a similar manner, except without the dissimulation. He was effective in teaching the Word of God in his culture because he was able to find the common ground shared between himself and those with whom he came into contact.

For though I be free from all men, yet have I made myself servant unto all, that I might gain the more. And unto the Jews I became as a Jew, that I might gain the Jews; to them that are under the law, as under the law, that I might gain them that are under the law; To them that are without law, as without law, (being not without law to God, but under the law to Christ,) that I might gain them that are without law. To the weak became I as weak, that I might gain the weak: I am made all things to all men, that I might by all means save some. 1 Corinthians 9:19-22 [KJV]

This isn’t to say that we are to pretend to be something we are not as Logan did. But instead, if we, like Paul, can first establish a common link with those to whom we speak, the Word of God that we teach will have a greater impact.
