The Terminal

Sermon / spiritual topic: Waiting; Purpose; Patience

Author-verified transcription

Being a survivor is not limited to those shipwrecked on a deserted island like Tom Hanks was in Cast Away. Sometimes, we can be cast off and isolated within a sea of humanity like Tom Hanks in the new Stephen Spielberg film, The Terminal.

Hanks plays Viktor Navorsky, an Eastern European immigrant traveling to NYC for personal reasons. Upon his arrival at the customs gate, we learn that his small country of Krakozhia has suffered a political upheaval. With confusion as to which faction controls the country, the US has revoked all travel visas. But with war raging in Krakozhia, return flights have been cancelled and the borders closed.

Viktor has fallen through an immigration crack and is now labeled as being "unacceptable" - a man without a country. He cannot enter the US and he cannot return home. He's stuck - delayed at the airport indefinitely.

Viktor must make the best of his unusual situation by figuring out ways to meet his basic survival needs - food, shelter, and companionship - all within the confines of the NYC's international passenger terminal.

Viktor is a thorn in the side of Frank Dixon, an airport official bucking for promotion. He can't let an "unacceptable" into the country but keeping him there is an embarrassment to him and a detriment to his potential career advancement.

At the same time, Navorsky's popularity grows as he becomes something of a folk hero to the hundreds of airport employees whom he comes to know. His persistence, fairness, and ingenuity wins him friends, fans, and favors.

Tom Hanks is very amusing - especially early in the film when Navorsky first appears and speaks precious little English. His character's genial cluelessness as frustrated officials try to explain to him the complexities of his situation is priceless.

Stanley Tucci is perfectly cast as an ambitious bureaucrat wrapped in the red tape that defines his life. The scenes between Hanks and Tucci are well-timed and brilliantly executed.

Catherine Zeta-Jones appears as a stewardess who is drawn to the simple honesty of Navorsky. While it is a delicate role that is played well, the romantic relationship between the characters doesn't work for me and is a subplot that distracts from the more interesting element of the film.

"All good things come to those who wait." So the saying goes. In Viktor's case, it happens to be true. Waiting was Viktor's salvation. Because he did not break the law, the airport officials had to allow him to stay. By staying, he was able to experience the American culture through the microcosm of airport life and achieve the goals he had originally set for himself.

Contrary to popular perception, waiting is not a passive activity. We can wait as we continue to strive to attain those things which are important to us. By waiting we simply mean that we continue to looks toward the future for something that is not yet available. It does not mean that life stops for us.

But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; und they shall walk, and not faint. Isaiah 40:31 (KJV)

Waiting upon the LORD simply means that everything we do is done with the understanding and knowledge of his eventual return. Having that mindset strengthens and invigorates us as we live our lives today.
