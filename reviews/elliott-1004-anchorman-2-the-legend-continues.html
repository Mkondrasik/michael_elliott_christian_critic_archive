Anchorman 2: The Legend Continues

Author-verified transcription

The lunacy continues in Anchorman 2: The Legend Continues as America’s dumbest newscaster returns to the screen with his team of broadcast nitwits in tow. On the surface, this sequel to the 2004 comedy appears to offer nothing particularly new. It is just a rehash of the same sophomoric humor of the original film. But there is one notable exception. After stripping away the idiotic behavior of the characters, the questionable taste of the filmmakers, and the puerile nature of the jokes, there is a substantive message at the heart of it all for those willing to consider it.

Ron Burgundy (Will Ferrell), after losing his job and wife in a station reorganization, has an opportunity to resurrect his career when an Australian mogul has the crazy idea to launch a 24-hour news channel called GNN. Though the industry pundits of the 1980s scoff at the idea, Ron reassembles his crack (smoking) news team to take New York by storm. Amazingly, they become the ratings darlings and it isn’t long before newsrooms everywhere are copying their imbecilic pandering in order to keep their portion of the market share.

Will Ferrell, who also co-wrote the film, stays consistent in his portrayal of the titular egocentric character. While Burgundy cannot help being inappropriate in just about every situation, that joke wears thin the longer the movie drags on. Steve Carell generates laughs with his dumber than rocks routine as Brick, the clueless weatherman. David Koechner crosses the humor line into uncomfortable territory as sportscaster Champ. Paul Rudd reprises his role as ladies man Brian Fantana and, as in the first film, pretty much stays in the background.

Anchorman 2 is rife with cameos by recognizable stars and celebrities who come together in a cleverly conceived but poorly executed climactic scene. Perhaps the humor of Ferrell and his friends on display here is an acquired taste. If it is, I am sorry to say that it is an acquisition I have yet to make.

The surprising success of Burgundy’s news broadcast can be traced back to the brainstorming session where Ron simply says, “Why do we have to give people what they need? Why can’t we just give them what they want?” With that mantra, GNN sets out to redefine the news. Gone are the substantive, investigative reports on the central issues of the day, replaced by sensational, titillating, manipulative stories that appeal to the baser instincts of the viewing public but does nothing to inform them on the important events happening in the world around them.

It is an astute observation that mirrors in an exaggerated way the declining state of today’s broadcast media. It takes but one cycle through the hundreds of channels available on most basic television packages to recognize the insipid quality that is passing as TV news and entertainment today. Perhaps this issue was more seriously handled in Paddy Chayefsky’s 1976 film Network, but Anchorman 2 makes a similar point in a comedic way.

Turning the news into a business dependent upon ratings and revenue streams was indeed a game-changer.

In my opinion, it weakened the industry and corrupted the quality of the product, launching the news media upon a slippery slope from which it has yet to recover. Instead of edifying or strengthening audiences with informative reports, stations waste precious airtime pandering to our weaknesses. If it is sensational, it will be covered regardless of whether it has relevance to our lives or not.

God’s simple exhortation to us is one the news industry would be well served to heed.

“Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.” Ephesians 4:29 (KJV)

“Let us therefore follow after the things which make for peace, and things wherewith one may edify another.” Romans 14:19 (KJV)

“Let all things be done unto edifying.” 1 Corinthians 14:26b (KJV)
