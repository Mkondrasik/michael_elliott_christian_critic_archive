Author-verified transcription

Many people expressed surprise at the news director Jonathan Demme was remaking the 1962 classic The Manchurian Candidate. I must confess that I wasn't one of them. Due to the controversy of the last US presidential election and the heavy campaigning taking place in this one, the timing couldn't be better for a political thriller with sinister undertones. Remaking a classic is always a risk but this is one that paid off handsomely.

Denzel Washington stars as Maj. Ben Marco. His current post has him giving speeches to various groups about the time he and his men were ambushed in the Kuwaiti desert during the Gulf War. According to his memory his sergeant, Raymond Shaw (Liev Schreiber), acted valiantly and despite overwhelming odds managed to repel the attackers. For this, Shaw was awarded the Congressional Medal of Honor.

The problem is that Marco has been having this weird dream about those events that feels more real to him than what he remembers. When others in his company express the same thing, he begins to suspect that someone has tampered with his mind and memory.

Meanwhile war hero Shaw has entered into the political arena and, thanks to his hard driving, politically savvy mother (Meryl Streep), he is in line to accept his party's nomination to be Vice President of the United States. A troubled and clearly disturbed Marco comes to suspect that whatever really happened to his company of men during the Gulf War is directly related to the presidential election. His investigation points to a powerful geo-political force known Manchurian Global but with the clock ticking down, can he get anyone to believe him?

Solid direction by Demme, a taut, intelligent script by Daniel Pyne and Dean Georgaris, and three strong performances by Washington, Streep, and Schreiber combine to make this version of The Manchurian Candidate well worth our while.

This is not a typical star turn by Denzel Washington. Throughout most of the film his character is disoriented, weak and confused; trying to piece together the truth from the unconnected fragments that remain in his mind. It is a convincing and selfless performance which gives room for others to share his spotlight. They take full advantage. Schreiber is impressive as a man who, perhaps against his will, has been groomed for greatness and browbeaten to play the role he's been assigned. Streep once again shows why she is considered to be the greatest living actress still working in films. Her characterization of a female Washington power broker is so detailed and nuanced that it brings a frightening reality to an unreal situation.

The film works mainly because it taps into the paranoid fear and anxiety caused by the ever-growing disillusionment and distrust of governmental power and the people who covet and wield it. The film, like its predecessor, is based upon the 1959 novel by Richard Condon which struck a nerve upon its publication and was considered explosively prescient by conspiracy theorists following the 1963 assassination of JFK - an event which caused the 1962 film to be pulled from circulation for nearly 25 years.

Marco is concerned that he and his men have been brainwashed and "rewired" by unknown parties for unknown reasons. He fears that they are no longer in full control of their thoughts or memories. It is scary stuff, indeed. God gave us absolute control of our minds for a reason. Because we have the ability to choose what we think and what we believe, we have an impenetrable defense system against unseen spiritual enemies. It is our God-given, free-will right and responsibility to control our thoughts. And we can't say that God doesn't point us in the right direction.

Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ. 2 Corinthians 10:5 (KJV)

Our enemy would love to be able to brainwash us and control our thoughts. He can't. All he can do is try to deceive us with lies and distractions. When we renew our minds to the truth on a daily basis we have the ability to free ourselves from his agenda and keep our lives on track.
