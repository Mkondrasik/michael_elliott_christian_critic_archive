Author-verified transcription

Boys will be boys... except when girls are around. Then they become sexist pigs. That's the message being delivered in North Country, a new film by Niki Caro (Whale Rider) that tells a (largely fictionalized) story of the landmark legal case which led to the introduction of sexual harassment policies throughout corporate America.

Charlize Theron stars as Josie Ames, a Minnesotan gal who left her abusive husband, and moved in with her less than totally supportive parents. She now needs a way to provide for her two kids. She turns to her hometown's mine which has, in 1989, only recently begun hiring women for what is still considered by most who work there to be a man's job. It is obvious that a feminine presence at the mine is not welcomed by anyone there.

She, and the handful of other women employed there, are subjected to the most sexually degrading treatment seen this side of a misogynist website. Told to "take it like a man," Josie endures the humiliation and emotional abuse but when it turns physical, she bravely chooses to take a stand by seeking a court action.

Instead of money, all Josie wants is the opportunity to have what every male employee has - the ability to go to work without being traumatized or raped.

Theron follows her award winning performance in Monster by de-glamming her appearance once again (although not as drastically as the serial killer look she adopted for the earlier film). Ames has not led an easy life and Theron appropriately allows the wear to show. She then backs up the surface work with a deeply felt performance.

She is ably assisted by a strong supporting cast. Frances McDormand plays a pivotal role as the leading female voice at the mine. Her strength of character shines through her scenes even as the strength of her character begins to diminish through a debilitating disease.

Sean Bean, as McDormand's husband, and Woody Harrelson, as the hockey playing attorney who accepts Ames' class action suit, are on hand to provide a few male voices who are not totally despicable. All others play characters that should make men ashamed for their gender.

It comes down to a question of respect. Men are typically stronger physically than women and they can (and apparently have) used that physical advantage to intimidate, bully, and abuse. But might doesn't make right. It never has and it never will.

Sexual harassment is a loathsome action that has no place in a civilized or Christian community. Our women deserve better.

Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered. 1 Peter 3:7 (KJV)

"The weaker vessel" should not be misinterpreted. It is a phrase that implies honor and value. Precious might be a term better suited for our times. A fragile heirloom is one that we protect and value. Our women deserve the same treatment. Their value goes far beyond any object we can name. To treat women disdainfully or with disrespect is the act of a weak and cowardly man. Kudos to all women who have risked all in order to stand for what is right.
