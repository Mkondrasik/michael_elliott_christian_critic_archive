Are We There Yet?

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 21, 2005
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Colossians 3:12-13 Numbers 14:18 Ephesians 4:1-3

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Patience; longsuffering; parenting; forgiveness; maturity

Author-verified transcription

Are We There Yet? is a movie that asks the wrong titular question. After sitting through this film the question that comes to mind is “Why did we go at all?” It is based upon an old, tired concept that has been seen many times before (most recently in Cedric the Entertainer’s failed Johnson Family Vacation). Having director Brian Levant (Snow Dogs) rehash it brings no new life to the story.

Ice Cube plays Nick, a confirmed bachelor who likens children to cockroaches with the one difference being that you can’t squish the former.

When he sets his sights upon Suzanne (Nia Long), a hot mama who works nearby his Portland sports memorabilia store, his libido gets the better of him. Despite the fact that she has two children who label their mom’s prospective suitors as “the enemy,” Nick forges ahead to build a relationship with Suzanne while trying to forget the kids are in the picture.

When the kids’ estranged father reneges on a promise, Nick has little choice but to volunteer by stepping in and agreeing to transport the children some 300 miles so Suzanne can keep her job which hinges on her being in Vancouver on New Year’s Eve. Suzanne flies ahead foolishly expecting Nick and the kids to follow close behind.

From that point on the film consists of one exaggerated mishap after another as the inexperienced bachelor with a brand new Lincoln Navigator and the precocious children with no regard for personal property square off against one another. Do we need more than one guess as to how it will all end?

Ice Cube has an easygoing cinematic manner about him that offers a certain appeal although he lacks the razor sharp edge of a Bernie Mac which might have helped to heighten some of the comedy. On the plus side, his portrayal is geared to a PG audience and is well within the safety zone of a parent’s “is it age-appropriate” radar.

It is a shame that the filmmakers did not know how to keep their film entertaining while staying within that zone. They often felt the need to flirt with crossing over the line for the sake of what is intended to pass as humor. Most of those moments come courtesy of Satchel Paige, an animated bobble-head doll voiced by comedian Tracy Morgan, who I suppose is meant to represent Nick’s bachelor subconscious. He sits on the dashboard of the Navigator and passes observations on everything from Suzanne’s cleavage to how best to dump her children.

Any parent who has traveled with his children has heard the question “Are we there yet?” more times than could be counted. Patience is a virtue that takes time to develop. Though children often lack the self-control and discipline to be patient, parents show their love and maturity by handling their charges with what the scriptures call long-suffering.

Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering; Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye. Colossians 3:12-13 (KJV)

Longsuffering is the quality that enables us to restrain from responding to provocation with anger or frustration. The best example of longsuffering in action can be seen in the way God handles us. Even when we show ourselves to be slow to learn, He continually and lovingly reminds us of the way we should go.
