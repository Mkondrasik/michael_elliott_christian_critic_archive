Little Voice

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 17, 1999
Rating
3½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Music; worship; remembrance; joy; praise

Author-verified transcription

For someone with such a little voice, the character for whom the new movie, Little Voice, is titled makes a big impression.

Little Voice (Jane Horrocks, Life Is Sweet), or L.V. as she is otherwise known, is about as timid a person as one will ever meet. Content to stay in her room, deathly afraid to speak to anyone (even her own dreadfully obnoxious mother), she has only two passions in her life. One is the memory of her dead father. The second, is the music that he loved... especially the songs of Judy Garland, Shirley Basset, and Marilyn Monroe.

Over and over she would play those songs... until she knew every nuance and could mimic every phrase. Enter Ray Say (Michael Caine, Blood and Wine), theatrical booking agent for such dismal acts as Take Fat and a geriatric knife thrower. While in the middle of a sexual dalliance with L.V.'s mother (Brenda Blethyn, Secrets and Lies), Ray hears L.V. singing in her room and a star, as they say, is born.

Except that this particular star-to-be has no interest in being discovered. Singing, for her, is a personal thing... to be shared between herself and her dead father (who appears to her in visions). Getting on a stage and appearing in front of people would be terrifying to her while, at the same time, cheapening the memories that the music gives to her.

Miss Horrocks is absolutely fabulous (to borrow the phrase from the TV show in which she also appears) as L.V. Knowing that she performed all her own songs sends our appreciation of her talents into the stratosphere. She is mesmerizing to watch.

Michael Caine does some of his best work in decades as the seedy talent agent who latches onto L.V. as his ticket to climb to the top. His final scenes show the work of a man who has perfected his craft.

Brenda Blethyn is a literal whirlwind as the loud, boisterous, selfish, free-spirited but crude mother of L.V. There is no danger of Mari being nominated for mother-of-the-year.

Ewan McGregor (Brassed Off) plays against type as a pigeon-keeping phone man almost as introverted as L.V.

There isn't a weak character in the movie. Writer/Director Mark Herman (Brassed Off) has done a marvelous job in adapting the play by Jim Cartwright (in which Miss Horrocks also starred) and in assembling a cast that truly give seamless performances.

L.V. used music to keep the memory of her father alive. The early believers in the Old Testament did much the same thing.

I call to remembrance my song in the night: I commune with mine own heart: and my spirit made diligent search. Psalms 77:6

Songs were used to bring to remembrance the Word and ways of God. Upon escaping from the bondage of Egypt and watching as the Red Sea parted, allowing Israel to cross, only to fall upon the pursuing armies of the Pharaoh, the first thing Moses had his people do was to sing a song about the victory. Why? One reason was to record the event in a memorable way so no one would ever forget what had happened.

The Levites were the priestly tribe. But in addition, they were also the musicians, the singers. That's how closely tied music was to the teaching of the Word.

Certainly, L.V. was not singing to the glory of God. In fact, what she was doing bordered on spiritualism, as her singing was dependent on seeing ("feeling?") the presence of her dead father. But we should never let a counterfeit weaken the spiritual validity of the genuine. Music and song remain a valuable as well as a valid way to manifest the joy and love we have for God.

Speaking to yourselves in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord; Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ; Ephesians 5:19-20

Even a little voice can make beautiful music when it expresses the love and joy one has in one's heart.
