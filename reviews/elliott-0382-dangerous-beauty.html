Author-verified transcription

Venice in 1593 was not the ideal place and time to be a wife. Back then, women were considered to be little more than property - birthing machines designed to churn out male heirs for their husbands. At least, such is the tale told by Dangerous Beauty, an exquisite little film with a delightful soul at its center.

That soul belongs to Veronica Franco, played by the beautiful Catherine McCormick (Braveheart). Veronica is a woman whose only flaw appears to be her birthright. Deemed unfit to wed by "proper" Venetian society, she turns to the only recourse left open to her... to live the life of a courtesan. A life with which her mother and grandmother before her were well familiar.

As depicted in this film, which is supposedly based upon a true story, the life of a courtesan differed markedly from that of a wife. Rubbing elbows (and presumably other body parts) with men of high rank and prestige enabled her to keep abreast (no pun intended) of current events and matters of state. She was encouraged to develop her intellects as well as her bedroom skills and was well rewarded for demonstrating either.

Veronica, under the tutelage of her mother, Paola, played by the still beautiful Jacqueline Bisset (The Deep), rises quickly in the court to become one of the most sought after courtesans in all Venice. Meanwhile, she is being carefully observed with growing consternation by Marco Vernier (Rufus Sewell of Dark City) who, by first rejecting Veronica's profession of love out of a misplaced sense of familial obligation, set her on her present course. His subsequent marriage of convenience does nothing to stifle the love he has always held for Veronica. A love he held even while he was rebuffing her.

Also on hand is Maffio (Oliver Platt of Executive Decision), poet and brother of Marco, who is first attracted to Veronica as an appealing intellectual equal, then later pits himself against her before the Inquisition as he renounces her as a witch.

Ah yes, the Inquisition. A time when the devil blatantly used religion for his own sadistic purposes. Religion, as it is portrayed in this film, is dark and foreboding; perpetuating and promoting fear, guilt, terror and torture. Not what we would call godly attributes.

But it was nothing new. The Pharisees of the gospels, which orchestrated the trial, torture and execution of Jesus Christ, were the religious leaders of their day. The devil has always used "religion" as a way to reach weak-willed, weak-minded people. It is why we should always demand of our spiritual leaders that they teach us the true doctrine of God's Word and not just denominational dogma which can be subject to devilish infiltration.

A film which has as its lead a courtesan, (which is kind of like the equivalent of today's thousand dollar hooker), is sure to have scenes that many may find offensive. As Paola began teaching her daughter the ways of a courtesan (in effect, how a woman may physically please a man), I started visualizing the letters of complaint that would be sure to come my way. Because I am compelled to say that if more of today's mothers would speak as openly and honestly about sex as did Veronica's mother in this film, our world would be the healthier for it. God made us as two sexes for a reason. He instilled in us a natural physical attraction for the opposite sex. Much of the "sexual confusion" that exists today stems from the unwillingness of parents to speak and teach openly about the beauty of the interrelationship of men and women which God has designed.

This is not to be construed as an endorsement for adultery or promiscuity which is predominantly featured in this film. Veronica makes clear that she would rather have entered into a marriage vow with the man that she loved. But that being denied to her by a restrictive and oppressive culture, she turned to the only life that remained. It may not have been the one she desired, but it was the one that enabled her to survive. The married men with whom she slept... that's another story.

Paola, while instructing her daughter, said it best. "Desire," she said, "begins in the mind." While many men would probably like to think that certain of their body parts have minds of their own, (since that would absolve them of any responsibility), the truth is, we do have control over our sexual appetites. Our minds decide what it is that we desire.

Many times, we let our minds be influenced by what we see around us. That is called temptation. As we control our minds (focusing upon the Word of God), we are able to resist the temptation and make sound decisions for our lives. For married men that would include living the following truth:

"Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth. Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe; let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love. And why wilt thou, my son, be ravished with a strange woman, and embrace the bosom of a stranger?" Proverbs 5:18-20 (KJV)
