The Nut Job

Author-verified transcription

The animated children’s movie, The Nut Job, may be interesting to look at but it is sadly uninteresting to watch. The artwork is great, the vocal talent is competent, but the story is pedestrian at best.

Surly Squirrel (voice by Will Arnett) is a resourceful, adventurous non-hero type whose main interest in life is looking out for number one - himself. He lives in a park that is home to an odd assortment of animals that have all agreed to form a collective in order to have a better chance of surviving the coming winter. Based on the food supply they’ve already stored, they may not make it.

With such slim pickings inside the park, Raccoon (voice by Liam Neeson) dispatches the ever-reasonable Andie (Katherine Heigl) and the ever-posturing Grayson (Brendan Fraser) to the city to search out a solution to their dire need. Surly is already there, having found a nut factory that also happens to be the hideout for a gang of mobsters planning to rob the bank next door. After all the hijinks that ensue, Surly is eventually going to have to make a choice between fending for himself and looking after the needs of the park and its animals.

Although the artwork is at times stunning, the story is weak, the jokes are stale, and the characters are not memorable in the slightest. The vocal artists, try as they might, are eventually dragged down by the leadenness of the material.

The scriptural lesson that can be taken from the film, as simplistic as it seems, is no less meaningful or true. Surly learns that selfishness is a lonely and ultimately unsatisfying way to live life. Though he may be able to carve out an existence for himself by isolating himself from others, mere existence is rather a limited goal to set for one’s self. Certainly we should aspire to a greater life for ourselves.

“The thief cometh not but to steal and to kill and to destroy. I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.” John 10:10 (KJV)

Jesus did not accomplish all that he did so that we could merely exist. His goal for our lives was that we might have a life that was more than abundant. The way to achieve this goal is to adhere to the commandment that Jesus came to teach:

“For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.” Galatians 5:14 (KJV)

Our lives are so much richer; have so much more meaning; and bring so many more rewards when we lovingly share them with others.
