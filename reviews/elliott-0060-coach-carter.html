Coach Carter

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 14, 2005
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Proverbs 29:18Matthew 9:36Hosea 4:6

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Vision; education; discipline; leadership; expectations

Author-verified transcription

“I am not a role model.” Basketball star Charles Barkley drew deserved fire with that oft-quoted remark because the sad reality is that celebrity athletes are held up to be role models... and they are not always positive ones. The message that is often sent to youths by professional athletes who act like uneducated thugs is that talent or fame will excuse bad behavior.

Coach Carter tells a story inspired by the actions of one man who sought to counter that message. Ignoring the low expectations coming from the parents and teachers around him, he decided to make a difference in the lives of the California basketball players of Richmond High. In 1999, this coach took an undefeated basketball team in the middle of their Cinderella season and forbade them to play basketball until they brought their grades to an acceptable level. Along the way he also taught them the fundamentals of discipline, respect and, most importantly, having a vision or goal to reach for in one’s life.

Samuel L. Jackson plays Carter as a strongly principled man, absolutely certain of his actions and beliefs. The character is perhaps drawn to be a bit too saintly to be believed but the strength of Jackson’s performance overcomes the one-dimensionality and formulaic nature of the script.

The screenwriters (Mark Schwahn and John Gatins) do a better job with the supporting characters. The various players and their girlfriends are clearly delineated and given decent backstories to add depth and flavor to the otherwise predictable plot.

Rob Brown (Finding Forrester) and singer Ashanti (in her film debut) play a young couple facing hard decisions as they try to plan for a future that seems anything but bright. Robert Ri’chard (Light It Up) plays Carter’s son and freshman player who must earn his way onto his father’s team. Rick Gonzalez (The Rookie) plays a brash kid who uses basketball as a way to keep himself off of the dangerous gang-populated streets of his neighborhood.

Director Thomas Carter (Save the Last Dance) stumbles a bit early on but soon finds the proper tempo for his film. As the characters become more clearly defined and the positive messages (which reflect biblically sound values) become evident, we find ourselves being caught up in the story and its eventual outcome.

Carter was the recipient of a community-wide fury as a result of his actions. Parents and teachers alike viewed high school basketball as being the high point for these kids who, they believed, were destined for a life of crime and/or disappointment. What they lacked (and had lacked for many years according to the film) was the one element that provided Carter with the passion and drive to stay the course that he had set. What made Carter different was the vision that he had for the boys he coached. He viewed basketball not as a coveted pinnacle to be reached but as a stepping stone to a much more valuable prize - a college education.

No other character in the film believed that the students of Richmond High had a prayer of attending college. Carter refused to adopt that defeatist attitude. By setting a vision and challenging his players to accept it as theirs, he led them to a victory far more important than mere athletic competition could provide.

“Where there is no vision, the people perish.” Proverbs 29:18a (KJV)
