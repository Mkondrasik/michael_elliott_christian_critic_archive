Stop the presses and hold all calls! We may have an early winner for the worst movie title of the year award. The nominee: How Stella Got Her Groove Back.

This movie tells the little known tale of a poor little 40 year old rich girl whose groove has somehow gone missing. If you've ever had to go without your groove, you can certainly appreciate her dilemma. Not finding it in all the usual places, she jets off to Jamaica in search of her wayward groove. Of course, what she finds in Jamaica is a 20 year old Adonis who will just have to do until her groove turns up.

Angela Bassett (What's Love Got To Do With It) is the grooveless Stella and looks years younger than the 40 year old character she plays. Newcomer Taye Diggs plays her romantic interest, 20 year old Winston Shakespeare. (I'm surprised they didn't go with their alternate title: Shakespeare in Lust.) Whoopi Goldberg (Ghosts of Mississippi) is also present for too brief a time as Delilah, Stella's best friend and vacation companion

Sadly, Whoopi or no, there is little in this film to recommend. The situation is, of course, ridiculous. The dialogue is stilted. Both the characterizations and the plot are forced and predictable. A stressed out woman goes on vacation, meets a younger man, decides to have a fling, it becomes something more, the normal complications ensue. But really, who cares?

The relationship between Stella and young Winston is obviously based on nothing more than a physical attraction, a fact that is emphasized by constantly dressing the attractive Miss Bassett in beachwear, nightgowns, and other such sexually suggestive garb. Mr. Diggs, they hardly bother to dress at all, allowing the camera to spend much time lingering in close up examination of his muscular build. But outside of the sexual attraction, these two characters share nothing. Their relationship, lacking any kind of substance, is therefore as uninteresting as last week's classified ads.

Terry McMillan (Waiting To Exhale) adapted the screenplay from his own novel with assistance from the prolific Ronald Bass (Rainman, My Best Friend's Wedding). But their combined and considerable talents could not create substance where none existed.

Just so the experience is not a total loss, let's walk away with this spiritual insight:

The reason Stella went to Jamaica in the first place was because she was over-worked and over-stressed. Anybody, no matter who they are or what they do, will need to take time to refresh themselves in order to rest both the body and the mind so as to perform at the optimum level:

And the king, and all the people that were with him, came weary, and refreshed themselves there. 2 Samuel 16:14 (KJV)

The Lord give mercy unto the house of Onesiphorus; for he oft refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chain. 2 Timothy 1:16 (KJV)

Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall: But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. Isaiah 40:30-31 (KJV)

True Christians know the importance of how and when to refresh themselves. It has nothing to do with Jamaica or a physical encounter with a person half our age. The way Stella "got her groove back" in the movie is nothing more than a temporary stopgap; a distraction that disguises the real problem. There is no guarantee that her groove is back to stay. In fact, we should probably get ready for the sequel, " Whoops, There Goes Stella's Groove Again."
