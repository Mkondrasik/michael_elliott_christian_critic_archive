Author-verified transcription

Yes Virginia, there is a Santa Claus. He just doesn’t look like the jolly old elf in picture books. In the animated film, Rise of the Guardians, Santa is a gigantic muscle-bound, knife-wielding, tattooed Russian who goes by the name of North.

He’s also not alone. It seems that the Man in the Moon has chosen a handful of individuals to protect the children of the world, and thus keep their hopes and dreams alive. North (Alec Baldwin), along with the Easter Bunny (Hugh Jackman), the Tooth Fairy (Isla Fisher) and the Sandman, are The Guardians. To their ranks is added a greatly confused Jack Frost (Chris Pine) who not only doesn’t seem to fit in; he doesn’t even know why he is who he is.

Before he can figure it out, the Guardians and the children they protect face a new threat. Pitch Black (Jude Law), AKA the Boogeyman, has grown tired of children not believing in him. Using fear as his main weapon, his goal is to dispel the belief children have in the more famous and popular fantasy figures and replace it with his nightmarish reality. The Guardians, growing weaker with every child that no longer believes, must rise up to defeat Pitch and save the children from constant dread.

The animation is excellent, the story, based on a series of books by William Joyce, is original, and the film speeds by with plenty of action and humor to delight the target audience.

When Jack confides in North that he was never told what he is supposed to do by the Man in the Moon, North consoles him by saying “You just need to find your center.” Each of the Guardians has a purpose to fit their roles. For Santa, it’s about finding the wonder in the world around him; Tooth preserves memories with every tooth she collects; Bunny reminds the world of rebirth and new beginnings. That Jack hasn’t found his center means that he is not yet aware of his purpose.

Living a purposeless life is a soul-defeating exercise. Purpose brings meaning to a person’s life. When we discover our purpose, we are defined by it. Our purpose becomes our center and imprints itself on all that we do.

Our God, unlike the Man in the Moon, is not silent as to our purpose. He does not force us to figure it out on our own. All we need do is to believe God’s Word to the point that we agree to obey it.

“All has been heard; the end of the matter is: Fear God [revere and worship Him, knowing that He is] and keep His commandments, for this is the whole of man [the full, original purpose of his creation, the object of God's providence, the root of character, the foundation of all happiness, the adjustment to all inharmonious circumstances and conditions under the sun] and the whole [duty] for every man.” Ecclesiastes 12:13 (Amplified)

By accepting this as our purpose, everything else becomes informed by it. Our jobs, our relationships, our hobbies, our interests… everything that we do is tied or bound to our overriding purpose of loving God and keeping His commandments. It is a rich and rewarding way to go through life.
