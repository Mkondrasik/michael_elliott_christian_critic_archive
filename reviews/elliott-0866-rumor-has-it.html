Rumor Has It

Sermon / spiritual topic: Marriage

Author-verified transcription

How’s this for a premise? A young woman discovers that not only was the 1960s book/movie The Graduate based on actual events... her family served as its inspiration. In Rumor Has It... such is the discovery of Sarah Huttinger (Jennifer Anniston) who, upon learning the news, freaks out to such an extent that she threatens the tenuous hold she has on her own relationship with fiancé Jeff (Mark Ruffalo).

She goes out in seek of Beau Burroughs (Kevin Costner) who, rumor has it, bedded both her mother, now deceased, and grandmother (Shirley MacLaine). Wouldn’t you know that he makes a play for her as well? To her surprise, and despite the perversity of the situation, she does not resist his advances. Coo coo ca choo.

Unfortunately, screenwriter Ted Griffin (Matchstick Men) can’t do much with the idea. The film plays flat and lifeless – filled with clichés and recycled jokes. Rob Reiner, who replaced Griffin as director ten days into the shoot, makes no big mistakes but average isn’t good enough to compensate for a weak script.

Anniston has good comic timing and instincts (not quite good enough to steer her clear of this project) but needs material. In order to carry a film, one must first have something to lift.

MacLaine, in a supporting role intended to be a showcase, works far too hard to wrest memorable moments from unmemorable lines. There’s an old show business adage, “Never let ‘em see you sweat.” Though she’s making a game effort, Ms. MacLaine’s perspiration is showing, figuratively speaking.

Costner underplays his role, making sure he doesn’t embarrass himself but adding little to the film in the process. Only Mark Ruffalo, as Sarah’s boyfriend/fiancé, and Mena Suvari, as Sarah’s bouncy sister, provide reasons to pay closer attention. Those times are quickly forgotten in the mediocrity that surrounds them.

All in all, it is an average film within which is buried a jewel of an idea. Someone just didn’t dig deeply or carefully enough to unearth it.

Part of Sarah’s problem is the cold feet she’s developing over her engagement to Jeff. She’s not sure she should be getting married to him and sees in Beau the “adventure” she thinks she might be missing. The girl is obviously confused. Life is an adventure in and of itself. It’s a roller coaster that twists and turns; one that does loops and spins. Marriage is not the ride... but it is a seat belt that can help keep us from being thrown off.

Staying with this analogy... deciding who to sit next to is crucially important. It is best to choose a partner that is willing to be there from beginning to end; a partner that will keep the car on the track; and a partner that will share every moment of the ride with you.

Sarah is not thinking clearly about her life. She’s considering all that she doesn’t have instead of focusing on all that she does. The former way of thinking will bring nothing but regret and heartache – doubt and worry. It is best to think more positively. Hope, joy, and happiness follow those who do.

Every wise woman buildeth her house: but the foolish plucketh it down with her hands. Proverbs 14:1 (KJV)
