Wimbledon

Author-verified transcription

When playing tennis, love is not a word you want to hear bandied about on your side of the score card. In Wimbledon, it’s the only thing keeping one player’s head in the game.

Paul Bettany stars as Peter Colt, an aging tennis pro making his final appearance on the hallowed grounds of Wimbledon. Once ranked 11th in the world, he has consistently dropped in the rankings over the years as younger, faster, and stronger players began to dominate the field. Having drawn a wild card berth for the prestigious event, Colt plans to announce his retirement after what will surely be his inevitable loss in an early round. He’s got an embarrassingly cushy job teaching randy middle aged ladies at an exclusive tennis club waiting for him.

His plans change a bit when he runs into Lizzie Bradbury (Kirsten Dunst), a rising tennis star with a volatile temper and a reputation for being both merciless and fearless on the court. Sparks fly when these two first meet and in no time at all, they are playing at something other than tennis.

It is a situation that disturbs Lizzie’s controlling father (Sam Neill) who sees romance as a distraction that threatens to derail his daughter’s championship career track. For Colt, meeting Lizzie has energized him to such an extent that he begins to play with the ferocity of a top seed. Inspired by love, he begins to win - and fall in love.

At the same time, the prediction of Lizzie’s father also proves true. She does lose focus and her play does suffer because of Peter. Can two tennis players complete their love match and still compete at the top of their game?

Paul Bettany, often cast in the supporting role of best friend or confidante (A Beautiful Mind, Master and Commander, A Knight’s Tale), shows that he has the acting chops to headline a picture. Showcased in this lightweight romantic comedy is Bettany’s easy-going charm and audience appeal. He’s clearly a nice guy in a nice guy role. What’s not to like?

Kirsten Dunst has less room in which to work her rather limited character arc but she is attractive and makes for an appealing object of desire for Colt.

The screenwriters try to fill out their threadbare script by including a few needless and distracting subplots - Peter’s dysfunctional family background; a sports agent who gave up on Peter long ago; a lame rival for Lizzie’s attention - but the film works best as we track Peter’s inexplicable success to his eventual championship match.

What is it about champions that drive them to excel and demand from themselves more than their opponents? Peter obviously always had the ability to play and win but when Lizzie began to show an interest in him, he began to believe in himself once again.

Confidence breeds success. Believing that we have the ability to achieve a goal is the first step to seeing that goal become an accomplished reality. When we trust in God and believe that he has enabled us to succeed beyond our wildest imagination, we shall begin to receive the many victories that have always awaited us.

I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me. Philippians 4:13 (KJV)
