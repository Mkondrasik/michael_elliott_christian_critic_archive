Author-verified transcription

Hidalgo is a kind of throwback to the old family adventure stories of the 60s and 70s. A man, a horse, an impossible task, and the will to accomplish it despite the odds... that pretty much sums up this tale which is claimed to be loosely based upon a true story. (Note: this claim has been soundly debunked as being untrue. The story of the film appears to be based upon little more than the fertile imagination of Frank T. Hopkins.)

Ocean of Fire is a 3,000 mile horse race across the deserts of Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Syria. When the Wild West legend of Frank Hopkins (Viggo Mortensen), a Pony Express dispatcher and long-distance endurance rider reaches the ears of the proud Sheikh Riyadh (Omar Sharif), he invites the half-breed Hopkins and his mixed-breed mustang Hidalgo to enter the contest and compete against the best full-blooded Arabian horses ever bred. If the distance and the harsh and dangerous environment weren't enough, Hopkins will also have to face sabotage, tribal warfare, and zealous opponents who will stop at nothing to ensure their victory.

Added to his troubles is the misery of the sheikh's daughter (Zuleikha Robinson) who must marry one of the contestants should he win. It is a fate that she would rather not face. She looks to Hopkins to deliver her from a loveless marriage.

Director Joe Johnston (October Sky) keeps a strong hand upon the production and allows the story to unfold slowly but securely. It starts a bit tentatively but gains momentum after the first 30 minutes and literally sails to a strong and rewarding finish.

Viggo Mortensen, last seen as Aragorn in the Lord of the Rings trilogy, makes for a strong and virile leading man. Quiet and unassuming but confident in his horsemanship and in the strength of his horse, he provides the strength of character and dominant will that sets the tone for the picture.

Joining him is his equine co-star. The horse has as many expressions as Viggo and uses them to great effect. It is a bit of a "Disneyfied" treatment but one which lightens the mood and establishes the special bond between this man and his steed.

Omar Sharif plays the sheikh who invites the "infidel" to the competition and comes to respect and admire him for his honorable qualities.

The race itself is a grueling affair which manages to strip all pretense and bravado away from the participants. As Hopkins is portrayed, he spends much of the film downplaying his Indian heritage. (His mother was a full-blooded Sioux, another "claim" which is being disputed by historians). By the film's end, he embraces his roots and accepts who he is. This action gives him the strength to continue and finish the race.

There are times when our "race" seems to be too hard and we may be tempted to quit. At those times, we should reflect upon our spiritual heritage. We should recognize and accept who we are in Christ. When we do, we will be infused with the strength and the might of our Father. We shall be renewed and we will discover that we have the ability to continue and finish our course.

He giveth power to the faint; and to them that have no might he increaseth strength.
