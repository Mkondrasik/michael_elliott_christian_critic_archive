Author-verified transcription

It has been said that the only thing wrong with this world are the people living on it. Unfortunately, in Crash, that statement has the ring of truth to it. As Pogo once said, "We have met the enemy and he is us."

The subject is racism or bigotry and Crash displays it in blistering fashion as it weaves the lives and stories of multiple personalities together to form a tapestry of intolerance, fear and misunderstanding.

Matt Dillon is a cop who vents his mounting frustration by finger pointing at the minorities he blames for some perceived injustices in his life. Terrence Howard is a TV producer who, with his wife (Thandie Newton), is accosted during a routine traffic stop. Brendan Fraser is a politician looking for a political spin after his SUV is carjacked at gunpoint. His wife (Sandra Bullock) simply wants to distrust all minorities because she feels that political correctness is what put them in jeopardy in the first place.

Ludacris and Larenz Tate are two street toughs who like to spout philosophical on the subject of race relations but then turn around and act in a manner which proves that some stereotypes are based in truth.

Added to that mix is a Persian storekeeper (Shaun Toub) who believes that everyone is out to cheat him; a locksmith (Michael Pena) trying to escape his past as well as his future; and a lily white rookie cop (Ryan Phillippe) who has yet to learn the way prejudicial thoughts and beliefs creep into one's psyche.

This is a well-written and insightful screenplay from the pen of Paul Haggis (Million Dollar Baby) who also makes his directorial debut. His ability to orchestrate so many characters and story lines around a central theme is remarkable. While there may be some lulls in the pacing and some of the dialogue may be a bit forced, the flaws of the film can be easily brushed aside in favor of its compelling story and nicely acted ensemble.

There are so many stories and characters that I'm sure everyone will have their favorite. Mine comes from watching Michael Pena's sensitive portrayal of a loving father doing his best to raise his child in a world that doesn't often make it easy.

Most of these characters are touched by prejudicial feelings and tendencies from both sides - as victim and as perpetrator. All are changed as a result; some for the better.. some The message is clear. There is and always will be differences between us. Just as there are differences between a carrot, an onion, a stalk of celery, and a pound of beef. But if we can get past our fears and distrust of those differences, we might learn that when we combine them, we can make a wonderful stew.

And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him: Where there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond nor free: but Christ is all, and in all. Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering; Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.
