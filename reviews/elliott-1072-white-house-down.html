White House Down

Author-verified transcription

In true Die Hard fashion, director Roland Emmerich places an ordinary man in unordinary situations and forces upon him the mantle of hero. White House Down places the presidential palace under attack and only one man stands in the attackers’ way. Ironically, he was just that morning deemed unfit for the Secret Service.

John Cale (Channing Tatum) was a low level cop on security detail looking to graduate to the elite status of the Secret Service presidential detail. When his interview did not go as well as he had hoped, he tries to salvage the moment by taking his politically minded little girl (Joey King) on a tour of the White House. Wouldn’t you know… at that precise moment terrorists decide lay siege to the seat of power, killing many and taking the rest hostage – including Cale’s daughter.

After Cale fortuitously rescues the president (Jamie Foxx) from the hands of the enemy, the rest of the film deals with their attempts at escape, rescue of the hostages and resolution of the armed conflict. Emmerich keeps the energy high, the banter light, the explosions huge and the logic immaterial. This is popcorn entertainment and it doesn’t disappoint.

In addition to Tatum and Foxx who seem to immensely enjoy playing off of one another, the film benefits from the talents of Maggie Gyllenhaal, James Woods, Richard Jenkins and the young Joey King.

Titles are handed out in this world as often as candy on Halloween and for the most part they are just as non-substantial. David was a shepherd; Jesus, as far as the world knew, was the son of a carpenter; and yet both men led those who followed them to victory and salvation. At the beginning of the film, Cale was a “nobody” by the world’s standards. But, when trouble struck, he was willing to dedicate his very breath to saving the people he could save. It wasn’t in any job description he had. It certainly wasn’t something he was forced or directed to do. He just happened to be there and then rose to the challenge to be the hero needed in that moment.

Like Cale, we don’t need a title to be a hero to save people. We don’t need to wait for permission or direction. We have been empowered by our God to take action if and when we see a need. We don’t do it for glory or attention. We certainly don’t do it for money or fame. We do it because someone once did it for us.

“Hereby perceive we the love of God, because he laid down his life for us: and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren. But whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?” 1 John 3:16-17 (KJV)

Granted, our acts of heroism may not involve saving the president or ending a nuclear threat, but who said they had to be? The heroes most people are looking for are simply those willing to give their time, attention, and brotherly love to them in times of need.
