Author-verified transcription

Now You See Me is an entertaining crime caper cloaked in a magical cape. Four street magicians scraping out a living are hand selected by an unknown benefactor to form a formidable union. Within a year, billing themselves as the Four Horsemen, they not only attract the attention of the general public but also of the local police and Interpol.

That kind of attention should have been expected. Their first grand finale consisted of magically transporting an audience member from their Vegas stage to a bank vault in Paris where they made three million euro disappear. The police are so baffled they turn to a magic debunker to help cut through all the hocus-pocus so they might solve what was most definitely a crime.

Jesse Eisenberg, Woody Harrelson, Isla Fisher and Dave Franco play the quartet of prestidigitators, each with their own brand or specialty. Mark Ruffalo is the police detective who is obviously out of his depth. Morgan Freeman plays a man who has built a career out of exposing magicians and their secrets. And Michael Caine makes an appearance as an entrepreneur who bankrolls the Four Horsemen’s elaborate tricks. It is an impressive cast and the film, implausible as it may be, is immensely enjoyable to watch because of the work of its actors.

Magic, we are told, is “targeted deception.” As Eisenberg’s character likes to say, “Look closely. The more you think you see, the easier it will be to fool you.” And that certainly is the sole purpose of magic – to deceive; to make people believe something is happening that could not possibly be happening.

Magic has a long and storied history – certainly dating back to biblical times. There are records that show that Joseph, Moses, and Daniel all encountered those practicing “magic.” So did the believers in the first century church. But each biblical record where magicians are referenced illustrates a stark contrast between the deceptions and tricks of the magicians and the miraculous acts that God performed through his prophets and apostles.

Magic tricks are just that. They are merely tricks that have no substantive value. God’s “tricks,” otherwise known as “miracles,” are much more desirable. Based in truth and powered with love, God’s miracles are not only not designed to deceive… they come from a source that cannot deceive… which make them infinitely better than anything a magician may have up his sleeve.

“And the king communed with them; and among them all was found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah: therefore stood they before the king. And in all matters of wisdom and understanding, that the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm.” Daniel 1:19-20 (KJV)
