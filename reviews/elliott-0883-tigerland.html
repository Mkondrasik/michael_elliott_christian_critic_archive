Tigerland

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 29, 2000
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 49 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Mark 137Psalms 18:34Ecclesiastes 3:8

Sermon / spiritual topic: Authority; Discipline; Preparation

Author-verified transcription

As the adage goes, "War is hell." By necessity, so are the places which prepare men for war. Such a place is Tigerland - considered in 1971 to be the second worst place on earth.

Attached to Fort Polk Louisiana and designed to simulate jungle combat conditions, Tigerland was the last phase of training that an infantry soldier received before being shipped to Vietnam. It is within this inhumane setting that director Joel Schumacher and screenwriters Ross Klaven and Michael McGruther explore a man's attempt to hold onto his humanity.

The central character of Tigerland is Roland Bozz (Colin Farrell, The War Zone), a draftee whose disobedience stems from his unwillingness to allow the army to strip him of his individuality. His blatant defiance sows seeds of discontent throughout his entire company.

Just as the nation was divided over our involvement in Vietnam, the men of Company A each had their own differing view or expectation. For Paxton (Matthew Davis, Urban Legend: Final Cut), Vietnam was to be an adventure to serve as fodder for his writing. For Miter (Clifton Collins, Jr., Price of Glory), it was to be a validation of his worth as a man. For Wilson (Shea Whigham in his film debut), it was an opportunity to kill in the service to his country. For Bozz, however, it was simply a place where men died for no good reason... and so, he figures, there is no good reason to go.

Using handheld 16 millimeter cameras, Schumacher adopts a naturalistic, almost documentary style which gives a gritty realism to the harsh conditions of infantry training. The bond of friendship and camaraderie which ultimately develops within the combat-bound Company A is enhanced by the tension and "improv" feel which the ensemble cast brings to each scene.

The language is extremely raw and uncensored throughout and there are scenes of explicit sexuality and nudity which merit the film's R rating.

On the whole, Schumacher has elicited some remarkably believable performances from his relatively unknown cast. The men playing the drill sergeants are as intimidating as the real thing. Irish actor Colin Farrell does a passable American accent and stands out as Bozz, a natural leader who chooses to lead men out of the service rather than into battle.

By making the rebellious Bozz the anti-hero of the film, one's acceptance of Tigerland's message is dependent upon the wisdom of hindsight. Knowing what we now know, it is hard to argue with Bozz's position that going to Vietnam doesn't make sense. But still, I found it difficult to embrace his character fully due to his blatant disobedience and disrespect for authority.

Without getting into either a justification or denouncement of the war itself, the fact remains that the infantry training was designed to prepare these soldiers for what they would face in Vietnam. The hope being that what they learn at Tigerland might help to keep them alive during combat. Bozz's rebellious attitude had a negative impact on the training which the entire company received, thereby putting them at mortal risk.

War is never a desirable thing. But we would be naive not to realize that it is a reality of the crooked and perverse world in which we live.

"And when ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars, be ye not troubled: for such things must needs be;" Mark 13:7a (KJV)

In such an instance where war is ominously approaching, the behavior of Bozz is a luxury that does not belong amidst a group of men being asked to risk their lives for a cause. With a life or death struggle looming on the horizon, all energies should be focused upon preparing those who will be called into battle. It is the disciplines that we develop during training or preparation time that carry us through the harsh realities of life's obstacles... whether it be a physical war or a spiritual battle.
