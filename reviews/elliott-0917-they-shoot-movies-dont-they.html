They Shoot Movies Don't They?

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 23, 2000
Rating
2½ stars

1 hr: 27 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Fear; Believing

Author-verified transcription

The specter of the Blair Witch Project resurfaces more than once in the newest (is it real or isn't it?) independent film, They Shoot Movies, Don't They? Writer/director/producer Frank Gallagher follows writer/director/producer Tom Paulson around with a camera crew as the latter tries to find the $80,000 needed to complete his movie, Mirage.

Paulson is painted as a kind of wunderkind: a high school baseball phenom headed for the pros until an injury forces him to choose a new career path. He selects filmmaking and knocks one out of the park with his first effort. That film leads to a developmental deal with a major studio where he is handsomely paid to sit around and wait for an opportunity to make another film.

He eventually tires of waiting so he quits the studio and puts his life's savings into making his own film, Mirage. His story really begins as the film enters the post production phase. Needing $80,000 more to complete the editing and marketing to land a distribution deal, Paulson embarks on the bane of all independent filmmakers... financing the dream.

Here's the rub... and don't read further if you would like to experience the film unsullied by spoilers of someone who has seen it. This documentary isn't real. Like The Blair Witch Project, which purported itself to be genuine, this film is the inventive scripted story of the filmmakers. In fact, in the commentary included on the DVD is a story of how the filmmakers tried to sell the distribution rights to the same man who made BWP into a phenomenon. It is a telling commentary on the state of Hollywood and the fruits of deception.

Judging it simply as a film, They Shoot Movies Don't They? is a compelling look at dreams, obsession and fear. Tom Wilson plays the lead character with the amiable charm of someone for whom things come relatively easy. As he finds his own dream beginning to slip away, he begins to doubt himself and his vision which sends him into a downward mental spiral from which he may not recover. His naturalistic performance is what sells us on the possibility that perhaps what we're watching is real.

The supporting cast fills the peripheral roles that gravitate towards the center of Wilson's universe with varying degrees of success. Some appear comfortable in front of the camera, others don't. Surprisingly enough, this also helps perpetuate the illusion of a documentary.

Though a fictional story, Paulson's plight is familiarly real. Fear of failure is something that we have all experienced to one degree or another. We watch the lead character struggle to bring his film up to industry standards. We commiserate with him. We've been there before - perhaps not in the same arena - but fear is universal and has the same crippling effect upon those who succumb to it.

Fear is not a lack of believing. It is worse than that... it is an act of negative believing. When we allow fear to grab hold of our minds and remain rooted and grounded so that it permeates all our thoughts, we should fully expect to receive exactly what it is we are fearing. Believing is a law which works in both directions: negative and positive.

For the thing which I greatly feared is come upon me, and that which I was afraid of is come unto me. Job 3:25 (KJV)
Therefore I say unto you, What things soever ye desire, when ye pray, believe that ye receive them, and ye shall have them. Mark 11:24 (KJV)
