The Fast and the Furious

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 22, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 2:8Psalms 115:4-81 John 5:21

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Idolatry; priorities; passion; putting God first

Author-verified transcription

Fast cars, loose women, explosions, guns, wrecks and a kickin' sound track... all wrapped up in a polished celluloid package. It doesn't matter that the story is lame or that the dialogue is cheesier than a wheel of gouda. The Fast and the Furious will bring in its millions faster than its souped up cars can race down Manhattan's streets.

As far as the plot goes... hmmm... well, there's been a string of truck hijackings performed by a precision team behind the wheels of their hot rod Honda Civics. There is also a subculture in New York City (so what else is new?) that uses the streets of the city that never sleeps as their personal drag strip. Is there a connection? Sure. Why not?

Enter Brian Spindler (Paul Walker, The Skulls). Driving a green, nitric oxide-powered muscle car, he is trying to gain respect from the leader of the pack, Dominic Toretto (Vin Diesel, Pitch Black). He's already captured the attention of Dom's sister, Mia (Jordana Brewster, The Faculty) much to the chagrin of team member Vince (Matt Schulze, Blade).

Let us not forget the Asian motorcycle gang who is there because... uh... well... never mind that. But there's this cool Asian motorcycle gang in the movie too.

Let's face it. The regular critical analysis just doesn't apply to a movie like this. Straight off the B-movie factory conveyor belt, albeit with a bit more flash and dazzle, either you're going to love it because it is what it is, or you're not going to see it for the same reason. If you belong to the former, you won't be disappointed. If you side with the latter, you'll never know how good or bad the movie is.

Vin Diesel is rapidly though unexpectedly reaching sex symbol status and this role may just push him over the edge. Dom has a confidence gained by being the best at what he does. Centered and focused, Dom is like a Zen master of cars and that self-assured confidence will cause many women to swoon.

Paul Walker plays the main protagonist and his good-looking, California-guy appearance lets us know enough about his character that any acting would merely be superfluous.

Michelle Rodriguez follows her stunning debut in Girlfight being cast in this film in the rather flimsy role as Dom's main squeeze (but she does get to punch out a guy.) Chad Lindberg (October Sky) plays a mechanic with ADD who seems to look and sound a lot like Giovanni Ribisi.

Director Rob Cohen (The Skulls) can take whatever credit audiences care to bestow upon the film. The production style, the packaging, the visual elements, and the many stunts all contribute to the making of a rapidly moving, but substantially vapid experience.

Is there anything in this film that can remotely be used to remind us of any biblical truths? None of these characters have any interest in spiritual matters. Even when they say grace before a barbecue cookout, they give thanks to the "car gods" for pistons, camshafts, and turbocharged engines.

And perhaps that's a lesson we can walk away from this film remembering. We all have interests in life about which we can get passionate. For some it can be cars. For others it might be movies. But no matter where our interests may lie, we are not to elevate it above our love of and for God. To do so would be a form of idolatry.

Their land also is full of idols; they worship the work of their own hands, that which their own fingers have made: Isaiah 2:8 (KJV)

When placed into its proper perspective, many things in this life can be enjoyed fully. The danger lies when we allow something of this world to replace God as the number one priority in our hearts and minds.

Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen. 1 John 5:21 (KJV)
