Author-verified transcription

It all begins with a work crew from a chain gang picking up armadillo road kill. With an opening like that, things can only get better. Happy, Texas does get better, but only marginally.

When a traffic accident sets three convicts free, much hilarity is supposed to occur as two members of the sad-sack trio decide to masquerade as homosexual beauty pageant organizers in order to cover their scheme to break into a bank.

Jeremy Northam (The Winslow Boy) is Harry Sawyer, the brains of the duo. Befriending bank president Jo (Josephine) McLintock (Ally Walker, Kazaam) by becoming her "girlfriend" and confidant, Harry gains access to the bank's vault. Unexpectedly to him (but to no one else in the theater), he also falls in love with her.

Steve Zahn (You've Got Mail) is Wayne Wayne Wayne Jr., who will never be mistaken as being the brains of any duo. While Harry is making the arrangements to rob the bank, it falls upon Wayne to organize the beauty pageant that will serve as their alibi. Much to the boys' disappointment, the event is called The Little Miss Fresh-Squeezed Pageant.

The tightly wound Wayne is ill-equipped to deal with any kind of human being, much less a room full of five year old girls. Still, he throws himself into the task of choreographing the talent portion of the contest to our mild amusement.

Had director/co-writer Mark Illsley, in his feature film debut, chosen to keep the focus on the "fish out of water" format by following Wayne's hapless progress with the girls, there may have been more to the film to recommend. But Mr. Illsley, after setting the premise, inexplicably downplays that particular story line in order to follow the unusual love triangle involving Harry, Jo, and Sheriff "Chappy" Dent (played by William H. Macy, Mystery Men).

Chappy, emboldened by the arrival of the openly homosexual couple, professes his attraction to Harry and they begin to "date." Harry, who is only pretending to be homosexual, must play hard-to-get without alienating the enamored sheriff. But he also cannot profess his attraction to Jo without blowing his cover.

William H. Macy brings a sweet vulnerability to the role of Chappy. It is wonderful characterization that deserves a better film draped around it. Happy, Texas is far too uneven a comedy to recommend highly.

Steve Zahn, who was given an award for this role during its run at the Sundance Film Festival, overplays a bit too much for my taste. Wearing a walrus mustache which muffled most of his lines and adopting explosive physical mannerisms, I found him to be more of a caricature than character and out of sync with the rest of the film's more realistic portrayals.

Spiritually, I must again point out that the Word of God is clear on the subject of homosexuality. While it is a free will choice of every individual, God lets us know that choosing homosexuality is wrong. It is contrary to the truth of His Word as well as the law of nature. It is, to sum up, a lie.

Because they exchanged the truth about God for a lie and worshipped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever! Amen. For this reason God gave them up to degrading passions. Their women exchanged natural intercourse for unnatural, and in the same way also the men, giving up natural intercourse with women, were consumed with passion for one another. Men committed shameless acts with men and received in their own persons the due penalty for their error. Romans 1:25-27 (NRSV)

The residents of Happy, Texas seem more than willing to accept the pretended homosexuals into their community without a second thought as to the influences of spiritual error that would accompany them. This kind of blind tolerance may be desired by the homosexual community, but God's Word commands us to reprove the "unfruitful works of darkness." [Ephesians 5:11] These last three paragraphs may serve as my reproof to those who have, inadvertently or otherwise, bought into the lie.
