All The Pretty Horses

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 25, 2000
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Galatians 6:7-8 Proverbs 6:14-15 Romans 2:6-10

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Choices and consequences; sowing and reaping; decision-making

Author-verified transcription

All The Pretty Horses is a post-WWII Western which is in desperate need of a lot more "giddiyup." Pretty or not, if any horse on the ranch ever appeared this lifeless, it would have to be put down.

Matt Damon (The Talented Mr. Ripley) stars as John Grady Cole, a young cowpoke who, after his family's ranch is sold out from under him, decides to head for the browner pastures of Mexico. With the American West of 1949 becoming more and more commercialized, he figures that the wide open spaces that exist south of the border offer the only opportunities left for a true cowboy.

Cole and his longtime buddy, Lacey Rawlins (Henry Thomas, E.T.) saddle up and head for the Rio Grande. Along the way, they are joined by Jimmy Blevins (Lucas Black, Slingblade), a young teenage runaway with a valuable horse, unusual gun, an irrational fear of lightning, and a major attitude. Although they suspect Blevins of having stolen the horse and gun, Cole and Rawlins allow him to ride with them for no other reason than the argument which, at that time, still evoked postwar pride: "He's an American."

Eventually Cole and Rawlins find work on a massive ranch owned by Don Hector Rocha y Villarael (Ruben Blades, Cradle Will Rock). While their abilities and knowledge of horses earn them the respect and admiration of Rocha and the rest of the ranch hands, it is their association with Blevins and Cole's strong attraction to Rocha's beautiful daughter (Penelope Cruz, Woman On Top) that earns them a stay in a brutal Mexican penitentiary.

Based on the award winning and best selling novel by Cormac McCarthy, the screenplay by Ted Talley (The Silence of the Lambs) is a disappointment, failing to effectively communicate the universal themes of this epic coming-of-age story. The film is not aided by the rather unfocused and plodding direction of Billy Bob Thornton (Slingblade).

The cast, however, does deserve some accolades, although there is a lot of assembled talent which remains under utilized. The trio of Damon, Thomas, and Black give believable renditions of the prototypical American cowboy. Black is especially good at portraying the unapologetic ornery manner of a rambunctious youth. The other supporting actors simply aren't given enough screen time to develop their characters past a one-dimensional descriptive.

They include: Penelope Cruz, as a woman whose honor and reputation is threatened by her forbidden love of Cole; Sam Shepard (The Right Stuff) as the lawyer who dashes Cole's hope of regaining title to his family's land; Bruce Dern (Middle Age Crazy) as a sympathetic judge who hears a humbled Cole's confession; and Ruben Blades as the Mexican landowner.

While the film leaves much to be desired, it is full of good intentions. Both the American and the Mexican characters are treated respectfully and the youthful desire to follow and stick to one's own beliefs which these young men demonstrate make them both admirable and likable.

There are also moments which illustrate biblical principle profitable for our own use. Rawlins, recognizing that Blevins may bring trouble their way, wonders if they shouldn't ride alone. "Every dumb thing I ever done," he reasons with Cole, "was because of a decision I made beforehand."

It is a very wise man who can trace back the negative results received in life to a decision that, if made differently, would have produced a more desirable end. Cause and effect affects us all. Today's decisions have an impact on the experiences we will have tomorrow. As we decide to take action every day based upon the godly knowledge we know to be true, God promises us that every good thing will be made available to us.

Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. Galatians 6:7 (KJV)
