About Schmidt

Michael Elliott

2 stars

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Purpose; time; fulfillment; wise living; life's path

Author-verified transcription

In It's A Wonderful Life, Jimmy Stewart asked the question, "Would the world be a better place if I had never been born?" The answer he was given was heartwarming, turning the film into a feel good holiday classic. In About Schmidt, Jack Nicholson asks himself the same question but there's nothing "feel good" about the answer he receives.

Warren Schmidt (Jack Nicholson, The Pledge) of Omaha Nebraska is an average man leading an average life who, upon retiring from his average job, begins to realize that the sum parts of his life don't add up to much. When his wife of 42 years dies, he is driven even deeper into a personal despondency.

The only bright spot is his daughter Jeannie (Hope Davis, Hearts in Atlantis) but she is set to marry Randall Hertzell (Dermot Mulroney, Lovely & Amazing), a waterbed salesman with a walrus mustache and limited appeal. Schmidt climbs aboard his new Winnebego and heads out in a last minute attempt to stop his daughter from doing what he's done his entire life: settling for less. Along the way, he expresses his inner thoughts through a series of letters he writes to the Tanzanian orphan he sponsors.

Nicholson has built a career by playing larger than life characters: Randall McMurphy, Jack Torrance, The Joker, Col. Jessup, to name a few. He inverts his over-the-top persona here to play a severely repressed individual. Schmidt is a character who has been obviously beaten down by life. We get the impression that there is potential within him that the world has never seen. Ironically, he worked as a life insurance actuary, specializing in risk assessment, but he has never taken a risk where his own life is concerned. It is something that now weighs heavily upon him and Nicholson plays it perfectly.

Director Alexander Payne (Election) is obviously more interested in character observation than in telling a narrative tale and so we watch Schmidt shuffle through his mortal coil on his way to an ignominious end. The detailed characterizations are interesting but often depressingly sad and the overall feeling we're left with by the end of the film is one of disappointment.

Kathy Bates (Misery) has a supporting role as the divorced and colorful mother of the intended groom. As extroverted as Schmidt is introspective, Bates provides the only real spark of energy to the film. Hope Davis plays the daughter Jeannie but has little opportunity to shine. Likewise, Dermot Mulroney plays a one-joke mullet-cut stereotype which could have been developed to a greater comic effect. But comedy doesn't seem to rank too highly on Payne's necessity meter. He's much more interested in poignancy and so rests the film's focus upon the slumped shoulders of its title character.

I can think of few things more disturbing than to reach the nadir of one's life with the realization that much of it was "unlived." To guard against this possibility, we would do well to follow the exhortation of God. After all, who would know better than the Creator of Life, how best to use that creation to its fullest potential?

"Look carefully then how you walk! Live purposefully and worthily and accurately, not as the unwise and witless, but as wise (sensible, intelligent people), making the very most of the time (buying up each opportunity), because the days are evil." Ephesians 5:15-16 (Amplified)

It is certainly available to live a long and unfulfilled life. This world has provided us with ample opportunity to waste our time with inconsequential activities. A wise person will look down the road he is traveling to determine if the journey will be worth it. If the answer is no, then another path should be selected. There is a path worthy for all who choose to travel upon it. It leads to a destination where no one will despair over the road not taken.

Thou wilt show me the path of life: in thy presence is fulness of joy; at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore. Psalms 16:11 (KJV)
