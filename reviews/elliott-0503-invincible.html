Author-verified transcription

Thanks to Rocky, Philadelphia will long be known as an underdog's city - where a common man who may be down on his luck can still get a shot at hitting the big time. Ironically, in 1976, the year Rocky was being released, a Philadelphian underdog story was actually being lived. Vince Papale, a 30-year-old out-of-work schoolteacher, part-time bartender, and life-long Eagles fan was given an opportunity to play football with his beloved team. His amazing story is told in the new film, Invincible.

When college coach Dick Vermeil (Greg Kinnear) enters the NFL to lead the Eagles out of their 11 season losing streak, he decides to shake the lethargy from his team by holding open tryout for anyone in the community. Hundreds show up including a reluctant Papale (Mark Wahlberg) who has a realistic view of the odds against making the team. But his solid work ethic, raw talent, and heart catches the eye of Vermeil who believes that a "team with more heart can defeat a team with more talent."

Surprisingly Papale, who never played college ball, makes the first cut and then the second, actually getting playing time in the Eagle's preseason games. But does he have the stuff to make it all the way past the final cut and into the rarified air of an NFL professional football player?

The film is extremely well cast. Wahlberg, much smaller than Vince Papale was, is athletic and muscular enough to be believable as a player. He blends a "can do" attitude with the natural doubts that can creep into a man's thinking - especially if that man has had some bad times of late. Wahlberg has a working man's persona that fits this Philadelphia native perfectly.

Kinnear isn't given all that much to do as Vermeil but has a few nice understated scenes that capture the essence of the man.

Papale is given plenty of community support, none as dear to him as that received by the patrons of Max's Tavern, where he bartends. The actors who comprise this bunch are stellar, conveying the look and feel of a blue collar town in depressed economic times. Kirk Acevedo plays Tommy, Vince's best friend and confidante. Other cast members who lend authenticity to the story are Michael Rispoli as the local barkeeper, Kevin Conway as Vince's father, and Elizabeth Banks as a displaced NY Giants fan who becomes a love interest for the recently divorced Papale.

Vince returned home one night and found that his wife had left him and taken just about everything they owned. She left behind a scrawled note that read: "You'll never amount to anything. You'll never make any money. You'll never make a name for yourself." Vince carried this note with him and reread it often. It was just about the worst thing he could have done.

If we read or hear something enough times, we may begin to believe that it is true. When we are at that point, we are making it true. Believing equals receiving. It is important that we do not place negative thoughts or images into our minds. They just might stick there.

Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers. Ephesians 4:29 (KJV)

Vince did not need to think about the mean-spirited opinions of his ex-wife. He did not need to think about all the reasons why he might not succeed. What he needed was the positive reinforcement that all things - even long shots - are possible. Dreams do come true for those who believe they can and work hard to make them happen.
