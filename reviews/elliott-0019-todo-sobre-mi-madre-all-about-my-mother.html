Todo sobre mi madre (All About My Mother)

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 10, 2000
Rating
No stars — cannot recommend
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Matthew 19:14 Psalms 139:14 Deuteronomy 22:5

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Creation; gender; authenticity; a mother's love

Author-verified transcription

My "star" ratings are generally given for the artistic merit or quality of the films I review. However, there are certain films which, while done well from a purely artistic perspective, contain such a high degree of spiritual error that a favorable artistic rating from me could be misinterpreted as an unintended recommendation. Pedro Almodovar's critically acclaimed All About My Mother falls into this category.

Cecilia Roth (Laura y Zoe) stars as Manuela, single mother of Esteban (Eloy Azorin, Cuba '98) a teen aged writer who has never been told the truth about his father. On his seventeenth birthday, after watching a theatrical performance of A Streetcar Named Desire, Manuela promises to tell Esteban all about his father. Before she does, Esteban is killed in a car accident, trying to get an autograph from the actress playing Blanche DuBois.

Manuela decides to return to Barcelona to tell her ex-husband about their son and finds herself surrounded by and involved in the lives of a group of women and women wannabees. There's La Agrado (Antonia San Juan, Ataque Verbal), a transvestite prostitute with a sharp wit, a quick mouth, and a heart of gold; Huma Rojo (Marisa Paredes, Life is Beautiful), the lesbian actress playing Blanche; Nina (Candela Pena, Insomnio), Huma's drug-addicted partner both on and off the stage; Rosa (Penelope Cruz, Hi-Lo Country), an HIV-positive, pregnant nun; and Lola (Toni Canto, Spanish Fly), an AIDS infected dying transvestite who has shared a relationship with more than one of the other characters.

This unsavory collection of characters tell a moving and compassionate story of a mother's love and a woman's strength. The film provides strong roles for a number of actresses who make full use of them. Ms. Roth, in particular, shines as Manuela. The void generated by the loss of her son is vividly communicated on her face and in the way she holds herself. She becomes the solidity in the lives of the people around her. That she effectively portrays this strength while maintaining the fragile emotional state of her character is testimony to Ms. Roth's ability as an actress.

The filmmaker appears to take the view that all women are actresses to one extent or another, even dedicating this film to actresses who have played actresses. This running theme goes off track as soon as the transvestites hit the screen. The presentation of these gender bending characters in the sympathetic light of tolerant acceptance is where we must draw the line in our appreciation of the artist's depiction of "reality."

The character of La Agrado, in an impromptu monologue, gives a litany of surgical procedures (and their respective costs) that he/she has endured to become "authentic." He/she explains that a woman becomes authentic the closer she comes to looking like the dream she has of herself.

Such logic is utter nonsense and runs contrary to the Word of God. Authenticity does not come at the point of a surgeon's scalpel or with packets of silicone. It is the height of egotism and spiritual blindness on the part of an individual to ignore God's initial design of his/her human body and decide for his/her self which gender to "join." The Scriptures state that we are awesomely and marvelously made. And we were deliberately made as one of two sexes.

And he answered and said unto them, Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, Matthew 19:4 (KJV)

Instead of accepting the error of these tragically confused individuals who choose to live the lie of transvestitism, we should be encouraging or teaching those individuals how to trust in God's initial plan and accept His original design of their sexual classification. After all, anyone who can fashion a complex living machine like the human body which has such flexibility, strength, and versatility must know what He's doing.
