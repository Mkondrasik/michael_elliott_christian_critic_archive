Almost Famous

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 22, 2000
Rating
3 stars

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Parenting; raising children; moral foundation; values; instruction

Author-verified transcription

Sex, drugs, and rock and roll. This mantra from the '70s has turned the head of even the most mature. But is it possible to thrust a fifteen year old into the center of such an environment and expect him to come out unchanged? "Almost Famous" tells the story of William Miller (Patrick Fugit in his feature film debut), a young, inexperienced high schooler who, in 1973, lands an assignment with Rolling Stone Magazine to get the inside scoop on a up and coming rock group called Stillwater.

Leaving his anxious, over-protective mother (Frances McDormand, "Wonder Boys") at home, baby-faced William tries to get his story while in the midst of the morally loose and decadent lifestyle associated with rock musicians on tour. Drugs, groupies, late nights, and artistic temperament are all a part of the scene and introduce William to a world completely different from the one he left behind in the happy valley of suburbia USA.

Assisting him from afar is the sage advice of his mentor, Lester Bangs (Philip Seymour Hoffman, "Magnolia"), the real life rock journalist who first coined the phrase "heavy metal," and who believes that the heart of rock and roll is being corrupted as it moves more and more into commercialization.

Stillwater, the almost famous fictional band of the title, is comprised of friends who have come up through the ranks of rock and roll together to reach the plateau of borderline stardom where they currently sit. Lead guitarist, Russell Hammond (Billy Crudup, "Jesus' Son") is perhaps the most talented of the bunch which is a source of some long unspoken resentment on the part of frontman and lead singer Jeff Bebe (Jason Lee, "Mumford").

William (nicknamed "the enemy" by the group) is invited to join the band's entourage as they tour America, and is therefore privy to all their insecurities, compulsions, and indulgences. He also begins making friends with them which threatens whatever journalistic integrity he may have once had.

Mr. Fugit is ideally cast as the young wunderkind. He looks every bit of his fifteen years which is integral to the heart of this coming of age story.

Ms. McDormand is nothing less than awesome in her role of an opinionated and doting mother and philosophy professor who drives her eighteen year old daughter (Zooey Deschandel, "Mumford") out of the house due to her restrictive rules. Her conflict in allowing her son to travel with Stillwater is real and palpable and easily identifyable to any parent.

Kate Hudson ("Gossip") plays Penny Lane, a self-described sixteen year old "band-aid" who supposedly follows the musicians because of her love for their art. Much more than a sex-starved groupie but far less than a partner in a committed relationship, Penny is an enigmatic muse in a strange and bizarre world. Ms. Hudson plays it flawlessly.

Writer/director Cameron Crowe drew from his experiences as a fifteen year old cub reporter for Rolling Stone and has crafted a film which casts a wistful and loving eye on the era and upon those who lived it. The heavy sexuality which the film depicts may be accurate, but to see the various rock groups who drift in and out of this film so casually involved in sexual encounters with fifteen and sixteen year old girls was, for this reviewer, uncomfortable to watch.

The irony is that William, though the youngest, is perhaps the most mature, responsible and grounded individual on the tour. This can be only attributed to the manner in which he was raised. Such qualities are learned, not inherited. It is the parent's job is to provide the moral framework within which a child shall grow. To point out the boundaries between right and wrong and teach them how and why to stay within them. It is this instruction that helps to shape the character of the person the child becomes.

Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it." Proverbs 22:6 (KJV)

As parents, we must invest the time to help our children build that strong foundation of principles and values upon which they may build the rest of their lives.
