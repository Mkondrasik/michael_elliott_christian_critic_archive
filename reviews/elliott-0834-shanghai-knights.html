Shanghai Knights

Author-verified transcription

Chon Wang rides again in Shanghai Knights. This slaphappy action comedy reunites Jackie Chan and Owen Wilson in a sequel of their hit Western, Shanghai Noon.

In Knights, Chon Wang (still pronounced "John Wayne" and played by Jackie Chan of Rush Hour 2) learns of his father's murder and of the theft of the imperial seal which has been entrusted in his family's care for generations. His sister Lin (Fann Wong, a Singapore actress making her US film debut) has tracked the murderer to London and has asked her brother to join her.

He stops off to collect his share of the gold which he foolishly left in the care of his ex-sidekick, Roy O'Bannon (Owen Wilson, Behind Enemy Lines). The irascible Roy, of course, has already spent the money but is ready for a new adventure and so teams up with Wang as they head to England to avenge his father's death and recover the stolen seal.

Wang discovers Lin is in prison, accused of attempted murder. It is a charge stemming from her pursuit of her father's murderer, who happens to be Lord Rathbone (Aiden Gillen, Queer as Folk), an English nobleman in line for the throne.

To gain his sister's freedom and avenge his father's death, Wang must prove Rathbone guilty. After Roy takes a romantic interest in Lin, Wang also has to worry about his intentions. He may be a fine sidekick, but Wang knows he's not good enough for his sister.

The best thing about Shanghai Knights is that it doesn't take itself too seriously. There is nothing deep or meaningful about this film. It is just meant to provide an evening's diversion and playful entertainment. And it succeeds.

Director David Dobkin (Clay Pigeons) seems to understand that with a talent like Chan, it is best simply to take a supporting role by keeping the film moving along, maintaining the audience's interest until its time for another fight sequence. Fans will be pleased to learn that there are plenty in this film.

Chan does battle in a revolving door, in an umbrella shop, in a crowded marketplace and even in the inner workings of Big Ben.

Jackie Chan has long been compared to the physical comedians of the silent film era. We can see why in Shanghai Knights. References to Charlie Chaplin, Buster Keaton, and Harold Lloyd are sprinkled throughout the film. Even song and dance man Gene Kelly receives tribute during one of Chan's precisely choreographed fights. And the sequence works beautifully.

Yes, there is a story being told. Yes, there are other actors involved. But truth be told, the reason to see this film is all Jackie. He is at the top of his game, seeming to get better or sharper with every film.

The timing and precision which he demonstrates comes as the result of much disciplined practice. There are no shortcuts. Chan excels at his craft because of the diligent, disciplined preparation and practice which has taken place before the cameras started to roll.

Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings; he shall not stand before mean men. Proverbs 22:29 (KJV)

As the old joke goes, there's only one way to get to Carnegie Hall... Practice. The same can be said about any discipline of life - from martial arts to living by the Word of God.
