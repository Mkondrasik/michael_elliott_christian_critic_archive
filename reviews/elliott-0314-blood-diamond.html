Sermon / spiritual topic: Good and Evil

Author-verified transcription

The Bible clearly states that the love of money is the root of all evil. Blood Diamond shows what happens when such a perverse love is allowed to rule within a country unchecked by a higher, purer love.

The manipulated diamond market has created such a demand for its stones that countries rich with them become easy targets for unscrupulous men who use violence and brutality to advance their greedy goals.

Sierra Leone was a country overrun with such warlords. When a fisherman named Solomon (Djimon Hounsou) is torn from his family and forced into a mining camp, he discovers a rock that sets into motion events that will lead to many deaths and much suffering.

White African turned diamond smuggler Danny Archer (Leonardo DiCaprio) learns of the fisherman's find and sees the valuable diamond as his ticket out of the hell he has helped to create. Together, along with American journalist Maddy Bowen (Jennifer Connolly) whose credentials give them privileged access, they seek to recover the prized stone and escape Africa - Danny with his fortune, Maddy with her story, and Solomon with his family.

During their journey, we see countless scenes of heartless monsters indiscriminately murdering their countrymen, butchering women and children, and corrupting an entire generation to form armies of "baby killers" or child soldiers. Disturbing isn't a strong enough word to describe some of what we see.

DiCaprio is brilliant as the self-motivated smuggler; basically a good man in a bad situation who does bad things in the hopes they will lead to a better life. He has grown into one of the finest actors of his generation and his Academy Award nomination for this film is certainly understandable and well-deserved.

Djimon Hounsou is a powerful force in his own right, capturing the intense love of family that drives his character and the anguish of his being unable to protect them against the unspeakable evils and terrors within a country being torn apart by civil war.

Jennifer Connolly is the audience's touchstone - an observer and activist frustrated by what she sees but cannot change. She is the heart and compassion in a film that has little to show of either.

Director Edward Zwick and screenwriter Charles Leavitt don't fill in all the holes or answer all the questions that the film raises. Neither have they made a "message film" in the purest sense. While the movie is centered in the diamond trade and the civil unrest that took place in the impoverished nation of Sierra Leone, it does so in the guise of an action/adventure.

There is a scene where Archer and another character discuss the nature of good and evil. "Do you believe people are inherently good?" the character asks. "No," replies Archer. "They're just people."

The point being made is that the potential for good and evil resides within each one of us. Because of this we each have an individual choice as to which quality we will exhibit. This choice is not a one time action that will determine our character forever. Our character is not formed through a single act or choice. It is formed over our lifetimes through the hundreds of willful, conscious decisions we must make every single day of our lives. God encourages us to choose wisely.

For he that will love life, and see good days, let him refrain his tongue from evil, and his lips that they speak no guile: Let him eschew evil, and do good; let him seek peace, and ensue it. 1 Peter 3:10-11 (KJV)
