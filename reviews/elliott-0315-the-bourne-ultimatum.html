The Bourne Ultimatum

Author-verified transcription

The world's most popular assassin is back in theaters. Jason Bourne lights up the screen for the third and perhaps final time in the thrilling The Bourne Ultimatum.

Director Paul Greengrass gives us more of what made the first two films worldwide successes - action, action and more action.

Jason Bourne (Matt Damon), super CIA-killer and amnesiac bounces around the globe as he continues to try to piece together the shattered fragments of his memory to determine who he is and how he came to be what he has become.

But there are those who don't want the truth uncovered and will stop at nothing to put an end to Bourne's identity crisis... by making sure that he never remembers.

And he isn't without help. CIA operatives Pamela Landy (Joan Allen) and Nicky Parsons (Julia Stiles), who have tracked Bourne ever since the first film, have finally come to the realization that perhaps he might be in the right. Of course, they can't come right out and announce it... they are, after all, in the CIA. But they do manage to work behind the scenes to offer some assistance.

The stunts have always been the featured element of the Bourne series and part 3 does not disappoint. This is an action-packed, adrenalin rush from beginning to end filmed using a now expected gritty realism. Handheld cameras, quick-cut editing, off-centered framing all underscored with a magnificent heart-pumping musical score by John Powell gives the film an unflagging energy that fills the theater.

Matt Damon fits comfortably back into the persona of the troubled but talented Bourne. He has the necessary physicality to make us believe he's capable of the superhuman feats we are seeing. At the same time, we feel the human drama of the man who dislikes what little he knows of himself and his need to discover it all.

David Strathairn, as a top CIA official with much to hide and all the resources of the US government with which to hide it, is very effective as the chief bad guy. He plays off of Joan Allen's cooler more rational bureaucrat quite well. Paddy Considine also has some nice moments as a newspaper reporter who stumbles across Bourne's origin.

Jason Bourne, we discover, was a product of an experimental training program which its developers dubbed "behavior modification." Using draconian methodology, they broke down the man (mentally, emotionally, psychologically), in order to rebuild him to fit their black operations needs.

While this sounds abhorrent, the principle behind it is something that has been going on for millennia. Behavior modification is exactly what God requests of us. To take the learned behavior to which we've been conditioned by this world and dismantle it - breaking it down so that we might rebuild ourselves in His image.

"Strip yourselves of your former nature [put off and discard your old unrenewed self] which characterized your previous manner of life and becomes corrupt through lusts and desires that spring from delusion; and be constantly renewed in the spirit of your mind [having a fresh mental and spiritual attitude], and put on the new nature [the regenerate self created in God's image, (Godlike) in true righteousness and holiness." Ephesians 4:22-24 (Amplified)
