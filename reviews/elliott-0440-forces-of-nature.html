Author-verified transcription

There's an old joke floating around the Internet that goes something like this: "Marriage is like a three ring circus... First comes the engagement ring. Then comes the wedding ring. After that comes the suffering." That is the kind of jaded view regarding marriage that lies at the heart of Forces of Nature.

Ben Holmes is getting married... if only he can make it to the church on time. Actually, it's not a church, it's the southern home of his fiancee's parents. Trouble is, he is still in New York, having been delayed by his grandfather's over stimulation at the bachelor party which led to his hospitalization.

While Bridget, his lovely fiancee, waits patiently in her satin pumps, Ben valiantly tries to make his way down to Savannah. He's not alone. The cute and sassy Sarah is also destined for Savannah to sell off a business and write off her no-good husband.

After their plane crashes in a freak accident on the runway, the pair team up in order to reach their shared destination. Their transportation woes continue as the forces of nature conspire to keep them from Savannah, even as it draws them closer to each other.

Ben Affleck (Armageddon) is Ben Holmes, the "lucky" groom and jacket cover copywriter who begins to question his impending nuptials after spending some time with Sarah. Mr. Affleck gives Ben an easy manner, making him likable even if a bit boring. Supposedly a master in "blurb-ology," he's been having difficulty writing his marriage vows, let alone keeping them.

Sandra Bullock (Practical Magic) tries to play the wild and effervescent Sarah with a certain free-spirited abandon, but between her heavily applied makeup and the crass mannerisms she affects, she merely plays Sarah as a loose tramp, making it extremely hard for us to root for any possible relationship between her character and Ben.

Maura Tierney (Primary Colors) is the put-upon bride-to-be who is forced to stay with her insufferable parents (Ronny Cox, Murder at 1600 and Blythe Danner, The X-Files) while being tempted with the attentions of an old boyfriend (David Strickland, Delivered). For a couple on the cusp of marital bliss, Ben and Bridget sure are having problems with the fidelity issue.

Director Bronwen Hughes (Harriet The Spy) and screenwriter Marc Lawrence (Life With Mikey) have borrowed from more than one movie to come up with Forces of Nature, most notably Planes, Trains, and Automobiles. While the framework might be similar, the end result is not -- mainly because the plot devices used to keep Ben and Sarah from Savannah are implausible and, at times, ridiculously contrived.

What is most distressing is the attitude towards marriage that is presented in this film. Throughout his ill-fated journey, Ben meets people who are totally disenchanted with the institution of marriage. From his own grandfather who calls it "a prison" to a fellow passenger on the plane who calls it "a lie", everyone he meets seems to have a negative view towards the relationship that was designed by God Himself.

And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him. Genesis 2:18 /KJV]

That "help meet" turned out to be woman. A marriage union where the man and the woman agree to stand together in the eyes God is as strong a bond there is. God even outlines the proper relationship in His Word.

But I would have you know, that the head of every man is Christ; and the head of the woman is the man; and the head of Christ is God. 1 Corinthians 11:3 /KJV]

Seeing as God is perfect, anything that He designs is bound to be perfect. A marriage that fails is not the fault of the institution itself, but rather of the parties who are improperly functioning within that institution.

It is no wonder that in a society that ignores the godliness of the marriage relationship (or just gives God a cursory nod by maintaining a religious facade), divorce is high, adultery is higher, and disillusionment is rampant. It does not have to be so. But it will take more spiritual understanding than these characters bothered to demonstrate during the past two hours.
