Author-verified transcription

From whence does inspiration flow? Long ago, inspiration was something attributed to the Muses, nine daughters of Zeus who were each responsible for a different aspect of artistic expression. Anyone “blessed” by the favors of a Muse were capable of producing works of infinite artistry. However, fall out of favor, and that unfortunate soul might never have an original thought again.

Such is the legend that Albert Brooks (Mother) incorporates in his newest movie, The Muse. Mr. Brooks plays Steven Phillips, a Hollywood screenwriter who is being told that he has “lost his edge.” While he doesn't know exactly what that phrase means, when the studio pulls his deal and releases him from his contract, he figures that it can't be good.

Seeking advice from friend and still successful screenwriter, Jack Warrick (Jeff Bridges, Arlington Road), Steven learns of Sarah (Sharon Stone, Gloria), believed to be one of the Muses who has settled in Hollywood to help its artists make movies. Jack sets up a meeting, telling Steven that if Sarah takes him on as a client, his problems are over. She does and Steven quickly learns that his problems have just begun.

Muses, we come to discover, have extremely expensive tastes which the mortals (whom they grace with their presence) are expected to accommodate. Obviously accustomed to lavish praise, adoration, and continual attention, Sarah begins to make off-the-wall demands of Steven, causing him to question whether “inspiration” is all that it is cracked up to be.

Albert Brooks plays a beleaguered character as well as anyone in the business. While Steven is paying all the bills and running all the errands, he sees everyone else around him benefiting from the “powers” of the Muse. Understandably, his reactions and comments are sardonically amusing.

Sharon Stone, showing a seldom seen humorous side, is appropriately flamboyant and flighty as the spoiled daughter of Zeus. Her character is supremely confident, resulting in a nonchalance that immediately makes all others feel subservient.

Andie MacDowell (Michael) is on hand as Steven's loving wife, Laura, who not only accepts the Muse, she is inspired by her to follow her dream of being the next Mrs. Fields... much to the chagrin of Steven who is still struggling with his summer comedy script, inspiration or no.

The Muse, which Mr. Brooks penned with longtime collaborator Monica Johnson (Mother), is dryly humorous and contains many examples of the star's unique perspective regarding the industry in which he works: “Being a screenwriter is like being a eunuch at an orgy... except they [the eunuchs] get to watch.”

The ultimate weakness of the movie lies within a single factor. As co-writer, director, and star, Mr. Brooks is wearing one hat too many. He may be a very talented man, but he did his film a disservice by trying to direct it as well as star in it.

Both Ms. MacDowell and Mr. Bridges give lackluster performances which a stronger director would not have tolerated. Many of the scenes (such as the initial meeting between Sarah and Laura) played flat and screamed for a reshoot.

Still, the movie addresses an interesting premise... Is inspiration of a divine nature? Certainly the Bible supports the claim. Not the daughters of Zeus nonsense, but the true God Who gave his Word via inspired utterance:

All scripture is given by inspiration of God. 2 Timothy 3:16a [KJV]

The last 5 words of the above referenced scripture are derived from a single Greek word, “theopneustos,” which literally means “God-breathed.” True inspiration does not have to be purchased... Indeed, it cannot be bought. It is obtained by those who have humbled themselves to listen for the still, small voice of their heavenly Father and who have proven themselves faithful to be obedient to His Word.

The truth regarding inspiration may not be as funny as Mr. Brooks' version, or result in an award-winning screenplay for Steven, but as far as our lives are concerned, it will have a much greater impact.
