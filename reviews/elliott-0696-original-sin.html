Author-verified transcription

"This is not a love story." So we are told in the opening minutes of Original Sin. OK. Fine. But if we're going to receive that kind of disclaimer, why not tell the whole truth? This is not a good story either.

Antonio Banderas (Spy Kids) stars as Louis Durand, a wealthy Cuban landowner who must spend so much time making money that he's never had an opportunity to look in the mirror and discover that he shouldn't have too much trouble getting a girl. Ready to start building his dynasty, he places the 19th century version of a "Desperately Seeking Susan" ad in a Baltimore newspaper in an attempt to find a wife and potential bearer of his future heirs.

He gets a response (he probably would have gotten more but he forgot to mention the "rich" part) and anxiously awaits the arrival of Miss Julia Russel (Angelina Jolie, Tomb Raider) whom he plans to marry the minute she disembarks. One look at her and Louis is ready to sign over the deed to his family homestead. In fact, he puts her name on all his bank accounts without a second thought, giving her full withdrawal privileges. To no one's surprise (excepting Louis) she uses them.

The plot then thickens, rather like an artery after a full course meal at Louie's Deep Fried Fatburgers. Is Julia Russel who she appears to be? Will Louis exact his revenge upon her? Who is Billy and Bonnie and will they be as pathetic as the rest of these characters?

Original Sin is hardly original and the sin is one of omission. The filmmakers have omitted logic and any semblance of rational thought or motivation. Their characters are driven by the basest of emotions while the audience must settle for being driven by something a bit less feral... something like boredom.

There's relatively little point in discussing the artistic merits of a film like this. Any positive contributions are far outweighed by the sheer absurdity of it all. Antonio Banderas still knows how to convey that smoldering Latin look. Angelina Jolie still gets the desired response by generously displaying her assets but there isn't much that they can do to salvage the ridiculously over-melodramatic nature of the story.

The cinematic tricks that director Michael Christofer pulls from left field: incessantly panning cameras and quick cuts from montage to montage, will do little to keep Original Sin from receiving the critical ridicule which will surely come its way. In fact, the odd directorial choices may just add fuel to the fire.

But even in an undeserving picture such as this, there can be an element which strikes a spiritual chord within us. For me, it came near the beginning of the film as a character tried to pinpoint the difference between "lust" and "love." His definition, succinct as it may be, made a lot of sense.

"Love," he said, "is when you want to give and then give some more. Lust is when you want to take and then take even more."

Yes, it is simplistically defined. But there is truth that lies behind that simple statement. Love is all about giving. The best example of the giving nature of love that we can see is found in John 3:16.

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. John 3:16 (KJV)

We love God so we give of ourselves to do His Will on this earth. In contrast, we can see from 1 John 2:16 that the taking nature of lust is in opposition with the giving nature of God.

For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world. 1 John 2:16 (KJV)

Lust and love are truly spiritual opposites. So much so that they originate from different sources. We know that God is love from 1 John 4:8 and we know from 1 John 2:16 that lust is not of God but rather it is of the world (which is run by the god of this world, Satan).

Hence we have two opposing attitudes or mindsets... and we have been given free will as to which one we will choose. May God grant us the wisdom to choose rightly.
