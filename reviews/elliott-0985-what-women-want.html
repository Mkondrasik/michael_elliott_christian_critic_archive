What Women Want

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 15, 2000
Rating
3 stars
Running time
2 hr: 7 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Colossians 4:6Matthew 5:13Ephesians 4:25

Sermon / spiritual topic: Honesty; Commitment

Author-verified transcription

Judging from the female response of the screening audience, if Mel Gibson (Braveheart) truly wanted to know What Women Want, he need only gaze into the mirror.

In What Women Want, Gibson plays Nick Marshall, a genuine “man’s man.” This means that he calls every woman “babe” and has more testosterone than most hockey teams. As a crack ad executive, his successful commercial concepts have either included bikinis, beer, or both. He is about to discover that the times are changing. Recognizing that women have become more of a economic force in our society, when a coveted position opens for a creative director, his firm decides to pass him over in favor of a “skirt” who might be better able to design campaigns targeting the female market.

Darcy Maguire’s (Helen Hunt, As Good As It Gets) first command decision is to have every ad exec pitch a feminine product. Not an easy task for a chauvinistic “man’s man,” but he throws himself into the challenge by personally testing each product, trying to get into the feminine mind. He succeeds beyond his wildest expectation.

A freak accident gives him the unusual ability to hear the thoughts of the opposite sex. It is quite a shock, finding out for the first time what women really think of his swaggering, condescending demeanor, but he decides to try and learn how to use this newfound talent to his personal and professional advantage.

What Women Want rides squarely on the comedic shoulders of Mel Gibson and it succeeds largely because he does. As the self-absorbed, Sinatra-loving, politically-incorrect womanizer, he infuses his character with such a rakish charm that we like him despite his negative qualities. Demonstrating a comedic flair and gracefulness which his action/adventure persona has only hinted at, Gibson deftly pokes fun at his own machismo.

Helen Hunt, in her third of four movies which will be released this year (Cast Away opens next week), plays Darcy as an independent thinker with a penchant for speaking what’s on her mind... a trait which the mind-reading Nick finds is not often practiced by those he meets. It is a quality both rare and attractive. Their screen chemistry is obvious and their scenes together are both funny and warm.

The talented supporting cast, which includes Marisa Tomei (My Cousin Vinny) as a love-scarred waitress, Mark Feuerstein (Woman on Top) as Nick’s friend and confidante, Alan Alda (M*A*S*H) as the president of the ad agency, Delta Burke (TV’s Designing Women) and Valerie Perrine (Lenny) as a pair of ex-showgirls turned secretaries, all take a back seat to Gibson who appears content and capable of driving this picture from beginning to end.

Director Nancy Meyers (The Parent Trap), doesn’t offer anything new in terms of insight into the male/female relationship. What Women Want is not a think piece. It is what it is... An enjoyable, light and frothy romp which dances around the differences and similarities of the sexes without bothering to delve into anything as humorless as psychological depth.

Since much of the humor is sexually-oriented and more appropriate to an R-rated film, parents should be advised that the PG-13 rating given to What Women Want is debatable and misleading.

Darcy’s tendency to speak her mind is indeed refreshing. To carefully gauge one’s words, thinking before we speak, so that we always mean what we say and say what we mean is an admirable quality to develop. God calls it having “speech... seasoned with salt.”

Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man. Colossians 4:6 (KJV).

In the Eastern culture, salt is representative of commitment... a pledge or covenant which would be unthinkable for an Eastern man or woman to break. Having one’s words seasoned with salt is to speak truthfully, without duplicity or deceit. For people to live by such a high ideal is not just what women want... It’s what we should all want. It is, after all, what God wants.
