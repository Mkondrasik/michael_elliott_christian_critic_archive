Beyond the Sea

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 29, 2004
Rating
2 stars

2 hr: 01 min

Published Web Version

Sermon / spiritual topic: Vision; focus; single-mindedness; success; prosperity

Author-verified transcription

In Beyond the Sea, Kevin Spacey turns a party impersonation into a cinematic hat trick by directing, producing, acting, singing, and dancing his way through the life of Bobby Darin.

I should say that, vocally, Spacey, who did all his own singing, is very impressive. His phrasing and tonal qualities match that of the late singer almost perfectly. Unfortunately the rest of the film doesn't measure up to the same standard of excellence. Perhaps Spacey decided to wear one or two hats too many.

Looking at it from a narrative standpoint, the film suffers from a lack of a strong structure and form. I daresay that it is not a good sign when a film opens with the characters arguing about how the movie should begin. They eventually decide to open with Darin as a young boy (played by William Ulrich) after he learns that he has a heart condition that will more than likely put him in the grave before his 15th birthday.

Darin outlived his doctors' gloomy predictions until he finally succumbed to his heart ailment at the age of 37. During his abbreviated life he became a celebrated singer, a Grammy winning recording artist, an Oscar-nominated actor, and husband to film icon Sandra Dee.

With so much crammed into such a short lifespan, it is surprising that the movie plays as one-dimensionally as it does. The musical numbers are pleasing but without much meaning and the non-melodic sections of the film don't offer much to hold our interest.

Spacey reminds us of Darin's vocal talent but doesn't succeed in showing us the man behind the music. The behind-the-scenes reenactments of his most popular hits (Mack the Knife, Splish Splash, Beyond the Sea, Dream Lover) shed no light on the what or why of their development. We are left with no more sense of the man Bobby Darin than we already formed from listening to his records.

Kate Bosworth costars as Sandra Dee who married Darin when she was a mere sixteen years old. She is effective in a scene involving wedding night jitters, but is pretty much pushed into the dim light of the background for the rest of the film. The spotlight rarely strays from Spacey's side.

There's a definite theatricality to the screenplay by Lewis Colick. He continually breaks the "fourth wall" convention, has his characters breaking into song and dance a la musical theater, and develops a "relationship" between the 10 year old Bobby Darin and the adult Darin. None of it works particularly well.

The good news is that the music is memorable and will have people humming and singing as they exit the multiplex.

Also worth remembering is the key to Bobby Darin's success. As a young child, he developed a vision for his life and he kept that vision in the forefront of his mind as he worked toward that goal. It was his single-mindedness that gave him the focus that is necessary for success.

A double minded man is unstable in all his ways. James 1:8 (KJV)

The biblical keys to success and prosperity are relatively simple - work heartily; live within your means; and share of your abundance. Whenever we apply those three key elements to whatever goal or vision we have set for ourselves, we will always be pleased with the results.
