Walking Across Egypt

1 hr: 40 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Long-suffering

Author-verified transcription

Walking Across Egypt is a delightful independently made film which deserves ardent support from both Christian and family oriented audiences. Released in 1999, it generated some positive responses, winning awards at a couple of film festivals. Now on video, it is hoped the film may reach a wider audience.

Mattie Rigsbee (Ellen Burstyn, Playing By Heart) is an elderly churchgoing widow living alone in a small Southern town. She keeps herself ultra busy by doing for others - helping with church activities, inviting strangers to meals, feeding stray dogs who just appear on her doorstep.

But she also recognizes that her age is causing her to slow down. When a stray appears at her door, she calls the dogcatcher (Mark Hamill, Star Wars), knowing that she doesn't have the time or energy to give it a home. Thus begins a string of events that causes Mattie to question her own giving nature.

Hearing a sermon in church where the minister, Rev. Vernon (Edward Herrmann, Double Take) preached on Jesus' exhortation to "do unto the least of these," Mattie's conscience is pricked because she has been made aware of someone who fits that description. The dogcatcher's nephew, Wesley (Jonathan Taylor Thomas, Home Improvement) is locked away in a juvenile penitentiary for stealing a car. Abandoned by his parents at an early age, the boy is on the road to nowhere and traveling fast.

Mattie decides to visit the unloved and unwanted boy and the rest of the movie is about whether or not her giving can have an impact on the life of this teen who has never been given anything but neglect.

This is a sweet and moving story of love and redemption, with just enough grit and hardness to it to give it a proper balance. It is a real treat to watch Ms. Burstyn in this heartfelt role. Her Mattie is a proper Southern lady with little tolerance for improper language or behavior. Jonathan Taylor Thomas may be a bit too clean cut for the role of the troubled teen, but he plays the part well and shows the emotional upheaval within his character brought about by a love and generosity that he never experienced before.

Other noted performances come from Mark Hamill as Wesley's uncle, Judge Reinhold (Beethoven's 3rd) as Mattie's disapproving son, Gwen Verdon (Marvin's Room) as Mattie's nosy friend and neighbor and Pat Corley (Murphy Brown) as the gruff but kindly Southern sheriff.

The film, directed by Arthur Allan Seidelman (Harvest of Fire), moves at a slow and natural pace. Based on the novel by Clyde Edgerton, it is not only character-driven but it is infused with such warm and inviting characters that the drive is like a pleasant open-air jaunt down a country lane.

The relationship between Mattie and Wesley is an odd one and not without its ups and downs. As the movie progresses, we see the impact that Mattie's heart of giving has upon the stubborn and rebellious Wesley. But it is not an instantaneous change and Mattie has plenty of opportunity to practice what the scriptures call "longsuffering."

Put on therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, longsuffering; Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye. And above all these things put on charity (love), which is the bond of perfectness. Colossians 3:12-14 (KJV)

It is Mattie's unwillingness to write the boy off as an "incorrigible" that gives him the time he needs to respond to the love and kindness she offers. Longsuffering is not the same as being tolerant to disobedience or error, but rather an enduring patience to continue to teach somebody how to obey or walk in truth. It is a quality much to be admired. After all, where would any of us be if God did not show His longsuffering to us?

The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance. 2 Peter 3:9 (KJV)
