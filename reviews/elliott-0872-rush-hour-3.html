Rush Hour 3

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 10, 2007
Rating

1 hr: 24 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Brotherhood; Family Ties

Author-verified transcription

Have you ever noticed that the longer rush hour lasts, the slower things get? It should therefore be no surprise that the fastest hands in the East and the fastest mouth of the West have lost a bit of acceleration in Rush Hour 3.

Jackie Chan and Chris Tucker reprise their roles once again for the third installment of the buddy cop franchise that helped to frame (or should that be fund) their careers. In this version of more of the same, Inspector Lee (Chan) and Detective Carter (Tucker) must travel to Paris in order to hunt down and stop the diabolical Chinese "Triad," an organized crime outfit that makes the Italian Mafia look like Romper Room.

For Lee, it's personal. A chief member of the Triad is Kenji (Hiroyuki Sanada), a boyhood friend who he considers to be as close as a brother. For Carter, it's a chance to meet chicks.

That's pretty much it. On their way to find a secret list of the leaders of this nefarious organization, they get their butts kicked, they kick some butt of their own, and they meet various ladies clad in various degrees of clothing.

As I read through my past reviews of the first two Rush Hour movies (important because I forgot all about them soon after they were viewed), I realized that what I liked best about the previous films (both of which received two stars from me) was the physicality and humor of Mr. Chan. It is sad to say that the 50+ year old actor can no longer slide by on his moves. He's going to have to act now to keep a career in front of the camera and his acting was never his strong suit.

To make up for the apparent decline of athleticism from his partner, more emphasis (and therefore responsibility) has been placed upon Mr. Tucker who heretofore had been the weak link of the pairing. While normally that wouldn't bode well for the movie, director Brett Ratner has learned a trick or two and manages to keep the pace of Rush Hour 3 moving at a steady clip. And when in trouble, throwing in a little "Yu's on first" routine doesn't hurt at all.

The fight scenes are few and far between and, with the exception of the finale atop the Eiffel Tower, not all that special. Still if you are a fan of the series, the third time around won't disappoint you.

Lee's dilemma centers on his feeling of obligation towards his "brother," now a notorious gangster, even though that familial bond is not reciprocated, evidenced by Kenji's repeated attempts to kill him.

Jesus Christ spoke to this issue:

And the multitude sat about him, and they said unto him, Behold, thy mother and thy brethren without seek for thee. And he answered them, saying, Who is my mother, or my brethren? And he looked round about on them which sat about him, and said, Behold my mother and my brethren! For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother. Mark 3:32-35 (KJV)

Jesus did not define "family" by blood but rather by spirit. Anyone who shared the same God-given spirit and manifested it by doing the will of God in every situation was loved by Jesus as his brother. In a similar respect, those who did not share in the same goals and values were not given preferential treatment over those who did simply because they shared the same mother.

Jesus is not saying family is bad or should be forgotten. He's simply saying that our love extends far beyond the physical ties.
