The Alamo

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 9, 2004
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Galatians 1:3-4 1 John 3:16 John 15:13

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Sacrifice; courage; giving one's life

Author-verified transcription

“Remember The Alamo!” With 13 movies already made about the historic stand which helped bring independence to the territory now known as Texas, it’s kind of hard to forget. The most recent version is now in theaters and, according to the filmmakers, it hopes to clarify some of the inaccuracies of earlier filmed depictions... even though they do present inaccuracies of their own.

Dennis Quaid stars as Gen. Sam Houston, a hard drinking, emotionally troubled man who nevertheless was passionately committed to Texas independence. Opposing him is the self-appointed Mexican dictator Antonio Lopez de Santa Anna Perez de Lebron (Emilio Echevarria), often simply referred to as Santa Anna.

With Houston trying to raise an army, and Santa Anna looking to cement his claim on the territory, a small Spanish mission in San Antonio becomes a pivotal defense point. Manned by an inadequate force (under 200 men) but blessed with a large number of cannons, the Alamo has had a history of serving as a symbolic representation of territorial control. At this particular time, the Texans are in control but Santa Anna and his army of 2400 soldiers aim to take it back.

Inside the Alamo are federal soldiers under the command of a young, untested lieutenant colonel named William Barret Travis (Patrick Wilson). His troops are joined by a number of militia volunteers and their unofficial leader and somewhat famous knife-fighter is none other than the fatally ill Jim Bowie (Jason Patric). In their midst is a legendary figure - frontiersman and Tennessee congressman, Davy Crockett (Billy Bob Thornton), although he prefers to be called David.

Though each have their own reasons for being at the Alamo, once Santa Anna’s army arrives and surrounds the mission, the differences between them pretty much evaporate.

The film itself runs in spurts. At times it is an engrossing drama of tragic proportions and at other times it is droning rhetoric. Thornton develops an interesting and conflicted character, portraying Crockett as a man trapped by his own legend. Coming to Texas because of the promise of free land, he feels he cannot leave the Alamo to escape the certain slaughter because of what it might do to the reputation he has built.

Newcomer Patrick Wilson also turns in a decent performance as a young commander struggling to gain the respect of his men. Unfortunately, the less famous characters who were present at the Alamo will not find lasting fame because of this film. They, the less famous defenders of the mission, kind of blend together in a nameless stew of victims.

What is evident is that everyone who stayed at the Alamo knew beyond any doubt that survival was improbable at best. The question we must ask ourselves is this: What drives a man to make the ultimate sacrifice? What cause is important enough for one to give his life? It is a question that has real resonance for a Christian because our Lord chose to give his life for us.

Grace be to you and peace from God the Father, and from our Lord Jesus Christ, Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil world, according to the will of God and our Father: Galatians 1:3-4 (KJV)
