Domino

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 14, 2005
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Truth; conviction; discernment; resisting compromise

Author-verified transcription

Domino Harvey – model… celebrity offspring… bounty hunter? It sounds like an odd combination and Domino, the film based on her life, falls into line accordingly. Director Tony Scott mixes gritty realism with broad comedic strokes that generates more incredulous laughter than anything else.

According to the film, Domino (Keira Knightley), whose father was Lawrence Harvey (The Manchurian Candidate), was disfranchised from life at an early age and turned to bounty hunting as a way to divorce herself from her privileged pedigree. She bullied her way into the not-so-refined business of bounty hunting by accosting Ed Mosby (Mickey Rourke) and Choco (Edgar Ramirez) who track fleeing felons for bond bailsman Claremont Williams III (Delroy Lindo).

The four soon become as thick as thieves even though they operate on the sunny side of the law… for the most part. It is when one of the members drifts well into the shadows that trouble begins. It doesn’t help matters that Domino is being thrust more and more into the public spotlight.

It isn’t difficult to guess at what might be true and what probably isn’t. Her name was Domino and she was a bounty hunter. Everything else can probably be discounted as fiction.

Tony Scott, who showed his affection for directorial excess in Man on Fire, continues in the same vein with Domino. Subtlety is a quality to be admired and it is an indication of an artist’s maturity. In this regard, Scott shows signs that he remains in a state of pre-adolescence.

Keira Knightley is a fast rising star and turns in a star’s performance. But even a star (especially a newly formed one) can be led astray. She turns in a decent performance in an unmemorable film.

Unfortunately, the best supporting actors play characters that do the most to derail the movie. Delroy Lindo’s character keeps a stable of DMV ladies (Mo’Nique, Macy Gray, Shondrella Avery) whom he uses as sources for potential contracts. These ladies have a story all their own (which includes an outrageous appearance on the Jerry Springer show). But even though they are lively and funny, they take the film and twist it all out of tonal shape.

Scott also includes a sweet but bizarre love story, a link to terrorism and Afghan politics, and a side story involving reality TV. There’s simply too much going on without ensuring that all elements are working to further the story. To underscore this Scott and screenwriter Richard Kelly (Donnie Darko) continually add segments restating salient plot points just in case we might have missed them the first time around. They needn’t have bothered. Most of us have long checked out of this improbable tale.

One who should have checked out is Domino herself. In the film she bemoans the fact that she “felt” something wrong about their last contract yet proceeded anyway. When we know or intuitively feel something is not right (morally or spiritually) we should have the courage of our convictions to stand up against it and refuse to be a part of it.

Cease, my son, to hear the instruction that causeth to err from the words of knowledge. Proverbs 19:27 (KJV)

God gave us a standard of truth so we might be able to avoid that which might steer us away from His will for our lives. We need to heed his direction and ignore that which might tempt us from it.
