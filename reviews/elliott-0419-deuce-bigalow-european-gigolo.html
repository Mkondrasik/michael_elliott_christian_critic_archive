Deuce Bigalow: European Gigolo

Author-verified transcription

The problem with Deuce Bigalow: European Gigolo is not that it is crude. It is not that it is politically incorrect. It is not even that it is in poor taste.

Don't get me wrong, DB: EG is all those things. But it is also unfunny and that is the one thing in a comedy that today's audiences just won't forgive.

After losing his wife, Kate, to a freakish turtle-feeding accident, Deuce Bigalow, male gigolo (Rob Schneider), travels to Amsterdam in response to a request from his pimp friend TJ (Eddie Griffin). He arrives at a time when a serial man-whore killer is on the loose and TJ has become the prime suspect.

In order to prevent a miscarriage of justice, Deuce must come out of retirement and gigolo his way through the client list of the deceased man-whores as he searches for clues to the real killer's identity.

For whatever comedic reason, this client list consists of women with physical deformities, each one more bizarre than the other. One woman is a giant, another - a hunchback. One speaks through a hole in her neck; another is a Chernobyl mutant. Even the so-called "normal girl" who serves as Schneider's love interest (Hanna Verboom) has a severe case of OCD with over 130 different manifestations.

Director Mike Bigalow badly bungles the laughs in this painfully obvious and ill- conceived comedy. The humor is mainly mean- spirited and relies upon faux sexual names and practices such as the non-descript "Portuguese breakfast," the aforementioned physical abnormalities, and having us continually catching the homophobic TJ putting his hands down the pants of dead men. All of which is done with the intent of building laughs. None of which succeeds.

Rob Schneider is one of those actors whose career prompts people to ask the question "How does he continue to get work in this business?" He is known primarily for his appearances on Saturday Night Live and for his cameo catch phrase in Adam Sandler movies ("You can do it!") He would be well advised to stick to sketch comedy and walk-on roles. His brand of humor or ability to deliver it is something that should only be taken in short, small doses if at all.

Eddie Griffin is all attitude: pimped out, full of bling, and retro-boring. It's clear that he's trying to make the best of a bad script but there's no saving this piece of Euro-trash.

Deuce Bigalow: European Gigolo is one of those movies that Hollywood insists on making as a fast and easy way to build revenue.

It is not because the idea behind the film is good. It is because the idea behind the film worked once before. As the old adage goes - "Fool me once, shame on you... Fool me twice, the sequel makes money." Hopefully, this one won't. Without meaning to assign undue redemptive value to this morally bankrupt film, there is nonetheless a moral lesson thinly laid atop the pile of immorality presented to us.

Deuce Bigalow has the unusual and largely unappreciated ability to see past the freakish facade of the women he meets. Whether they are physically, mentally, or emotionally challenged, Deuce sees a glimmer of good in them and then will fan that glimmer until it glows.

Whatever our faults may be, each and every one of us deserves to be treated with respect and love. For all his buffoonery, Bigelow never judges those he meets based upon appearance. He instinctively knows what many of us still need to learn. There is so much more to a person than his or her looks. But to appreciate the hidden qualities, we need to spend the time and make the effort get to know the person. God would have us do nothing less.
