Agent Cody Banks

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 14, 2003
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: Amplified
Sermon / spiritual topic: Practice; Spiritual Maturity; Confidence; Training; Discernment

Author-verified transcription

Only one thing strikes fear in the heart of CIA special agent Cody Banks. More than bullets, more than runaway cars, and much more than vicious henchmen twice his size. Knowing that Cody is only 15 years old makes the answer obvious. All that other stuff is easy. It's talking to a pretty girl that is hard. Having once been a 15 year old kid, I can certainly identify with him. Been there - done that - got the tee shirt to prove it.

Revisiting a premise explored by Spy Kids, Agent Cody Banks combines the action of a James Bond film with the adolescent pathos of a teen comedy. The result is more successful than we might expect, although I must sound a note of caution. The MPAA, which assigns the parental advisory to films, seems to be employing a sliding standard these days. They've given this one a PG rating. It is far too sexual and a bit too violent for that. A PG-13 rating would be more appropriate and representative of the film's content.

Seattle teenager Cody Banks is a typical high school student with a big secret. The summer camp that he's been attending for the past three years is, in actuality, a CIA training ground for their junior agent program.

When a crucial CIA investigation points to a Seattle scientist, Cody's "sleeper" status comes to an end. He is activated and given his first assignment: Get close to the scientist's 15 year old daughter, wrangle an invitation to her pending birthday party to find out what the girl's father is up to.

Unfortunately, all that will require Cody to actually talk to her - an activity not covered in his CIA training. At this stage in his maturity, suave and debonair are ill-fitting descriptions.

It is a cute and amusing premise and Frankie Muniz (Malcolm in the Middle) is likable as the secret agent who is lacking in art of PG-13 seduction. He's also surprisingly effective in the action scenes. He makes for a decent pint sized action hero. James Bond as a teenager.

It is a shame that the film is a bit too sexy for the audience demographic, but this is due primarily to the way the filmmakers use the beautiful and busty Angie Harmon (TV's Law & Order) who serves as Cody's CIA "handler." It was much too easy for them to build up her cleavage instead of building her a fully dimensional character to play.

Hilary Duff (Lizzie McGuire) is the blonde, similarly one-dimensional target which Cody must acquire. Keith David (Barbershop) gets to play a stereotypically bellowing CIA chief. Ian McShane (Sexy Beast) and Arnold Vosloo (The Mummy) are menacing enough as the bad guys looking for world domination.

Confidence is not something that can be taught... although it can be learned. The more we work at something, the more confident we are that we will be successful. In turn, success breeds more confidence. Cody is quite confident when it comes to physical combat because of all his CIA training but his lack of experience with the opposite sex has left him with zero confidence in that category.

This principle can be applied to our spiritual understanding as well. The more we apply ourselves to understand spiritual matters, the more skillful and confident we become.

For everyone who continues to feed on milk is obviously inexperienced and unskilled in the doctrine of righteousness (of conformity to the divine will in purpose, thought, and action), for he is a mere infant (not able to talk yet)! But solid food is for full-grown men, for those whose senses and mental faculties are trained by practice to discriminate and distinguish between what is morally good and noble and what is evil and contrary either to divine or human law. Hebrews 5:13-14 (Amplified)

In other words, practice makes perfect. It is true regarding one's physical and social skills. It is also true regarding spiritual abilities and awareness.
