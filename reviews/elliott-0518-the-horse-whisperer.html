Author-verified transcription

Some guys look great in a cowboy hat. Robert Redford (Up Close and Personal) happens to be one of them. In The Horse Whisperer, based on the novel by Nicholas Evans, Mr. Redford has found a character which fits his screen persona perfectly. Tom Booker is the title character, a man who has a special method of handling horses. His gentle, patient, and no nonsense way works well on humans too, judging by the reaction he gets from those he encounters.

One such example is Annie McLean (Kristen Scott Thomas, The English Patient). She is a New York City magazine editor whose daughter Grace (Scarlett Johansson, Home Alone 3) was in a horrific accident while riding her horse, Pilgrim. Both the horse and daughter suffered severe injuries, physically as well as psychologically. Since Tom won't leave his ranch to see them in New York, Annie makes the decision to take the uncooperative Grace and Pilgrim to Montana in order to seek him out, leaving behind her career, her husband Robert (Sam Neill, Event Horizon) and the stagnant relationship which their marriage has become.

Mr. Redford's natural ease in front of the camera immediately allows us to trust Tom Booker's inherent goodness. We get the sense that as we watch either the character or the actor/director we will see a man secure in who is he and what he does. As director, his keen eye and appreciation for Montana's natural beauty makes The Horse Whisperer a visual treat.

Ms. Thomas offers a bit more of a complex personality. Annie presents a strong, confident image, both as a successful New York businesswoman and as an apparently successful wife and mother, but her character lacks the grounded center which would provide her with the stability and strength needed to make that image a reality. As a result, Annie is a woman on the edge, masking her fears and struggling to maintain control in the face of mounting pressures.

Mr. Neill gives a marvelously restrained performance as Annie's husband, Robert. While it is clear that he wants his marriage to work, he also realizes that he only counts for one half of the equation.

What more can be added to the topic of adultery? As Annie spends more time away from her husband and more time with the very attractive and sexy Tom Booker, it would be crazy not to expect there to be a physical attraction at play. Real marriages are not made of fairy tale fantasies. Within one's lifetime there will be a myriad of temptations and every reason imaginable not to honor the commitment one has made before God. Just remember, these temptations do not come from God our father. They originate from one hell-bent on destroying the things of God. Our comfort lies in knowing that we have been given the strength and power to resist all temptations of our spiritual enemy.

I found it interesting that when Annie made the decision to take her daughter (with horse in tow) to Montana to find a man she hadn't yet met, she could offer no logical explanation. Her reason: "I just know it's the right thing." That is not as lame an excuse as one might think. God has given to woman the spiritual responsibility of child rearing. He will work within a mother to guide her hand as she makes those decisions that will be the best for the child. I've learned to trust my wife's instincts when it comes to the raising of our two boys. Sometimes a decision cannot be explained to the senses mind, but that does not negate its spiritual validity.

Much of the film has to do with our ability to conquer our fears. You'll notice that I said conquer. Not befriend. Not get comfortable with. Not accept. Conquer. Fear is from the devil. It is the number one weapon in his arsenal against men. We have the spiritual ability and responsibility to conquer our fears. Through the grace of God and through our believing in what He has made available to us, we no longer have to be enslaved in the bondage that fear always brings. Within the context of this movie, perhaps the old cliché says it best: "When you fall off a horse, the first thing you need to do is to get right back on it."

If we allow fear to dictate our actions, we admit defeat before we even get started. Bravo to Mr. Redford and company for riding this horse all the way to the finish line.
