The Animal

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 1, 2001
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Genesis 1:25 Leviticus 18:23 Deuteronomy 27:21

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Creation and kinds; bestiality; species; evolution

Author-verified transcription

In as many years as I have been writing Movie Parables, exploring how we can see biblical truths reflected in the films of today, I never once thought it would be necessary to address the error of bestiality. But who am I to underestimate Hollywood? In The Animal, Rob Schneider (perhaps best known for his cameo appearances in Alan Sandler films) is shown comically attempting to seduce a goat. Before we proceed with the review, let the record show: Bestiality is bad... or should I say “baaa...aaa’d?”

Schneider plays a pathetic, asthmatic schnook named Marvin Mange who dreams of one day becoming a police officer... that is, if he could ever pass the physical tests. Until then he spends his days as a lowly file clerk in the evidence room. One day, when the entire police force is away playing softball (yes, these are the plot devices... it’s that kind of film), Marvin decides to answer a 911 call. He never makes it to the scene of the crime. Swerving to avoid a seal in the road (don’t ask) his car plunges off the side of a steep embankment.

He awakens to find fur on his fanny and a body full of heightened capabilities. He can swim like a fish, run like a stallion, and make love like a rabbit... OK, run like a rabbit and make love like a stallion.

We come to learn that some roadside quack (Michael Caton, The Castle) pulled him from the wreckage and using an experimental surgical procedure called “radical transpeciesectomy” replaced his damaged organs with animal parts.

His new and improved animal senses land Marvin a place on the police squad as a supercop and also get him a girlfriend (Colleen Haskell, TV’s Survivor). Now if only he could learn to harness those out-of-control animal instincts.

Schneider, who I admittedly never much cared for as a comic actor, does play the “Sad Sack” character quite well. Not brilliantly mind you, but adequately for the jokes to work and the story to make what little sense that it does. His costar, Colleen Haskell, is extending her fifteen minutes of fame which she garnered from her appearance on the first Survivor TV series. Here, she plays an animal lover and environmentalist who will take in strays if they have no place else to go. Haskell is at once sweet and lovable. Best of all, unlike most of the more experienced members of the cast, she doesn’t overact.

John C. McGinley (Any Given Sunday) makes up for that by mugging his way through the role of Doug Sisk, a macho cop who likes to wear his uniform three sizes too small and views Marvin as an embarrassment to the force and a threat to society.

Director Luke Greenfield, in his feature film debut, does his best to milk whatever comedic mileage Schneider and company manage to extract from what is essentially a one-joke movie. Whether licking food off Haskell’s face, licking his own private parts, or marking his territory by urinating on Haskell’s front door, Schneider’s beast-like behavior is mildly amusing, largely because he seems almost embarrassed to be doing it.

If the film sounds ridiculous, it is because it is founded on a ridiculous theory. One doesn’t become animalistic simply by drinking badger milk and having the heart of a lion (literally). One certainly doesn’t begin to have sexual longings for creatures outside of one’s own species. God designed the animal kingdom to fall within clearly defined and separate categories.

And God made the beast of the earth after his kind, and cattle after their kind, and every thing that creepeth upon the earth after his kind: and God saw that it was good. Genesis 1:25 (KJV)

The operative phrase in the above scripture is “after their (or his) kind.” The word “kind” is translated from the Greek word “genos.” There cannot be a mixture or evolution between species because each species must develop or evolve “after his kind.” All evolution can only take place within the parameters of each species. This is the scriptural documentation that refutes the evolutionist claims that man “evolved” from an ape or out of the primordial slime.
