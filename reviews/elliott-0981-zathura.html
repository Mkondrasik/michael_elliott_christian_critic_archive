Author-verified transcription

When two quarrelling brothers discover an old board game they soon find themselves in the middle of a life or death adventure where each “roll of the dice” causes a new danger to come their way. Sound familiar? It should. Author Chris Van Allsburg’s (The Polar Express) stories were the source for both the 1995 film Jumanji as well as the new release Zathura. The tales are remarkably similar but this time instead of jungle creatures stampeding through a suburban neighborhood, Zathura sends the players (along with their house) into orbit.

The only way to return home is to finish the game but each time they advance their pieces, they run the risk of landing on a square that will bring death or destruction upon their doorstep. From meteor showers and killer robots to an armada of spaceships manned by meat-eating lizards, the boys must learn to put their petty differences aside and work together to survive and play another day.

Their older sister (Kristen Stewart) isn’t much help. She has been frozen in cryogenic sleep since the second turn. They do get some assistance from a stranded astronaut (Dax Shepard) who seems to know an awful lot about the game.

Director Jon Favreau keeps the pace humming at a decent clip, incorporating a sibling rivalry against the special effects laden backdrop of a sci-fi adventure. There’s not a lot of plot and few surprises - just the anticipation of wondering what calamity will be next. His major flaw was in targeting the film for an older audience. The PG-13 rating is deserved and, more than likely, will be a disqualifier for many parents with children within the one age group that would have most enjoyed the story.

The young actors spend a lot of time screaming at one another which does get somewhat tiresome. And their roles are a bit one dimensional. The two boys vie for the attention of their divorced Dad (Tim Robbins) with the older (Josh Hutcherson) blaming his younger brother (Jonah Bobo) for ruining the family dynamic. Their teenage sister has long since disassociated herself from both her siblings.

It is this family story that holds the most spiritual meaning for us and continues to resonate long after the sci-fi effects cease to impress. The family bond is not easily broken but once done, is doubly difficult to restore.

A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city: and their contentions are like the bars of a castle. Proverbs 18:19 (KJV)

Fortunately, God has provided a way for quarrels and disagreements to be settled within a family environment before matters escalate.

Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother. Matthew 18:15 (KJV)

The key principle is that, as brothers, we are to forgive and find common ground upon which we may stand together. Any disagreements or arguments that may threaten to come between us should be placed aside. There is nothing on this earth that is more important or more valuable than the relationship we have with our brothers and sisters in Christ.
