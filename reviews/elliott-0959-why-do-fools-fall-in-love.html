Why Do Fools Fall In Love?

Author-verified transcription

Why Do Fools Fall In Love? That's not only the name of a new movie. It's not only the name of the 1955 hit song by Frankie Lymon and The Teenagers upon whom the movie is based. It can also be the question of Mr. Lymon's life.

Told in a series of flashbacks driven by a court hearing, Why Do Fools Fall In Love? traces Mr. Lymon's sudden rise to the heights of popularity and subsequent fall from grace. The movie is told from the perspective of his wives. That's wives (as in plural). In total, there are three of them. He just never got around to divorcing any of them. And now, they are all claiming rights to the late Mr. Lymon's estate. That's the reason for the court hearing.

Halle Berry (The Rich Man's Wife) plays Zola, a glamorous entertainer, who sang with The Platters. Frankie met her while on tour. Vivica A. Fox (Booty Call) plays Elizabeth, a sassy, street-wise woman from the 'hood who Frankie rescued from a department store cop who was about to arrest her for shoplifting. Lela Rochon (Waiting To Exhale) plays Elizabeth, a prim and proper Georgia schoolteacher who Frankie met while on leave from his military duty.

Larenz Tate (The Postman) offers a fine portrayal of Mr. Lymon, showing a great deal of charisma while on stage before hundreds of adoring fans. He also makes the darker side of Mr. Lymon's personality believable as Frankie, searching for the "high" he used to get from performing, turns to drugs and alcohol.

The movie, however, belongs to the ladies. This is their story... or more appropriately, stories.

Director Gregory Nava (Selena) sometimes lets the "emoting" go a bit over the top, but overall, he has crafted an enjoyable film with a sensational soundtrack.

It is always sad to see or hear about a wasted life. Hit song or no hit song, if this movie depicted just a fraction of the truth, Mr. Lymon wasted his life. To have that much talent, that much ability, and to be recognized for it at such a young age... You would think that any man would be satisfied with such a life. Yet, Mr. Lymon abandoned his friends, continually left his wives, taking what was most precious to them and destroying it.

Someone should have shared the scriptures with him. God details how a man is to care for his wife. Of all the analogies that God could have used in describing a marriage, He chose to compare a husband's love for his wife to Jesus Christ's love for his church.

Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish. So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. Ephesians 5:25-28 [KJV]

Fools have no business fall in love. In fact, in biblical terminology, they are incapable of it. Biblically speaking, a fool is someone who has rejected God. Psalms 14:1 [KJV] says:

The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God.

Love, in its purest form, is defined by God and only available via God:

And this is love, that we walk after His commandments 2 John 1:6 [KJV]

Therefore, biblically speaking, fools can't fall in love. Thus, yet another "imponderable" question that has puzzled man for ages can be answered to our complete satisfaction by turning to and believing the truth of God's Word.
