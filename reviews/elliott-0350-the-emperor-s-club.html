The Emperor's Club

Author-verified transcription

"The mediocre teacher tells. The good teacher explains. The superior teacher demonstrates. The great teacher inspires." -- William A. Ward

In The Emperor's Club, William Hundert (Kevin Kline, My Life as a House) is a great teacher. As the assistant headmaster and professor of Western Civilization at the St. Benedict Academy for Boys, he believes that the benefit associated with studying the classics lies in recognizing and adopting the values and principles which the great thinkers of the past exemplify. Such ethical and moral values define Hundert's existence and he strives to pass them on to the generations which follow him.

Teaching kids to count is fine, but teaching them what counts is best.-- Bob Talbert

His aim therefore is not only to fill young minds with knowledge, but also to help mold their characters and establish the standards by which they will live their lives. Reproving a student for walking on the grass, he teaches, "Walk on the path, young man, where great men have walked before you." When the student concurs because it is better for the grass, Hundert's curt reply is "No. It is better for you."

His is a noble endeavor but as a great sculptor needs pliable clay, great teachers need willing students. Into Hundert's life walks the disrespectful Sedgewick Bel (Emile Hirsch, The Dangerous Lives of Altar Boys) who is anything but willing or pliable.

Though Hundert sees potential in this obviously intelligent Visigoth (Hundert's description), Bell, the son of an influential senator, shows little interest in learning what his teacher has to offer. His ambivalence and lack of moral discipline prove to be somewhat infectious and much of the film deals with the conflict between Hundert and Bell as they vie for control of the class.

Kevin Kline is not only an accomplished actor, he's an intelligent one which allows him to spout philosophical platitudes and wax poetic without losing any audience appeal. His Hundert is neither insufferable nor pompous. He simply believes with all his heart that the life of a teacher is one that holds great importance. We cannot argue with that.

"A teacher affects eternity; he can never tell where his influence stops." - Henry Adams

Director Michael Hoffman (One Fine Day), working from a script by Neil Tolkin (Jury Duty), depicts an idealistic world of academia but the ideals are so lofty and so good that it is hard for me to find fault with that. The Emperor's Club boasts a strong central character and a solid supporting cast including Embeth Davidtz (Bicentennial Man), Harris Yulin (Training Day), Joel Gretsch (The Legend of Bagger Vance), Patrick Dempsey (Sweet Home Alabama), Steven Culp (Thirteen Days), and Edward Herrmann (The Cat's Meow).

Like Goodbye Mr. Chips, Mr. Holland's Opus, and Dead Poets' Society, all of which are fine films featuring a teacher as the central character, The Emperor's Club focuses upon the lasting impact a teacher can make upon his students... and vice versa.

Everyone, at some point or another, has had need of a teacher. How else is information passed from person to person and generation to generation? If there were no teachers, there would be no advancement, no intellectual growth, no opportunity to understand things beyond our own grasp. Those who want to learn and grow will recognize this very easily.

And Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest? And he said, How can I, except some man should guide me? And he desired Philip that he would come up and sit with him. Acts 8:30-31 (KJV)

The power unleashed by the dynamic combination of a student who is hungry to learn and a teacher who is willing to instruct is so great that it can change the world... one person at a time.
