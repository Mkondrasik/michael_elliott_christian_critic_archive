Analyze This

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 5, 1999
Rating
4 stars
Running time
1 hr: 43 min
Source
Published Web Version

Scripture references

Colossians 3:15Psalms 62:8Philippians 4:6

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Emotions; peace; anxiety; spiritual household; emotional balance

Author-verified transcription

A mob boss who cries like a baby over an insurance company's TV commercial? Fuggedabowdit! If da udder bosses hear about dis, da guy's a dead man. What he needs is a shrink!

He finds one in the entertaining new comedy Analyze This. Paul Vitti is the mob boss who begins experiencing bouts of anxiety or "panic attacks". He seeks out the psychiatric help of Dr. Ben Sobol with the one caveat: "If I turn fag, I'll kill you."

Their resulting "sessions" are hilarious. When Dr. Sobol explains what an Oedipal complex is, Vitti has a recognizably Italian machismo reaction. "Are you %$&#*% nuts?? Have you seen my mother?" When Sobol suggests that Vitti work out his aggression by "hitting" a pillow, Vitti pulls out a .45 and blows it to smithereens.

As directed by Harold Ramis (Groundhog Day), Analyze This is the funniest, most delightfully enjoyable comedy to hit the screen since Arthur. The screenplay by Peter Tolan (My Fellow Americans), Mr. Ramis, and Ken Lonergan (The Adventures of Rocky and Bulwinkle) delivers punchline after punchline, beautifully timed and executed by a very talented cast.

The pairing of Robert DeNiro (Ronin) and Billy Crystal (My Giant) may seem a bit odd on paper, but they work brilliantly together, playing off of one another to great comic effect.

Mr. DeNiro has, in the course of his career, been known to play a mobster or two (including the granddaddy of them all, The Godfather). Here, he is given an opportunity to parody many of his earlier roles which he does flawlessly. There is even a dream sequence which mirrors a scene from The Godfather in which Mr. DeNiro's character plays the role of Fredo, the weakest Corleone. When told the dream Vitti characteristically replies, "Me... Fredo? I don't think so."

Anyone aspiring to act in a comedy should study the work of Mr. DeNiro. Comedy is about more than pratfalls and rubber faces. Mr. DeNiro reminds us that while situations may be comedic, characters must always be real. His portrayal of Paul Vitti is fully believable which makes it side-splittingly funny.

Mr. Crystal plays the straight man (although he has his moments in the comedy sun) as the good doctor whose life and practice are turned upside down by this newest patient. Accustomed to dealing with sexually frustrated housewives, the situation with Vitti befuddles him. "What's my goal here? To make you a happy, well-adjusted gangster?" he asks with only a slight tint of sarcasm.

Lisa Kudrow (The Opposite of Sex) is very amusing as Sobol's frustrated fiancee whose wedding keeps getting interrupted by Vitti's urgent psychological needs. Joe Viterelli (Jane Austen's Mafia!) is a scream as Jelly, one of Vitti's henchman, a self-described moron who is fiercely loyal to his boss.

Analyze This is not a family film (no Mafia pun intended), largely due to the excessive language and mob related violence which liberally runs through the script. However, if your sensibilities can handle the obscenities, you're in for a rewarding theatergoing experience.

Emotions, when properly balanced, are a wonderful thing. They provide the seasoning of life. We would be a pretty bland bunch of people without them. But when allowed to get out of balance, as in the case of Paul Vitti, they can immobilize us.

And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which also ye are called in one body; and be ye thankful. Colossians 3:15 [KJV]

It is God's eternal peace that should rule and dominate in our hearts, not the ever changing wind of emotion. Staying a part of a spiritual household with whom we can share our whole heart helps to keep our proper emotional equilibrium. Otherwise, we may find ourselves needing the services of a Dr. Sobol faster than we can say "ba da bing, ba da boom, betty boop."
