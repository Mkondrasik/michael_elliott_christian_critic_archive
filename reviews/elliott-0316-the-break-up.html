The Break-Up

Sermon / spiritual topic: Relationships

Author-verified transcription

The Break-Up takes the genre of romantic comedies in a different direction - past the point when the couple discovers that "happily ever after" only works in fairy tales.

We meet Gary (Vince Vaughn) and Brooke (Jennifer Anniston) on the day of their first meeting and as the opening credits roll we see them in that blissful state of infatuation and love. Then the movie begins in earnest with the relationship turning sour.

Gary, along with his brothers, own and operate a tour bus company. Brooke works in a snooty art gallery. She's upper class. He's got no class at all. She wants to be appreciated. He wants to be left alone with his sports channels and video games. These two opposites, once mutually attracted, are now beginning to be repelled.

The problem: back in their sweetheart days, they bought a condo together and neither one wants to move out. Does anyone remember War of the Roses? Like that 1989 film, this is a dark and rather depressing "comedy" about two people who go out of their way to hurt the person they once loved.

Vaughn, whose rapid-fire delivery and normal Joe persona has made him a star, provides his fans with more of the same although he does seem to be swiftly outgrowing his familiar frat-boy personality.

Anniston plays it straight for the most part, the victim of Gary's insensitivity. It's a disappointing performance which flattens the film though she does have a few moments that remind us that she's capable of much more.

Perhaps because the characters they play aren't communicating all that well, we never really get the sense of any chemistry between Vaughn and Anniston. Far more believable is the relationship between Vaughn and longtime friend and collaborator Jon Favreau who plays Johnny O, one of Gary's Chicago buddies.

The biggest problem that faced director Peyton Reed seems to have been in deciding what kind of film to make. Should it be a comedy about two people who love each other but can't stand each other? Or should it be a serious examination about the problems of keeping a relationship together? He splits the difference by trying to capture the essence of both and fails to succeed at either.

The heart of the film involves an issue that men and women have been striving to resolve ever since God created them: How to make a loving relationship work.

Honest communication would be a big start. Brooke's desires do not match her actions and she expects Gary to know intuitively what she wants or needs from him. Gary is a selfish, self-centered oaf who does what he wants without much regard of the effect his actions have on those around him. Neither party has the proper attitude or mindset to strengthen their union.

God lays it all out for us in the Bible. He is the one who designed the male/female relationship - perhaps if we adhere to His original plan, we'd receive the benefits and blessings He intended the relationship to bring.

Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband. Ephesians 5:33 (KJV)

When the wife respects her husband's role as head of the house and the husband loves his wife with the same selfless love as Christ loved the church, a strong foundation is built upon which a marriage can stand forever. It is when we focus on what we "get" from a relationship rather than on what we can "give" that we stand on shaky ground making it impossible to build anything of lasting value.
