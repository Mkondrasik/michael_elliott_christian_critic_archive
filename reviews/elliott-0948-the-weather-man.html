The Weather Man

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 28, 2005
Rating
2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 35 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Philippians 4:12-132 Corinthians 6:4-102 Corinthians 12:9-10

Sermon / spiritual topic: Victim Mentality; Mental Attitude; Contentment

Author-verified transcription

To Dave Spritz (Nicolas Cage), happiness is as elusive as predicting a cloudless day. And he should know... he's The Weather Man.

A local TV weather reporting celebrity whose star is on the rise (despite the fact that he holds no degree in meteorology), Dave's career success has not carried over to his personal life. His father (Michael Caine), an ailing Pulitzer Prize winning author, is disappointed in him; his ex-wife (Hope Davis) hates him; his daughter (Gemmenne de la Pena) is overweight and depressed; his son (Nicholas Hoult) has been forced into a counseling program and into the clutches of a possible pedophile. Only his role as a Chicago TV weatherman seems to be working out for him... except for the fact that his "fans" have a habit of throwing fast food at him whenever they spot him on the street.

Director Gore Verbinski (Pirates of the Caribbean) directs Cage and the rest of the cast as if they're all overmedicated. Sluggish, almost lifeless, they slog their way through a script by Steve Conrad (Wrestling Ernest Hemingway) which is ostensibly about one man's search for himself. Frankly, it doesn't take long for us to realize that there isn't much to find.

Cage gives an adequate portrayal of a man who is lost - and knows he is lost - but is unsure of how he came to be this way or how to correct it. The problem with The Weather Man is not Cage's performance, it's the fact that his character is being used as the film's anchor and seeing as Spritz is not really grounded or rooted in anything... neither is the movie.

The apparent message seems to be the same profane catch phrase that Forrest Gump was once credited with coining. The G rated version is: "Stuff happens." The film seems to be saying that since "stuff" happens in life, we have to suck it up and let it happen. After all, no one said life was going to be perfect.

All this may be true - life isn't perfect and "stuff" does indeed happen. But I'm uncomfortable with the film's implication that we are all victims to life's unexpected and often unwanted surprises.

We may not be always able to control what happens to us as we go through this world but we can always control how we respond to it. That is quite a different thing than simply sucking up and accepting whatever happens.

I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me. Philippians 4:12-13 (KJV)

In his letter to the Philippians, Paul is not adopting a victim's mentality of merely accepting trials and tribulations. He is boldly stating that he can handle the trials and tribulations of life because he does not allow himself to be dictated to or controlled by outward circumstances. He is driven and fueled by an inner strength - the strength of God in Christ in him.

Whether or life is perfect or imperfect is immaterial. The important thing to remember is that we have perfection within us. Regardless of what life's situations may be, we can respond to them with the power of Christ and the love of God.
