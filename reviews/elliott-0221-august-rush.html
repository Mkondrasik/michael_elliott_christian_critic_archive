August Rush

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 21, 2007
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Body, Soul, Spirit

Author-verified transcription

The fear of separation or isolation is a common one, and thus is a common theme in movies. August Rush traces a young orphan's unrelenting quest to find the parents he has never met by reaching out to them in song.

A chance encounter leads to a one night stand between a classically trained cellist (Keri Russell) and a budding rock star (Jonathan Rhys Davies). Though they are kept separated, their brief union does result in a baby boy which, implausibly enough, neither knows exists.

When the boy (Freddie Highmore), who (also implausibly) has never been adopted, grows to be eleven, he decides to leave the orphanage in whose care he was placed. He's following the music he hears in his head, certain that it will lead him to the parents he believes exist and still want him.

That music leads him to New York City and a Fagin-like street musician named Wizard (Robin Williams) who takes the boy firmly under his wing so as to exploit his unexplainable musical talents.

Meanwhile, his parents have been floundering in their lives because they feel the absence of something they can't define. The cellist is now unhappily teaching music in Chicago and the rock star leads an empty corporate life in LA. But the music draws them back to New York for the inevitable reunion.

And inevitable it is. There's nothing in the script that doesn't deviate from the screenwriting 101 playbook. Boy meets girl... Boy loses girl... Offspring conducts a concert in Central Park...

The story is at its best when focused upon the adventures of young Evan Taylor (nicknamed August Rush). Freddie Highmore is fine in a role that just a few years back would have been played by Haley Joel Osment. He has a nice, likeable presence and infectious smile.

Jonathan Rhys Davies also does some good work as a tortured soul who gives up his dream of music in order to prosper in this world only to find no peace in his decision. Keri Russell, who was so perfect in this year's Waitress, is wasted here in an undeveloped role. She seems to have been directed to forever stare wistfully off into the distance.

Robin Williams has the showiest performance which he overplays. He gets the larceny aspect of his Oliver Twist inspired character but lacks the heart beneath the heartless veneer that is so needed to complete the performance.

The three main characters in the film all feel a sense of loss that they cannot fully explain. It is a feeling that every human being on the planet experiences at one time or another because of the way we were originally designed.

When we are born we are not fully complete. There's a missing component that has been omitted ever since the first man was made. We may appear to be complete with a functioning body and soul but our spirit - that connection with God - was removed from the human element when Adam fell from grace. This missing connection leads to a kind of hunger... a recognition that something is lacking in our lives. As a result, most of us begin personal quests - seeking what it is that we intuitively sense is missing.

God's plan for man calls for a spiritual reconciliation between us. Jesus Christ is his answer - the way to satisfy our hunger and make us completely, completely, complete.

And ye are complete in him, which is the head of all principality and power. Colossians 2:10 (KJV)
