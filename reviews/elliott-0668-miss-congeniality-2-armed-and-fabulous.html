Author-verified transcription

America's favorite snorting beauty queen is back as FBI agent Gracie Hart returns to the catwalk in Miss Congeniality 2: Armed and Fabulous.

Hart (Sandra Bullock) had to retire from active field duty because her fame and popularity made her useless as an undercover agent. She's given a choice: get comfortable pushing papers behind a desk or become the "face of the FBI" as a PR spokesperson. She chooses the latter and accompanied by a queer guy for a straight gal style consultant (Diedrich Bader), hair and makeup assistants, and a personal bodyguard (Regina King) none too happy about her charge. Hart glams her way across the US of A to put a positive spin on the image of the Bureau.

When Cheryl, the reigning Miss United States, is kidnapped along with the pageant's host (William Shatner), Hart decides to ignore orders and reassign herself so that she might save her friend. Her decision forces her bodyguard Sam to become her reluctant partner.

The gimmick of the original film was to take a rough and tumble gal of action and transform her to a beauty queen for the purpose of uncovering a sinister plan. The sequel tries to put a backward spin on the premise by having Hart turn into the "Barbie doll" agent she was pretending to be in the first film then taking her back through the steps of rediscovering her true self through the course of the film. It doesn't work nearly as well.

Bullock falls into the same sequel trap that has bested far better actors. Instead of working to develop a strongly identifiable character, she simply coasts on the strength of her work in the original film. As a result, much of the humor plays flat and lifeless.

Regina King, playing a tough, no nonsense woman with anger management problems, is Bullock's counterpoint. She fills the role nicely but is not in the position of carrying the film or providing the humor.

Supporting cast members William Shatner and Heather Burns are given precious little screen time but try to make the most of what they have. Diedrich Bader, as a flamboyant makeover artist, has the most opportunity to steal a scene or two but delivers such a stereotypical one note performance that he is quickly forgotten.

Screenwriter Marc Lawrence has churned out an uninspired story where situational humor reigns despite the complete lack of sense or logic in getting the characters in those situations in the first place.

Fame and celebrity is a two-edged sword that can be as much of a hindrance as it is an aid. Certainly, in Gracie's case, it is an unwanted stumbling block that keeps her from doing the job she loves to do. When she eventually embraced the celebrity status that was offered her, she changed into the kind of egocentric person she vowed never to become.

That's not to say that fame is always a bad or negative thing. Certainly the news of Jesus Christ was spread across the land during his time causing multitudes to come to his side during his lifetime on earth. The difference is in how one handles the temptations and trappings of fame. It is why meekness and humility are such important qualities to master. Success will always breed a certain amount of fame. But the minute we let that fame go to our heads is when the successes will begin to end.

Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour humility. Proverbs 18:12 (KJV)
