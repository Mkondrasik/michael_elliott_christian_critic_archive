xXx: State of the Union

Author-verified transcription

If The Interpreter represents the brainy side of the action thriller genre, xXx: State of the Union is its polar opposite. This film is for people who like their movies explosive, loud and dumb.

You might remember the first xXx, a largely forgettable vehicle for Vin Diesel who played an extreme sports enthusiast tapped by the government for a "special mission." Diesel bowed out of the sequel (we're told that his character was killed in Bora Bora) which opened the door for a new xXx to be chosen.

This time, NSA Agent Gibbons (Samuel L. Jackson) looks to go further "off the grid" to find someone "more dangerous" with "more attitude." Enter Darius Stone (Ice Cube). We know he fits the bill because he's been shackled and tossed into a maximum security prison. Besides that, he's got one killer sneer.

The NSA is recovering from a professionally mounted attack on one of their facilities that left 16 agents dead. Stone's mission, should he choose to accept it, is to track down the source of the attack and find out who is behind it and why. As luck would have it, it's the same person responsible for him spending nine years behind bars.

xXx is filled with impossible stunts, improbable situations and cheesy one-liners. When questioned why he didn't serve his full 20 year sentence, Stone replies. "They let me go early for bad behavior." When warned that he's the prime suspect for a dastardly crime, Stone is unfazed: "I was born looking guilty." And when told his code name would be triple X, he candidly replies "Sounds like a porn star" which, come to think of it, was the same complaint many critics made after the first film was released.

Whatever entertainment can be had from this spewed out product from the Hollywood machine should be credited to Ice Cube. He adopts the proper attitude, plasters on an appropriate scowl, and looks good throwing punches. Hopefully, if this franchise continues, he'll follow the lead set by Diesel and have his character contract some fatal illness while kayaking in the Amazon.

Willem Dafoe and Samuel L. Jackson pick up what I would expect to be a decent paycheck for the use of their famous visages while Sunny Mabrey and Nona M. Gaye provide the cleavage to keep the male interest hooked whenever something is not blowing up.

Dafoe's character utters the ill-fated words when told that Stone is threatening to upset his plans. "What can one man do?" It was meant to be a rhetorical question implying a negative response. But let's catalogue what one man did...

He healed the sick. He raised the dead. He made the lame walk. He made the blind see. He fulfilled the law in all its components and set men spiritually free from the bondage that had lasted for thousands of years.

There is no limit to what one man can do when he is backed by the power of God.

One man of you shall chase a thousand: for the LORD your God, he it is that fighteth for you, as he hath promised you. Joshua 23:10 (KJV)
