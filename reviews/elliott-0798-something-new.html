Author-verified transcription

There's nothing new about Something New. It's just another silly romantic comedy where the audience is smarter and more mature than any of the on screen characters. At least, one would hope that is true.

The idea for the screenplay was born from a magazine article which cited a statistic stating that 42.4 percent of all black women have never been married. That's a scary stat... especially if you happen to be a single black woman who has placed her romantic life on hold in pursuit of a career. Such is the case with Kenya McQueen (Sanaa Latham) an accountant on track to make partner at her firm but without a partner to celebrate her success.

Suddenly a man shows interest. He's sensitive; he's attractive, he's gainfully employed. Too bad he's the white guy landscaping her backyard (Simon Baker). While she eventually gives into his charms (or perhaps it's the way his biceps ripple while planting bouganvillas) she doesn't feel quite right about it and tries to keep her interracial fling a secret. As if.

Her parents (Alfre Woodward, Earl Billings) are part of the black upper class. They still attend cotillions and expect their daughter to marry up - into an elevated or equal societal plane - albeit one that doesn't cross the color barrier.

Her friends, also single black female professionals, abide by the philosophy, "Let it go; let it flow," which seems to mean "if it feels good do it..." just so long as it doesn't involve dating outside of one's race.

Complicating matters is the arrival of Kenya's IBM (ideal black male), played by Blair Underwood a professional black man who connects with Kenya on every level except one - the romantic one. Kenya will have to choose between the love she feels and the love she thinks she should feel.

The superficiality of the screenplay by Kriss Turner is mirrored by the one dimensional quality of the characters. Mother = ambitious and judgmental; Father = quiet and understanding; Girlfriend #1 = smart and sassy; Girlfriend #2 = sassy and stupid; etc... They all play their roles, collect their paychecks, and quickly fade from memory.

Certainly interracial relationships are not without challenges but Something New fails to dig deeply into them. It simply settles for a surface treatment where Kenya's awkwardness over mixed race dating is the chief obstacle to overcome.

The film does manage to illustrate how important it is for a couple to be able to fully share with one another. The analogy God uses to describe a fully committed relationship (ie. marriage) is "one flesh." The husband and wife become so close (in purpose and in believing) that they might as well be one person.

The same strength can be shared by those who join the "one flesh" of the body of Christ. As we renew our minds (our thoughts and believing) to the Word of God, we enter into fellowship with God and with one another. Fellowship is translated from a word that means to make common. Despite our differences (our varied cultures, backgrounds, races, etc...) it is available to fully share with one another through the renewing of our minds.

Fulfil ye my joy, that ye be likeminded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind. Philippians 2:2 (KJV)
