Author-verified transcription

Her, the latest film from the imaginative mind of Spike Jonze, is not just another love story. It is a sad and telling commentary on loneliness, isolation, and the false comfort we derive from an overdependance on technology.

Set in an undisclosed future that cannot be too distant from us, Theodore (Joaquin Phoenix) is a professional letter writer for people who can’t be bothered to take the time to be intimate but still want to keep up the appearance of intimacy. Moping around after a painful divorce, Theodore is one of the first to try out a new computer operating system (OS) that is designed to learn and develop itself throughout the span of the continued use of its owner.

Immediately, Theodore is struck with how lifelike his OS (who names herself Samantha) appears to be. Intelligent, funny, and oddly self-aware, this detached voice (Scarlett Johansson) is the most “human” contact Theodore has managed in the past year. It doesn’t take too long for things to get weird.

Joaquin Phoenix does an admirable job playing a rather unhealthy and uncomplimentary character. Theodore is both sad and miserable. He seems to be a genuinely nice guy who appears to be stunted when it comes to human interaction. Phoenix somehow shows his weaknesses and vulnerability without so much as a wink or nod to the audience while simultaneously maintaining the sympathy we feel towards him.

Scarlett Johansson, as the sexy voice on the other end of his computer link, adequately conveys the “humanity” of the OS and takes us through an emotional range without the benefit of our ever seeing her. However, the construct of the film puts her at an extreme disadvantage and I never quite bought into the concept of her being a product of microchips or advanced programming.

Theodore’s loneliness, though silently suffered, speaks volumes and explains so much about his actions and how he comes to many of the decisions that he makes. We, as God’s creations, are not designed to be alone. We crave companionship. We desire fellowship. We look for friendship. We need love. We are not complete without it.

“The Lord God said, “It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a helper suitable for him.” Genesis 2:18 (NIV)

God, after creating Adam, acknowledged that man was going to require human companionship. Our friends, families, and loved ones help us to become the best we have it within us to be. Alone, we are limited in both our understanding and potential. It is when we have others supporting us, encouraging us, and challenging us that we find the drive to reach greater heights and accomplish greater things.
