Author-verified transcription

In the small town of Mystery, Alask, hockey is not just a pastime, it is a way of life. Sex seems to be the pastime. TV Producer David E. Kelley (Ally McBeal) and Austin Powers' director Jay Roach team up to bring us an R-rated version of The Mighty Ducks, using adult players instead of kids.

In the remote and environmentally harsh town of Mystery (it's a mystery why people would choose to live there), hockey is such an integral part of the residents' lives that they've elevated it to a near-religion status. Because there are no neighboring teams to speak of, the best players (as decided by the city council) play each other every Saturday as a way to hone their skills. Playing in The Saturday Game is a benchmark of success and the highlight of every Mystery man's life.

Former resident Charles Danner (Hank Azaria, Godzilla) who left Mystery for the relative warmth of New York City and the offices of Sports Illustrated, has written an article about The Saturday Game which has caught the fancy of the public. The PR mavens of the NHL decide to pit these "boys of winter" against a real team... The New York Rangers.

For a town that identifies itself by how well its hockey is played, such a game could have disastrous effects. As Judge Burns (Burt Reynolds, Boogie Nights) says in the town meeting in which the game was discussed, "We have always had two things in Mystery... Our dignity and our illusions. I suggest we cling to both."

As one might expect, the judge's advice is ignored and the game is scheduled. It's a good thing too because the whole movie builds to it. It just does so in the most over-sexed way imaginable. The mayor's wife (Lolita Davidovich, Play It To The Bone) is having an affair with one of the players. The judge's daughter (Rachel Wilson, Anywhere But Here) wants to have sex with another one. The team captain (Russell Crowe, LA Confidential) is jealous because his wife (Mary McCormack, Private Parts) used to have sex with the reporter, Danner. It's amazing that they have the time or energy to play any hockey.

Mr. Crowe gives a nice, subtle performance as the sheriff/hockey team captain. A couple of steps slower than the younger players with whom he plays, it is only a matter of time before he must step off the ice and out of The Saturday Game. Burt Reynolds also offers an understated performance as the severely sober judge who, though he never played in the town's premiere game, knows enough about the sport to coach them against the professionals.

The framework of the script is one that could have been used to make an inspiring family-oriented tale of sportsmanship, teamwork, overcoming stronger opponents, or a number of other worthy messages. Why Mr. Kelly and Sean O'Byrne felt the need to sprinkle their screenplay with enough profanity and sexual situations to garner its well-deserved R rating is beyond me. They could have attracted a much larger audience for this sports tale among the teen and preteen age group, but the heavy slant towards adult-oriented material make it inappropriate for younger viewers.

It's a pity, because sports films do offer many opportunities to engage in a spiritually minded discussion. Our spiritual walk can be likened to a competition against a strong opponent. As one of the players learns during the course of the movie, trying to do it all by oneself, does not lead to victory. There is a reason hockey is a team sport. It is available to excel individually, but only within the framework of the team environment.

Likewise, God set us in a family, or more appropriately, a household, for a reason. Our Christian walk is an individual (and daily) decision, but as we stand, in fellowship, as a unified "team" or "body", our strength is indeed multiplied.

That there should be no schism in the body; but that the members should have the same care one for another. And whether one member suffer, all the members suffer with it; or one member be honoured, all the members rejoice with it. Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular. 1 Corinthians 12:25-27 (KJV)
