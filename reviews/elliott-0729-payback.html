Author-verified transcription

To paraphrase a popular saying, "Paybacks are hard"... but they are no harder than the movie Payback which opens in theaters this Friday. Directed and co-written by Brian Helgeland (L.A. Confidential), Payback has the gritty feel of a 1950's dime store novel.

The dialogue ("Hubba, hubba"), the Mickey Spillanesque narration, and the overly simplistic plot are all borrowed from an earlier age. Even the look of the film resembled a black and white picture more than the technicolor movies of today. Steely grays and metallic blues were the directorial hue of choice which, of course, added to the hardened finish of the film.

Mel Gibson (Ransom) is Porter, a thief with a strange sense of justice. After a successful heist, his partner, Val Resnick (Gregg Henry, Star Trek: Insurrection) and his wife (Deborah Kara Unger, The Game) double cross him, taking the $70,000 which was his share of the cut, and leave him for dead.

He doesn't stay dead for long. Pretty soon, he's up and out for vengeance. The fact that "his" $70,000 was used to pay a huge criminal organization simply known as "The Outfit" doesn't bother him. Somebody is going to pay him the money, even if he has to kill everybody along the way.

Mr. Gibson relies on his charm and quirky sense of humor to make Porter work. After akkm thus us bit a nice guy that he's playing. Porter has no problem in killing without justification or provocation. He's a thief and a liar to the nth degree. The only reason the audience can root for him is because every other character in the movie is even worse.

Gregg Henry plays Val, an opportunistic sadist without any moral code whatsoever. Lucy lie (TV's Ally McBeal) plays a sadomasochistic dominatrix named Pearl who amazingly became an audience favorite as she "lovingly" administered backhands, groin kicks, and nipple twists to the various men who paid her for that abuse.

William Devane (Missles of October), James Coburn (The Nutty Professor), and Kris Kristofferson (Blade), all play high-ranking members of the crime cartel. Their overly casual amusement of the effrontery of Porter who dares to hold them at gun point is humorously incongruous. Maria Bello (TV's ER) is Rosie, a high priced whore with a soft spot for Porter who used to drive her between tricks.

In case you've lost track of who the movie's "good guys" are: Porter (thief, killer) is a good guy. As is Rosie (prostitute). Pearl (sadist, masochist, prostitute) is sometimes good, sometimes bad, depending upon whom she's beating up at the moment. Everyone else (including the crooked cops) are the bad guys.

There has got to be something wrong with a story whose "good guy" is not judged by his own actions but is rather comparatively judged by the actions of those around him. It is as if the filmmakers want us to say, "Sure, Porter's a killer... but at least he's not crazy like Val. He just wants his money." (Like we're supposed to forget that he stole the money in the first place?)

You can be assured that God will not be making such comparisons. Each of us will be judged according to the actions we take while on this earth.

So then every one of us shall give account of himself to God. Romans 14:12 [KJV]

It doesn't matter how accomplished a liar we may have been in the past, there is no lying to God. He knows our hearts... He has seen our actions... And He will reward each one of us according to our deeds.

For we must all appear before the judgment seat [bema] of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad. 2 Corinthians 5:10 [KJV]

Today, we have the ability to store up rewards that will last throughout eternity. It merely takes the discipline to live our lives according to the standards set by God. It is sometimes hard, resisting the temptations of this world, but it is always worth it. At the bema, we'll receive God's abundantly wonderful "payback" for the work we did in His name.
