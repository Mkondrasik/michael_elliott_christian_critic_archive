The DaVinci Code

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 19, 2006
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Truth

Author-verified transcription

Isn’t it ironic that the world’s latest literary blockbuster has become the world’s latest cinematic bust? The DaVinci Code fails in just about every way that its source material succeeded.

Weighed down by its own ponderous self-importance, the movie slogs solemnly along its 2 ½ hour running time proving once again that an accomplished director, an award winning screenwriter, a great cast, and a controversial subject adapted from a bestselling novel are no guarantee for movie magic.

Dan Brown’s entertaining read of fiction seemed a natural for a screen adaptation. The action flowed effortlessly from page to page and from chapter to chapter, causing many a sleepless night as readers around the world found it difficult to put down. Surprisingly director Ron Howard completely misses the mark, managing to turn an exciting, page-turning adventure into a complete bore.

The film carries none of the intrigue, mystery or excitement factor of the book. The story itself is unchanged. Symbologist/author Robert Langdon (Tom Hanks) is summoned to the Louvre, now the site of a bizarre murder, unsuspecting that he is the chief suspect. He escapes capture thanks to the interference of Sophie Neveu (Audrey Tautou), a police cryptologist. Trying to stay one step in front of the police, they set out in pursuit of the truth, attempting to solve a mystery that has remained hidden for 2000 years. It is a mystery involving secret societies, religious art, famous historical personalities, and cryptic messages that, if solved and made public, would shake the very foundation of Christianity.

Tom Hanks, as consistent a presence as there is in Hollywood, turns in an uncommonly weak performance. He’s unfocused, uncentered, and uninteresting as a character. Audrey Tautou does better - as does Ian McKellan as Sir Leigh Teabing, an eccentric scholar and expert in the history of the Holy Grail. Paul Bettany turns in a chilling performance as Silas, an albino monk turned executioner to “protect” the church.

The controversy deals with the secret that Langdon and Neveu eventually uncover – that the Holy Grail is not the cup of Christ but rather the bloodline of Christ. The story contends that Jesus Christ and Mary Magdalene were married and had a daughter who in turn produced offspring and the genetic line continues to exist into our day.

The book has spawned an absurd amount of attention as people lined up to embrace or refute the “facts” as presented by Dan Brown. Enough evidence has been brought forth to expose Brown’s “facts” as mere suppositions and flights of fancy. They work well as the spine of a mystery thriller but fail to hold water when examined from a historical or ecumenical perspective.

Whatever danger exists is found in the possibility that people will view The DaVinci Code as being based in truth. The message being delivered by Howard, Goldsman and company is summed up by Robert Langdon who says, “The only thing that matters is what you believe” (i.e., truth is what you make it). That, my friends, is a lie.

Truth does exist but we had no part in the making of it. We are to seek it, study it, and hold it as a treasure but should not make the mistake of thinking that whatever we believe is true. People believe all sorts of things to be true - many of them are wrong and ultimately harmful. Nothing is more harmful than to be wrong in what we believe regarding Jesus Christ.

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. John 14:6 (KJV)
