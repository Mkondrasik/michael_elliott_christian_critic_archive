Centro do Brasil [Central Station]

4 stars

Author-verified transcription

Add Central Station to the list of accomplishments for Academy Award winning producer Arthur Cohn (The Garden of the Finzi Continis). This moving story, artfully photographed and beautifully told, follows the unusual relationship between a old and embittered woman and the young boy who slowly works his way into her heart.

Dora spends her day in a busy train station of Rio de Janeiro, hiring herself out as a letter writer for the illiterate masses. Afterwards, she will take the letters home and sort through them... making fun of them... sending a few on to their destinations... destroying most of them.

It was at Central Station where she first meets Josue. Ana, his mother, pays Dora to write a letter to Josue’s father, a man whom he has never met. When Ana is killed in a bus accident, Josue is abandoned at the station.

Dora, calculating how she can use this to her advantage, befriends Josue... just long enough to sell him to the black market, supposedly earmarked for a foreign adoption. Through a series of events, Dora and Josue must flee Rio. And so they set off in search of Josue’s drunken and wayward father.

Director Walter Salles Jr. (Midnight) has taken a predictably familiar story line and infused it with such love and tenderness that it is a real joy to watch. The instrumental music score, which is perfectly conceived, helps to lay an emotional foundation upon which each scene is built. I’d be hard pressed to select another film this year whose score was as effective.

Fernanda Montenegro (Four Days In September) is captivating as Dora. Here is a woman who has not lost her humanity as much as she has purposely set it aside in order to survive the harsh and brutally unforgiving environment in which she lives.

Life in Brazil apparently can be hard, requiring each individual to look after themselves, first and foremost. When a train pulls into the station, a stream of people start pouring into the cab from the windows, long before the doors open, just to get themselves a seat. It’s every man (or woman) for himself.

Living in such an environment, Dora has, by necessity, closed off her heart, not allowing herself to be touched by the letters that are dictated to her. It takes a young boy in need of a friend to open it again.

Vinicius de Oliveira plays the determined young Josue with just the right touch of Brazilian male bravado underscored with the quite natural fear of a child. He makes as much an impression on us as his character makes on Dora.

Their relationship is the heart and soul of this movie. Each of them have something to give to the other. He reawakens in her a humanity and love that she forgot could exist. She gives to him a security and hope that he desperately needed.

After fleeing Rio in a taxi, then taking a bus to their next destination, Josue remarks that he prefers the taxi. Dora teaches him that the bus is better because it has a route. You always know where you are on a bus. On a taxi, it is too easy to get lost.

We may use that same analogy in a spiritual context. The Word of God is the bus. It has a route that, when taken, guarantees that one will reach one’s desired destination. At any time we can, of our own free will, get off the bus -- but it just makes more sense to stay on until we get to where we want to go.

The world’s way is the taxi. It may move faster but because it has no route to follow, it is easier to get lost. You never know if you are getting closer to your destination or if you’ll ever reach it at all. It is easier to be cheated and misled. The ultimate cost of traveling this way is also higher. Most of us have learned that by experience.

Wherein in time past ye walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience: Ephesians 2:2 [KJV]

We don’t have to go the world’s way. We can get on the bus that is the Word of God, staying on a proper and clearly marked route, completely assured that we will reach our destination where God Himself awaits.
