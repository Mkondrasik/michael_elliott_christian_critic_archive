Author-verified transcription

Anyone who has stayed around a ballpark long enough has certainly learned that umpires are blind… at least if we’re to believe the shouts from the crowd. Trouble With The Curve puts a spin on the familiar boo chant to tell the story of an aging baseball scout struggling to hold onto his job despite his failing eyesight and old school ways.

Clint Eastwood stars as Gus, a grumpy old man who happens to be a legendary scout for the Atlanta Braves. When a brash up and comer (Matthew Lillard) suggests that Gus is past his prime, Pete, Gus’s long time friend and front office ally (John Goodman), goes to bat for him one last time. Gus is given the job to scout a minor league player rumored to be the real deal. Problem is, Gus is having trouble keeping his eye on the ball… or anything else for that matter.

Sensing his friend is having some trouble, Pete enlists Gus’s daughter Mickey (Amy Adams) to stay close and help him through this assignment. Though their relationship has always been a bit rocky, she agrees and is soon sitting alongside her none-too-happy pop, somehow watching the game while keeping up with a stream of texts to and from her law office. “It’s called multi-tasking,” she explains. Together, they must figure out if the player they are there to see is worth a very valuable first round pick.

We learn that the friction between Gus and Mickey stems from a decision that Gus made early on in their relationship. It was a decision that Gus truly believed was a good thing for Mickey but, because it wasn’t well communicated, it had a devastating effect on the young girl.

It is something to bear in mind. Oftentimes, when we think we have found a good solution to a problem, God can always do us one better. Instead of settling for what is good, God wants us to reach for what is best. There is a big difference.

“Now unto him that is able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think, according to the power that worketh in us.” Ephesians 3:20 (KJV)

The “curve balls” that life will throw at us can often trip us up and lead us to unwise or unprofitable decisions...decisions that would have us settling for less than the best. God has something better in mind. He pays no attention to the curveballs of life. His is a straight and narrow path that leads to victory, salvation, and deliverance. Why should we trust in anything else?
