Author-verified transcription

Sometime in August, another entry in The 1999 Florida Film Festival, is something of a disappointment. Disappointing because, early on, it shows promise. There's just enough of a hint... a shadow of some special quality that might surface at any moment, that we stay with the film and its characters. Suddenly, more than halfway through the film, it inexplicably changes direction and loses all that worked for it up to that point. Whatever special quality that might have been remained hidden and unrealized.

Sylva Kelegian plays Marcia, an over thirty nude model who is trying to recover from a broken relationship she obviously didn't want to have end. For a reason that is not completely explained or communicated, she adopts a false persona (Greta) and hires an unconventional private investigator/drunk/poet named Igor (Michael Medeiros, Drunks) to follow her (Marcia) and report back to her (Greta) on her (Marcia's) actions. When he returns with a rather bland report, she begins to remake herself to become more interesting.

As Igor continues to follow her, he becomes intrigued and starts to remake himself as well in order to become more attractive for her. This playful cat and mouse flirtation has its moments of sweetness that could have been developed into something quite delightful.

I regret to say that writer/director Caio Ribeiro, in his feature film debut, had other plans for his film. Three quarters of the way through the movie, he has his characters engage in some kind of weird word association exercise which eventually leads to decisions which were unsupported by anything seen previously and uncharacteristic for the personalities we have come to know.

Sylva Kelegian has some humorous moments as Greta/Marcia but overall, her character lacks the depth to make her internal struggles believable.

Michael Medeiros effectively plays a strong, sensitive but shy poet who surprises himself by falling in love. Of the pair, his characterization is more fully realized although he too has a rough time making the ending believable given the odd script and directorial choices which were imposed upon him.

Both actors were required to do blatantly gratuitous nude scenes. There's no rationalization for them as they neither furthered the plot nor added to our understanding of these characters. To submit the actors and audience to these needless scenes was merely a poor directorial decision.

Mr. Ribeiro also tries to make his odd philosophic points via unexplained abstract characters: a small boy, a dying woman, a roommate with a bandaged arm... These obscure and esoteric references are intrusive to the main story and relationship between the lead characters. Poorly conceived and executed, they serve only to distract the audience. Perhaps had Mr. Ribeiro stayed true to the relationship which he helped to build between his characters, along with the question of how and why one loves, the film may have been more satisfying.

In the course of the flirtation, Igor says to Marcia, "Every day that man does not love, it is a betrayal of the whole universe... It is a betrayal of God." There is a lot of truth in that statement. God has given us many commandments in His Word. "Let he who stole, steal no more," "Children, obey your parents in the Lord," and "Be ye thankful," to name just a few.

To hear and ignore such commandments is to, in effect, disobey God. To love, with the heart and mind of God, is the highest aspiration to which man can strive to attain.

Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another. [1 John 4:11 KJV]

Love is an aspiration God commands us to have. To not love... is to not love God.

For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: [1 John 5:3 KJV]

Perhaps it is this kind of love that was the hint of that special quality that this production danced around without ever embracing. If so, it explains the rather hopeless feeling at the end of the movie. The world can be a pretty dismal place if one chooses to live without accepting the love of God.
