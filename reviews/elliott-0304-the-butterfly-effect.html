The Butterfly Effect

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 23, 2004
Rating
2 stars

1 hr: 53 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Change; present choices; habits; renewing the mind

Author-verified transcription

Every action has a consequence. According to "the chaos theory," a butterfly flapping its wings can start a chain reaction that will result in a tidal wave halfway across the world. So says the theory anyway.

In The Butterfly Effect, a man finds himself in a position to prove that theory true.

Evan Treborn (Ashton Kutcher) hasn't had an easy life. Ever since he was a child, there would be times when he would just black out. These events, of which he had no memory, generally occurred during times of high stress. Entering into therapy, Evan was encouraged to keep journals in the hopes that he would begin to recall that which his psyche tried to repress.

We jump forward in time and Evan is now a college student studying psychology. By revisiting his old journals he surprisingly finds that, through them, he is able to somehow transport himself back to the time of his childhood blackout incidents.

Because his life isn't perfect, Evan tries to make things right by revisiting the past and correcting mistakes that he, as a small child, made. Unfortunately, every small correction he makes leads to bigger and worse consequences. The more Evan tries to fix the "present" by changing the "past," the worse the "future" looks.

It is an interesting sci-fi premise but it is weakened by a substandard cast and a pair of novice directors. Ashton Kutcher may be amusing in a slight TV comedy series but he proves himself unable to carry a dramatic film such as this one upon his shoulders.

To his defense, he isn't given all that much support. Amy Smart, while attractive, doesn't show much range even though she is asked to play four different variations of the same character. The other actors in the film are relatively unknown and for good reason.

The premise of the film is interesting enough to keep us involved but not without the awareness that it could have been so much more intriguing in the hands of more capable artists. The Butterfly Effect is at best a "B" movie with a bit of a curiosity factor.

Of course, we know that it is impossible to change the past. History is history and nothing we do today will change what we did before. But that doesn't mean that we cannot change the future.

The past is cemented. The future is uncertain. It is only in the present that we can effect change. The changes we make in our lives today will definitely affect what our lives will be like tomorrow. Nothing that has been done in the past can stop us from changing our today (and by extension, our tomorrow). If we wish to change our lives, now is the only time that matters.

But now ye also put off all these; anger, wrath, malice, blasphemy, filthy communication out of your mouth. Lie not one to another, seeing that ye have put off the old man with his deeds; And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him: Colossians 3:8-10 (KJV)

There is no time like the present to change bad habits and adopt good, healthy habits. The sooner we do, the sooner our futures will look brighter.
