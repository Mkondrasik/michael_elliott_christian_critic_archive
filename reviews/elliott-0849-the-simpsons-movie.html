The Simpsons Movie

Author-verified transcription

There's no need to wonder (as the Pepsodent ad-men used to tell us) where the "yellow" went... The Simpsons' Movie must have used up the nation's supply of all the light shades of ochre in their attempt to hype what must be the most anticipated animated film in cinematic history. And to no one's surprise... it worked. A 70 million dollar opening weekend for an expanded version of a cartoon that's playing for free on TV??? At least Groening and company had the sense to lampoon themselves by cleverly addressing this phenomenon in the film's first few minutes.

Though I purposely avoided the frenzy of the opening weekend, the sense of celebration continued through to the following week even though the crowds had thinned substantially. For Simpsons' fans, the anticipation must have been electric. For the rest of us... not so much. In fact, we're still wondering what all the fuss was about.

Sure, it's irreverent. Yes, it pokes fun at easy targets. OK, Homer's a buffoon of immeasurable depths. But there's really nothing here that we haven't seen before (unless you want to count Bart's genitalia).

The plot is simple and could have easily been condensed into a 22 minute episode. But the plots aren't the reason why The Simpsons are considered to be a television classic. The irreverent wit of the humor coupled with the timeliness of the events it satirizes is what has always captured America and formed its legion of fans.

It's just that Groening isn't necessarily making commentary on the social issues of our time as much as he is simply observing them and then turning them back upon us in a humorous way. Because of this, the jokes tend to come quickly and leave even faster. But, by the film's end, the whole experience, for this reviewer, was rather shallow.

I suppose the overall message that we could glean from the Simpsons is one of forgiveness and redemption. Even a man as insensitive, boorish, childish, selfish, and thoughtless as Homer Simpson deserves an opportunity to atone for his sins. And even though he has repeatedly proved himself unworthy of forgiveness, he always repents his ways and begs for another chance. And his family always gives it to him... just as we are told we should by the same God who has so graciously forgiven us for all of our shortcomings.

Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven. Matthew 18:21-22 (KJV)

If we live long enough we'll be making more than our share of mistakes and missteps. How thankful should we be that we have a God so ready to forgive us? He wipes our sins from his memory and sees us as he wishes for us to see others - for the potential of good that is within each one of us.
