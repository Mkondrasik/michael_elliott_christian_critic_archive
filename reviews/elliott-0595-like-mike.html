Author-verified transcription

The NBA gives itself a public relations boost by cooperating fully with the makers of Like Mike, a family-oriented film about a young orphan who has a dream to play basketball just like Mike. The Mike in question is, of course Michael Jordan, who does not appear in this film, even though just about every other high profile player manages to make an appearance.

Calvin Cambridge (hip hop star Lil Bow Wow, All About the Benjamins) is a 4 foot nothing, 13 year old orphan whose “game” isn’t good enough to challenge the other kids at the orphanage. That changes when he is given a pair of basketball shoes that have the initials MJ inscribed on the inside lining. When Calvin slips these magical sneakers onto his feet, he discovers moves and shots that will eventually earn him a place on the roster of the struggling Knights.

At first a publicity stunt designed to increase attendance, Calvin’s play galvanizes the team and they begin winning. For the first time in their team history, playoff contention looks like a real possibility. Also for the first time, prospective parents now look like they may be interested in adopting Calvin, if they can get past the evil Mr. Bittleman (Crispin Glover, Back to the Future) who, as the caretaker of the orphanage, is Calvin’s legal guardian and has a financial stake at keeping him parent-less.

Make no mistake, Like Mike is purely fantasy of the highest order. In this fictitious story, the trash talk between NBA superstars is rated G, their off court antics involve mostly the playing of nonviolent video games, and their personas are so squeaky clean as to be a publicist’s delight.

In addition to seeing such NBA superstars as Allen Iverson, Jason Kidd, Tracy McGrady, Alonzo Mourning, and David Robinson sharing the court with the young fictional phenom, Like Mike also features Morris Chestnut (The Best Man) as Calvin’s team roommate; Jonathan Lipnicki (Stuart Little) as Calvin’s best friend and fellow orphan; Anne Meara (The Daytrippers) as a strict disciplinarian nun; Robert Forster (Mulholland Drive) as the coach of the Knights; and Eugene Levy (American Pie) as the team’s main spin doctor.

The story, by Michael Elliot (no relation), who also wrote and produced Carmen: A Hip Hopera for TV, is both silly and predictable but, to its credit, once it establishes its credulity-stretching premise, it stays faithful to it. Even though there will be a fair amount of eye-rolling early on, its impossible to completely dismiss this film as being ridiculous. It is ridiculous, of course... but it is also refreshing, disarming, and just outright enjoyable despite its ridiculousness.

Lil Bow Wow (rumor has it that he’s dropped the Lil, shorting his name to just Bow Wow) may not be ready to face Denzel Washington or even Haley Joel Osment in an acting version of the basketball game of “horse,” but he does have charisma to spare and is able to project a genuine affability that, along with his million dollar smile, will take him a long way in this business.

I was gratified to see that the filmmakers did not take the oft trod road of having the sudden and overwhelming success go to Calvin’s head. In Like Mike, Calvin stays rooted, not letting his national fame and popularity change who he is (or who he wants to be.) In a world where ego is too often allowed to become over inflated, (especially when dealing with professional athletes), it was odd to see excellence of skill and humbleness of spirit actually coexisting within the same character. It is, we should observe, how God would like us to act:

As a prisoner for the Lord, then, I urge you to live a life worthy of the calling you have received. Be completely humble and gentle; be patient, bearing with one another in love. Make every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace. Ephesians 4:1-3 (NIV)

Part of what keeps Calvin grounded is the unselfish nature of his dream. For it turns out that his greatest dream was not to become a basketball star... it was to become someone’s son. To be a part of a family. That is a dream that is worthy of coming true.
