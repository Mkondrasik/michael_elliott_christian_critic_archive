The Cell

Author-verified transcription

Of all the places to visit, there is nothing quite as unappealing as a trip into the mind of a seriously sick serial killer. This perverse journey is what makes The Cell such a hard sell among discriminating moviegoers. The images and scenes which depict the mental derangement of this particular individual are raw, misogynist and definitely disturbing.

Jennifer Lopez (Out of Sight) stars as Catherine Diane, a sympathetic child psychologist testing a daring new procedure which is hoped will lead to a breakthrough in the treatment of coma patients. She and her colleagues are able to inject her thought patterns into the mind of a comatose patient where interaction is possible. It is a form of virtual reality without a controlled framework. She is currently working with a young boy trapped within his own mind (which has all the characteristics of a barren desert landscape.)

Meanwhile, FBI agent Peter Novak (Vince Vaughn, Psycho), is trying to locate and stop the bizarre actions of Carl Stargher (Vincent D'Onofrio, Full Metal Jacket), a serial killer who drowns his female victims in a Houdini-like glass box then bleaches their skin, painting them like dolls. The FBI is able to track him down but not before the next victim is captured and entombed in the booby-trapped cell and not before the sicko collapses into a coma brought on by a physical abnormality called Whalan's Infraction.

With only forty hours until the glass case is filled with water, the FBI bring Stargher to Catherine who agrees to enter his twisted and deranged mind in the hope of discovering the whereabouts of the girl.

Without going into needlessly graphic detail, the gruesome scenes within the mind of Stargher reflect the fact that he is one sick individual. His mind is also so strong that Catherine runs the risk of getting lost within it if she should begin to believe that his perceptions are real.

Despite the positive reviews that many secular critics are bestowing upon this film, there is little to recommend about it. The story is actually rather bare-boned, with very little substance to fill out its framework. Both Mr. Vaughn and Ms. Lopez underplay their roles almost to the point that we might think they were auditioning for one of the catatonic patients. There is next to no chemistry between them. Even though the story has a built in race against time element, there is little sense of urgency to the picture, instead choosing to linger on each "Dali-esque" perversely surreal image.

Mr. D'Onofrio, has the showiest role, appearing in many guises which represent the mental image his character has of himself. But director Tarsem Singh, best known for directing music videos and commercials, never gave us the impression of understanding Stargher or how his mind really works.

The mind, of course, is the spiritual battlefield where the conflicts between good and evil take place.

I've heard it once described in this way: Your mind is a room furnished with thoughts. As owner of the house, you choose how to decorate that room. Though others may try to influence your decisions or choices, ultimately it is your responsibility as to the thoughts you allow to remain in your "room."

And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God. Romans 12:2 (KJV)

The renewing of one's mind is not the result of some supernatural or mystical transference. It is simply the personal decision to change one's thinking to agree with the good and acceptable and perfect will of God. That is our privilege and responsibility to do as His children.

Speaking as one who has seen the effects of someone "renewing their mind," there is indeed a transformation that takes place. It is a transformation as clearly evident as seen when light floods into a darkened room. The proof is in the doing of it.

As Catherine says in the film when Stargher follows her back into her mind, "My world... My rules." The one thing that we can truly control is our own mind. May we furnish it well.
