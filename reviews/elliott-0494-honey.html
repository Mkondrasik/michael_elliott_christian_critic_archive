Author-verified transcription

Honey is a perfectly predictable, somewhat shallow story of a girl from the 'hood, who gets a shot at the brass ring only to find that once there, the brass is tarnished. Think Flashdance, Glitter, Fame, or any of a dozen other movies from which Honey draws inspiration.

Jessica Alba stars in the title role: a bartender, club hopper, record store employee and dance teacher for underprivileged youths. Caught on camera while dancing at the club, she is brought to the attention of a music video director (David Moscow) who is looking for the next big thing to bring some life and freshness into his projects. Honey, he decides, is it.

Soon, not only is she (along with her well photographed abs) featured in his videos, she's choreographing them as well and making quite the name for herself amongst the artists. Of course, she had to leave the record store, the club, her classes, and her friends behind in order to pursue her newfound career.

But all is not sunshine and roses. The music executive is a jerk, the kids who were her students feel let down and Honey begins to realize that despite the fancy parties, the fame, and the money, she may have been a richer person when she was working in the 'hood, trying to make a difference in the lives of those who lived there.

Jessica Alba isn't much of an actress but she's certainly attractive and personable. She also moves well enough for us to accept that she could command the attention of a dance floor.

Joy Bryant costars as a girlfriend with "attitude" who encourages her and supports her during the rise, fall and subsequent redefining of her career. Mekhi Phifer plays Honey's decent, hardworking love interest and Lil' Romeo is one of the neighborhood kids who starts taking Honey's class (thus, keeping him off the streets and out of trouble.)

Director Billie Woodruff, fittingly enough, is well known in the music video business, having directed videos for Britney Spears, Usher, and The Backstreet Boys among others. It should come as no surprise to find that the musical scenes (of which there are many) are the best moments of the film. The narrative scenes are bits of fluff, having little substance or depth.

The film is improved somewhat by musical appearances by some familiar names in the R&B world. Playing themselves are: Ginuwine, Missy Elliott (who is very funny in her cameos), Tweet, and Blaque, among others.

The messages at the heart of Honey are as sweet as its title. Do what you love. Serve others and the community in which you live. Don't compromise your ideals or your integrity in order to get ahead. Don't judge success by how much money you earn. Let your life make a difference in the lives of others... especially in those that you love.

Good and decent principles all. For good reason. All of them have a godly source. The scriptures encourage us to live our lives along these lines. That isn't to say it is easy. It can be extremely difficult. Especially when we see others who appear to succeed who aren't following or adopting those ideals. The scriptures speak to that as well.

For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked. Psalms 73:3 (KJV)

In this world at various times, the ungodly will appear to prosper and the godly will appear to suffer. We are not to be swayed by those appearances but instead we are to remain steadfast and strong in our conviction and faith in God. The psalmist rid himself of the envy he was feeling by a simple reminder. Our future is assured. And our success is an eternal one. That is something the unrighteous cannot claim.

For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked. When I thought to know this, it was too painful for me; Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end. Psalms 73:3,16-17 (KJV)
