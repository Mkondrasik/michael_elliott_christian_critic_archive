Author-verified transcription

"If you're not first, you're last." That's the life wisdom imparted unto a young Ricky Bobby by his derelict dad and the message stuck. Talladega Nights: The Ballad of Ricky Bobby is all about going fast and furious - not just while driving a car but also while reaching for laughs.

Ricky Bobby (Will Farrell) has always dreamed about "going fast." He finally gets his opportunity when a NASCAR driver fails to come back to his vehicle during a pit stop. Bobby sits himself in the driver's seat, slams it into gear and doesn't look back. Before we know it, he's the darling king of NASCAR. He becomes the #1 driver with a "smoking hot wife" and the best friend a man could ever want.

When a gay French Formula One driver (Sacha Baron Cohen) enters the NASCAR scene, Bobby's reign as king comes to an end. He loses a race, loses his wife, loses his friend, loses his sponsor, and perhaps loses his mind. He then hits rock bottom, losing his drivers license and has to resort to delivering pizzas on a bike.

In typical inspirational sports fashion, Bobby must struggle to overcome his fears and climb to the top of his field once again... but this time, he's doing it for all the right reasons...

It's a familiar premise and that's the gag. Farrell and company are presenting a mild spoof on the genre. But their love of the genre shows through because with just a little tweaking, The Ballad of Ricky Bobby could have worked as a legitimate sports drama. But with Will Farrell and company, comedy wins out.

This is one of Farrell's most accessible characters. He's a likable, easy-going kind of fool. We laugh with him more often than we do at him. Farrell sometimes goes off in search of a bigger laugh (running around the track in his underwear) but those are the moments that don't quite work. In this film, he's at his best when he plays small and understated.

Some of the biggest laughs come from the supporting cast. Gary Cole is brilliant as Ricky's dad, an amateur driver who rejects any form of normalcy. Sacha Baron Cohen delivers laughs as Ricky's main competition - a Frenchman who can't help but butcher the English language.

John C. Reilly provides firm and stable support as Bobby's best friend and teammate Carl S. Naughton. Carl may not be the brightest bulb in the pack but his devotion to Ricky is evident to all.

Director Adam McKay (Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy) keeps a steady hand on the wheel but lacks the nerve to take the film to a higher comedic level. He plays safe, even with the improvised dialogue. As a result, the film has a plodding, methodical feel to it without the electric air that edgy comedy often carries.

Ricky Bobby used his father's comment, "If you're not first, you're last," as a mantra for his life. When his father hears it later, he admits that he was high when he first said it and it is nonsense. Ricky becomes confused and agitated as he has built his life and career on that expression. If it is a lie, he wants to know what he should do now - what should he believe?

It is something that happens all too frequently. People put their trust in and invest their lives and energies to falsehoods that provide no comfort or deliverance in times of need. When they finally realize their error, they are at a loss to know how to fix it and will too often turn to other lies which leads them to deeper troubles.

There is a true standard by which we can live and in which we will never be disappointed. It is a standard that has lasted for millennia and will continue to last long after we have left this earth. It promises us victory and rescues us from defeat. It is the truth of God who has made us more than conquerors in any situation.
