Danny Deckchair

Author-verified transcription

What kind of man ties dozens of helium balloons to his lawn chair just to see if it can get him off the ground? Danny Deckchair provides the answer to a question few people ever thought to ask.

Rhys Ifans stars as Danny Morgan, a cement worker who is so bored with his mundane life that he turns to silly stunts and antics simply because he's got nothing better to do. He gets more than he bargained for with this latest brainstorm.

The balloons not only lift him up off the ground, it carries him away with the wind - figuratively as well as literally. He finally drops from the sky into a small Australian village and into the life of a lonely meter maid named Glenda (Miranda Otto).

A few moments in the small town of Clarence convince him that he's better off there than in his Sydney home. He decides to stay - even though the Sydney media is falling over itself reporting his unusual disappearance and making him out to be a figure of inspiration to all dreamers.

The story is loosely based upon an actual event. In 1982, American Larry Walker tied 42 weather balloons to a chair and armed with a pellet gun (to shoot the balloons when he was ready to descend) he launched himself some 16,000 feet into the air where he understandably perplexed LAX air traffic controllers. His stunt garnered him some short term fame and a special award from something called the Bonehead Club.

Director Jeff Balsmeyer takes this strange event and uses it as a catalyst for a simple romantic comedy. His protagonist is someone who, because he is dissatisfied with his life, acts somewhat strangely. When he arrives in a different town among people who are drawn to his quirkiness, he finds a love and an inner peace he didn't know was available.

Rhys Ifans is typically cast as an offbeat supporting character but shows that he has a little of the "everyman" quality to him. He's fine as a romantic lead although his other performances show him to have more range. It's as if he's hamstrung in a role that requires him to be the film's center rather than a colorful part of an ensemble cast.

Miranda Otto is too intelligent and beautiful to be cast as a dateless meter maid but she's pleasant to watch and easily gains the audience's sympathy.

The remaining major character is played by Justine Clarke who, as Danny's Sydney girlfriend, portrays a status-seeking opportunist who cares more about being a local celebrity than in finding out what happened to him.

When he was living in Sydney, Danny considered himself to be one of the "little people" - an insignificant individual who, like many others, lived from day to day and from paycheck to paycheck with nothing to look forward to but his holidays.

Not until he crash-lands in Clarence does he begin to sense that, in a town filled with "little people," nobody is insignificant. With this recognition, he begins to appreciate his life and the lives of those around him.

God doesn't do insignificant. A purposeful Creator, he has enabled us with individual talents and abilities that, as we employ them and share them with others, contribute to the collective strength of his creation.

"And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you. Nay, much more those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary." 1 Corinthians 12:21-22 (KJV)
