The Banger Sisters

Author-verified transcription

Hollywood is a strange place. Where else does a mother try to follow in her daughter's footsteps? Kate Hudson won an Academy Award nomination in 2000 for playing a rock band groupie (sorry, "band aid") in the film Almost Famous. Two years later, in The Banger Sisters, her mother takes on the same kind of role. Oh, what a difference a generation makes. Behavior that, to some, might have appeared almost cute and adventurous at age twenty is revealed to be pathetically sad in one over 50.

The film opens with Suzette (Goldie Hawn, The First Wives Club) being fired from her bartending job at the Hollywood club, Whiskey-A-Go-Go, where she has honed her wild child/party girl image. In desperation, she packs up her broken down car and heads for Phoenix in search of her one time party buddy, "Vinnie" (Susan Sarandon, Igby Goes Down).

Back in "the day," they were known rather infamously as "The Banger Sisters." The name refers to their sexual proclivity which was sampled by most everyone on the rock scene. While the silicone enhanced Suzette longs to continue to live in her rather inglorious past, Vinnie has long since gone suburban.

Now a respected member of a conservative community, Vinnie (known to her upper class friends as "Lavinia") is the wife of a lawyer (Robin Thomas, Clockstoppers) with political aspirations; the mother of two teenage daughters, one of whom is valedictorian of her class; and the owner of a closet in which every article of clothing is a drab shade of beige. The last thing she needs is for her wild and colorful past to resurface. Or is it perhaps precisely what she needs?

This sexually charged comedy is blessed with a very talented cast, all of whom know how to mine for laughs. In this case, they don't have to dig too deep.

Goldie Hawn is the uncensored, untamed and unconventional center of the film around which all the other characters revolve. She plays the part to the hilt and provides the catalyst from which the comedy is spawned.

Much of the humor comes from how Sarandon and Geoffrey Rush (Quills), who plays a neurotic writer Suzette meets on her trip, react to the force of nature that is Suzette. Erika Christensen (Swimfan) and Eva Amurri (Made Up), as Vinnie's spoiled and troubled teen aged girls, have some fine moments. (Trivia note: Amurri is Sarandon's real life daughter).

Writer/Director Bob Dolman makes his directorial debut with The Banger Sisters (he previously wrote the screenplay for Far and Away and Willow). While Dolman keeps the pace moving and provides ample opportunity for his primary actors to shine in their respective roles, the overall message his film conveys is somewhat muddled.

The only character growth seems to come from Livinia, who learns to embrace her wild, impulsive nature and Harry, who learns that he likes sex. Suzette, the character in most need of growth and maturity, remains consistently irrepressible throughout.

The simplistic and misleading message that The Banger Sisters gives is that it is more important to be true to who we are than to conform to societal expectations. That logic, if carried to its extreme, fails. Our actions and behavior do have an impact on others and to ignore that reality is to live selfishly and irresponsibly.

As obedient children, do not conform to the evil desires you had when you lived in ignorance. But just as he who called you is holy, so be holy in all you do. 1 Peter 1:14-15 (NIV)

A "nonconformist" lifestyle is a misnomer. As we see in the above scripture, rejecting the loving boundaries established by God in His Word in order to "do our own thing" is simply to conform to the evils of this world. We can choose to act in a manner so our lives may be pleasing to God and be an example to all men. The Suzettes of this world need not persuade us to return to the error-filled ways of our youth. In truth, it is we who should be setting the example for them to follow.
