Author-verified transcription

It won’t win any awards and may not appear on many top ten lists come December, but for gleeful nostalgic fun, there aren’t many movies this year that will top Looney Tunes: Back in Action.

Remaining true to the biting wit and politically incorrect humor which had adults laughing just as much and as hard as their children, this new film runs daffy circles around the last Looney project: the much maligned Space Jam. Back in Action is a redemption of sorts, reminding us of the glory days when Chuck Jones and Fritz Freleng made it seem so easy.

The film begins on a back lot of Warner Brothers with Daffy arguing with WB executives for an increased role in Bugs' new movie. His pleas fall on deaf and dumb ears which insist that Kate, the VP in charge of comedy (Jenna Elfman), fire the duck.

Daffy and DJ, the security guard chosen to escort him off the lot (Brendan Fraser), soon find themselves in the middle of an adventure as DJ’s famous father (Timothy Dalton) turns out to be a spy pretending to be an actor pretending to be a spy. In any case, the guy is being held hostage by the evil chairman of the Acme Corporation (Steve Martin) who is after the powerful and mythical Blue Diamond.

DJ and Daffy must find DJ’s father and the diamond before the chairman. But they’ll face plenty of opposition along the way. Yosemite Sam and his gawl-durned henchmen try to stop them in Las Vegas. Acme’s desert operative, Wile E. Coyote tries to ambush them in the desert. And once they reach “mother” (Joan Cusack) in Area 52 (the government created the myth of Area 51 to confuse people), a host of alien creatures, led by Marvin the Martian, try to vaporize them.

All in all, it’s great fun and will especially be enjoyed by movie buffs and by those familiar with the studio’s classic cartoon short features of years gone by.

The casting of the human actors could not have been better. Brendan Fraser, who has played his share of animated personalities (George of the Jungle, Dudley Do-Right), is in his element as he shares the screen with the best non-human “actors” in the business. Jenna Elfman is every bit his equal with a wonderful comic timing encased in a very attractive package. Steve Martin is off the chart funny with an exaggerated villainous performance. His acting is every bit as animated as his furry and feathered costars.

Kudos go to director Joe Dante and screenwriter Larry Doyle for recapturing the magic in the bottle that was Looney Tunes. Their film is one that will delight young people and bring back fond memories of laughter to those who are not so young.

Laughter is a topic that is not studied or discussed nearly enough in the church today. It is an element of life that is vitally important. Laughter or humor definitely has healing properties.

A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones. Proverbs 17:22 (KJV)

Our God certainly has a sense of humor which can be seen reflected in His Word. Would a humorless God use a talking ass to communicate with a disobedient prophet?

And the LORD opened the mouth of the ass, and she said unto Balaam, What have I done unto thee, that thou hast smitten me these three times? Numbers 22:28 (KJV)

The record in Numbers 22 is an extremely humorous account of a serious situation. But there are many other times when God uses humor in His Word to communicate truths to us... truths that He desires us to understand.

Is Looney Tunes: Back in Action a great example of spiritual truth? No. But it can be enjoyed for what it is. An opportunity to laugh and enjoy the merriment that can heal hearts and souls.
