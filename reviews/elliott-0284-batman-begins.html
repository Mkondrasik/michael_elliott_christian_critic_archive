Author-verified transcription

Holy blockbuster, Batman! In Batman Begins, director Christopher Nolan has performed his own super-hero feat by rescuing what was a dying franchise. After the much maligned Batman and Robin, it looked like the Caped Crusader was ready to bid a not so fond farewell to Hollywood. Nolan had other ideas. By taking us back to the origins of the "Bat," Nolan not only breaths life back into the comic book world of Bruce Wayne and his alter-ego, he has given us one of the best super-hero films ever made.

Nolan's idea was to explore the conditions that made Bruce Wayne (Christian Bale) adopt the persona of the Dark Knight. After watching his millionaire parents gunned down in the street by a desperate man during economically desperate times, he grows to become a wealthy but unhappy young man. Riddled with guilt and fear, he travels the world in search of the darkness that fills his soul. He is, as his mentor would say, lost.

That mentor is Henri Ducard (Liam Neesom), a member of a mysterious group called the League of Shadows. Ducard sees in Wayne the potential to become a weapon in the fight against evil and darkness. Wayne trains under Ducard and leans how to overcome his fears. However, when Wayne learns of the true intentions of the League of Shadows, he dissociates himself and returns to the city of his youth - Gotham - where he invents an alter-ego to strike fear in the hearts of evil-doers.

We get to see the birth of Batman and how the legend grew. Christian Bale was a perfect choice to play this psychologically troubled character. Bale provides the depth and emotional weight to both sides of his dual role. He anchors the picture and because we believe him in the part, we buy into the story.

It also helps that he has a great supporting cast surrounding him. Michael Caine as the faithful butler and family friend Alfred; Gary Oldman as police detective Gordon; Morgan Freeman as the head of Wayne Industries' forgotten R&D department where Batman finds all his "wonderful toys;" Liam Neesom as the mysterious Ducard; Cillian Murphy as the creepy Dr. Crane (aka Scarecrow); and Katie Holmes as Bruce Wayne's childhood friend and adult love interest. All perform wonderfully but, unlike the other Batman films, the supporting characters all take a backseat to Bale. This is his show and the film is stronger because of it.

More than any other film, Batman Begins clearly shows the fine line that Bruce Wayne is walking - the line that separates good from evil. We believe that this Batman is capable of crossing that line in order to fight a greater evil. He also knows that if he does, he loses the fight.

Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good. Romans 112:21 (KJV)

We cannot conquer our enemies by adopting their values (or lack of values), mindsets and practices. Only by standing strong on the side of truth can we hope to overcome those who stand against it.
