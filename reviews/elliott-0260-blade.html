Author-verified transcription

One of the problems with vampire movies is that they always seem obliged to out-bloody their predecessors. Blade is no exception. Within the first five minutes of this film, fountains of blood shower down upon the devilish dancers of a vampire disco, who continue to boogie into a frenzy driven (wouldn't you know) by a bloodlust unseen since... well, since the last vampire movie.

Suddenly, showing up to put an end to the party is Blade (Wesley Snipes, Passenger 57), a vampire hunter with a personal score to settle. You see, Blade is part vampire himself. While his mother was pregnant, she was attacked by one of these denizens of the dark. Blade was born shortly thereafter. Hence he shares some but not all of their attributes: Being in sunlight does not affect him, but he does have their "thirst" for blood. He exhibits many of the super-human abilities of the vampires he hates, but he is not immortal as they are.

He is also not alone. Joining him in his vendetta is Kris Kristofferson (Lone Star) as Whistler. Years earlier, finding Blade living on the streets, Whistler somehow realized the young boy's unique nature and took him in, training him to wage war against the vampires. N'Bushe Wright (A Woman Like That) is also on hand as Dr. Karen Jensen, who alternates between being a hostage and a helper.

Stephen Dorff (City of Industry) is Deacon Frost, the main vampire bad guy. He is no longer content with living in the shadows while the inferior humans rule the world. As he puts it, "[Humans] are our food, not our allies." So he is ready to lead the vampires in an attempt to conquer the world.

Mr. Snipes plays Blade as an unemotional machine, unless you count pain as an emotion. He's fine in the fight segments when he is calmly blowing up vampire heads or nonchalantly slicing the demons in half. He's rather dull when asked to engage in a little conversation.

Mr. Dorff brings a bit more personality to his character. That is not to say that it is a pleasant personality... just that there is more of it.

Director Stephen Norrington (Death Machine) uses all technical wizardry and tricks that he can find. The vampires leap great distances, demonstrate incredible feats of strength, and when "killed", they first "skeletize" and then vaporize into little flakes of charcoal.

However, all the technical wizardry in the world is not enough to disguise the fact that Blade is a silly excuse to go to the movies. There is no artistic or spiritual redemption to this cinematic bloodfest.

Even the premise is based on a lie. Supposedly, the vampires' nature is derived from an abnormality in their blood. The good Dr. Jensen discovers this as she sets about to find a cure. But if this were the case, Blade should have been unaffected. All the blood of a fetus is self contained -- it comes from the sperm. The mother does not pump any blood into it. That is why the scriptures can say that Jesus Christ had innocent blood. The sperm that led to his conception was created by God, and therefore would have been completely pure from any contaminants in his natural mother's bloodstream.

It is why in Hebrews 2:14 [KJV] the scriptures say:

Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh AND blood, he [Jesus Christ] also himself TOOK PART of the same...

The emphasis in the above verse is mine. The part that Jesus Christ took was the flesh, being born of woman. He did not take of the blood for he was the only begotten son of the Father God. And the perfect sperm that God created in Mary carried the perfect blood of the one who would become our savior.

Perhaps that can be our spiritual lesson from this most unworthy film... that the only blood worth "lusting after" (in the purest form of the word) is the blood that cleanses us from sin. The blood of Jesus Christ our Lord.
