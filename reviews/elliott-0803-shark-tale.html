Author-verified transcription

Being a little fish in a big pond doesn't amount to much of a life - especially if the "pond" happens to be the ocean. This is according to Oscar, the central figure of Dream Works Animation's newest CGI feature film, Shark Tale.

Oscar (voiced by Will Smith) is a fast-talking hustler with big dreams - and a big mouth to match. He aspires to something more than being a tongue-scrubber at the Whale Wash, a job which he feels places him at the bottom of the reef.

Lenny (voiced by Jack Black) has the opposite problem. He is a great white shark and son to Lino (voiced by Robert Deniro), the godfather of the shark mob which runs the reef. As such, he's expected to be a ruthless killer like the rest of the family. But Lenny is a sensitive soul and a vegetarian to boot. He just wants to be himself.

When a chance meeting places Oscar and Lenny together, they conspire to make both their dreams come true. With Lenny's help, Oscar becomes known throughout all the reef as a "shark slayer" and Lenny is presumed dead (and free to finally live life his way - disguised as a fun-loving dolphin). But they should have known that Lino wasn't about to let anyone take over his turf (or should that be surf?).

Shark Tale keeps the jokes and sight gags coming at a furious pace. These will often sail over the heads of the younger members of the audience and more than a few of them are given at the expense of the integrity of the story and characters. But because of the rapid fire nature, we aren't given much time to think about the jokes that miss.

In addition to the colorful artwork and design, the film is greatly enhanced by the celebrity cast of voices. Will Smith conveys the boisterous energy of a braggadocio; Robert Deniro spoofs his godfather persona with glee; Martin Scorcese, as an excitable blowfish who becomes Oscar's business manager, is a stitch and Angelina Jolie impresses as a sexy dragon fish that sinks her gold-digging teeth into Oscar's newfound fame. Jack Black is vocally unrecognizable as the sweet and high-pitched Lenny but manages to capture the essence of the effeminate shark.

Although the humor is plentiful, this fish story stays in relatively shallow water, depending upon stereotypes and clichés to move its tale forward. It also boasts enough product placements and cultural references to feed a school of whales. What it lacks is the distinguishing element which has always lifted the Pixar films above any of Dream Works' animated efforts - a huge and readily identifiable heart which grounds the film and connects the characters to the audience.

Oscar's initial view of success is as shallow as the film. Fame and fortune are highly overrated achievements. Unfortunately, it is so promoted in our culture that it has, for many, become the benchmark by which a life is valued. Only those who attain it know how empty a goal it truly is.

And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth. Luke 12:15 (KJV)
