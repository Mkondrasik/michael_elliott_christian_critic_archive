Author-verified transcription

In Red 2, a number of aged (but seemingly ageless) covert operatives are forced out of retirement in order to find out why people are trying to kill them. This sequel to the popular 2010 film reunites most of the members of the original cast while introducing a number of new but recognizable faces. The sense of fun and quirky humor stays intact.

Frank Moses (Bruce Willis) is a legend in the world of black ops. His abilities and talent for killing are indisputable. These days all he wants is to settle down into a normal suburban life with his sweet and innocent girlfriend, Sarah (Mary Louise Parker). But when the outlandishly odd Marvin (John Malkovich) discovers that someone is leaking information about a highly classified operation called Nightshade using their names, countries around the world hire their best assassins to terminate Marvin and Frank. It proves to be a feat easier said than done.

Director Dean Parisot assembles an A-list cast to bring this story to life. Willis, Malkovich, and Parker all reprise their roles as do Helen Mirren as the droll MI-6 assassin Veronica, and Brian Cox as the ex-KGB operative Ivan Simonov. Added to the cast are Catherine Zeta Jones as a Russian spy who shares a past with Frank, Byung-hun Lee as Han Cho Bei, the world’s foremost contract killer, and Anthony Hopkins as Bailey, the brilliant scientist whose long lost invention sits at the center of all the frenzied gunplay and explosions.

Each of these actors has a solid lock on their characters and appears to comfortably fit into their respective personas. Malkovich, Parker and Hopkins are especially delightful to watch as they bring a level of the unexpected to an overly familiar genre.

Throughout the film, the relationship between Frank and Sarah is a focal point with many characters offering their own opinions on how to make a relationship work. Frank is trying to keep Sarah insulated from the dangerous people and activities from his CIA life. Sarah is becoming bored with the new domestic-centric lifestyle Frank is trying to build. Their friends are seeing what they apparently cannot – they are drifting apart because they are not like-minded.

“And the two shall become one flesh, so that they are no longer two, but one flesh.” Mark 10:8 (Amplified)

Frank’s error is in trying to keep his life and activities separate from his committed relationship with Sarah. As his friends try to explain, commitment is an all-in proposition. By picking and choosing what parts of himself to share with Sarah, he is inadvertently distancing himself from her. Granted, this film presents it in an exaggerated form (the life of a paramilitary, world-saving hero cannot be an easy one to share), but the truism remains. The marvel of the one-flesh relationship is a difficult one to explain or comprehend but when mastered, it is the strongest, most rewarding relationship one can ever know.
