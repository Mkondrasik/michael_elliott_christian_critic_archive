Serendipity

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 5, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 25 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Signs; Discernment; Providence

Author-verified transcription

A happy accident. That is the definition given to the title of the newest entry to the ranks of romantic comedy: Serendipity. And who's to say that it doesn't fit? A successful romantic comedy is a fragile thing... a delicate balance that even the most experienced filmmakers can't always duplicate.

The film begins with a chance meeting over a pair of cashmere gloves. The guy, Jonathan Trager (John Cusack, America's Sweethearts), is, of course, smitten immediately. The girl, Sara Thomas (Kate Beckinsale, Pearl Harbor), is, of course, flattered but wary. A second chance meeting leads to a magical evening of ice skating, flirtation and courtship... even though both admit to being involved with other people.

Jonathan eventually persuades Sara to give him her phone number only to have it blown away in a gust of wind. Cue the serendipity. Sara, spooked by the "omen", refuses to give the number to him again. At least not directly. Relying on fate, she proceeds to write her name and number in a book which she will sell at an undisclosed used book store. Likewise, Jonathan writes his number on a five dollar bill which is promptly put into circulation.

If either one comes upon the phone number of the other, then "fate" meant them to be together and que sera sera. Seven years later, separated by time and distance and now engaged to other people, they still can't stop thinking about their one evening together. Tired of waiting for "serendipity" to strike twice, Jonathan and Sara decide to give it a helping hand by independently combing NYC in search of the other.

While not a perfect example of the genre, Serendipity does have its moments of sweet, lighthearted, romantic fun. The film is aided greatly by the work of the always personable John Cusack. It is important in a romantic comedy that we like the leads involved and want them to get their hearts' desire. Cusack is so affable that we are rooting for him from the start.

Kate Beckinsale doesn't share the same immediate connection with the audience as her male costar. But she is an attractive counterpart for Cusack and their initial scene together does click sufficiently to make us understand why he would still be thinking about her seven years later.

Jeremy Piven (The Family Man) is building a nice career for himself in a series of best friend/brother roles as he adds another one to his filmography. Molly Shannon (Saturday Night Live) plays it relatively straight as Sara's "new-agey" friend and supportive sounding board. Eugene Levy (American Pie 2) adds his droll humor to the mix as an uptight sales clerk.

Peter Chelsom, who was last credited with directing the critically despised Town & Country, finds a certain level of redemption here. Working from a script by first-timer Mark Klein, Chelsom has crafted a movie where the laughs may not be deep or sustained, but they are consistent, with a "feel-good" aura emanating from the screen. The pacing tends to drag a bit in spots but not so much that we'll be eyeing the exits or checking our watches.

The movie is, of course, centered around Sara's naive belief that there is no such thing as happenstance so every odd occurrence or quirky event is to be interpreted as a sign from a higher unseen power by which our actions should be governed.

"And he sighed deeply in his spirit, and saith, Why doth this generation seek after a sign? verily I say unto you, There shall no sign be given unto this generation." Mark 8:12 (KJV)

The problem with depending upon "signs" to determine a course of action is that we then make ourselves a prime target for deception. Signs can be feigned and lead us to places we'd rather not visit.

"For false Christs and false prophets shall rise, and shall show signs and wonders, to seduce, if it were possible, even the elect." Mark 13:22 (KJV)

God established His truth and set it in our midst in a written form so we would have a readily available standard by which to live our lives. We need not go looking for any other sign other than that which He has already given.
