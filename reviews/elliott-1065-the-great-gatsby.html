The Great Gatsby

Sermon / spiritual topic: Moderation; Self-Control; Wealth; Excess

Author-verified transcription

F. Scott Fitzgerald’s great American novel comes to life onscreen in Baz Luhrmann’s rendition of The Great Gatsby. Set in the 1920s, Gatsby is essentially a tragic love story amid a decadent but dying way of life.

We hear the story told from the perspective of Nick Carraway (Tobey Maguire), a relatively poor would-be writer turned stockbroker who has come to live on Long Island for a season. There he meets his neighbor, the enigmatic and charismatic Jay Gatsby (Leonardo DiCaprio), whose fantastic wealth is showcased by the epic parties he holds every weekend at his West Egg estate.

What Nick comes to discover is the extravagance that Gatsby lavishes at every opportunity has a purpose behind it. Her name is Daisy (Carey Mulligan) and she happens to be Nick’s cousin and Gatsby’s reason for being. Unfortunately for Gatsby she is married to blue-blooded (and two-timing) Tom (Joel Edgerton) who lives across the bay in the more prestigious East Egg.

Nick witnesses and is ultimately disillusioned by the rich and fatuous lives and habits of the well-to-do denizens that frequent East Egg although he remains fascinated or intrigued by the character of Gatsby. Director Baz Luhrmann brings a heavily stylized hand to the production, some of which works while some falls flat. In his favor is the performance of Leonardo DiCaprio who brings the necessary charm and gravitas to such a beloved literary figure.

Luhrmann spends much of the film’s running time focusing on the elaborate and hugely successful parties that Gatsby throws. Alcohol flows freely; people dance with wild abandon; celebrities, criminals, and political figures mix and mingle without regard to propriety or decorum. The impression of course is that Gatsby and those in his financial circles are living the good life. Of course, by the story’s end, we see the emptiness of that life and the shallowness of those who live it.

The temptation to indulge in excesses is nothing new to man. The scriptures are filled with examples to serve as illustrations and warnings for us to heed. Moderation is not something particularly esteemed in Gatsby’s world and, to be honest, it doesn’t often appear to be highly regarded in ours. In God’s view however, it is a quality much to be desired and a building block to a life promised to be more than abundant.

“For this very reason, adding your diligence [to the divine promises], employ every effort in exercising your faith to develop virtue (excellence, resolution, Christian energy), and in [exercising] virtue [develop] knowledge (intelligence), And in [exercising] knowledge [develop] self-control, and in [exercising] self-control [develop] steadfastness (patience, endurance), and in [exercising] steadfastness [develop] godliness (piety), And in [exercising] godliness [develop] brotherly affection, and in [exercising] brotherly affection [develop] Christian love. For as these qualities are yours and increasingly abound in you, they will keep [you] from being idle or unfruitful unto the [full personal] knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ (the Messiah, the Anointed One).” 2 Peter 1:5-8 (Amplified)

Money does not make the man but many a man has been unmade by the temptations that come with having money. It is a wise man that develops and exercises the self-control to avoid succumbing to excess in any category of life.
