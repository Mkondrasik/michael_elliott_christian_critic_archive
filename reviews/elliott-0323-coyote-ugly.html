Coyote Ugly

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 4, 2000
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Job 23:10-11Titus 2:11-12Proverbs 2:6-16

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Integrity; means and ends; temptation; perseverance

Author-verified transcription

The phrase "Coyote Ugly" is explained in the film which uses it as its title. Tattooed bar owner Lil (Maria Bello, Payback) says it: "Have you ever woken up after a one night stand to find that person's head resting on your arm? If you'd rather chew your arm off in order to leave without waking that person up... that's coyote ugly." When asked why she named her bar after this unusual and ill-fitting phrase, her simple answer is, "Cheers was already taken."

Such are the tender sentiments expressed in this salacious and exploitative film. The bare bones (no pun intended) plot, is designed to justify having the beautiful and scantily clad hardbodies (AKA "coyotes") dance suggestively on the bar where they serve drinks to the rowdy and wildly appreciative NYC crowd.

Into this setting is thrust a shy, semi-innocent, struggling songwriter from New Jersey, Violet Sanford (Piper Perabo, The Adventures of Rocky and Bullwinkle). Trying to make ends meet while peddling her songs to uninterested music executives, she braves the crazed, sexually-charged atmosphere of this particular watering hole, auditioning to become a coyote. Soon this fish out of water is swimming with the best of them, serving drinks, breaking up fights, and wiggling her assets to the delight of the barflies.

Joining her are three other pieces of eye candy: the assertive and aggressive brunette coyote, Rachel (Bridget Moynahan, Whipped); the assertive and kind blonde coyote, Cammie (Polish actress Izabella Miko, in her American film debut); and the assertive and smart black coyote, Zoe (model Tyra Banks). Between the three of them, there's more than enough attitude to go around, but while their physiques may be fully developed, their characters are not.

Attached to this elongated music video, is a love story between Violet and Kevin, a struggling... well, we're not quite sure what he wants to be. But he's a nice enough bloke played by Australian actor Adam Garcia (Bootmen). John Goodman (What Planet Are You From?) also lends his support in the role of Violet's rather inept father whose sage advice includes, "I don't care how many bars you have to dance on, you're not giving up on your dream."

There are simply too many incredulous activities going on to generate much interest other than that of a voyeuristic nature. For instance, we are asked to believe that Violet, who gets stage-fright to such a degree that she can't sing her own songs unless she's alone in the dark, will have no difficulty when called upon to quell an out-of-control bar riot by singing sexily along with a juke box.

At first, I thought that the film may try to investigate the worthy topic of the danger in compromising one's principles in order to pursue one's dreams. Ah, me... Such naiveté. Far be it for Hollywood to cast a negative moral judgment upon licentious and libidinous behavior. In the world of Coyote Ugly, flat stomachs and bare midriffs are the keys to success, alcohol flows freely but never makes one drunk, and it is perfectly okay for women to exploit themselves for financial gain.

Contrary to many peoples' beliefs, the ends do not always justify the means. At least not in God's eyes. And not when the right means will ensure a better end. We should try to remember that the journey we take is just as important as the destination to be reached.

But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold. My foot hath held his steps, his way have I kept, and not declined. Job 23:10-11 (KJV)

The swarms of people seen patronizing the bar featured in the film seem to be best described by the Word as those whose only thought is to "eat, drink, and be merry..." It is a philosophy that may be long on appeal but short on wisdom.

Coyote Ugly may be attractively packaged and it may contain some pleasant tunes written by Grammy award winner Diane Warren. But beneath its surface is an emptiness as depressing as a bar after last call is given and the lights are turned on. Now that's coyote ugly.
