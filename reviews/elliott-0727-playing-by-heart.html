Author-verified transcription

"Talking about love is like dancing about architecture." So says one of the characters in Playing By Heart. The point being made is that talking about love is pointless... it can't be communicated fully in a verbal form. But we've got six (maybe seven) stories that will give it a try anyway.

Playing By Heart is not as much a full length motion picture as it is a series of vignettes that are strung together and ultimately tied up in a way that is contrived and unsatisfying. Each vignette deals with a relationship and with how love functions in that relationship.

Paul (Sean Connery, The Rock) and Hannah (Gena Rowlands, Hope Floats) are the oldest couple. They are wrestling with Paul's mortality as well as the fallout of a decision he made some twenty-five years ago.

Meredith (Gillian Anderson, The X-Files) is deathly afraid of any kind of relationship even after meeting too-good-to-be-true Trent (Jon Stewart, The Faculty).

Gracie (Madeline Stowe, Twelve Monkeys) seems to be content with arrangement she has with adulterer Roger (Anthony Edwards, TV's ER).

Mildred (Ellen Burstyn, The Spitfire Grill) is trying to find new ways to communicate and connect with her dying son, Mark (Jay Mohr, Mafia!) who contracted AIDS from his recently deceased homosexual partner.

Joan (Angelina Jolie, Playing God) is relentlessly pursuing the standofish and blue-haired Keenan (Ryan Phillippe, 54) who seems to be completely uninterested in her.

And then we have Hugh (Dennis Quaid, The Parent Trap), who, for the first hour or so, plays an enigma. We aren't sure what he's doing or why he's there. But all will be made clear by the end of the film.

Each of these characters are dealing with love and relationships in one way or another. Writer/Director Willard Carroll (Tom's Midnight Garden) weaves the storylines together, keeping them distinct and keeping us involved and interested in each outcome. The ending of the film has a forced feeling to it as if he tried too hard to connect the characters' lives together. He need not have bothered. Each story was strong enough to stand on its own. Tying them together in such an obvious manner somehow cheapened the overall movie.

Each actor in this mega-cast does a fine job... especially the amazing Angelina Jolie, who shines brighter than any star with whom she appears. Being able to stand out in the midst of this highly esteemed cast is quite an accomplishment. Miss Jolie lights up the screen and makes an indelible impression.

Playing By Heart is about love and especially about the choices we make for love. The manifestation of love is all about making choices.

For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous. 1 John 5:3 /KJV]

To manifest love (as God defines love) means to make the deliberate decision to do the will of God (His commandments.)

For Paul, it meant being faithful (Proverbs 5:18-20). For Meredith, it means not being fearful (1 John 4:18). For Gracie, it means renewing her commitment to her own husband (Ephesians 5:33). For Joan and Keenan, it means trusting each other enough to be honest with one another. (I John 3: 18).

The choices we make in life determine how much love we can give and receive. So many people in our culture get it wrong by confusing the physical act of sex with the spiritual nature of love. The two are not mutually inclusive. They operate on different planes of reality.

God defines love and leaves it for us to choose whether we will accept it or not. Choosing God's love and claiming it for our lives is the wisest decision we can ever make.
