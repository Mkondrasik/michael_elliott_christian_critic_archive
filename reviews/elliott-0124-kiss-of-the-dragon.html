Kiss of the Dragon

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 6, 2001
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Deuteronomy 32:301 Samuel 14:6Joshua 23:10

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: God’s power; courage; overcoming odds; trusting God

Author-verified transcription

For the record, I have nothing against martial arts films. Many of them have proven to be quite enjoyable and the artists who perform in them can be quite amazing. Kiss of the Dragon is neither enjoyable nor amazing. The best accolade I can muster for it would be “dull.”

Jet Li (Romeo Must Die) stars as Liu Jian, but in an apparent nod to the linguistic ineptitude of American audiences, the Parisian bad guys just call him “Johnny.” He comes to France as part of an unexplained “helping hands” program between law enforcement officials of different nationalities.

Of course, once on Parisian soil, he is framed for a murder he did not commit. And though he has proof of the actual murder in his hand, he spends the next hour and a half single-handedly destroying the corrupt police force who set him up instead of exposing the bad guy.

Added to the mix is a corn-fed gal from North Dakota turned French hooker... against her will, of course. She’s being forced to turn tricks by the same French cop who is out to disgrace Liu Jian. Bridget Fonda (Monkeybone) plays the distraught Jessica whose young daughter is being held in “police custody.”

As they often contain ridiculous plots, the saving grace to “chop socky” movies comes instead from how well the action sequences are filmed. In this instance, director Chris Nahon does not demonstrate the finesse needed to elevate Kiss of the Dragon above the ranks of B-movie status.

Jet Li, while highly accomplished in the martial arts, does not have the polished leading man persona of a Bruce Lee or Jackie Chan. He may have the skill to pull off a largely non-speaking supporting role as he did in Lethal Weapon 4, but he’s got a ways to go if he’s going to be expected to carry a picture like Kiss of the Dragon.

Bridget Fonda is capable of much more than what we see of her here. She, like costar Tcheky Karyo (The Patriot) who plays the sinister Parisian cop, falls victim to an underdeveloped script which demands illogical actions from the characters it manipulates. Both deserve better. Neither come off looking particularly well.

Jet Li has been very vocal in his observation that Kiss of the Dragon is an R-rated film and as such is not suitable for children. Indeed, the violence quotient is quite high. Some martial arts films depict a sanitary version of violence. Not so in this film, where people who lose... die. And they die violently. Parents should take note.

A gimmick that runs throughout the film is the bracelet of acupuncture needles that Liu Jian wears and uses during the course of the action. The title of the film is even derived from a particular “forbidden” position of said needles. A position that will bring a particularly gruesome, convulsive death.

Among the various fight scenes choreographed in the film, is one where Liu Jian stumbles into a classroom filled with dozens of martial arts students against whom he must contend. Even though the astronomical odds are stacked against him, he doesn’t shrink in fear. Fully confident, he takes them on and defeats them all.

There have been times when God’s chosen men have had to stand to do battle against mightier and more numerous opponents. Jonathan and his armor bearer took on twenty Philistines which led to a triumphant victory for the Israelites; Samson defeated a thousand men armed with nothing more than a jawbone of an ass; Gideon and three hundred men defeated the Midianites whose multitude was said to be numbered as sand by the seashore.

We should always remember that when we stand with God, no obstacle is too great; no opponent is too mighty.

How should one chase a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight, except their Rock had sold them, and the LORD had shut them up? Deuteronomy 32:30 (KJV)
