Ocean's Eleven

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 7, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 5:20Luke 16:15Revelation 3:17

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Good and evil; crime; moral standards; greed; discernment

Author-verified transcription

Hollywood "cool" was once defined (rather self-servingly) by The Rat Pack (Frank Sinatra, Dean Martin, Sammy Davis Jr., Joey Bishop, and Peter Lawford), a small band of Vegas chums who downed martinis, chased women and made a few forgettable pictures together. One of them, Ocean's Eleven, has now been remade by sizzling hot director Steven Soderbergh (Traffic, Erin Brockovich) with an A-list of Hollywood talent. George Clooney, Brad Pitt, Julia Roberts, Matt Damon, Don Cheadle, and Andy Garcia starring together in the same picture? What could be cooler than that?

As it turns out, a lot. Not that Ocean's Eleven isn't without its charm. That, it has in abundance. It's just that this "Ocean" isn't all that deep.

The minute Danny Ocean (George Clooney, Out of Sight) is released from prison, he wastes no time in planning his next job. Going for the big score, his newest target is a trio of Las Vegas casinos owned by Harry Benedict (Andy Garcia, The Untouchables). The payoff is over 150 million dollars and to claim it, all he need do is to break through a security system that is the envy of most nuclear missile bases. He's going to need help. Lots of help.

His crew of con men include his second in command (Brad Pitt, The Mexican), a novice pickpocket (Matt Damon, Good Will Hunting), a demolition expert (Don Cheadle, Traffic), an aging con artist (Carl Reiner, The Dick Van Dyke Show), a pair of getaway drivers (Casey Affleck, American Pie 2 and Scott Caan, American Outlaws), a blackjack dealer (Bernie Mac, Life), an electronics nerd (Edward Jemison, Schizopolis), a financial backer (Elliott Gould, M*A*S*H) and a Chinese acrobat (Shaobo Qin in his film debut). This hodgepodge crew of oddballs are the eleven of the title. Thrown in amongst all this testosterone is Julia Roberts (Erin Brockovich) who adds a wrinkle or two as Ocean's ex-wife and Benedict's current girlfriend.

Soderbergh uses the neon "glitz" of Las Vegas to good advantage here. Everything is shine and sparkle, a polished veneer that helps to hide the lack of substance beneath the surface.

The film starts off slowly with the finding and the formation of Ocean's team. Once the plan is hatched and implemented, the ride begins in earnest and picks up sufficient momentum to carry it the rest of the way.

All members of the cast do what has been asked of them. As with the original Rat Pack, Clooney and company don't lose themselves within some complex characterization. For the most part, these are movie stars, pure and simple, who play off their Hollywood personas. There's a playful ease to the picture; good, unselfish ensemble work by the cast; and a sure hand behind the camera. All in all, it's an enjoyable couple of hours spent in a darkened theater. Our appreciation will last just long enough for the lights to come up and then we'll be moving on to more substantial fare.

Of course, it should be pointed out that once again we are made to applaud the resourcefulness and ingenuity of a charming band of high tech bandits. Nothing new here... we're talking about Hollywood standards after all. This year has already given us many films where the "good guys" also happen to be criminals: Bandits, The Score, Heist, American Outlaws... the list keeps growing. We should pause to remember that such cinematic "heroes" are hardly praiseworthy.

Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter! Isaiah 5:20 (KJV)

There is nothing charming about thievery, the lust for riches, or the attempt to become rich without earning it through honest and legitimate labor. While this may sound strangely like an old-fashioned sentiment, it remains the truth... truth that most of us recognize. Is it only Hollywood that has an unhealthy fascination with crime and criminals?
