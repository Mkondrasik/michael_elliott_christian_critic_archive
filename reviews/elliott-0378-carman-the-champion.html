Carman: The Champion

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 2, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 22 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Galatians 5:6James 2:81 John 3:18

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love; faith; motivation; Christian witness

Author-verified transcription

In the past, feature films and Christian entertainment have not been merged with overwhelming success. Although during the past few years the efforts to incorporate a Christian message into big budget motion pictures have increased, those released have received mixed reactions from the public and generally negative ones from the critics. All that may be about to change with Carman: The Champion.

Christian recording artist Carman conceived and stars in this new motion picture which bears some resemblance to Sylvester Stallone's Rocky. But more than a pale derivative, The Champion is indeed a mainstream movie with a Christian center that should have some appeal to those of all beliefs.

Carman plays ex-boxing champion Orlando Leone who, after refusing to take a dive in a title bout, retires from the ring to follow his late father's dream of building a center for inner city youths and adults who need a place to help them improve their lives. Ten years later, a pastor of a small but vibrant inner city church, he is struggling financially and at risk of losing the building which he has invested all his savings to buy.

While working nights as a security guard for a high class hotel, Orlando must quell an out of control party in the room of the current cruiserweight champion, Keshon Banks (Jeremy Williams in his film debut). When Orlando knocks the drunken champ out with one punch, the stage is set for a revenge match on which the title, Keshon's pride, and Orlando's dream of building his center are all on the line.

Carman does a fine acting job and looks the part of a professional fighter who is just a little past his prime. The boxing sequences are believable and well choreographed. For his fans, Carman also exercises his musical talent by recording six new songs which are featured on the film's soundtrack.

Patricia Manterola (Acapulco, Cuerpo y Alma) is cast as Orlando's love interest, a single mother with a young son who is mixed up with a bad crowd. Michael Nouri (Flashdance) plays Freddie who is in the conflicting position of being the brother of Orlando and the manager of Keshon. Jed Allen (Beverly Hills 90210) is on hand as the crooked boxing promoter.

The film is not perfect: the romance between Orlando and Allia is forced upon us too quickly; the boxing promoter is too openly evil; the brother's characterization is a little muddled; and gambling, win or lose, is not something that should be encouraged as a way to receive deliverance... These are minor glitches however and they do not get in the way of our appreciation for the total production.

Director Lee Stanley (Held For Ransom) keeps the action moving and is adept at framing the fight scenes. The real success of the filmmakers however is how they avoid the trap of overt proselytizing which has been the downfall of other Christian projects. In The Champion, Orlando's faith is at the center of all he does but it is not necessarily verbalized in every scene.

The story stays in the forefront of the film which keeps the audience involved and interested.

We see one of the greatest lessons that can be taken from the film by examining the motivational forces which direct the characters' actions: Keshon was motivated by his pride; the boxing promoter was controlled by his insatiable greed. Orlando's motivation was the greatest of all. It wasn't pride, greed, jealousy, or ambition. All that Orlando did, he did for love and out of a genuine desire to help others.

For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love. Galatians 5:6 (KJV)

The word "worketh" in the above verse is the Greek word "energeo" from which we can see the English word "energize." The motivation of love fuels or energizes our faith or believing. And it is our believing that can move mountains. Carman's believing in this project and his love for God clearly shows in the final result.
