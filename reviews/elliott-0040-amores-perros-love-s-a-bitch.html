Amores perros (Love’s A Bitch)

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 6, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 13:4-8 Galatians 5:14-15 2 John 1:6

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love; consequences; false/selfish love; violence/cruelty

Author-verified transcription

Whenever a film starts out with a disclaimer that states “no animals were harmed in the making of this picture” we should know that we’re about to see scenes depicting the abuse or mistreatment of the animals that weren’t harmed.

The Mexican film, Amores perros (Love’s A Bitch), by new filmmaker Alejandro González Iñárritu has such scenes in great abundance. But for all the cruelty suffered by the many dogs in the film, the cruelty which the human characters inflict upon each others (and themselves) is just as brutal and damaging.

The two and one half hour film tells three distinctly different stories woven so that the main characters in one tale serve as the secondary characters in the others. In all three stories, which are set in Mexico City, dogs and the relationships they have with their masters are predominantly featured (hence the play on words inserted in the title.)

First up is the most graphically violent of the three tales. Octavio (Gael Garcia Bernal) loves his brother’s wife (Vanessa Bauche) but lacks the financial resources to convince her to run off with him. That is, until he discovers that his dog, Cofi, is a natural killer. Repeatedly pitting the “can’t lose” Mastiff against other battle-scarred canines in back alley dogfights soon has him amassing both wealth and enemies.

Iñárritu then dramatically shifts gears for the second tale, which leaves the squalor and the bloody environs of the dog-eat-dog life of the streets for the more privileged status of the rich and famous. Daniel (Álvaro Guerrero), a successful magazine publisher, has decided to leave his wife and children to set up house with a gorgeous supermodel (Goya Toledo) who also adores him. Their airbrushed picture perfect relationship begins to show its blemishes however after she becomes badly injured in a devastating car wreck. The disintegration of their “love” is mirrored by their futile attempts to rescue her beloved pooch who is trapped beneath the floorboards of their apartment.

The third story deals with a vagrant (Emilio Echevarría) known as El Chivo, translated The Goat. A onetime revolutionary who now roams the streets with his pack of mangy but devoted mutts, El Chivo is also a hired assassin. A life-changing realization forces El Chivo to reflect upon his life and family which he lost to an ideal that was never reached.

Iñárritu’s debut film has received a great deal of worldwide acclaim, having won numerous film festival awards as well as nominations for both the Oscars and Golden Globes. The acclaim is certainly understandable. Though not for all tastes, including mine, it is nevertheless an impressive piece of artistry coming from a novice filmmaker. Raw and edgy, it has an energy and vitality that is undeniable. It is also brutal and uncompromising in its depiction of the seedier elements of an impoverished country. It contains many images which are quite unsettling or disturbing to see.

While all the actors are deserving of commendation, the work of Emilio Echevarría as El Chivo is especially noteworthy. His multifaceted performance keeps us continually guessing as to what might exist beneath the grimy, street person veneer he presents to us.

It may surprise some to find that upon reflection, this intensely violent, graphically bloody film is about love. Or rather, it is about what love isn’t. In each story, the main characters make poor decisions. Some because they are pursuing what they think is love; others because they turn away from the love that is already in their lives. Either way, the characters learn their lessons the hard way... through the consequences of their wrongful actions.

Of course, we disagree with the tongue in cheek title of the film. We much prefer God’s definition of this oft-confused state of being:

Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres. Love never fails. 1 Corinthians 13:4-8a (NIV)
