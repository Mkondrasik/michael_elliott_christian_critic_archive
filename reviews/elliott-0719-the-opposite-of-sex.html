Author-verified transcription

Homosexuality continues its wild popularity ride through Hollywood. Last month, there was The Object of My Affection. Now, here comes The Opposite of Sex.

In the former, a homosexual was seen as a hugely popular elementary school teacher. In this film, the lead homosexual is an independently wealthy high school teacher. Two films... two sympathetic homosexual characters working as educators of our young... Hello?? Could there be a subliminal message being communicated here?

The major difference between the two movies is that The Opposite of Sex happens to be both well-written and well-made. The Object of My Affection was neither. It could simply be written off as a bad movie. The Opposite of Sex can not be as easily dismissed.

Here's the plot: Christina Ricci (The Ice Storm) is Dedee Truitt, a 16 year old package of trouble with a highly developed wit (which she is not afraid to use), a highly developed body (ditto), and a completely underdeveloped sense of morals and ethics.

Upon her step-father's death, she runs away, making a bee line for the Indiana home of her older half-brother, Bill (he's the aforementioned homosexual school teacher). After she manipulates her way into his home, she sets her aim a little lower and goes after his sexual partner, Matt (Ivan Sergei of Dangerous Minds).

Being no match for this devious little tramp, Matt soon finds himself playing for the other team (if you know what I mean). Things then begin to happen in quick order. Dedee announces that she's pregnant, which forces Matt to announce to Bill that he's no longer "gay". After Bill kicks them out of the house, Matt's other sexual partner, the pierced and flamboyantly homosexual Jason (Johnny Galecki of Suicide Kings) appears. He blames Bill for Matt's departure so he makes up a story about how Bill sexually accosted him as a student. Bill and Lucia (she's the sexually frustrated sister of Bill's previous sexual partner who died of AIDS) head out in search of Matt and Dedee so Jason will recant the story and life can return to... well, we can't really say normal, can we?

For a movie titled The Opposite of Sex, its characters sure do seem to spend a lot of time engaged in it. Writer/Director Don Roos (Boys On The Side) keeps us involved in this complex plot of his by having the trashy-mouthed Dedee narrate throughout the entire film. It is an odd narration to be sure, but it works for the film.

Christina Ricci does a marvelous job in her role of a bad little girl who promises not to get any better. "I don't have a heart of gold and I don't grow one later," she tells us in her opening narrative.

Martin Donovan (The Portrait of a Lady) is the bland but perpetually nice Bill. Lisa Kudrow (TV's Friends) can't seem to shake her Phoebe characterization enough to make us believe her as Lucia (pronounced Loo-sha). Lyle Lovett (Short Cuts) makes an appearance as Carl, the cop who befriends Bill and Lucia (while looking and acting exactly like... well, like Lyle Lovett).

The major disappointment about The Opposite of Sex is not the witty script, which is very humorous in its offbeat way. Nor is the problem with the acting or directing, both of which are superior. The disappointment lies in the fact that none of these characters are remotely likable. Quite frankly, we don't care about them. Their selfish and self-centered behavior demonstrates a complete disregard for how their actions may affect others.

Dedee, at 16, has already perfected lying. She shows herself to have no aversion to stealing; no remorse as she destroys lives; no compunction against killing. That right there tells you whose influence she's under. The devil is known as the father of lies (John 8:44). His goals, as stated in John 10:10, are to steal, kill, and destroy. It looks like Dedee's batting 1.000 in the devil's ball game.

Mr. Roos even takes a few pot shots at the Christians depicted in this movie. They are portrayed as either ill-educated backwoods rubes or as hypocritical, ill-mannered bullies. Either way, not a flattering picture.

Homosexuals, on the other hand, seem to be flourishing. Dedee, commenting on how AIDS was thought to be a governmental plan to rid the world of homosexuals, said, "It's not working. Look. There seems to be plenty to go around."

It probably does seem like there are more homosexuals today then ever before. But obviously it is not because they are genetically reproducing. (Like, duh!) Homosexuality is something that has to first be accepted in one's mind before it is manifested physically. If there are more homosexuals today, it is because more people are open to the idea of homosexuality. There is no other reason. Movies such as this one, and The Object of My Affection, and My Best Friend's Wedding, and a host of others, are helping to lead people to the increasingly popular notion that homosexuality is something that should be accepted -- even embraced. That is, quite simply... a lie.
