Anna and the King

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 17, 1999
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Joshua 23:10 Leviticus 26:8-9 James 5:16

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Faith against odds; divine strength; leadership; courage

Author-verified transcription

Anna and the King is not your parents' King and I. Like the Rogers and Hammerstein version, it was inspired by the written memoirs of the British schoolteacher Anna Leonowens and her experiences in Siam during the 1860's, but this nonmusical is much more violent and more immersed in adult themes than the G-rated versions. Nobody's whistling a happy tune here.

Jodie Foster (Contact) plays the widowed schoolteacher who accepts a position in the palace of the King of Siam to teach his eldest son the ways of the world (which initially she equates to the ways of England.) Upon spending some time in the foreign culture of Siam, she slowly begins to learn that "most people do not see the world the way it is, but the way they are."

A leader does not have the luxury of such a narrow view. King Mongkut of Siam (Yun-Fat Chow, The Corruptor) desires to lead his people down a path of enlightened progress only to find resistance from those who are distrustful of the western world. A coup appears to be in the making, using the British colony of neighboring Burma as a covering excuse for hostilities.

Change, the king knows, is inevitable. But he also knows that it does not happen overnight. Most of the head-butting that takes place between Anna and the king is derived from her impatience and frustration with the perceived injustices caused by Siamese tradition and thought.

Filmed in Malaysia, the cinematography is quite spectacular. Director Andy Tennant (Ever After) makes the most of the exotic locale, capturing the splendor of the palace courts, the lushness of the terrain, and the sights and sounds of an 1860 Eastern monarchy.

Where the film fails is in the narrative retelling of the story. Weighed down heavily by its self-imposed epic nature, the movie plods from point to point, taking twice as long to reach its destination as needed. The movie's pace and length continually work against it and eventually do it in.

Miss Foster is appropriately proper as the outspoken and opinionated Anna. Quick with a diplomatic and intelligent response to the king's interrogation, it is easy to see how she would have caught the eye (and heart?) of a ruler.

As the Siamese king, the Chinese star Yun-Fat Chow struggles with two languages, Thai and English. Still, he manages to convey the strength, compassion, and internal struggles of a man who carries the future of an entire nation on his shoulders.

Ling Bai (Wild Wild West) plays the tragic concubine Tuptim, who dares to love someone other than the king to whom she was given. Her "treachery" results in a trial where a guilty verdict will result in harsh and brutal consequences. Ironically, it is Anna's interest and involvement that brings about the logical but unexpected results.

During the screening, I had the added privilege to sit next to someone who had spent five years in Thailand (formally Siam). She informed me that while the King and I myth is based upon historical personages, the Thai people insist that the influence this one woman had upon their king is grossly exaggerated.

While I don't doubt that to be true, the film's fictional account does serve to illustrate the impact that one person may have upon many. As we see King Mongkut, astride his horse, boldly facing the traitorous hordes with only two men at his side, I was reminded of this verse:

One man of you shall chase a thousand: for the Lord you God, He it is that fighteth for you, as he hath promised you. Joshua 23:10 (KJV)

Numbers alone do not determine the victor. Joshua, Samson, Gideon and others all faced seemingly unwinnable battles and yet emerged victorious because they believed without wavering in the promises of God. We would do well to remember that the next time the odds appear to be stacked against us.
