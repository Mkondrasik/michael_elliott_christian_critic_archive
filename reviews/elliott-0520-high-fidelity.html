Author-verified transcription

Some men never seem to grow up. They tend to hang onto the past with a death grip which would make any professional wrestler envious, resisting all attempts to mature even as they remain bewildered as to why they keep failing in their relationships with the opposite sex. Rob Gordon (John Cusack, Being John Malkovich) of High Fidelity is such a man.

Rob is the miserable owner of the retro record store, Championship Vinyl. He, along with the two "musical morons" he has working for him, have a cute habit of forming top five lists to demonstrate their vast encyclopedic knowledge of music. After his current girlfriend, Laura (Iben Hjejle, Besat) dumps him, Rob is inspired to muse over his top five breakups of all time, ranked in order of humiliation or emotional devastation. We then are not only granted a peek at his past as he relives the various breakups (starting with the first occurrence in the seventh grade), but are also granted a second look as Rob contacts these ex-girlfriends to try and figure out why women keep leaving him.

Watching this self-absorbed, unfaithful, non-ambitious loser try to rationalize his life, we find that the question of why women leave him to be relatively easy to answer. The real question is why do they stay as long as they do? The answer to that question is found in the performance of Mr. Cusack. His likable demeanor offsets the stereotypically "clueless" male behavior he exhibits when it comes to commitment and adult relationships.

Most of the humor of this quirky comedy can be found in the record store scenes between the two different employees who share a snobbish attitude and "elitist" taste in pop music. Jack Black (Cradle Will Rock) plays Barry, diehard rock music fan who may have sat too close to the stage speaker during a KISS concert. He not only marches to a different drummer, he cranks the volume up to 11 so others may hear the beat.

Todd Louiso is the introverted and mousy Dick whose nebbish ways serve as a comedic foil to Barry's exuberance.

Director Stephen Frears (The Hi-Lo Country) and a team of writers use the convention of having their central character directly address the camera in an extended narration in this adaptation of the novel by Nick Hornby which has developed a British cult following. Because of Mr. Cusack's disarming self-deprecation, the convention works, giving us a humorous albeit shallow insight into a male psyche. "Do I listen to pop music because I am miserable or am I miserable because I listen to pop music?" is about as deep as Rob's introspective analysis goes.

The filmmakers have also been able to entice a number of recognizable names to fill the supporting roles. Catherine Zeta Jones (Entrapment) and Lily Taylor (The Haunting) are two of the women from Rob's past. Lisa Bonet (The Cosby Show) plays a popular singer with whom Rob has a brief fling. Tim Robbins (Arlington Road) has some amusing moments as a suitor vying for the affection of Rob's current girlfriend. There's even a surprise appearance by a modern rock "legend" who offers Rob advice on matters of the heart.

The best advice he could receive is obvious to us all: "Grow up and take responsibility!" Rob's stunted development, preventing him from becoming a mature adult, is at the root of all his problems. Growth is a sign of life. As individuals full of life, we should always be seeking ways to grow and further ourselves. This would naturally include ways in which to grow spiritually.

But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night. And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither; and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper. The ungodly are not so: but are like the chaff which the wind driveth away. Psalms 1:2-4 (KJV)

When we consider the alternative, growing up sounds downright appealing.
