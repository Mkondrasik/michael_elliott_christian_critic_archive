Author-verified transcription

Just like Pokemon The First Movie, Pokemon the Movie 2000 opens with a short animated film -- this one called Pikachu's Rescue Adventure. For all the electricity emanating from Pikachu's chubby little cheeks, there is little spark to this boring "pokemon characters only" piece of nonsense which centers around Pikachu and friends saving some eggs from a powerful storm. Thankfully, the main feature, subtitled The Power of One, is not as excruciating to sit through.

Yes, the characters are still simplistically drawn, colored, and animated. Yes, the Pokemon characters still speak gibberish. But the background graphics have improved to the point where there is some degree of artistry to be appreciated. And the screenplay includes a touch more wit and humor.

The story is also much more interesting than the first venture. It centers around a plan of a Pokemon collector who desires to capture a rare Pokemon sea monster named Lugia. To do so, he must first disturb the balance of the "titans of fire, ice, and lightning," large birds which dwell on three separate islands. In the process, he upsets the balance of nature and threatens to bring destruction to the entire planet Earth.

But Ash Ketchem and his friends are on the scene and somehow Ash has been designated as the only one who can restore balance and calm the natural elements. Following the tribal traditions of the nearby island residents, Ash must collect three glowing spheres from each of the islands of the three titans. Then everything will be okay.

This rather simplistic plot sets the stage for a much more exciting film than its predecessor. There's lots of adventure and more thrilling scenes of bravery and rescue. There is also a higher degree of self-aware humor in the dialogue. The incredibly wealthy "villain" admits that he is not a Pokemon trainer, he is a collector. "It all started with one Mew card, and look where it has led," he states, gesturing around his affluent surroundings.

Later our hero, whose unusual name is mentioned in a tribal legend, wishes that his mother named him Bob instead of Ash. There's even a plot twist involving Team Rocket, Ash's chief rivals.

As far as content is concerned, it is G-rated with nothing overtly offensive in the film. Most of the action/violence occurs between the clashing titans whose interaction upsets the natural balance of the elements which Ash must then overcome to fulfill the tribal prophecy.

Near the end of the film, there is a brief scene with Ash's mother where she apparently gives him carte blanche to do whatever he wants (needs?) to do without seeking her approval or permission. This obviously contradicts Ephesians 6:1 which explicitly instructs children to obey their parents. Still... overall, Pokemon the Movie 2000 is a rather innocuous piece of entertainment for our children though I would still recommend monitoring to ensure that they do not get swept away by the whole Pokemon marketing craze.

The idea of a world's savior is of course nothing new to us. In the film, Ash's arrival was predicted by tribal legend. As the "chosen one," Ash and Ash alone, would be the one able to bring salvation and hope to people. The overriding theme of this installment of the Pokemon series is that one man can make a difference. To that we can soundly say, "Amen."

We too have a book of prophecies in which a savior is named. Jesus Christ, the only begotten son of God, came to fulfill those prophecies and bring salvation to all men.

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. John 14:6 (KJV)

Today, because of the spiritual "power of one," we all have access to the prevailing power of God. One man, Jesus Christ, makes all the difference in the world.
