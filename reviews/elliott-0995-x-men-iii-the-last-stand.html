X-Men III: The Last Stand

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 26, 2006
Rating
3 stars
Running time
1 hr: 44 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Joshua 24:15Deuteronomy 30:19John 17:14

Sermon / spiritual topic: Choice; Free Will

Author-verified transcription

The third installment to the X-Men franchise brings the conflict between humans and mutants to a violent close. With a new director and a lot of familiar faces, The Last Stand is serviceable entertainment that won't surprise too many fans but neither should it disappoint them too badly.

When a pharmaceutical company discovers a cure for mutation, the lines are quickly drawn. Those who view their mutant powers to be a burden look to the announcement with hope. Others see the discovery as a threat to their existence. Professor X and his X-Men sit on the sidelines, preaching patience and tolerance.

It is a message lost on Magneto who is forming a mutant army to stop the "cure" from being distributed. He has a formidable secret weapon in the form of Jean Gray (Famke Janssen) who spectacularly died in the last film. She has risen from the dead as Phoenix, a deeply troubled but powerful force.

Brett Ratner, replacing Bryan Singer who left one superhero franchise to direct another one (Superman Returns), has taken great care to match the tone and look of the previous films. He must juggle screen time between multiple characters (not always successfully) but by keeping the film's focus on the action rather than the relationships, he sidesteps the film's inadequacies and keeps the story moving at an accelerated pace.

The Last Stand sees the demise (by death or cure) of a number of familiar characters as well as the introduction of a few more. Hugh Jackman returns as a slightly tamed Wolverine. He still good for an acerbic barb or two but he's a shadow of his former independent self. Storm's (Halle Berry) role is beefed up a bit although she doesn't do much with the additional screen time. Other favorite cast members such as Cyclops (James Marsden), Mystique (Rebecca Ramijn), Rogue (Anna Paquin) and Professor X (Patrick Stewart), are given a decent scene or two and then relegated to the sidelines.

Kelsey Grammer is a good addition to the cast as Hank McCoy/Beast, a furry blue mutant turned politician/diplomat. Vinnie Jones, as the helmet wearing Juggernaut, isn't as interesting a newcomer. Cameron Bright adds to his roster of creepy kid roles as Jimmy, whose body contains the X-gene cure.

The thematic question at the heart of The Last Stand is the idea of choice or free will. Those who have the mutant gene are given an opportunity to suppress it and become "normal." The big question remains: Who defines normal?

Keeping a clear mind in the midst of a diverse range of opinions is sometimes difficult. What we should remember is that God granted every man free will. Forcing one's own will upon another is flat out wrong. In The Last Stand we see the issue from both sides. The government tries to impose its "cure" upon those mutants it most fears; and Magneto tries to destroy the "cure" so that no mutant could ever make the choice to change back to a human form.

Both parties are in the wrong because both parties deny the individual's right to choose. Whether a person chooses well or poorly is not the primary issue. It remains to each one of us to make our own choice and deal with whatever consequences come as a result of that choice.
