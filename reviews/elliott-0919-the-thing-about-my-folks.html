The Thing About My Folks

Sermon / spiritual topic: Marriage

Author-verified transcription

In the book of Ephesians, God refers to marriage as a "great mystery." Those who find themselves in the middle of one know firsthand just what an understatement God's description can be. In The Thing About My Folks, Ben Kleinman (Paul Reiser) tries to unravel the befuddling mystery of his parents' marriage.

His father, Sam (Peter Falk), has arrived unexpectedly on Ben's Manhattan doorstep with news that shakes the family to the quick. It appears that his wife of 47 years (Olympia Dukakis) decided to leave him with no explanation other than an enigmatic note taped to the refrigerator. While Ben's sisters leap into investigative action (with plenty of unsolicited speculation), Ben's job is to keep his father distracted by taking him on a road trip. He was planning to look at a house in upstate NY anyway.

The trip turns into a bonding experience for both of them. As Ben, who narrates the film, explains, "The best time I had with my father was during the worst time of his life."

Paul Reiser's screenplay, which he wrote as a tribute to his own father, focuses primarily on the father-son relationship. With an eye acutely trained for the nuances of human interaction, Reiser has crafted characters that are immediately familiar.

Not that the screenplay is without its flaws. It could have used a rewrite or two to raise the comedy bar a bit. The dialogue is surprisingly salty for a family oriented film. And the scenes where Ben and his dad attract a mother/daughter combo, both of whom appear to be 20 years too young for them, seem out of place for the overall tone of the film.

The good news is that Peter Falk continues to prove he is incapable of turning in a performance that isn't warm and lovable. Even when he's depicting someone who is irritating on the outside, part of us finds ourselves delighted to be with him.

Paul Reiser's main contribution to the project is his screenplay. As an actor he's perhaps too close to the material to do it full justice. It seemed to me that he rushed through many of the transitional moments in the characters' relationship. Instead of allowing his character to butt heads against Falk's exasperating but lovable Sam, he often slid past the conflict to arrive at an accord too quickly.

There are no wild revelations or surprising plot twists. Sam's marriage is no more of a mystery than anyone else's... or less of one for that matter. It is what it is — a unique relationship forged by the mutual acceptance of two willing participants.

Even though God provides the overall framework for how a marriage is structured, there is great maneuvering room within that structure. Each marriage is arranged differently based upon the decisions, compromises, and agreements made within that marriage. Within the God-given structure, each husband and wife must reach a consensus on what works for them while keeping in mind the needs of each other.

"The wife hath not power of her own body, but the husband: and likewise also the husband hath not power of his own body, but the wife." 1 Corinthians 7:4 (KJV)

When each partner within a marriage focuses on how best to meet the needs or bless his/her spouse, that marriage is on a successful track. When either member chooses to shift his focus inward to act selfishly - without considering the impact the decision will have on the person he or she has vowed to love and honor - the union begins to move off course and onto a rocky road.
