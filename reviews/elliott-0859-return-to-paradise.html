Return to Paradise

Rating
3 stars

1 hr: 51 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Sacrifice; Choices; Moral Dilemma

Author-verified transcription

The irony of the title Return to Paradise is forcefully felt. For the three friends who indulged themselves in the cheap drugs, easy sex, and temperate climate that was available in Malaysia, perhaps in their minds, they were living in a kind of paradise. But a couple of years later, when two of them are asked to return, it is clear that it will not be to such "idyllic" conditions.

The reason is this: After Sheriff (Vince Vaughn, Pyscho) and Tony (David Conrad, The Last Days of Disco) returned to the United States, Lewis (Joaquin Phoenix, U Turn) was arrested in Malaysia for drug possession. Drugs that the three of them bought while together. Under an unusual and strictly enforced Malaysian law and because of the quantity of drugs involved, unless Sheriff and Tony willingly return to accept their share of the responsibility, Lewis will be hung as a drug trafficker. If they do return, they will be imprisoned for up to six years in some pretty horrifying conditions.

Director Joseph Ruben (Money Train) and screenwriters Wesley Strick (The Saint) and Bruce Robinson (Jennifer Eight) pose an interesting moral dilemma. Just as we come to a conclusion as to what we might do if we were in the same situation, they throw in a twist or wrinkle that forces us to rethink our position.

Vince Vaughn does a superbly credible job as Sheriff, a man for whom the dilemma is not theoretical. Having every reason in the world not to return to Malaysia, and with all the self-preservation within him telling him to stay, how can he ignore the growing recognition that another man's life is dependent upon his actions and decisions? Mr. Vaughn plays the internal conflict for all it's worth.

Anne Heche (Six Days, Seven Nights) is Beth Eastern, the American lawyer working hard to procure freedom for her client. Miss Heche keeps a lid on the emotions which are evidently churning beneath her practiced demeanor thus bringing a controlled tension to all of her scenes.

Joaquin Phoenix is the hapless Lewis. Already having spent two years in a Malaysian prison and with the prospect of death looming large on the horizon, Mr. Phoenix's character is clearly a man on the edge, if not already pushed over. It is a courageous performance.

"Would we go back?" is the question that Return to Paradise asks us to consider. If it were us, would we leave the relative comfort and freedom that we have and place ourselves in tortuous conditions for the sake of a friend? What if we didn't really know the person all that well? What if there were no guarantee that our sacrifice would change anything?

The movie very ably strips away the rationalizations that we oftentimes use to justify our decisions and boils the question down to this: "Do we have the inner strength to do what is right?"

Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends. John 15:13 [KJV]

True love is selfless, reflected in the love of Jesus Christ which he manifested by dying for our sins. He had free will. He could have avoided crucifixion at any time.

Thinkest thou that I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels? Matthew 26:53 [KJV]

Sheriff and Tony could easily ignore the situation, let Lewis suffer the consequences of their joint action (no pun intended) alone, and avoid the sacrifice that they are being asked to make.

I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service. Romans 12:1 [KJV]

Our very lives are to be a sacrifice to God. We could easily ignore that calling and continue to live in ignorance or defiance of God's will. Or we could accept the responsibility that we have been given as God's children and submit ourselves totally to Him. The difference in the analogy is this: By submitting to God and His Word, we someday will truly return to paradise.
