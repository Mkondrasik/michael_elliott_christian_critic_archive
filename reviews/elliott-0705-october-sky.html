Author-verified transcription

Many people dream about reaching for the stars. But for some, dreaming just isn't enough. October Sky tells the true story of four young high school boys who not only had such a dream, but also the courage to pursue it.

The year is 1957. Russia has just launched Sputnik into orbit. Suddenly, the residents of Coalwood, W. Virginia are linked to the rest of the world as they crane their necks upward to see the man-made satellite streaking across their evening sky. This event makes quite an impression on Homer Hickam (Jake Gyllenhaal, Homegrown), who, after that moment, decides he wants to build rockets.

Easier thought than done. A young boy growing up in Coalwood has only two possible futures: To either be a football hero and win an athletic scholarship to a university or work in the mines like his daddy did before him.

Homer is no jock - a fact made painfully obvious during football tryouts. Homer and his friends are destined for the mines unless they can beat the million to one odds and win the National Science Fair, a feat no student from Coalwood has ever accomplished. The winner of the fair is sure to receive many offers for academic scholarships which is reason enough for Homer and his friends to try.

Suffering through the ridicule of their peers, the derision of the town, the disapproval of their parents, and their own early failures, these boys hold true to their vision, enduring the various pressures and overcoming the many obstacles that could have easily caused them to quit.

Based on Rocket Boys, the autobiographical novel of Homer Hickam, October Sky is, simply put, a Hoosiers for the slide rule crowd. What is most surprising is how effective it is. This movie may do for science "geeks" what Top Gun did for Navy pilots.

Director Joe Johnston (Jumanji) and screenwriter Lewis Colick (Ghosts of Mississippi) have crafted a thoroughly engrossing movie and have elicited wonderful performances from their relatively unknown cast.

Jake Gyllenhaal is spellbinding as Homer, the boy/man with just the right combination of passion and integrity. Chris Cooper (The Horse Whisperer) plays his father, one of the leading forces at the mine. Theirs is a wonderfully dynamic relationship as each man shares the same passion and drive... only focused in different directions. One, for what man can do beneath the earth. The other for what man might do above the earth.

Laura Dern (Citizen Ruth) is perfectly cast as the teacher which inspires and encourages the "Rocket Boys" and their quest.

That encouragement was greatly needed as few treated these boys seriously. Regarding them as kooks, weirdoes, or worse, losers chasing a fool's dream, the town summarily dismissed them at first.

How often as Bible-believing Christians have we felt so spurned? Jesus Christ explained the reason to his disciples:

If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before it hated you. If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you. John 15:18-19 [KJV]

The key for the boys was to stay true to their vision. They were not seeking the approval of their neighbors or classmates. In fact, they were risking that approval in order to attain something that no one (or at least few) thought was possible.

As we strive to keep God's commandments, to dwell in like-minded unity, and to walk the spiritual path of righteousness, we risk being ridiculed and scorned by the unbelieving world. We could take the easy way out and quit. Forsake the Word of God and live by the fluctuating standards of the world. We could gain the approval of men... but at what cost?

The Rocket Boys stuck to their plan, risking all, and as their homemade rocket soared into the heavens, so did the promise of their futures, unbound at long last from the earthbound shackles of their past.
