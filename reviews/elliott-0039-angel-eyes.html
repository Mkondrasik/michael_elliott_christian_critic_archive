Angel Eyes

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 18, 2001
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Revelation 21:4 Isaiah 25:8 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Grief; hope; death; healing; God’s promise; loss

Author-verified transcription

Two emotionally lost and damaged souls are drawn to each other in Angel Eyes, a weepy-eyed romantic drama by director Luis Mandoki (Message in a Bottle.) While the preview trailers seem to hint of a spiritual or supernatural element to the film in the vein of The Sixth Sense, it is merely a tease. Instead, Angel Eyes tells a simple earthbound story of two broken hearts who connect with one another and help each other heal or deal with the pain of past losses.

Sharon Pogue (Jennifer Lopez, The Wedding Planner) is Chicago police officer who must have skipped a few too many anger management classes. Her rough and tumble style is just one indication of the presence of some unresolved issues in her personal life, not the least of which is her growing alienation from her own family.

While in pursuit of a drive-by shooter, she is heroically saved by a bystander who calls himself Catch (Jim Caviezel, Frequency). Catch is an odd sort, a man who wanders the streets of Chicago as if in a daze while doing good deeds for people.

After their encounter, a very slow moving and strange courtship evolves between them, as they gingerly reach out to one another while purposely avoiding those areas too painful to visit. As the relationship develops, the desire to know more about the other’s past becomes greater than the need to forget their own.

Angel Eyes is at best a hit and miss production. It does deliver the emotional goods, providing scenes which will trigger the tear ducts of the more sensitive members of the audience. But the set up of those scenes is often clumsily handled.

Jennifer Lopez is fine as the coarse-talking, aggressive Chicago cop who likes working the graveyard shift because she has trouble sleeping at night. Her tough act is simply a defense mechanism which prevents people from seeing her vulnerability.

Jim Caviezel’s performance becomes irritating after a few minutes. Although we can intellectualize what he is going for, Catch’s unusual trance-like behavior and demeanor is simply too bizarre for us to believe that a policewoman would be comfortable letting him into her personal life, not to mention her home.

The supporting cast, which includes Sonia Braga (Kiss of the Spider Woman) and Shirley Knight (As Good As It Gets), have relatively little to do as the focus remains steadfastly upon the two leads. By far, the most effective portrayal comes from Terrence Howard (The Best Man) as Pogue’s understanding and concerned partner.

Director Luis Mandoki appears to want to keep us guessing as to the identity of Caviezel’s character but the transparent and melodramatic script from Gerald Dipego (Phenomenon) makes it rather obvious right from the beginning. Additionally, the family dynamic which serves as the background story for Lopez’s character includes an incomplete and rather unsatisfying look at spousal abuse... one that remains ultimately unresolved.

The core of the story belongs to Catch and it is his healing process which drives or affects everything else. Grief and how one handles it is at the heart of this production. Grief is a crippling emotion which can linger, laying waste to a life long after the cause of the grief has passed by.

What we can remember is that grief is only of this world. There is a sting to death that will one day vanish. There is an evil in this world that will one day be vanquished. The hope of tomorrow that God gives us to hold onto will help give us the strength to endure the painful events of today.

And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. Revelation 21:4 (KJV)

That is a promise of God which continues to be no small comfort in times of pain or loss. We may still have to live through difficult times, but we never have to face them alone and we never have to lose sight of the hope that God has given us.
