Author-verified transcription

"If you don't believe in God, how can you believe in his adversary?" This is the question posed to Arnold Schwarzenegger (Eraser) in the most recent religious/millenium/end of the world tale, End of Days. Of course, the answer is you can't.. but just try telling Arnold that. As he says to Satan, the prince of darkness, "You think you're bad? I make you look like a choir boy." That was a paraphrase. The actual dialogue was drowned out by the incredulous laughter of the audience.

The plot of End of Days is fairly easy to understand, even if difficult to believe. Satan is coming back to earth to have sex with a woman who was selected for said purpose at the time of her birth. If such a sexual union should take place, the result will be the conception of the antichrist and the annihilation of life as we know it. (This, by the way, is not scripturally accurate nor is it even close to being so.)

Arnold's job, should he choose to accept it (and we know he will), is to provide coitus interruptus until January 1, 2000 at which time Beelzebub must return to the hell from whence he came. It won't be easy. Satan has his devil worshipping followers helping him. Meanwhile, the Roman Catholic church is divided over the issue. Half want to protect the girl, the other half want to kill her. Arnold.. well, he's just doing his job.

End of Days is kind of like The Omega Code, only with more sex, more gore, more explosions, more obscenities, and fewer religious publicists touting the film. Guess what?. Those things did not make for a better movie. Only a louder one.

Mr. Schwarzenegger should be credited with knowing his audience who seem happy to see him struggling against insurmountable odds. His movies have progressed from his combats with human soldiers to robot soldiers to alien creatures to fictional creatures to finally the "Big Cheese" himself... Satan, the source of all evil.

The last one is a step that shouldn't have been taken. The believability of the script is minimal at best. And as wonderful as Mr. Schwarzenegger is at spouting off those memorable one-liners of his earlier films, he isn't really capable of portraying an emotionally and spiritually conflicted moment which this script continually asks of him.

Also in the cast are Gabriel Byrne (Stigmata) as the ultimate villain. Making the most of his time in the darkness, Mr. Byrne exudes the confidence of evil incarnate, showing no remorse, no mercy, and more than a little glee. Robin Tunney (The Craft) as Christine York, Satan's intended, has little to do other than look confused and scared (not unlike many members of the audience.) Kevin Pollak (She's All That) is cast as Arnold's wisecracking sidekick which pretty much seals his "fate" according to the myriad of formulaic films from which End of Days borrows its structure.

Director Peter Hyams (2010) moves the action as briskly as the script by Andrew W. Marlow (Air Force One) will allow. The explosions are bright and loud, the blood is oozing properly, the cast screams on cue. Everything is working the way it should. So why isn't it a better film?

The problem lies in the premise itself. As big and muscular and resourceful as Arnold may be. he's no match for God's arch-enemy. Only two people in history are ever recorded to have been confronted directly by the devil. While we all get confronted daily by the devil's works and his devilish realm, only twice has it been recorded that the devil directly confronted man.

Once, with Eve (Genesis 3:13), when he was successful. The second time, with Jesus Christ (Luke 4:1-13), he was unsuccessful. Jesus Christ's victory over the devil was due to his unwavering belief in the promises of God as recorded in the scriptures. More than once he responded to a devilish attack, "It is written" referring to the standard which God had given man.

With all respect given to Mr. Schwarzenegger's arsenal, the Word of God is the only weapon that is effective against Satan
