The Art of the Steal

Author-verified transcription

With the ever rising crime rates getting national attention and drawing criticism on everything from corrupt legislators to underpaid, overworked law enforcement departments; to poor parenting or general societal problems, it is somewhat surprising that filmmakers continue to go to the criminal elements to represent the “good” guys and provide inspiration for their stories. The Art of the Steal features charismatic players scheming, stealing, lying, double-crossing and wise-cracking their way to another big score. And it is all intended for our entertainment.

Kurt Russell stars as Crunch Calhoun, a wheelman who took the fall for a failed art heist in Poland. The man who threw him under the bus was Nicky, his own half-brother (Matt Dillon), who decided to deal for his freedom by depriving Crunch of his. Because of Nicky, Crunch ended up serving a difficult five and a half years in a Warsaw cell.

Upon his release, Crunch eventually chooses to reunite his crew for another go at the brass ring that will, of course, need to be stolen. This time, the target is a rare, Guttenburg printed edition of the little known Gospel of James. The gang is all there, with nicknames intact: The Rolodex (Kenneth Walsh), The Forger (Chris Diamantopoulos), The Apprentice (Jay Baruchel) and even The Idea Man (Matt Dillon) who squealed on Crunch during their last heist.

This comic caper has its fair share of amusing moments, most of them coming from the odd couple comprised of comedian Jason Jones as an incompetent Interpol agent and veteran actor Terence Stamp as his reluctant informer with a superior intellect and wit. Stamp’s dry observations of Jones’ lack of ability are rich with humor.

Crunch opens the film with a discourse on the importance of trust. That he was making his observations from behind the bars of a prison cell made them even more poignant. Trusting the wrong person will most often bring only sadness and pain. There is a lot that goes into trusting someone: we are placing our reliance, our confidence, our dependence, even our future upon someone else’s character and actions. It is a risk that can cause devastating harm by those who prove unworthy of the trust given them.

“A friend you can’t trust in times of trouble is like having a toothache or a sore foot.” Proverbs 25:19 (CEV)

It is difficult to remain friends with a person we are unable to trust. It would be like putting up with the pain of a decayed tooth or not treating a broken ankle. Trust is an all-important and fragile element to any relationship. All the more reason why our most important relationship – the one we have with God – is cemented by a sure trust that cannot be betrayed. Because God is incapable of lying, He and the words He has given us can be trusted beyond measure.
