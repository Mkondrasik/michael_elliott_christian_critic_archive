Few men have had the opportunity, strength, or courage to literally change the world. Martin Luther did. The events of his life are accurately told in a 1953 black & white film which was nominated for two Academy Awards. This film, titled after its central figure, has recently been released to DVD format in honor of its 50th anniversary. We pray that its new found accessibility will enable a whole new generation to experience and appreciate it.

The film begins in the 16th century with Martin Luther, (British actor Niall MacGinnis), a promising young law student, changing the course of his studies to pursue the spiritual truth that keeps eluding him. He commits himself to God and the church, becoming a monk of the Augustinian order. Despite his arduous studies and devotion, he cannot reconcile the practices of the Roman Catholic Church led by Pope Leo X, with the teachings of Christ and the epistles of St. Paul.

His quest for absolute truth leads him to post a document on the church door of Wittenburg where he served as a priest. He hoped that this document, known as the 95 Theses, would lead to an honest debate of his disagreement with the practical and doctrinal errors he believed to exist within the Roman Catholic Church. Instead, it led to a division within the body of Christ and branded him as a heretic and enemy of the holy Roman empire.

Produced by the Lutheran Film Associates, under the supervision of Robert E. Lee, the former head of the LFA, the film has been long recognized and applauded for its dramatic portrayal of this pivotal time in human history. As an added feature on the DVD, Lee narrates a documentary history on the making and distribution of the film and the impact it has made around the world.

Niall MacGinnis portrays the fiery reformer with an incredible intensity. Luther was a man whose devotion to God and the scriptures was unparalleled. He risked his position, his reputation, and his life in order to protect the integrity of the Word of God. MacGinnis captures not only the intestinal fortitude of the man but also the massive conflicts which rose within him as he found himself forced to choose between obeying God and obeying his church leaders.

The Roman Catholic practice of selling indulgences to its followers was at the heart of Luther's objections. Purchasing an indulgence, the church taught, assured that certain sins would be forgiven. Luther found this practice abhorrent and contradicted by his scriptural studies. The key verse which led him to the spiritual light was found in the book of Romans:

For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith. Romans 1:17 (KJV)

Faith, not works nor money, is the key requirement for salvation. Because Jesus Christ paid the price for our sins once and for all time, then the selling of indulgences is meaningless in terms of God's forgiveness.

Luther's contributions to Christian faith are immeasurable. He believed that scripture must be the sole authority for spiritual truth. Because of this, he believed that scripture must be made available in the language of the common man. We take for granted that we can pick up a Bible and read or study it whenever we want. That wasn't the case in the 16th century. Martin Luther helped to change that and in so doing, changed the world.
