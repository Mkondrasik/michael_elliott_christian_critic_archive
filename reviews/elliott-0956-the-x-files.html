The X-Files

Michael Elliott

June 19, 1998

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Unequally Yoked; Collusion

Author-verified transcription

The X-Files is the latest entry in the seemingly endless Hollywood parade of TV series to make the quantum leap to the big screen. I am, apparently, the last person left in America who has yet to see even one episode of this popular show. At least that is the impression I draw from the incredulous look I received from my teenager when I first shared with him my X-Files naiveté. For this reason, I approached the film with an eerie sensation of being an outsider looking in. How much of the film would I understand? How long before a non-explained reference would send me off into a tailspin of confusion?

I'm happy to say, my concerns were unfounded. True, there are many things left unexplained, but I'm told that is part of what gives The X-Files its vast appeal. Some things are never explained. Others, even after the explanation is given, still can't be understood. Rest assured, even if you can't follow everything, there's enough here to please the avid fan as well as the novice viewer.

The film opens in what appears to be the Antarctic but, in actuality, is purported to be North Texas in the year 35,000 BC. The next scene jumps ahead some 37,000 years to the same site in our day. It quickly becomes evident that this site harbors a deadly alien presence - a virus which, incredibly, has survived all this time. Faster than you can say "cover-up", the victims of the present day alien attack are whisked away by a team of well-organized, well-funded, non-identified people.

We first meet FBI agents (and ex-X-Filers) Fox Mulder and Dana Scully as they casually search for a bomb that shouldn't exist. At least it shouldn't exist where they are looking for it. Of course they find it. Their subsequent investigation leads them (as you knew it would) to things extraterrestrial and conspiratorial.

Director Rob Bowman (who, having directed 25 episodes of the TV series, certainly has a handle on the genre) and screenwriter/producer Chris Carter (who originated the series in 1993) draw us deeply into the world of The X-Files as they keep us guessing and second guessing as to who is who, who knows what, and who can be trusted.

David Duchovny and Gillian Anderson as the FBI team of Mulder and Scully certainly have developed an effective working chemistry during their years together on the TV series. Their characters not only compliment one another well, they also give off all the appropriate signals which indicate to us that there is a strong physical attraction behind their professional relationship. Although The X-Files relies heavily upon special effects, it is the strength of the relationship between these two that keeps this film from becoming just another "Bug From Outer Space" movie.

This is not to say that the special effects are unimpressive. On the contrary, they are most impressive. Be advised that the depiction of the alien virus will probably make your skin crawl and there are many bloody or graphic scenes which may not be appropriate for all ages or temperaments.

When looking for a spiritual lesson to be derived from this science fiction adventure, we could point to the role that those in the mysterious political/industrial cartel play as they operate in collusion with the alien beings. If there is one thing that God continually teaches us in His Word, it's to resist the forces of evil - not cater to them - not negotiate with them - we are to resist them. II Corinthians 6:14-16 (KJV) says it this way:

Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness? And what concord hath Christ with Belial? or what part hath he that believeth with an infidel? And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols?

Of course the answer to all these questions is none! It is impossible to give in to evil without giving up that which is good. This is an important lesson which bears constant repeating seeing as how our real life politicians and business leaders have proven themselves in the past all too capable of forgetting this simple truth.

Lies, duplicity, evasion, secrecy... these are the tools of our adversary - the devil. How much stronger as a people would we be if our country's leaders conducted themselves by the principles of honesty, integrity, decency, and truth? Alas, the way things are going, our generation may never know.
