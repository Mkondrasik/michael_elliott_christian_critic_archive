Enemy at the Gates

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 18, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 4:23-24Colossians 3:10Ephesians 2:14-15

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Renewing the mind; the new man; transformation; truth

Author-verified transcription

Enemy at the Gates is the first of nine war films scheduled to be released in 2001. Rather than use the large scale approach as seen in most epic movies involving a world war, the filmmakers have instead taken a decisive point during WWII and almost allegorically reduced it to the conflict between two men.

Based upon actual personages, Enemy at the Gates is set in Stalingrad during the great siege of 1942. The Russian army is being overwhelmed by the superior strength and training of the German forces and yet are trying to hold the line at Stalingrad. At first, it appears futile. The Soviets are ill equipped to stand against their invaders. What’s more, their main motivation is fear and intimidation. The enemy lies ahead waiting to kill them. Their own generals have machine guns lined up behind them prepared to shoot and kill them if they try to return. For the soldiers, death is almost a certainty.

Danilov (Joseph Fiennes, Shakespeare in Love), a young political officer fighting the propaganda war, offers a better motivation. Hope. To the Russian people he gives a hero behind whom to rally. To the Soviet soldiers, he gives an example to follow. Vassily Zaitsev (Jude Law, The Talented Mr. Ripley), a simple shepherd from the Urals with a deadly accurate aim is promoted to the Soviet sniper division where, thanks to Danilov, his exploits become the stuff of legend.

So successful is this propaganda that the German forces begin to become demoralized. To shift the momentum back to their side, the Germans send their most accomplished sniper, Major Koenig (Ed Harris, Pollack), to draw young Vassily out and kill him.

Thus begins a cat and mouse game between the two snipers set amidst the devastating destruction and massive death that war always brings.

Jude Law portrays a simple man who is elevated to larger-than-life status without buying into his own propaganda. Vassily has a good sense of who he is and what his limitations are. A much more complex character is found in Danilov, the scribe whose idealistic conviction in pure socialism is challenged by the events which swirl around him and when something he desires most eludes his grasp.

Rachel Weisz (The Mummy) portrays Tania, a Russian soldier for whose affections Vassily and Danilov find themselves competing. Ed Harris gives Koenig a steely confidence and aristocratic arrogance as the German sharpshooter.

Director Jean Jacques Annaud has wisely chosen not to have his actors attempt Russian or German accents. While the story is about a battle between those two nationalities, its themes are universal. For the most part, the Russians speak with British accents and the Germans with American. While immediately noticeable, it isn’t particularly distracting.

Being a war picture, Enemy at the Gates is rife with tense scenes and disturbing images. When seen through a sniper’s scope, killing the enemy is not an abstract idea. It is a calculated individual act which, when performed, will result in the taking of a human life. This is a brutal and bloody reality of war which is continually on display throughout the film.

A disillusioned Danilov discovers the fallacy of his socialist philosophy as he proclaims, “Man will always be man. There is no new man. There will always be something to envy.” These are words from a disheartened individual which ring true only because he was looking to a flawed, man-made doctrine for the answers.

Truthfully, there is a “new man” which is every bit the ideal Danilov sought. This “new man” is achieved through the mindset of any believing man or woman who, armed with the love and power of God, chooses to live according to the righteous doctrines of God.

And be renewed in the spirit of your mind; And that ye put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness. Ephesians 4:23-24 (KJV)
