The Day After Tomorrow

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 28, 2004
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Stewardship; creation; environment; responsibility

Author-verified transcription

That fictitious old lady Mother Nature is having a bad day and lashes out at the planet Earth in this summer’s mega-disaster film, The Day After Tomorrow. Helmed by Roland Emmerich (Independence Day), the film uses dazzling special effects, bad science, and heavy-handed politicizing to depict the effects of a modern-day ice age speeded up to the nth degree.

Climatologist Sam Hall (Dennis Quaid), who is studying the effects of global warming, is first to predict the coming disaster, which he believes future generations will have to face. His warnings, of course, are ignored by the Dick Cheney look-alike playing the vice-president. In an obvious leftist dig at the current administration, the president in the film is depicted as being ineffectual and ill-informed.

To Hall’s horror, the weather systems across the globe begin to intensify and become unstable due to the collapse of the North Atlantic Current. Soon every continent faces unimaginable terror as the weather turns exceedingly nasty.

Tornadoes rip through the heart of Los Angeles; a tsunami wave puts all of Manhattan under water; hail the size of grapefruit pelt the Far East; and a freeze is expected that is so severe that any living thing exposed to it for more than a second will be instantaneously and permanently iced.

Because Hall’s earlier warnings went unheeded, there is little that can be done for the country except to hunker down and try to survive until the weather systems stabilize. Waiting isn’t good enough for Hall whose son (Jake Gyllenhaal) is stranded in Manhattan with a few of his schoolmates. After briefing the president on the hopelessness that exists in the northern states, he decides to leave DC and travel (by foot!) to NYC in order to collect his son.

Emmerich, who so memorably blew up the White House in Independence Day, expands his destructive vision The Day After Tomorrow by wiping out the Hollywood sign, the Statue of Liberty, most of Manhattan, and a few other notable landmarks.

The special effects take center stage in this 200 million dollar spectacle – the actors and any semblance of a narrative line are secondary considerations. While the actors gamely give it their all, notably Jake Gyllenhaal as a smart high school student who repeatedly risks his life to save the girl he doesn’t have the courage to ask out on a date. Still, even Jake is eventually swept aside by the overwhelming sensory overload that Emmerich dumps upon us.

The film’s major flaw is in its overt politicizing of the environmental issues that supposedly lie at the heart of this film. The movie’s depiction of the political administration is blatantly a condemnation of the Bush presidency and its arguments devolve into an overly simplistic “I told you so” rationalization unsupported by scientific fact. Frankly, it is hard to imagine anyone giving credence to this piece of exaggerated popcorn entertainment. I’m rather surprised that it has received the kind of political attention that it has.

There is nothing in this film that should drive anyone to become an environmental alarmist. More importantly, there is no biblical evidence supporting the claims of the film’s characters. However, we should note that God does tell us to be a good steward of that which he has given to us. God gave us this earth to be our temporary home. He gave us all of its resources to provide for our physical needs.

And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat. Genesis 1:29 (KJV)

But God did not simply hand us a free meal ticket. He expects us to work and protect what he has given us because he tells Adam in Genesis 2:15 to dress (or work) and keep (or protect) the land where he lived.

We should be thankful and respect everything God has given us. We can best do this by the way we choose to steward and utilize his gifts.
