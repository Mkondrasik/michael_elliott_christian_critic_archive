Special Weapons and Tactics... S.W.A.T. This elite subgroup within the elite organization known as the LAPD, was made culturally famous due to a popular TV show of the 70s. Now, adapted for the big screen, it delivers the kind of highly charged and explosive entertainment that summer moviegoers have come to expect.

There's no place for lone wolves on a S.W.A.T. team, even when their independent actions save lives. When partners Brian Gamble (Jeremy Renner, Dahmer) and Jim Street (Colin Farrell, The Recruit) disregard the orders of their superiors during a hostage situation, resulting in a successful outcome, they are regarded as heroes by everyone except those in command. Bounced from S.W.A.T., Gamble quits the force altogether while Street accepts a demoralizing demotion and waits for a second chance.

That chance comes in the form of Sgt. "Hondo" Harrelson (Samuel L. Jackson, Basic), an "old school" SWAT member who is brought in to help the LAPD with its deteriorating public image. Hondo is ordered to form a new SWAT unit and, though he is advised against it, chooses Street as a primary member of his team.

After intensive retraining set to the familiar S.W.A.T. theme song (da da duhhhh, da da duhhh, da da duhhh, da da duhhh, DA DA DUH!!), they are finally put to the test when a highly dangerous international criminal (Olivier Martinez, Unfaithful), who is wanted in numerous countries on numerous charges, happens to fall into the LAPD's lap.

S.W.A.T.'s mission, should they choose to accept it (sorry... lapsed into another 70s show for a second), is to transfer the multi-billionaire hoodlum into federal custody. It's a job made more difficult after he makes a televised vow to pay 100 million dollars to anyone who can break him out. Suddenly LA becomes a very popular place to be for anyone with automatic weapons and antitank missiles.

Like the TV show from whence it was born, S.W.A.T. is simplistic storytelling. Using stock characters and one dimensional motivations, it merely sets up the needed scenarios for the explosives team and stuntmen to jump into action. Joining Farrell and Jackson on the S.W.A.T. team are Brian Van Holt (Black Hawk Down) and Josh Charles (Dead Poets' Society) as a pair of veteran members, LL Cool J, now being credited as James Todd Smith, (Deliver Us From Eva) as a street cop with an impeccable arrest record, and Michelle Rodriguez (Blue Crush), once again playing a tough as nails character willing and able to mix it up with guys twice her size.

Director Clark Johnson (TV's The Shield) has the proper handle on the material. He's taken the best parts of the source material, stretched it out over 2 hours and blown it up to fit on a multiplex screen. It's a popcorn movie that, while enjoyable, will still most likely fade from memory soon after the lights come up.

Street's decision to stay in a menial job and patiently wait for another opportunity to apply for the S.W.A.T. team is actually an illustration of a biblical principle once employed by Joseph. Born a free man but sold into slavery by his brothers and later imprisoned for a crime he did not commit, Joseph did not complain or rail against his captors. He merely performed the tasks he was given to do to the best of his ability. He eventually became so important to the king he served that he was released from prison and given honor and responsibility that placed him over all other men.

A man's gift maketh room for him, and bringeth him before great men. Proverbs 18:16 (KJV)

If we are faithful to apply what we know to every situation that we are in, including those which on the surface might be deemed as being beneath our station or dignity, we'll find that the light of our lives will shine brightly, bringing honor, respect and glory not only to us but also the God we serve.
