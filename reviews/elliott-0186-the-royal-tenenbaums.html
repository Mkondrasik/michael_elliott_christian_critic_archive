The Royal Tenenbaums

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 4, 2002
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 54:13John 16:33John 14:27

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Family; children; peace; teaching God's Word; parenting

Author-verified transcription

You think your family has problems? Just wait until you get a load of the Tenenbaum clan. In The Royal Tenenbaums, director/co-writer Wes Anderson (Rushmore) is apparently trying hard to put the "fun" back into dysfunctional.

Patriarch Royal Tenenbaum (Gene Hackman, Heartbreakers) sired three genius children... actually two, he adopted the third one. He just didn't hang around long enough to raise them. Now some twenty odd years later, he returns to the family roost. He has to... he's out of money and being evicted from his hotel domicile. He fakes a terminal illness in the hopes that his wife (Anjelica Huston, The Golden Bowl), whom he never divorced, will take him back after so long an absence.

At the same time, the three young Tenenbaums also return with their heads full of neuroses: Chas (Ben Stiller, Zoolander), a financial wizard who made his first million in his early teens has grown paranoid and safety-obsessed after the accidental death of his wife; Margot (Gwyneth Paltrow, Shallow Hal), a playwriting prodigy who hasn't published in years and is unhappily married to a much older man (Bill Murray, Rushmore); and Richie (Luke Wilson, Legally Blonde), a tennis champion who suffered a severe emotional "meltdown" during his last tournament and has spent the last few years at sea haunted by the love he feels for his adopted sister.

Can Royal redeem his own failure as a father by helping his kids get their lives straightened out now that they are in their adult years? That is the question Wes Anderson and co-writer/actor Owen Wilson (Meet The Parents) float out to us in this dark and highly quirky human comedy. This is not a uproarious laugh-inducing comedy, but a quietly affecting film, rich in details and insight into the hidden recesses of a family life as far removed from the Ozzie and Harriet "ideal" as it can get.

As was true with Anderson's earlier film, Rushmore, this latest effort may be too quirky to gain mainstream acceptance. However, audiences attracted to more challenging fare will find that there is much to appreciate. The acting alone elevates The Royal Tenebaums above the norm. Without exception, the cast play their various roles to deadpan perfection. While it is Gene Hackman's performance as Royal which holds the film together, all the supporting roles combine to give The Royal Tenebaums its unique flavor. It is a wonderful ensemble achievement by all involved.

I only wish that I had enjoyed the total film experience as much as I did its individual components. The characters, though admirably portrayed, are difficult to embrace. The quirkiness, for me, wears a bit thin after a while and I found myself wanting to see a stronger narrative force to drive the picture forward.

One of the things I found most telling is the fact that the Tenenbaum children found fame and fortune at an early age and yet, despite their early successes, it is clearly seen that both happiness and peace eluded them in their adult years. For all their accomplishments... for all their wealth... for all their fame... for all their alleged "genius," they still lacked the one element that would have made them complete as individuals and filled the emptiness which ate at their souls.

And all thy children shall be taught of the LORD; and great shall be the peace of thy children. Isaiah 54:13 (KJV)

For all his failings, the greatest disservice that Royal did to his children was to not instruct them in the ways of the Lord. It is in God's love that we find the true peace, happiness, and satisfaction that will endure throughout our lives.
