Punch Drunk Love

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 1, 2002
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Words; edification; love; healing; self-worth

Author-verified transcription

Who knew the clown had layers? In Punch Drunk Love, Adam Sandler (Mr. Deeds) reveals a depth never before explored in his previous films that, even though juvenile, managed to make millions. Ironically, the audience which has made him a very wealthy star will probably not appreciate the one film that proves him to be an actor.

Sandler plays Barry Egan, a young man who has grown up in a household with seven sisters, all of whom make Peanuts' Lucy Von Pelt look like Sister Theresa. Being continually criticized, belittled and bossed (seven times over) has definitely taken a toll on Barry. He's got issues. Serious issues.

He owns his own business, selling toilet plungers wholesale and symbolically, his career choice makes perfect sense. Barry is all stopped up - the king of repressed emotions. When the pressure builds, which it does continually throughout the film, he eventually reaches a point where the slightest thing will cause him to erupt... either in uncontrollable anger or in a torrent of tears.

Into his strange and messed up life comes Lena Leonard (Emily Watson, Red Dragon). She's a decent, normal person with a loving heart. For some unexplained reason, she finds herself smitten with this emotionally damaged individual. Because of this, she is willing to overlook much: his violent acts of rage, his quirky behavior, a roomful of pudding, and much much more.

The film is ostensibly about love... specifically how it can heal and restore even those among us who may appear beyond salvation.

Writer/director Paul Thomas Anderson (Magnolia) developed Punch Drunk Love specifically with Sandler in mind for the lead. This offbeat romantic comedy has its darker elements but it is shades lighter than Anderson's earlier films. It does include some examples of the director's willingness to experiment and innovate. He's cast a number of amateurs to fill supporting roles in his movie. Of Barry's seven sisters, six of them are non-actors and four of them are related to each other.

Sandler's work shows that he's more than capable of stepping outside of the idiot savant persona that he puts on. His Barry is not unlike other Sandler characters with one notable exception... he casts aside the humorous facade and plays the character straight. It is a brave step for the actor and one that deserves recognition.

As Sandler gets serious, Emily Watson takes this opportunity to lighten up. She is best known for her darkly dramatic roles in Hilary and Jackie, Breaking the Waves, and Angela's Ashes. As Lena, she gets to play a woman in love who has to work up the courage to initiate a relationship with a man too shy and introverted to take the first step.

Philip Seymour Hoffman (Red Dragon) briefly appears as a phone sex entrepreneur and extortionist who figures Barry for another naive pervert from whom he can bilk more money.

What makes a man like Barry become a man like Barry? For years he endured the constant harangue of his contentious sisters. For years he repressed how it made him feel. It should be no surprise that this continual negative reinforcement has led to the stunting of Barry's emotional growth and self-confidence. The words people use definitely have an impact on those who hear them.

Let no foul or polluting language, nor evil word nor unwholesome or worthless talk ever come out of your mouth, but only such speech as is good and beneficial to the spiritual progress of others, as is fitting to the need and the occasion, that it may be a blessing and give grace (God's favor) to those who hear it. Ephesians 4:29 (Amplified)

Despite the many years of negative influence, a kind word, spoken genuinely and in love immediately begins to edify and restore Barry to wholeness. Love provides for him a strength and purpose which he had heretofore never known. His experience is perhaps not unlike the first time that we recognized and accepted the truth that God loves us despite all of our failings and shortcomings.
