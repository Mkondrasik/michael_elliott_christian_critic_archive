Author-verified transcription

He's back. He's a bit older, even nearing obsolescence, but he still packs the same punch that he did almost two decades earlier. Of course I'm referring to Arnold Schwarzenegger (Collateral Damage) in his signature role as the expressionless cyborg in Terminator 3: Rise of the Machines.

It has been ten years since John Connor (Nick Stahl, In The Bedroom) and his mother, Sarah, helped to prevent Judgment Day which was to marshal in the future war between man and machine. Sarah Connor is gone now and John is a loner, living, in his words, "off the grid," and wondering if they really were successful in changing the future.

With the arrival of T-X (Kristanna Loken, D.C.), a female Terminator with improved features followed soon there after by the re-appearance of the T-100 Terminator (Schwarzenegger), Connor gets his answer. They only postponed the inevitable. Judgment Day still lies before them. Failing to locate John Connor, future leader of human resistance, the T-X has been sent back in time to destroy all who would one day become his future lieutenants.

One of them, Kate Brewster (Claire Danes) has just recently become reacquainted with Connor. Coincidentally, her father is in command of the Skynet project, a computer program which will cause machines to become self-aware if it is ever activated.

Connor and Brewster, with the help of the outmatched T-100, must stay one step ahead of termination knowing that they have only three hours to reach Kate's dad and stop Skynet or else all humankind will enter into its darkest days.

Director Jonathan Mostow (U-571) takes the reins from James Cameron who helmed the first two Terminator movies and the franchise is in very good hands. The action sequences are outstanding and the pacing of the film is excellent. There may be a touch too much self-parodying taking place with some of Arnold's humorous one-liners but I suppose that was inevitable given the worldwide familiarity it has achieved.

It doesn't really matter what other roles Schwarzenegger may take during his career. This is the one for which he'll be remembered... and rightfully so. The part is tailor made for him and even with the many years that passed between the sequels, he manages to slip into the persona of the T-100 with ease. Kristanna Loken copies Arnold's mannerisms with a fair degree of success although she lacks something in terms of pure physical intimidation.

It is the character of John Connor which holds the most meaning for us. He knows what the future may hold; knows that it will include pain and suffering; but also is aware of his unique responsibility. It is a situation that our Lord and Savior also found himself. And like John, he also wondered if there could be another way.

And he went a little further, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt. Matthew 26:39 (KJV)

But, as we know, there wasn't. Jesus Christ had to find the strength to endure the pain and suffering so he could fulfill the prophecies and perform all that was required of him. He did so because he understood what was at stake.

Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of [our] faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God. Hebrews 12:2 (KJV)
