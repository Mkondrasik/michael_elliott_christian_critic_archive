Underdog

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 3, 2007
Rating
1½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 24 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 12:7-111 John 3:17Proverbs 3:27

Sermon / spiritual topic: Power

Author-verified transcription

"There is room to jeer... Underdog is here." The rhyme-popping, crime-stopping canine is effectively muzzled in this unimaginative live-action rendition of a 1960's children's cartoon.

In this film, a bomb-sniffing beagle with a defective schnozz, is booted from the police force after an embarrassing front page fiasco. He is then captured by mad scientist, Simon Barsinister (Peter Dinklage), who is experimenting with genetic manipulation.

The beagle, escaping Barsinister's clutches, is doused with a genetic cocktail that gives him super powers. He can fly, talk, crash through walls, bark the fur off of ugly mongrels. What he can't do is make us care about any of it.

Picked up off the street by a kindly security guard (Jim Belushi) to give to his rebellious son, Jack (Alex Neuberger), the dog tries to forget about his unique powers and settle in to become man's best friend. But Jack will have none of it. Embarrassed by his dad's decision to stop being the best cop on the force to take a dead-end job as a security guard for an empty building, he is thrilled to be the owner of a genuine super-hero.

Shoeshine, the name given to the beagle because he likes to lick footwear, isn't that thrilled. He's just waiting for the time when he'll mess things up like he always does.

The only thing familiar here is the catchy but simplistic theme song that opens the show. After that, they might as well have called it Genetic Mutation Dog and pitched it as an original idea. It would have still been a bad idea but at least that would have preserved the enjoyable memories of Underdog.

Actor Jason Lee gives voice to the title character but doesn't bring much to it. His voice somehow doesn't fit the beagle. Jim Belushi adds a little gentle sitcom personality to the mix. Peter Dinklage fares the best as the sinister Barsinister although even he is eventually undone by a terminally weak script penned by no less than three individuals.

That script, it must be said, goes for too many cheap laughs and fails at getting most of those. Thus references to poop, vomit, licking unmentionable areas, and sniffing the same are liberally sprinkled throughout the interminable running time. During the film, a five year old kept pleading with her mother, "I wanna go home." I hear you, kid.

Still, the purpose of this site is to look for meaning in the films we go to see. And even bad films sometimes stumble across some element of truth. For instance, when Jack explains to Shoeshine, "It's not that you have these powers that's important... it's what you do with them."

The same can be said of us. God has given us his spirit to dwell within us and it is capable of tremendous power that we are able to manifest in this world... if we so choose. We could also choose to let God's spirit remain inside us untested, unused and impotent. Having the power is meaningless unless we decide to act.

But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal. For to one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit; To another faith by the same Spirit; to another the gifts of healing by the same Spirit; To another the working of miracles; to another prophecy; to another discerning of spirits; to another divers kinds of tongues; to another the interpretation of tongues: But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will. 1 Corinthians 12:7-11 (KJV)
