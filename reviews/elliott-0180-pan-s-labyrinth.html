Pan's Labyrinth

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 29, 2006
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 3:151 John 2:251 John 5:13

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Eternal Life

Author-verified transcription

A young girl uses her vivid imagination to try to escape the horrors of war and the brutality of man in Guillermo del Toro's 2006 film, Pan's Labyrinth.

Ofelia (Ivana Banquero) has traveled to her new home with her sickly pregnant mother (Ariadna Gil) to join the side of her stepfather (Sergi Lopez) who has summoned them. A captain in Franco's army, the captain is a cruel, vicious man who seems to care only about two things: routing the rebels from the surrounding hills; and being present at the impending birth of his son. All the rest (especially Ofelia) is of little consequence to him.

It is thus certainly understandable that Ofelia escapes into a fantasy world whenever possible. Led by a dragonfly (fairy) down an ancient spiral staircase, Ofelia is ushered into a world and welcomed by a faun (Doug Jones) as its long lost princess. She need only perform three tasks to prove her identity. Unfortunately, fantasy born from a mind of a person surrounded by misery and violence tends to be shaded by the very realities they face. Ophelia is no exception and her "tasks" involve danger, death, and sacrifice.

Guillermo del Toro has made a visually arresting, somewhat disturbing adult fairy tale. I wouldn't go so far to say that he out-grims the Brothers Grimm but his film is very dark and not recommended for children (hence the R-rating).

The film uses a number of CGI effects while depicting Ofelia's adventures and they are quite effective but to Del Toro's credit, the film is not dependent upon them. There is more than enough human drama taking place to hold our attention.

Eleven year old Ivana Banquero was a wonderful find as she perfectly conveys the precarious position of her character. Too old not to understand what is happening but too young to do much about it, her only escape is through her fairy tales. But eventually the reality of her situation begins to invade even her fantasies and will leave her with a choice to make that will have grave consequences.

Sergi Lopez is saddled with the task of playing an evil character with no redeeming qualities but he makes us believe his one-dimensional captain. The other adult actors have the opportunity to give more compassionate portrayals and all are excellent in their respective roles.

Doug Jones takes on two of the more unusual beings Ofelia "meets" in her mind's eye: The all-knowing faun and a frightful creature that is predominantly featured in Ofelia's second nightmarish task.

Early on in the film, Ofelia tells a story to her yet-to-be-born brother. The story is about a rose that holds the promise of eternal life. But because the path to the rose is covered with thorns, no man dares to approach it.

Perhaps we know people who live with the same kind of fear of trepidation - people who, for reasons of their own, have not yet accepted the freely offered gift that God extends to all. Eternal life is a spiritual reality that is available today... and we don't have to fight monsters of our imaginations to attain it. Unlike the rose in the story, God has made that which we most desire readily available to all of us. And all that we must do to claim his gift is believe.

That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life. John 3:15 (KJV)
