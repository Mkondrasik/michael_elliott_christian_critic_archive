Any Given Sunday

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 22, 1999
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Matthew 23:12Matthew 12:251 Corinthians 1:10

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Teamwork; humility; leadership; unity; idolatry

Author-verified transcription

Somewhere in Oliver Stone's Any Given Sunday is a decent football game to be enjoyed. The problem is, Mr. Stone doesn't let us watch or enjoy it.

Tony D'Amato (Al Pacino, The Insider) is the head coach of the fictional Miami Sharks and a man who has sacrificed everything to the game he holds above all. In the midst of a dismal season, the future takes a bleaker turn as his first string quarterback, Cap Rooney (Dennis Quaid, Savior) goes down with a possible career ending injury. After his second stringer is hurt as well, D'Amato finds himself relying on a brash, unproven talent named Willie 'Steamin' Beamen (Jamie Foxx, Booty Call) to lead his team.

Leading a team comprised of spoiled, overpaid, ego-driven athletes proves near impossible for the young sensation who is drawn to the limelight like a moth to a flame. While he has the skills to be an incredible individual talent, he has yet to develop the maturity and humility to truly lead others. The scriptures clearly point out that in order to lead effectively, one must also have the meekness to follow. This is a lesson that Willie must learn and we see that as Willie's star rises, his selfishness causes his team to unravel.

D'Amato's unenviable job is to try to stop this team from imploding from the internal pressure, teach young Willie that there is more to being a quarterback than just throwing the ball, decide what to do with the his damaged franchise player, soothe the egos of the rest of the team... all while arguing with the interfering money-minded second generation owner, Christina Pagniacci (Cameron Diaz, Being John Malkovich).

Using a combination of actors and NFL players (Jim Brown and Lawrence Taylor among them) along with an MTV movie-making style of fast cuts and quick pans, Mr. Stone depicts the brutality and violent conditions in which the game of football is played with realistic accuracy. By bringing the camera onto the field and making it a part of the action, Mr. Stone hoped to paint the players as modern day gladiators gloriously engaging the enemy on a field of battle. But the heavily stylized approach also serves to distance us from the action and the players. We're simply too close to be able to mentally process the blur of activity we see and hear on screen.

Mr. Stone also shows the ugly side of the sport: The injuries to body and pride that increase as aging stars must give way to the unforgivable effects of time; the intoxicating temptation of fame, money and an affluent lifestyle; the exploitation of the players by the team's owners and the doctors they hire.

Al Pacino turns in another bravura performance as the totally driven coach with old-school values regarding the game. His character also shows the penchant our society has towards deifying the game and the people who play it. An idol is not always made out of wood and stone - it can be anything that is exalted to the highest position of one's thought or desires. By making football the most important thing in his life, it has subsequently become the only thing in his life, usurping even his wife and children.

Dennis Quaid does fine, surprisingly subtle work as the injured and aging star quarterback. Realizing that he can not play at the same level as when he first entered the league, he begins to doubt his ability to return. Mr. Quaid shows us the conflict within him as his wife and coach try to convince him otherwise, each for their own selfish reasons.

Ultimately though, the major theme of the film is one of teamwork. Eleven men, each with individual goals or desires, will always be beaten by eleven men who are working together to accomplish a common goal.

And Jesus knew their thoughts, and said unto them, Every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation; and every city or house divided against itself shall not stand: Matthew 12:25 [KJV]

It is a lesson that Mr. Stone would have done well to follow. Any Given Sunday tells an interesting tale, but Mr. Stone's directorial style works against the telling of it.
