Kill Bill Vol. 1

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 7, 2003
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Revenge; vengeance; justice; forgiveness; trusting God

Author-verified transcription

In addition to being a filmmaker, Quentin Tarantino (Pulp Fiction) is, first and foremost, a film fan. This has never been more evident than in his newest movie, Kill Bill Volume 1 which borrows generously from a number of genre films, notably those known as “grindhouse cinema” (spaghetti westerns, Japanese anime, and Chinese martial arts/revenge films).

It is called Volume 1 because when Tarantino finished putting the movie together, it ran well over 3 hours in length. Knowing that this running time would be a hard sell to mainstream audiences, but unwilling to shorten the film further, the producers made the unusual decision to release Kill Bill in stages. Volume 2 is set to arrive in theaters next year (February 2004).

The film tells a violent story of revenge. Uma Thurman (Pulp Fiction) stars as “The Bride” a.k.a. “Black Mamba”, a woman who was once a member of the Deadly Viper Assassination Squad, a crew of mercenary killers run by a thus-far unseen boss named Bill (David Carradine, Kung-Fu). We presume they were once lovers because we meet Uma’s character at her wedding. She’s pregnant. Bill is not the groom (although we are led to believe the baby is his.) Bill sends his assassins in to wipe out the entire wedding party. The very first image that greets us is a battered and bloody Uma pleading with Bill just before he puts a bullet in her head at close range.

Amazingly she survives, awakening four years later after a long coma. Escaping from the hospital, she begins to plot her revenge against those that had so terribly wronged her.

There are five names on her “kill list.” Volume 1 sets the back story and deals with 2 of the 5 prospects. Be warned: the body count in this film is much much higher than the few names on the list. Most of the rest are destined to be nameless victims of the wrath of the Black Mamba. But it is those five that are the key to what is important to the story.

Unfortunately, I don’t share Tarantino’s background nor his appreciation for the various genres he’s emulating. This is, without question, the bloodiest, goriest, most violent film I’ve ever forced myself to watch. I’m sure others exist, but I tend to avoid them. Even the extended animated sequence which tells the back story of O-Ren Ishii (Lucy Liu, Charlie’s Angels) is awash in blood. That said, it is impossible to deny the artistry at work in this movie. It’s just that I prefer art to be edifying and emotionally rewarding. Kill Bill is neither. It is pure exploitation without much of a point. Many argue that it should have been rated NC-17. I can’t disagree with them.

On the other hand, the film does have many strengths. Yuen Wo-Ping (Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon) continues to show why he’s considered by many to be the best martial arts choreographer in the business. Tarantino is well known for his excellent musical choices used to underscore his films and Kill Bill’s soundtrack will only further his deserved reputation. Perhaps the strongest element in the film is the performance of Uma Thurman. “Black Mamba” is a grueling role requiring her to play both victim and vigilante. Her emotional intensity and her physicality are both impressive even if her scripted character lacks full dimension.

Revenge is a terrible motive to adopt as one’s driving force. It feeds off negative energy. It hardens the heart instead of softening it. And it can never undo the past.

There is no guarantee that those who commit wrongs against us will be held accountable and made to pay restitution in this life. We should not be troubled by this. All such matters will be handled to our complete and utter satisfaction in the next life.

Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but [rather] give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance [is] mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. Romans 12:19 (KJV)
