Author-verified transcription

Many people are uncomfortable at the idea of being forced to share space with a stranger... especially when that stranger is a hired killer with plans to involve you in his latest contract.

This is the scenario that Lisa Reisert (Rachel McAdams) is dealing with in Red Eye. A professional “people pleaser,” Lisa works for a luxury hotel in Miami where she adroitly smoothes the ruffled feathers of unhappy guests.

Returning home from attending her grandmother’s funeral, Lisa finds herself seated next to an affable and attractive young man named Jackson Rippert (Cillian Murphy). Once the plane is in the air, Jackson gives Lisa the ultimatum that drives the film. She must convince her hotel staff to move the visiting Deputy Secretary of Homeland Security to a room that is more suitable for a planned assassination. If she refuses, her father will be killed by the man stationed outside his home.

Lisa has few options and even less space in which to make them. A large chunk of the film is set within the claustrophobic confines of a plane. Director Wes Craven specifically chose to construct his set to the exact specifications of an airline, complete with low ceilings and precious little leg room. The result is remarkably effective, building tension and a frustrating sense of impotence in Lisa’s ability to respond to the danger so close at hand.

Rachel McAdams’ star is quickly rising and this film only helps it along. She serves as the movie’s center and its emotional core. She is the connective link that makes the audience care about the outcome of the story.

On the other end of the spectrum, Cillian Murphy makes for a blood-chilling villain. His piercing eyes and intense manner are riveting and quite menacing.

Jayma Mays has a few amusing scenes as an easily rattled front desk clerk. Brian Cox offers solid support as Lisa’s dad. But it is the interaction between Lisa and Jackson that provides the film with its dramatic content and tension.

Red Eye is a by-the-numbers thriller that, although it offers few surprises, does not fail to entertain. Its 85-minute running time is brief enough to give us the adrenalin rush of the genre without stretching to expose the many weak elements of a bare-boned screenplay.

Lisa’s predicament seems one from which it would be impossible to escape. To her credit, she never stopped looking for a solution. There will be times in our lives where we will be tempted to give up. We may find ourselves in what we think is a hopeless situation. When we believe in God, there is no hopeless situation. There is nothing so complicated or so powerful that God cannot provide a way out.

There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it. 1 Corinthians 10:13 (KJV)

The best thing to do when being beset by seemingly impossible odds is to remain peaceful. As we continue to do our best to work through the situation, we should expect and be ready to act when the opportunity for escape presents itself. God promises that it will be there. We simply need to recognize it when it arrives.
