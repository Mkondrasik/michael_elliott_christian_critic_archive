Author-verified transcription

To say that Eddie Murphy’s newest comedy, Life, contains some obscenities would be like saying the Milky Way has some stars in it. It may be an accurate statement, but in no way does it tell the whole story.

Life’s title refers to the prison sentence given to Ray Gibson (Eddie Murphy, Doctor Dolittle) and Claude Banks (Martin Lawrence, Nothing to Lose). In 1932 they had the poor judgment to be in the wrong place at the wrong time. Convicted of a murder they didn’t commit, they enter into the Mississippi State Prison system which will prove to be their home for the next 67 years.

The language in the film is saturated with words that many will find offensive. If hearing these curse words will keep you from appreciating anything else, then by all means, avoid this Life.

But setting aside the offensive verbiage, there is much to appreciate about the movie. First and foremost, the performance of Eddie Murphy should remind people of just how capable a comedic actor he has become. Mr. Murphy takes his character of Ray from a hustling, energetic, conniving young black man in his twenties to an irascible, cantankerous old black man in his nineties. Mr. Murphy makes the age transition remarkably well. It is a fully formed, completely believable characterization.

Martin Lawrence plays Claude as a character headed on the road to conformity and respectability. Slated to start his new bank teller position in just a few days, he bumps into Ray at a nightclub and his entire life takes on a new direction. (Too bad for Claude.)

Mr. Lawrence’s work is likely to be overshadowed by Mr. Murphy’s but the truth is, it is the relationship between these two characters that drives this movie. The interaction between Mr. Lawrence and Mr. Murphy is both humorous and compelling. They play off each other brilliantly.

Director Ted Demme (Monument Ave.) and the screen writing team of Robert Ramsey and Matthew Stone (Destiny Turns on the Radio) keep the mood light, even in the midst of such dark and desperate surroundings.

While the focus is on Murphy and Lawrence, they are being ably supported by the rest of the cast: Nick Cassavetes (Face/Off) as the “boss man guard”, Lisa Nicole Carson (Eve’s Bayou) as a prostitute, Obba Babatunde (Multiplicity), as inmate Willie Long (who narrates the story of Ray and Claude as a flashback while two other inmates dig their graves) and Bokeem Woodbine (The Big Hit) as the mentally deficient Can’t Get Right who can hit a baseball a country mile.

We might think that a story of wrongful imprisonment, especially one that lasts for 60 years, would not seem to be fertile ground for a uproariously funny comedy. We’d be right. Life does not provide a laugh a minute. This is a comedy which strives for poignancy instead of laughs.

Where it fails is in the premise. There is nothing funny about a wasted life. Nor is there anything particularly poignant. The filmmakers, in depicting the events throughout the years, lose sight of the fact that these are two innocent individuals!

What Ray and Claude have done for 60 plus years cannot be called “life” by any stretch of the imagination. God created life and therefore defined it for us. He promises us that we can not only have life but we can have more than life:

I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly. John 10:10b [KJV]

There are plenty of examples that we can read in the Bible where people were wrongfully imprisoned. Joseph was thrown in jail, only to be released in time to save all of Egypt from starvation during the famine. Paul was imprisoned many times during his ministry, but he never stopped teaching and preaching the truth of God’s Word.

Ray and Claude didn’t accomplish much during their time of imprisonment except survive it. God wants so much more from our lives than mere survival. An abundant life is a life that is full, enriched, and profitable. A life that testifies to His glory. The lives of Ray and Claude testified to nothing more than the stupidity of the times. We can do better than that.
