Author-verified transcription

As a murder mystery, Twisted could have used a few more twists. As it stands, we can see the ending coming from a few victims away.

Jessica Shepard (Ashley Judd) is celebrating her promotion to homicide detective by downing a few shots with the "boys." Afterwards she stops by a neighborhood bar to pick up a guy and, as her custom is, have a quick and meaningless sexual encounter.

Imagine her surprise when her first homicide case involves a corpse with whom she was once intimate. (Back when he was alive, of course.) When the second victim and then the third turns up - both of whom also turn out to be previous sexual partners of Shepard - it has got to be a bit more than coincidence.

Added to her troubles is the fact that she drinks heavily, passes out, and cannot remember anything about the evenings when the murders took place.

She has a new partner, Mike Delmarco (Andy Garcia), whom she doesn't entirely trust. She has a mentor in John Mills (Samuel L. Jackson), the police commissioner, who has raised her from a child ever since her father (also a cop) unexplainably went berserk and killed both her mother and himself. She has an ex-boyfriend, another cop, who is a step away from becoming a stalker. And she has a department issued psychiatrist who is probing into her mind to learn the truth she is afraid she might discover.

Ashley Judd plays Jessica with a hard edge so we never really warm to her characterization. She's attractive but also self-destructive and there is little shown to us to explain her behavior.

Both Andy Garcia, as her partner, and Samuel L. Jackson, as her boss, have seen better days and better movies. They seem going through the motions in this film and, I'm sure, picking up a sizable check for their trouble.

The biggest problem with the film is the awkwardness of the script. It is so formula that all tension and mystery are nonexistent. I generally get sucked into films which keep me guessing. It was rather evident from the start how this movie would end and there's not enough skill in the telling to involve us to any great degree.

Jessica could have avoided a great deal of trouble simply by adjusting her behavior. Picking up strange men in bars for the purposes of having one-night stands and drinking one's self into oblivion are not the activities which will lead to positive self-development. Quite the opposite, they will lead one to destruction.

Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil. Ephesians 4:26-27 (KJV)

Our spiritual adversary is looking for weaknesses that he can exploit. If we have a chink in our armor of believing, we can be sure that is where he will strike. We need not give him any more ammunition than he already has by living a life that is out of bounds and in opposition to the standards of God. By stepping away from His protection, we place ourselves in danger and become subject to all kinds of spiritual attacks. When we ignore God, His commandments, and His will for our lives, we are, in effect, putting out the welcome mat for devilish influences to make themselves at home in our minds, our hearts, and our lives. And that is seriously twisted.
