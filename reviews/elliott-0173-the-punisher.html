The Punisher

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 12, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Vengeance; revenge; justice; forgiveness; restraint

Author-verified transcription

Who knew so many Marvel comic-book super-heroes were running around pitching projects to Hollywood studios to tell their stories? Although, to tell the truth, the central character of The Punisher, Frank Castle, is not so much a super hero as he is a sadistic vigilante.

Castle (Thomas Jane) was an FBI undercover agent who decided upon early retirement in order to spend more time with his family. Unfortunately, his last sting operation resulted in the death of the son of a brutal and shady "businessman" named Howard Saint (John Travolta). Saint avenges his son's death by having his thugs wipe out Castle's entire family line during a family reunion celebration. The henchmen make the fatal mistake of leaving Castle alive.

After he recovers from his wounds, he decides to receive justice in the only way he believes to be possible... by his own hands. Thus he begins to systematically destroy Saint's business empire, his associates, his family, and finally Saint himself.

His revenge doesn't come easily and it doesn't come without a price. Castle pronounces himself dead to the world and exists only to bring vigilante justice to those who are more powerful than the law itself. But to be effective, he must adopt methods that are as brutal and sadistic as the people which he finds guilty.

The Punisher is an extremely violent film that can't seem to decide if it is a comic book thriller a la Daredevil or Spider-man or if it is a semi-realistic crime adventure a la Die Hard. As a result, we are presented with extreme comic book violence depicted as realistically as possible. It is not a combination that I found makes for enjoyable viewing.

The actors can't really be blamed although John Travolta can't seem to shake his celebrity persona to make us forget who he is so we forever reminded that this is just a Hollywood product... and not a particularly good one at that.

An element which I believe was not fully exploited by the filmmakers was the contrasting humanity exhibited by Castle's apartment dwelling neighbors. Castle moved into a seedy apartment complex where his neighbors decide to take an interest in him. An obese chef (John Pinette), a pierced freak (Ben Foster), and a beautiful waitress (Rebecca Romijn-Stamos) who has rotten taste in men accept Castle as one of their own.

Had the film spent more time on the emotional conflict their offer of friendship would have had on Castle, this might have been a unique and compelling story. But the way it is told, it is simply another loud, explosion-happy, blood-soaked, gun-riddled, B-movie crime adventure film... albeit one with a higher budget.

Castle makes the point in the film that he is not after vengeance. What he seeks is punishment, which he has decided to dole out personally because the law was either unwilling or unable to do what he believes it was designed to do. Regardless of his semantics, revenge is what is fueling his actions in this picture. For that reason, Castle is no one to admire or emulate. Vengeance isn't ours to give or take.

Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. Romans 12:19 (KJV)

We can look forward to the day when all wrongs will be righted and all injustices will be corrected. Until then, all we have been exhorted to do is speak the truth in love. Vengeance is not an answer for us. In the movie Gandhi, the lead character eloquently puts it in this way: "An eye for an eye only ends up making the whole world blind." In the case of Frank Castle, that line could be changed to read "...making the whole world dead."
