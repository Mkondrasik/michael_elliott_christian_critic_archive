Author-verified transcription

For years, The Christian Critic has been exhorting Hollywood producers to turn to biblical records in order to get story ideas for their movies. After all, the records in the Bible are exciting, true, and (they should like this) free from royalties!

At long last, DreamWorks, Inc. has taken a first step by producing The Prince of Egypt. This animated musical endeavors to tell the tale of Moses and how God delivered His people from Egyptian slavery.

This familiar story is certainly one that is film-worthy. DreamWorks should be commended for bringing this rich biblical account to the secular movie houses.

In the same breath, it is important that we warn each other and guard ourselves against thinking that what we are seeing in the film is a true and accurate representation of this historical event. It should be noted that DreamWorks has taken "artistic and dramatic license" with the biblical record which lessens the spiritual impact that the movie could have had.

The filmmakers cannot, in the allotted time, give enough information and background history to accurately tell the story as it actually occurred. Therefore, young children and those who are unfamiliar with the record in Exodus may become confused and unable to follow the complex plot.

The relationship between God and Moses takes a back seat in this version. Instead, the emphasis is shifted to the relationship between Rameses and Moses, supposing that they were raised as brothers and as sons of the Pharaoh.

The movie shows the baby Moses being placed in the Nile by his mother to save him from a Pharaoh ordered execution, only to have him found by Pharaoh's wife who, taking pity on the babe, raised him as her own.

According to the actual account, it was the daughter of Pharaoh who found the baby, and gave him to a Hebrew woman to nurse and mother for the first few formative years of his life. The woman who was given this task? Moses' natural mother! It was not until Moses was older that he went to live in the Pharaoh's court. Before that time, his mother would have seen that he be instructed in the ways and traditions of his people. His heritage would not have come as a surprise to him as the movie suggests.

On an artistic level, I can appreciate DreamWorks' desire to depict Moses as a young, virile and handsome man. After all, he's the star of the show. In actuality, Moses was forty when he left the Pharaoh's court after killing an Egyptian in a dispute (the movie claims it to be an accident.) He was at least eighty when he returned to Egypt at the command of God to lead God's people out of bondage.

Aaron, Moses's brother, played a much more integral role in the true account. He was Moses' spokesman, more likely his interpreter, as after forty years, Moses probably forgot most of the language he would have needed to communicate in that land.

There are other omissions or changes which can be discussed, but why not rather concentrate on that which IS included? The burning bush through which God first spoke to Moses is there; as is the series of plagues which beset Egypt as a result of Pharaoh's hardness of heart. And of course, the parting of the Red Sea. The visual depiction of that miracle is awe-inspiring. It is amazing to think that real people were actually witness to that demonstration of God's power.

It would be nice to think that we would always remember the signs, miracles and wonders that God works in our lives. It would be nice to think that we would forever thank Him for His love and deliverance, keeping Him foremost in our mind. The truth is, we don't. Man doesn't. Even those who lived in that time and walked (on dry ground!) through the parted Red Sea with walls of water towering over them on either side, after a while began to forget what God had done for them, and incredibly, desired to return to the bondage of Egypt.

Perhaps The Prince of Egypt will be the forerunner of many biblically oriented feature films to come. While it may not be a perfect film, it does entertain and depict many of the biblical truths that continue to edify us today. So, go and see the movie. Afterwards, go home and read the book. And remember what God has done.
