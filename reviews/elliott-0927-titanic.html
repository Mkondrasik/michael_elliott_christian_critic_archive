Titanic

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 19, 1997
Rating
3 1/2 stars
Running time
3 hr: 14 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 5:25Isaiah 35:8-9Proverbs 27:12

Sermon / spiritual topic: Sacrifice; Spiritual Discernment

Author-verified transcription

Sure, the boat sinks. But what a grand telling of the sinking. Writer/Director James Cameron (The Terminator) has taken the epic story of the maiden voyage of this "unsinkable" vessel and brought it to human terms - using it as the backdrop for the simplest of movie plots, the love story.

Leonardo DiCaprio (William Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet) and Kate Winslet (Sense and Sensibility) play Jack and Rose, the tragic lovers who meet and mate on board the grandest ship of its day ever to cross the Atlantic. The movie begins in the present with a salvage operation underway deep beneath the sea. Rose, now 100 years old and played to perfection by Gloria Stuart (Here Comes the Navy), sees the salvage attempt on a news report and recognizes a drawing of her as a young woman. A drawing that was done aboard the Titanic. She contacts the salvage crew and the reminiscences begin.

The young Rose, born to the upper crust of society, is traveling back to America with her mother and fiancé. Already feeling trapped and pressured into agreeing to a loveless marriage, Rose meets young Jack, a struggling artist trying to make his way back home. He's gained passage on board the Titanic the only way he could afford - he won the tickets in a poker game.

Mr. Cameron spends much time developing the relationship between Jack and Rose. An unlikely coupling, Jack awakens that special something in Rose - the freedom to enjoy life and living. That's the funny thing about the love of money. It tends to choke off the desire to love anything else. Rose, who is being suffocated by the expectations of the wealthy set with whom she is associated, learns to breathe in life itself from Jack, who has nothing else to offer her. As it turns out, what he can offer her is exactly what she needs.

Once the Titanic meets the iceberg, the special effects team takes over. The job they have done is quite breathtaking in its own right. Modern technology has advanced to the point where Mr. Cameron and his team can actually make it appear that they are filming a ship slowly sinking headfirst into the Atlantic.

The movie, at three hours plus, never loses its pace nor its audience. Our attention is rapt, and although we know the outcome, we find ourselves wanting certain characters to be among the survivors. It should come as no surprise that, just as in life, we are often disappointed.

Much can be said of the love story and the ultimate sacrifice that a man can make for the woman he loves. Certainly there are spiritual comparisons that can be made. Husbands are commanded by God to love their wives as Christ also loved the church and gave himself for it. [Ephesians 5:25]

But a stronger spiritual message can be found in the cause of the accident itself. The Titanic, so the story goes, was believed to be unsinkable. Its captain, perhaps tempted by personal glory, made the decision to travel at full speed through a treacherous North Atlantic route - even in the face of reported warnings of iceberg activity in their vicinity. We, as Christians, also travel daily through treacherous waters. We live in a world which is controlled by our adversary. We cannot blindly forge ahead and expect to reach our destination unscathed.

Our spiritual enemy is much like an iceberg. Only a small percentage of what he is can be seen. The danger and destructive nature lies beneath the waters. Hidden... submerged... unseen. But nonetheless real.

We are called to walk precisely through this wilderness called life. God has given us a route to take that will keep us clear from harm and destruction. However, that route requires us to keep a diligent watch. The adversary desires nothing more than to steal, kill, and destroy. He cannot do that if we follow God's route for our lives. If we blindly go full steam ahead in life, we may miss the signposts warning us of the adversary's traps and snares. Our Christian walk is a walk of discipline and diligence. As we safely navigate through this dark and perverse world, our lives stand out as examples to others looking for safe havens.

Titanic is a romantic love story. And we, as romantic beings, like to think that it would be wonderful to love someone who would love us to the point of being willing to give their lives for us. But it is wrong to romanticize death. Our savior has already died so that we might live. We have no need for another savior to be sacrificed. Death is an enemy. Let us be examples to the world by steering around all icebergs that the devil may place in our path and reach our destinations safe and secure in the knowledge that our God will protect us as we go in the way which He has directed.
