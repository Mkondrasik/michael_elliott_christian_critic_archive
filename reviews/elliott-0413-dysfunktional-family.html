Author-verified transcription

In Dysfunctional Family, comedian Eddie Griffin (Undercover Brother) returns to his roots by taking his stand up act back to the place he grew up: Kansas City, Missouri. This comedy concert film is intercut with interviews and images of the people and places that helped make Eddie the man he is today: a loud, brash, profane comic able to offend just about anyone who isn't an avid fan.

The laughs may come, though not as frequently nor as raucously as the on screen audience would lead us to believe. The problem is that to get to those laughs we must first suffer through a barrage of profanities, racial slurs, and sexually graphic descriptions. If one can handle all that, Griffin offers some offbeat observations covering themes that bounce from religion to drugs to porn to child rearing to the difference between cats and dogs.

George Gallo (Double Take) is clearly out of his element as director of this concert film. His additions, cuts, and obvious manipulations of audience reaction weaken the overall experience and further distract from Griffin's talents. As if Griffin needed further distraction. As a comedian trying to build his popularity, he is his own worst enemy. Yes, he is talented. He just packages it in a way that dissuades us from paying him much attention.

The language used by Griffin throughout his concert is extremely coarse and crude which is not helping him sell his humor to a middle America which neither appreciates nor tolerates its use. Specifically, the "F" word and the "N" word get a workout in Griffin's routines. He explains himself by saying, in a bit of self-serving rationalization, that the more a word is used the less power it will have over us. Sorry, Eddie. That reasoning doesn't fly.

Words have an impact. The more they are used, the more impact they will have. The fact that a word designed to insult will, through overuse, become less shocking and more frequently used because its meaning is diluted cannot be considered much of a victory.

Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man. Colossians 4:6 (KJV)

The Greek word translated as "grace" is translated in other sections of the Bible as "acceptable," "benefit," "favour," "gift," "gracious," "joy," "liberality," "pleasure," and "thank." It is hard to imagine Mr. Griffin's vocabulary fitting any of those descriptive terms.

If we have a message to deliver, whether it be a message of deliverance about our Lord or a personal observation of life, the words we choose to use in order to communicate that message are often as important as the message itself. If we choose to use words that offend, the words will be heard... but the message will be lost. Even though I sat through Eddie Griffin's 90 minute concert film, I can't say that I've received anything of value from the experience.
