The Secret Life of Walter Mitty

Author-verified transcription

James Thurber’s short story, A Secret Life of Walter Mitty, was required reading back when I was in grade school. I do not know if it is still required but if any students need to do a book report on it and are thinking of watching the 2013 film to familiarize themselves with the story should think again. The movie bears little resemblance to Thurber’s masterwork.

In the movie, Walter Mitty (Ben Stiller) is a meek man in an obscure department within the iconic Life Magazine. He is attracted to a co-worker but hasn’t had the courage to talk to her yet. Oh, he can fantasize about her. And he does… frequently. Early in the film we see the courage he musters in his mind through his vivid imagination. He just never acts upon his desires or impulses.

That is until a film negative entrusted into his care by Life’s premiere photo correspondent (Sean Penn) goes missing. Mitty is forced into action to find either the negative or the reclusive photographer who took it. He uncharacteristically flies off to have an unbelievable adventure that trumps any fantasy he ever had.

And that’s where the movie veers astray from the solid foundation of Thurber’s classic story. Only a very small portion is devoted to Mitty’s forays into his imagination. The dominant story line follows an unmemorable man who, all of a sudden, is doing things few people ever dare to do.

Stiller, who also directed, proves to be a good choice as the title character as he comfortably fits both the meek, risk adverse character and the bold, risk taking adventurer. As director, he has a solid handle on the tone and style of the piece. If he had a script that better fleshed out the themes of Thurber’s tale, he might have realized the potential of delivering a very special experience. As it is, The Secret Life of Walter Mitty fails to be anything special. All we can do is imagine the kind of film it could have been.

Mitty suffers from a malady that is common to many… he lacks boldness. He has, for most of his life, passed through the world in a passive manner, accepting things as they are while dreaming of how they might be. His fear (of failure; of rejection; of ridicule) has kept him from living a life that could have been more than abundant. Instead, that fear has led him to internalize his desires and resort to daydreams in order to mentally experience the kind of life he is to afraid to claim.

As Christians, God has provided for us all things that we need to live a more than abundant life. But He is not going to live that life for us. We can, like Mitty, watch as the world passes us by, afraid to embrace the life we desire. If we do, our time will have passed before we know it and we will be none the richer for it. To experience all the victories and blessings that God wants to give us, we must manifest the boldness that is within us. We must act as well as think. We should give our dreams the legs they need to walk into our existence. Only then can our lives have the impact God wants them to have.

“Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.” Acts 4:13 (KJV)
