Slums of Beverly Hills

Author-verified transcription

Slumming is not an activity most people would associate with Beverly Hills but then, the members of the Abramowitz family who are the focus of Slums of Beverly Hills are not most people. A part of the lower middle class, the Abramowitz children are growing up with no mother, few standards and fewer social skills. In most cultures, that would be seen as a tragedy. Here, it is played for laughs.

The patriarch of this dysfunctional group is Murray Abramowitz (Alan Arkin, Glengarry Glen Ross), a forever-down-on-his-luck car salesman who, being a "loving" father, wants to keep his three children in the good schools found in the expensive district of Beverly Hills. To accomplish this worthy goal, he just keeps moving them from address to address - usually in the dead of the night to avoid paying the past due rent. As he puts it, "Furniture is temporary. Education is permanent."

His daughter, Vivian (Natasha Lyonne, Krippendorf's Tribe) has suddenly sprouted breasts, becoming "stacked" overnight just like her mother before her. Slums of Beverly Hills is really her story, as it deals with the trials and trauma of the young Vivian's uncertain transition into womanhood. It won't be easy, growing up in close quarters with her father and her two younger brothers who demonstrate all the tact and compassion of a raging warthog. Add to all this the antics and influence of their drug-rehab failure of a cousin, Rita (outlandishly played by Marisa Tomei, Only You), set it in the 1970's, and we see the beginning roots of Jerry Springer's audience.

Writer/Director Tamara Jenkins (Family Remains) has crafted a thoroughly convincing picture of a modern day nomadic existence supposedly drawn from real life experiences (although obviously exaggerated for comedic purposes). She is particularly blessed to have assembled one of the most slothfully delightful casts in recent memory.

Miss Lyonne's Vivian is amazingly tacky and cheap. She knows it. She hates it. She just doesn't know how to change it. Everything about her (her walk, intonation, expressions, gestures) are perfect for the character.

Mr. Arkin again proves that he is one of the drollest men working in film today. His deadpan reaction to finding Rita and his daughter dancing with a vibrator (yes, it is that kind of film... the sexual references are crude and numerous) is extremely funny.

Eli Marienthal (Jack Frost) and David Krumholtz (The Ice Storm), as Vivian's siblings, spend much of their time in their underwear. Mr. Krumholtz in particular presents a well-rounded (but intentionally shallow) character who is clearly following in his loser father's footsteps.

Miss Tomei brings an outrageous energy to her role of Rita, who helps introduce Vivian to the stranger things of womanhood like mustache depilatories and the aforementioned vibrator which Vivian uses in a scene that will have the audience either blushing, squirming, or (in the case of parents with children present), scrambling for the exits.

Slums of Beverly Hills is another witty, intelligent, well-scripted, well-acted, well-directed film that is spiritually repugnant. Any father who truly loves his children will set boundaries for them. The way he lives life will set an example for them that they may respect and willingly follow. Murray Abramowitz professed to love his children but nowhere in his actions was love (as God defines it) manifested.

We, as parents, do our children a major disservice by not properly disciplining them to obey a set standard for life. The scriptures say:

Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him. Proverbs 22:15 [KJV]

This scripture does not mean that we physically beat our children into submission. But we are to continually correct any improper behavior by pounding the proper behavior into their brains. In other words, we are to repeatedly teach them what the standard is until it becomes habitual for them to follow it.

The rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame. Proverbs 29:15 [KJV]

Parents must get involved in teaching their children the proper standard for life. There's no better guidebook than the scriptures. It's the book our heavenly Father uses to instruct us, His children.
