Author-verified transcription

Stealth is a fast moving action adventure that plays out more like a video game than a movie. It's about a trio of jet pilots testing their skills against an unmanned but heavily armed artificially intelligent prototype that has gone rogue. Think Top Gun with Hal of 2001: A Space Odyssey playing Val Kilmer's role.

Lt. Ben Gannon (Josh Lucas), Lt. Kara Wade (Jessica Biel), and Lt. Henry Purcell (Jamie Foxx) have risen through the ranks of the Navy to reach the pinnacle - piloting the sleek Talon-class stealth fighters which (it would seem) are the only aircraft at our government's disposal during times of crisis.

Joining their squadron is EDI (pronounced Eddie) which stands for Extreme Deep Invader, a stealth aircraft that needs no human pilot. It is equipped with its own computerized brain that enables it to learn and teach itself. On its way back from its first hastily assembled but successfully accomplished mission, EDI encounters the bane of all electronic devices - lightning.

The blast scrambles its brain and sets it on its own course - choosing targets and unleashing its massive firepower on the world at large. Gannon, Wade and Purcell must track and stop EDI before it manages to single-handedly start WWIII.

Stealth is the kind of film that asks us to accept bikini clad Jessica Biel in the cockpit of a billion dollar aircraft; tells us that high ranking US officers would stoop to murder just to keep their pet projects alive; and tries to convince us that Hollywood depictions need not be bound by physics or any other such mundane realities.

The actors playing air jockeys fill out their flight suits adequately although I have to assume that Jamie Foxx accepted this role long before the words Oscar-winning started to appear before his name. He is badly underutilized here.

Director Rob Cohen (The Fast and the Furious) takes his penchant for high speed into the air and out of the realm of believability as jet fighters travel at MACH 5 and yet somehow can still manage to turn on a dime and blow their pursuers into large pieces of shrapnel.

Cohen and screenwriter W.D. Richter leave nothing out of this highly predictable jet-fueled adventure: technology run amok, a lame romance, political deception, environmentally friendly fighter pilots, and an opportunity to say something like "I won't leave my wingman..."

To sum up, if mindless action, insipid dialogue, and stereotypical characters are the qualities you seek in a summer film, there's nothing stealthy about Stealth. It delivers in all these categories.

It also tries to sell us on an old idea... that machines can have souls. As EDI's learning curve continues to accelerate, he begins to develop emotional "feelings." I'm not sure what rationale screenwriters have to explore this particular issue but many have done so. Short Circuit, AI: Artificial Intelligence, Bicentennial Man, and Blade Runner are among the films that touched upon the idea.

Every breathing creature has a soul. This is God's definition and delineation. A soul is not something that can be artificially created or something that man can bestow upon an object. But that doesn't stop man from trying. From Frankenstein to EDI, we see cinematic examples of man trying to "create life." Regardless of what movies may ask us to believe, the reality is that such attempts will always fail. It isn't in man to create life. That is and always has been solely God's domain.

Who knoweth not in all these that the hand of the LORD hath wrought this? In whose hand is the soul of every living thing, and the breath of all mankind. Job 12:9-10 (KJV)
