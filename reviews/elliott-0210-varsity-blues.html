Varsity Blues

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 15, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

James 1:131 Corinthians 10:13Matthew 26:41

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Temptation; faithfulness; victory; courage; resisting temptation

Author-verified transcription

The residents of West Canaan, Texas believe in God. It is just that their god wears a helmet and shoulder pads and is able to throw a football through the eye of a needle. Varsity Blues takes an oftentimes amusing, and sometimes thought-provoking look at the world of high-school football and the near-idolatrous relationship it has with a small rural town.

Bud Kilmer (Jon Voight, The Rainmaker) has been the dictatorial coach of the Coyotes for the past thirty years. Winning at any cost is his motto and while most everyone in town recognizes him as a jerk... you can't argue with success... can you?

Second string quarterback Jonathan "Mox" Moxon can. Played by Dawson's Creek heartthrob, James Van Der Beek, Mox is the "dumbest smart kid I know" according to Coach Kilmer. Smart because of his obvious intelligence. Dumb because he won't play by Kilmer's rules. Resigned to sit on the bench throughout the season, Mox would rather read Kurt Vonnegut than a playbook and would rather play for the joy of the game than for the ego-satisfaction of Kilmer.

But then an injury to the first string quarterback, Lance Harbor (Paul Walker, Pleasantville) thrusts Mox into the limelight. Suddenly, he's experiencing the celebrity status of being the star quarterback in a town that worships the game. The temptations come fast and furious.

Mr. Van Der Beek has chosen wisely in his first feature film following his hit series by taking on a character which will only enhance his television popularity. Mox is a genuine nice guy with the ability to excel athletically and the heart to remain grounded in reality as the entire world around him exalts him to god-like proportions.

Jon Voight hits all the right notes as the maniacal Coach Kilmer. He lives in a world where the gridiron is a battlefield and his players are merely expendable soldiers. If one goes down, he finds another to take his place.

Ron Lester (Good Burger) as the obese lineman Billy Bob, somehow manages to escape turning in a characaturized performance and finds an inner depth beneath the surface joke of a character.

Director Brian Robbins (Good Burger) doesn't break any new ground here. But he does manage to entertain and engage his audience. It is spiritually disappointing that (once again) there isn't a single adult depicted in the film that shows any sign of wisdom or understanding. The parents and townsfolk are totally obsessed by the football god. Only Mox and his girlfriend Julie (Amy Smart, Strangeland) seem to have a handle on life.

Temptation is something that we all deal with on a daily basis. Not all of us are football heroes or movie stars, but temptation is not only for the elite. The thing we must remember is that no temptation is of God.

Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man: James 1:13 (KJV)

The purpose of temptation is to steer us away from the path that will lead us to victory. God wants us to have victory in our lives. If God were to tempt us for the purposes of "testing our faith" as so many like to believe, He would have to deny His own nature and contradict His Word. That He cannot do.

What He can do is to promise us that there is no temptation that we cannot withstand:

There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it. 1 Corinthians 10:13

Mox operated godly principles throughout the film. He wasn't perfect, but then who of us are? God doesn't ask us for perfection. He just asks for faithfulness. Faithfulness requires both courage and boldness. As Mox learns, the rewards of faithfully standing on the principles of truth far outweigh the temporary rewards of temptation.
