Author-verified transcription

Shades of UltraMan! (You kind of have to be a child of the ‘60s to fully appreciate that reference.) In Pacific Rim, set in the not too distant future, giant creatures from the sea arise to besiege the earth. Man’s only hope is the manufactured giant robotic suits piloted by a team of mind-synched warriors that have the size and armament to defeat the creatures who seem to get stronger with every attack.

Director Guillermo del Toro meets this Godzilla-like challenge head on with the technological advancements of the day. Using a largely unknown cast and a generic premise, Pacific Rim manages to meet expectations and presents a largely entertaining story of survival. At stake is human life as we know it.

Set in the near future, mankind has been under attack by kaiju (Japanese for “giant monster.”) These attacks have been increasing not only in frequency but also in the size of the creatures that arise from the deep. For a long time, man’s response has been to counter attack via Jaegers (German for “hunter”) but the powers that be have determined the Jaeger program to be obsolete so they scrap it in favor of building a giant wall. Bad idea.

Flash forward five years to when the kaiju breach the wall easily and man once again has to turn to the now rusted Jaegers and their untrained pilots to save the day.

Del Toro somehow makes this homage to the cheesy monster movies of a past era seem fresh and relevant. Charlie Hunnam and Rinko Kikuchi play emotionally compromised Jaeger pilots who must learn to work through their issues together. Charlie Day and Burn Gorrman play a pair of scientists who disagree on how best to study the alien beings. Ron Perlman makes a brief appearance as a black market dealer of kaiju body parts.

It takes two pilots to operate a Jaeger and this is accomplished through a neural link or “drift” akin to a Vulcan mind meld. The stronger the link, the more effective the pilots are at operating their massive machine. In order to maintain the link, it is important for the pilots not to linger or dwell on a single thought or memory. They must stay in the moment and not be immobilized by the past. Spiritually, we are to similarly renew our minds in much the same way:

“For who has known or understood the mind (the counsels and purposes) of the Lord so as to guide and instruct Him and give Him knowledge? But we have the mind of Christ (the Messiah) and do hold the thoughts (feelings and purposes) of His heart.” 1 Corinthians 3:16 (Amplified)

The stronger our link is to the mind of Christ, the more effective we will be in fulfilling the purposes of the Lord and doing the will of God.
