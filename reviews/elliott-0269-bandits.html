Bandits

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 12, 2001
Rating
1½ stars
Running time
2 hr: 5 min
Source
Published Web Version

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Crime; moral standards; good and evil; justice; consequences

Author-verified transcription

Take elements of Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid; mix in elements of The Sting; and then remove all traces of Paul Newman and Robert Redford. It may not be much, but what we're left with is the newest "crime pays" movie, Bandits.

Bruce Willis (Unbreakable) and Billy Bob Thornton (Pushing Tin) team up as Joe Blake and Terry Collins, a mismatched pair of outlaws who rob banks in order to bankroll their fantasy of becoming Mexican nightclub owners. After their spontaneous prison escape, they stumble upon a "foolproof" plan to rob banks without fear of capture... they kidnap the bank manager the night before, spending the night with him in his home before accompanying him to work the next morning.

The media seizes upon their unusual modus operandi and dubs them "The Sleepover Bandits." A reality TV tabloid show called Criminals-At-Large follows their exploits and immediately succeed in turning Joe and Terry into America's newest Robin Hoods. In fact, everything is going their way until they cross paths with Kate (Cate Blanchett, The Gift), an unbalanced and unhappily married woman who becomes not only a willing hostage but a more than willing sexual partner to both. The big trouble starts when she proves unable to choose between the two.

To tell the truth, it is difficult to choose any of them. She's an emotional basket case, Terry is a neurotic, over-planning hypochondriac, and Joe needs to work on his anger management. Together, along with their driver/lookout Harvey (Troy Garity, Steal This Movie), a self-taught stuntman wannabe, they bumble their way from one bank job to the next all the while stumbling through this bizarre love triangle they have formed.

Bruce Willis reverts to his familiar smirking persona, which, while amusing, offers no great insight to his character. Cate Blanchett, on the other hand, takes Kate so far to an extreme that we really don't care all that much about her or the choices she makes.

Of the three leads, Billy Bob Thornton has been given the most to work with and he uses it well. His stammering, facial tics, and obsession over brain tumors, tinnitus and other imagined ailments are genuinely funny but later turn poignant as he begins to believe that Kate can love him in spite of all his phobias.

Director Barry Levinson (Rain Man) mixes genres and delivers a jumbled film as a result. Bandits lacks the detail and precision that marks a quality "heist" picture and yet doesn't really have a truly sympathetic character needed for an effective romantic comedy. Additionally, the structure of the film could have been improved upon by staying within a traditional linear narrative instead of using flashbacks. Likewise the TV show documentary angle is forced and pads the film with unnecessary footage. It could easily have been dropped without the film suffering.

Joe and Terry are depicted as being "gentlemen bandits." They are courteous to a fault, even respectful to their victims. Because of this it is easy to downplay the fact that their activities are deplorable. They do get "busted" at one point in the film however, by a bank manager who is admittedly charmed by their manners.

"Good manners," she reproves them, "does not excuse criminal behavior."

Amen to that. Bandits is just one of a long line of films that present bad guys as "heroes." I don't know when the practice started but it would be nice to see it ended. But it has been going on for a long time... ever since biblical days and before.

But now we call the arrogant blessed. Certainly the evildoers prosper, and even those who challenge God escape. Malachi 3:15 (NIV)

Filmmakers who present those who break the laws of man and God as heroes must not have read through to the end of the Bible. Those of us who have know how the story really ends. Bad guys, no matter how successful their lives of crime may have been, will lose. God's justice will ultimately prevail.
