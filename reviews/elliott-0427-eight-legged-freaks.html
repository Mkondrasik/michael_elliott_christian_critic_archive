Author-verified transcription

There must be something about a film titled Eight Legged Freaks that makes an audience think they're on the set of Mystery Science Theater 2000. At least that was the case with the audience that sat with me. From the dimming of the lights to the running of the end credits, these people never shut their mouths.

Everybody wanted to add their running commentary to the goofy horror that was being shown them. All that was missing were those robot silhouettes at the bottom of the screen.

It made me realize that we really haven't had a good cheesy B-movie playing in theaters since... well.. since last week's Reign of Fire. But skipping over that one, we might have to go back to 1990's Tremors for the closest equivalent to this film.

In true B-movie fashion, there's little depth to the plot. A vat of toxic waste is accidentally dumped into the river just upstream of an exotic spider farm. As soon as you can say Arachnophobia, the little town of Prosperity is overrun with Winnebago-sized bugs.

Among the townspeople running for their lives is Chris McCormack (David Arquette, See Spot Run), who has returned to Prosperity to reopen his late father's failed gold mine. He would like to date Sam, the town's sexy, single-mother sheriff played by Kari Wuhrer (Sliders), if he could only find time to tell her.

The leading spider authority happens to be Sam's 11-year-old brainiac son (Scott Terra, Shadrach) but since he's "just a kid," no adult will take his warnings seriously... at least not until a few of them have become spidey snacks. Scarlett Johansson (Ghost World) plays Sam's rebellious teenage daughter.

Rick Overton adds a scene stealing comic performance as a deputy who, except for being a few hundred pounds heavier and a few hundred IQ points dumber, could be the movie reincarnation of Barney Fife.

Director Ellory Elkayem (Larger Than Life) unabashedly embraces the 1950s sci-fi genre that gave us such atomic powered gems as Them and Attack of the Crab Monsters. Less a spoof than a fond homage, Eight Legged Freaks invites us to return to the simple days of yesteryear when we didn't let the omission of logic or good acting stand in our way of enjoying the cheesy effects and stupid premise of a sci-fi fantasy.

This, of course, makes rating Eight Legged Freaks all the more difficult. Does it deserve high marks because it succeeds at copying bad films? Or should it be panned for the object of ridicule that it has set out to become? I'll leave that for others to debate. My two star rating stands. Some people will enjoy it; others will find it stupid.

There is a bit of off-color language and humor that is dependent upon sexual innuendoes. The violence is campy and more suggested than it is depicted. Be advised however that the spiders are big and hairy. If just the idea of that makes your skin crawl, it would be best to remain in the lobby while this film is playing.

Amazingly, there is one mention of spiders in the King James version of the Bible but it happens to be in reference to this verse: "There be four things which are little upon the earth, but they are exceeding wise." Proverbs 30:24 (KJV)

Seeing as the titular characters are anything but little, that verse doesn't necessarily help us bring this film into a spiritual light. However, we do know, from reading the record of David and Goliath, that superior size does not guarantee victory. And we also know, from reading the record of Gideon vs. the Midianites, that superior numbers do not guarantee victory. If we ever must face an enemy of overwhelming size and strength, we need not cower in fear. God's strength and power can enable us to vanquish any foe.

Of course, it's unlikely that we will ever come across an army of 10 foot spiders. The greater fear is that today's audiences will so enjoy the unusual guilty pleasure that is Eight Legged Freaks that our cineplexes will be overrun with an infestation of cheesy B-movies in the years to follow.
