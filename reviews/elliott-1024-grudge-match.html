Grudge Match

Author-verified transcription

You have to admit, the concept behind Grudge Match is genius. Casting the Italian Stallion and the Raging Bull in the same film and making them arch rivals is the stuff from which boxing dreams are made. It is just a shame that the film arrived about twenty years too late. Still, the old gents give it their all, playing to their strengths and even poking a little fun at their own images in the process.

A sports rivalry between Henry “Razor” Sharp (Sylvester Stallone) and Billy “The Kid” McDonnen (Robert De Niro) grew to epic proportions with each of them beating the other in two fifteen-round slugfests. But when the time came for the decisive and all-important tiebreaker, Razor inexplicably retires, walking away from a million dollar purse and frustrating sports fans everywhere.

That was thirty years ago and people are still obsessed with it. So much so that fast-talking sports promoter Dante Slate Jr. (Kevin Hart) has the bright idea of getting these two geezers to settle the unanswered question of who’s the better fighter once and for all. Surprisingly, they, for their own individual reasons, agree to take their boxing trunks out of mothballs to stage their geriatric comeback.

Stallone plays a version of his Rocky persona (a quiet, nice guy with a powerful punch) and likewise De Niro plays a LaMotta-esque character (a self-centered, selfish boxer whose only redeeming quality can be found in the ring). Kim Basinger is Sally, the girl caught between them; Alan Arkin has some fine moments as Razor’s ancient trainer and cornerman; and Kevin Hart provides much of the comedy as the desperate promoter looking to this grudge match as his ticket to the big time.

One of the saddest things in the world is to look back on one’s life with regret. The free will decisions we make during our lifetimes are key to our living without regrets. The secret can be found in our motivation of heart. As one of the characters remarks in the film, he has lived the past thirty years without the two things he values most. The decision he made back then to do without them was made out of spite and a desire for revenge. That decision ended up hurting him as much, if not more, than the target he originally had in mind and he came to regret the time he wasted as a result.

God does not want us to waste any of the time we’ve been given. When we make our decisions genuinely and selflessly, out of the goodness of our heart – when we allow God to direct our steps instead of making our own way – there should be no regrets. Even if the outcome was not what we expected, when our motivation of heart is pure and loving and based upon God’s Word, He says that He will honor and work within our choices to bless us as well as the choices we make.

“Grudge not one against another, brethren, lest ye be condemned: behold, the judge standeth before the door. Take, my brethren, the prophets, who have spoken in the name of the Lord, for an example of suffering affliction, and of patience. Behold, we count them happy which endure. Ye have heard of the patience of Job, and have seen the end of the Lord; that the Lord is very pitiful, and of tender mercy.” James 5:9-11 (KJV)
