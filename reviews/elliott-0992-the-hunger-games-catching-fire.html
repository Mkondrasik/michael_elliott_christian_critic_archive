The Hunger Games: Catching Fire

Sermon / spiritual topic: Enemies; Spiritual Warfare

Author-verified transcription

Katniss Everdeen returns to District 12 after her victory in last year's Hunger Games but she won't be staying long. In Catching Fire, Katniss (Jennifer Lawrence) learns that winning the games means never being able to say "it's over."

As she and Peeta (Josh Hutcherson) embark upon their twelve district victory tour to pay "homage" to the fallen tributes and serve as a propaganda tool for the administration, she is, in no uncertain terms, warned by President Snow (Donald Sutherland) to keep up the pretense of love that she and Peeta put on for the cameras or else her family would suffer the consequences.

What the Capitol fears most is beginning to spread - the long oppressed citizens of Panem are sensing a changing of the tide. They see hope on the horizon, and this hope is beginning to manifest itself in a rising swell of rebellious spirit. The symbol of their hope is Katniss herself.

With the 75th anniversary of the Hunger Games approaching, Snow and new gamemaster Plutarch Heavensbee (Philip Seymour Hoffman) devise a new wrinkle for the next games. They decree that the new tributes be drawn from a pool of previous winners. They believe it to be a win-win for them: either Katniss will be killed; or she will be seen killing the beloved ex-winners from the other districts. Either way, her influence over the masses will be dramatically ended.

But as her mentor Haymitch (Woody Harrelson) predicts, "this is going to be a game unlike any we've ever seen." All contestants have survived the crucible of the game once before and so are experienced killers. To survive now, alliances will have to be formed.

As one of the characters exhorts to Katniss as she drew her bow with him dead in her sights, "Remember who the real enemy is." It is an exhortation that should strike a resounding chord deep within our souls.

It is easy to forget, as we work through our many trials, tribulations, struggles and daily battles at work and at home, who our true "enemy" is. It is not the driver in the vehicle in front of us who just cut us off. It is not the unjust boss who took credit for our work and then denied us advancement in the workplace. It is not the spouse we trusted who wronged us or the child we love who defied us. It is not even our own selves when we doubt our abilities or act in a self-destructive manner.

Our enemy is invisible and works behind the scenes to invent crises, stir up unrest, disturb our peace, and instill fear in the environment around us. Much like the Capitol in Katniss' world, our enemy is a pretender who is filled with lies and utter disdain for our lives. But he can do nothing unless we allow him to work uncontested.

"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places." Ephesians 6:12 (KJV)

This is something we must remember the next time we find ourselves in a contentious or argumentative situation. If we engage in "battle" we lose because we are simply playing into the hands of our real enemy.
