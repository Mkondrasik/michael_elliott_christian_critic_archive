Author-verified transcription

Tom Cruise straps on a spacesuit to defend the earth’s remaining resources after a blistering alien attack makes the planet nearly uninhabitable in Universal Pictures’ Oblivion.

Cruise plays Jack Harper who, with his control/dispatcher/friend with benefits, Victoria (Andrea Risebourgh), live in relative comfort high above the decimated Earth. Jack roams the planet’s surface repairing the drones that protect the huge hydro-pod thingies that are sucking the water supply out of the ocean to produce energy. Of course he has to keep one eye open for the mysterious, largely unseen “scavs” who pose the greatest threat to what little existence he has.

It is when a transport ship containing sleep chambers with other humans in it comes under attack that Jack begins to question everything he thought he knew. He’s at a disadvantage when it comes to finding the truth because part of his security clause called for his memory to be conveniently “wiped.” It falls to a survivor of the alien war (Morgan Freeman) to help him fill in the gaps and lead him to a few surprising revelations… surprising to him anyway… perhaps not so much to us.

Oblivion is handsomely shot, overly long, and oddly familiar (not in a good way.) It feels piece-mealed together from any number of films we’ve already seen but the pieces don’t always fit as well as they should.

Jack, who often dreams vividly about things he has no memory of, has a sense that there is something being hidden from him; a truth or reality that is lying unseen behind the world that he knows. It is a condition familiar to us. Like Jack, we spend much of our lives unaware of invisible truths or realities that shape and drive much of what we physically see or experience in this world.

“For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse.” Romans 1:20 (KJV)

God’s promise to us is that truth will always be revealed to those who search for it. We are to develop our spiritual senses to the point where we can discern the hidden layers behind the deceptive, surface realities that are presented to us daily by this temporal world.
