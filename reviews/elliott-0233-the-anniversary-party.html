The Anniversary Party

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 9, 2001
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 3:16Ephesians 5:24-25Ephesians 5:2

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love; marriage; selflessness; giving; relationships

Author-verified transcription

Self-absorption is not all that interesting or pretty to watch... not even when being performed by society's "pretty people" (ie. movie celebrities). The Anniversary Party was written, and directed by Alan Cumming (Spy Kids) and Jennifer Jason Leigh (eXistenZ) who cast themselves as Joe and Sally Therrian, a married couple working in the entertainment business whose rocky union has made it to the sixth year.

To celebrate not only their anniversary but also their recent reconciliation following a year-long separation, they've invited their closest friends and enemies to a party at their house. Cast as these friends and neighbors are the stars/directors' real friends and companions. Somewhere in the midst of the feel good/feel bad revelry there is a point being made. The trouble is that it's a dull point and it's not made particularly well.

Sally is a famous actress whose career is backsliding as she gets older. Joe is a novelist about to make his directorial debut as his book, based on his marriage to Sally, is being turned into a movie. But since Sally is too old to play the lead, the part has gone to Skye Davidson (Gwyneth Paltrow, Bounce).

Cal (Kevin Kline, Dave) and Sophia (Phoebe Cates, Fast Times at Ridgemont High) come to pay their respects to their closest friends. Cal, also a famous actor, is currently working on a film with Sally while Sophia gave up her acting career to become a full time mom. Their two children are played by the actual offspring of Kline and Cates.

Also at the party, Mac (John C. Reilly, Magnolia), the unhappy director of Sally's current project and his neurotic wife Clair (Jan Adams, Happiness); business managers Judy and Jerry Adams (Parker Posey, Best of Show and John Benjamin Hickey, Love! Valor! Compassion!); photographer Gina (Jennifer Beals, Flashdance); and next door neighbors Monica and Ryan (Mina Badie, The Weekend and Denis O'Hare, Sweet and Lowdown) who are suing the Therrians over a barking dog.

When tablets of the drug Ecstasy are passed around, inhibitions are dropped, secrets are told, fears are revealed and tears are shed along with articles of clothing. There's nothing too ecstatic about it.

The Anniversary Party was filmed in 19 days on digital video and looks just good enough that we will surely be seeing more of this medium. This will be both a blessing and a curse. A blessing because there may be a few undiscovered talents working today who may now be able to afford to put their vision on the large screen. But it is also a curse because there are far more talentless people who can do the same.

The Anniversary Party is far from talentless. The cast is quite accomplished and certainly no one has damaged their career by their work in this film. The project just smacks of self-indulgence. While it can be argued that the film gives us a rare inside look at how the "image-makers" live, the movie fails to involve us or really let us into the lives of these characters. Thus when the histrionics begin (and continue), we simply aren't compelled to care enough to follow them down their angst-ridden path of self-discovery.

The filmmakers apparently wanted to explore different kinds of relationships and decisions made in the course of those relationships. Thematically, the movie might be summed up, "Love is not enough." If we take the Therrian's relationship as a definition of love, we'd be inclined to agree. But love, as defined by God, comes from a heart inclined to give.

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. John 3:16 (KJV)

The principal characters each professed to love the other, but they wanted that love to be conditional... on their terms. We truly love when we are willing to give of ourselves to bless the one we love.
