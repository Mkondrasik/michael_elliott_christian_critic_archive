Author-verified transcription

Take a ludicrous plot, mix in a sophomoric script, add an unhealthy dose of politically correct tolerance along with a complete disregard for spiritual truth, and you have the recipe used in the nauseatingly bad movie, The Object of my Affection.

To give full credit (or blame) to the responsible parties, look no further than to English director Nicholas Hytner (The Crucible) who sat at the helm of this fiasco and American playwright Wendy Wasserstein (The Heidi Chronicles) who adapted the screenplay from Stephen McCauley's book. Being marketed as a romantic comedy, The Object of my Affection is surprisingly neither romantic nor comedic.

Jennifer Aniston (Picture Perfect) stars as Nina Barowski, a pregnant woman who chooses to raise her child with her homosexual roommate rather than with the boyfriend who impregnated her. Paul Rudd (Clueless) costars as George, the sexually confused first grade teacher who befriends Nina and becomes not only her best friend, but also the object of her affection.

John Pankow (TV's Mad About You), as the father of the child, completes this bizarre "love" triangle (which more accurately begins to take on the shape of a hexagon as we meet many of George's sexual interests.)

No one comes off looking good in this blatant piece of propaganda for the "gay rights" movement. Miss Aniston has never looked less appealing than she does as this weak and miserable woman who keeps choosing for herself the wrong men. Mr. Rudd is even less appealing as a similarly weak man with the same problem. Mr. Pankow portrays nothing more than an extension of his TV sit-com personality. Even Alan Alda (Everyone Says I Love You) and Allison Janney (Primary Colors) as name-dropping literary agents, only add to the general state of confusion which this film painfully mistakes for humor.

Homosexuals want so badly to be accepted into mainstream society. This movie takes the viewpoint that they already are. There are no raised eyebrows over George's sexual disposition. The filmmakers even have George working as a highly acclaimed elementary school teacher. Are the parents of his students upset, scared, or furious at the school system for allowing their young and impressionable children to be exposed to an openly homosexual teacher? No! On the contrary, they applaud George and go out of their way to make him feel welcomed, loved and appreciated. How's that for a modern day fairy tale?

Regardless of the fantasies that practitioners of homosexuality may have regarding how they'd like the rest of the world to view them, the scriptures are clear regarding God's viewpoint on the subject. It's wrong. Not just sometimes wrong; not just borderline wrong; it's wrong.

"Thou shalt not lie with mankind, as with womankind: it is abomination." [Leviticus 18:22 KJV]

The filmmakers, apparently trying to "desensitize" us to the idea of same sex physical relations, went out of their way to include scenes of two men together in bed, two men kissing, hugging and generally being otherwise intimate with each other. Such scenes were repulsive to watch - for good and godly reasons. God considers homosexuality to be abhorrent. Anyone who manifests the true love of God and God's Word will have the same view.

Of course this view is currently under fire in our permissive culture. Those who hold themselves to the standard of God's Word are labeled "homophobes", "hate-mongers", "Nazis" and worse. What we, as Christians, should make crystal clear to our detractors who claim that we are spreading a form of sexual bigotry, is this: True Christians do not operate out of a sense of fear, hate, or oppression. By adopting for our lives the standard of God's Truth, our view on sexual preference becomes a simple matter of right vs. wrong. The Bible says homosexuality is wrong so we believe that homosexuality is wrong. There's nothing hateful about that. And we have nothing to fear from those who choose to live their lives in opposition to the will of God.

It was God Who gave us the free will to choose. With that freedom comes a responsibility. True Christians choose freely to obey and subject themselves to the higher authority of God. An authority Who is all love and all light. Those are two qualities that this film is sorely lacking.
