The Sentinel

Sermon / spiritual topic: Lies; Deception

Author-verified transcription

When your whole life has consisted of protecting the President of the United States, the last thing you want to hear is that you're being investigated for conspiring to assassinate your charge. Such is the nightmare that Secret Service agent Pete Garrison (Michael Douglas) is living in The Sentinel.

To make matters worse, leading the investigation is Garrison's protégé and ex-best friend, David Breckinridge (Keifer Sutherland), who has a personal axe to grind with the man who taught him everything he knows and took from him everything he loves... or so he believes.

Of course, Garrison knows he is innocent of any wrongdoing... at least anything that involves murder... but he also knows that an assassination plot is in the works and that there is a traitor within the service. He must therefore remain free to chase the real killers and unmask the "inside man". The problem is that nobody believes him... nobody except the First Lady (Kim Basinger) whose judgment may be somewhat clouded by a secret and intimately personal matter.

Thus the stage is set for a cat and mouse chase on a grand scale - Washington DC - where the stakes couldn't be higher. Unfortunately, this cat and mouse must be suffering from heat exhaustion because they move at a snail's pace - Tom and Jerry on Quaaludes.

Director Clark Johnson (S.W.A.T.) has a rather traditionally constructed screenplay that is badly in need of tweaking. Unfortunately, the tweaks he gives it barely register. The result is an average cop drama that is hardly thrilling or suspenseful. It does provide a bit of a peek inside Secret Service methods and means but such a look has been granted before in films far more captivating (In the Line of Fire).

Douglas hits his marks consistently as the agent who has something to hide, everything to lose and nothing to support his claims of innocence. Sutherland doesn't do much more than channel his 24 character of Jack Bauer. But seeing as it is a decent character that he does well, this isn't as much of a drawback as it may sound.

Eva Longoria is wasted as a rookie agent who has never, in her vast training, been told that cleavage is not something Secret Service agents are to present while on the job. Kim Basinger is nervously sexy but hardly believable as the First Lady.

What drives the film's suspense (what little there is) is the fact that Garrison cannot be honest with those who suspect him of treason because he is guilty of a moral lapse which he must continue to obfuscate. Lies, even small ones, grow exponentially with the telling. Left to themselves, they quickly outsize our ability to contain them.

Because ye have said, We have made a covenant with death, and with hell are we at agreement; when the overflowing scourge shall pass through, it shall not come unto us: for we have made lies our refuge, and under falsehood have we hid ourselves. Isaiah 28:15 (KJV)

It is far better to live honestly, speaking truth at all times and keeping our integrity intact. We may err at times but when we do, we should immediately admit it (confess it to God) and ask for forgiveness which will be immediately granted. It is our integrity and honesty that will enable us to weather the storms of adversity when they appear.
