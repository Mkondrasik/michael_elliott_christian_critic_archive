Author-verified transcription

In Valiant, a new Disney animated feature, the British heroes of WWII try to keep a stiff upper beak in the feathered face of adversity. In this movie, war is for the birds. For that matter, so is this film.

The Royal Homing Pigeon Service (RHPS) is looking for a few good eggs. Unfortunately, the enemy's falcon brigade has been far too effective in depleting their ranks. The fate of the free world now rests beneath the hapless wings of Squadron F which is formed entirely of misfits.

Valiant is a pint-sized pigeon who dreams of glory and is filled with patriotic fervor. He is befriended by Bugsy (Ricky Gervais), a molting, smelly aviator who gives new meaning to the phrase "dirty bird."

The rest of the flock, surprisingly easy to forget, consists of a pair of muscle-bound, bird-brained brothers (Brian Lonsdale and Dan Roberts) and an egg head named Lofty (Pip Torrens).

Their mission, led by the decorated Gutsy (Hugh Laurie), takes them behind enemy lines, introduces them to the French resistance, and ultimately pits them against the dreaded falcon Von Talon (Tim Curry) in one-on-one arial combat. It is odd subject matter for a kid's film.

And though the animation is first rate, the film fails to deliver. Its bird puns fall flat; the overall tone is rather humorless; and its vintage wartime references will sail far over the heads of its target audience while not being clever enough to engage the parents who brought them to the theater.

With technology being what it is, the only limitation to an animated film remaining today lies in the talent or imagination of those making it. Valiant either needed bigger dreamers or people better equipped to turn dreams into an animated reality. Instead what we get is a poorly conceived project with deeply flawed execution.

The predominately British vocal cast does what they can with a script that gives them little to work with. Scottish actor Ewan McGregor has the youthful energy for Valiant but fails to distinguish the bird so that he stands out from the rest of the flock. Ricky Gervais does a better job with Bugsy, the most identifiable character in the film, but the personal hygiene jokes get stale and smelly very quickly. Tim Curry goes over the top with the villainous Von Talon probably compensating for the lack of wit, humor, or fun within the script itself.

The filmmakers did try to pay tribute to war heroes - those who are valiant enough to stand in the gap to protect others from harm. Every war needs them, including the battles that are spiritual in nature.

"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places." Ephesians 6:12 (KJV)

God has called us to be those valiant ones. We are to resist or withstand the evil in this world and bring salvation to all who are oppressed and in need of spiritual deliverance.
