Author-verified transcription

Sixteen years ago, Tom Hanks showed us how it should be done in Big. In 13 Going on 30, Jennifer Garner shows us once again. She plays a young child inside of a grown person's body.

The film begins with Jenna's 13th birthday party. She invites the popular kids in the school because she badly wants to be accepted by them. They come only to pull a prank and embarrass her. Hurt, she rejects her best friend Matt and hides herself in the closet wishing that she could just be older so her life could be perfect.

Surprisingly enough, her wish comes true. When she comes out of the closet, seventeen years has passed. She's 30, with a job as a professional editor for a prestigious magazine and a boyfriend who is naked in her apartment.

That last item understandably freaks her out a bit. As Jenna (Jennifer Garner) comes to grips with her new grown-up reality, she discovers that the perfect life she wished for isn't all that perfect. First of all, her best friend Matt (Mark Ruffalo) isn't in her life at all. After her 13th birthday, they kind of drifted apart. He's now a photographer and engaged to be married but he still has feelings for the girl he knew back when.

Jenna discovers some other unpleasant things about her life. Most important is the fact that she isn't that nice a person. It seems that seventeen years ago, she made the choice to join the popular crowd. That decision changed other relationships and brought her to the place where she is at 30. It is a place she doesn't much like. But can she change it now?

Jennifer Garner turns in what for me was a surprisingly enjoyable comedic performance. She somehow managed to capture that strange and nebulous age that straddles childhood and adulthood. She is engaging, vulnerable, and delightful as the thirteen year old trapped in a thirty year old body.

Mark Ruffalo, as her best friend Matt, provides ample support. He underplays his character but does let us see the emotional truth of Matt which endears him to us.

Judging from the sniffles coming from the row behind me, I have to say that, as a romantic comedy, 13 Going on 30 delivers big time. The teenage girls who were in the screening audience with me responded with tears and cheers. I can't honestly say that I blame them. Were I a thirteen year old female I might have been sobbing beside them.

There's an old adage that says "Be careful what you wish for... you just might get it." It's a warning that has significance in this film. Jenna wanted to be older so she might have everything that she thought would make her happy. Upon getting her wish and all that she had desired, she finally came to the realization that there was an associated cost. A cost that she wasn't prepared to pay.

So it is with all our decisions. We choose our course and we live with the consequences. The only guarantee we have is if we choose a course that has already been proven successful. Fortunately, God has provided one for us.

"Thus saith the LORD, Stand ye in the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls." Jeremiah 6:16a (KJV).

Each step we take along our journey through life helps to shape and mold our character. We become who we are largely because of what we do. It is why God exhorts us to walk circumspectly or precisely - not as fools but as wise.
