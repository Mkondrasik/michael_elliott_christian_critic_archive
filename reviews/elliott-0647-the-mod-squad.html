Author-verified transcription

When movie makers go to the studios to pitch their projects, they generally have a “fallback” idea or two in their hip pocket to pull out in case their first pitch misses. It wouldn't surprise me to find out that a 90's version of The Mod Squad was some filmmaker's back up, desperation pitch. Regretfully, for the movie-going public, some studio executive must have thought it was a good idea. It wasn't.

Sure, Aaron Spelling's innovative TV series, The Mod Squad, was a cultural icon... back when “groovy” and “right on” were part of the vernacular of the day. But an “updating” of this anachronistic chestnut must have had failure written all over it in bright psychedelic colors from the very start. What's next? Gilligan's Island starring Adam Sandler? (Note to the studio executives: The above was a joke... No one is interested in seeing that!)

The appeal of the 1968 TV show was in the idea that the counterculture of the emerging youth movement of that age could carve out a niche for itself in the dreaded and not to be trusted establishment, succeeding where traditional methods failed. Stars Peggy Lipton, Michael Cole, and Clarence Williams III were the epitome of cool as members of the “lost generation” who found a purpose for themselves as undercover police officers.

The version for the new millennium stars Claire Danes (Polish Wedding) as Julie Barnes (runaway turned cop), Omar Epps (Scream 2) as Lincoln Hayes, (arsonist turned cop), and Giovanni Ribisi (The Other Sister) as Peter Cochran (general screw-up turned cop... now there's a frightening combination).

These three have little in common and less interest in working together until somebody kills their “boss”, Captain Adam Greer (Dennis Farina, Out of Sight). Now it is up to our teen aged miscreants to solve the murder, expose the high level corruption that exists in the police department, rid the streets of drugs and prostitution, all while being underscored by a soundtrack that sounds suspiciously like a bad bootleg copy of Shaft mixed with Starsky and Hutch.

The script by Stephen T. Kay (The Last Time I Committed Suicide), director Scott Silver (Johns) and Kate Lanier (Set it Off) is moronic, insulting the intelligence of even the lowest of target audiences. Most of the actors, who have comported themselves well in other projects, barely escape embarrassment here. The ones who don't, notably Giovanni Ribisi and Michael Lerner, are embarrassed only because they tried to make something happen with their characters, but were given little support from their director.

Mr. Silver simply did not demonstrate a firm grasp of the subject matter. Starting with a weak plot, (made weaker by poor editing and nonexistent pacing), he failed to generate any character or relationship with which we could associate or for whom we could cheer. This “squad” is more flawed than “mod”.

For a group of characters who apparently have been given just one last opportunity at redemption after making a short lifetime of mistakes, these three young people show little in terms of behavior or logic that would indicate that they have gained a maturity or understanding level to appreciate what is being made available to them.

Scriptures say:

Let no man despise they youth; but be the an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity. 1 Timothy 4:12 [KJV]

Our young people can be a brilliantly shining example to others if they can develop the meekness to follow the instruction of the Word. The only example this “mod squad” sets is how not to conduct one's life. We don't need the movies to see that example... We already see that every single day.
