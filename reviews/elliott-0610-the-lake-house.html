Author-verified transcription

Can true love defy (or even ignore) all natural laws to allow time and space to swirl together in one big smiley-face-filled cosmic concoction? Alex and Kate certainly hope so in The Lake House.

As the film’s progresses, we quickly discover that Alex (Keanu Reeves) and Kate (Sandra Bullock) are living in the same house but at separate times. He’s occupying the space a full two years earlier than she but, save for that one small inconvenience, they were made for each other.

With the assistance of a mailbox that contains a kind of postal wormhole, our two would-be lovers begin a bizarre but instant correspondence where we learn precious little about them other than the fact that they are cute and are played by actors who once worked together in Speed.

She’s a doctor who likes to keep her personal life at a distance - although this time-bending love affair is a stretch even for her. He’s an architect/contractor whose famous dad (Christopher Plummer) can hardly give him the time of day. The best personal relationship either of them has happens to be with a mangy dog that, as odd as it may seem, is the same animal. He just entered their respective lives at different times.

Obviously the suspension of disbelief is a prerequisite to watching this improbable story unfold. Fortunately, director Alejandro Agresti seems to be taking matters quite seriously and manages to bring some talent to his filmmaking. This is the reason for my two star review. His film just isn’t bad enough to be ridiculed. But neither is it good enough to overcome the many flaws in the script or the implausibility of the story itself.

Reeves and Bullock fail to recapture the electricity of their earlier collaboration although it may not be entirely their fault. It is admittedly difficult to generate heat when there is no friction. Characters that cannot see or touch one another due to the physics of unexplained time warps can have a tough time in that category.

The back stories of Kate and Alex also seem a bit unexplored or unexplained. More depth to the characters might have helped ease us past the incredulity of their situation. As it is, the strained relationship between Alex and his dad is forced and the awkwardness of Kate’s love life seems strange. A bit more examination in these areas might have built a stronger foundation to support the film’s time twisted love story.

I suppose the following could be considered a spoiler (but only if one believes that simple common sense should not be revealed if it is integral to the plot.): The “insurmountable” problem that is keeping Alex and Kate apart is the fact that they are living two years apart. It is a problem that has everyone in the audience wondering... why don’t they just make a date to meet in her time? He would only have to wait a couple of years for it to happen. We would think that two reasonably intelligent people would be able to figure this out a heck of a lot sooner.

Waiting to be united with a loved one is nothing new to those of Christian faith. Two years is a mere drop in the bucket of time when compared to the millennia we’ve already been waiting for our Lord who has promised to return to us.

Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain. Be ye also patient; stablish your hearts: for the coming of the Lord draweth nigh. James 5:7-8 (KJV)

Living in the hope of a promised future requires patience. But those who are faithful to that hope will receive a joy beyond measure.
