Author-verified transcription

One thing you can be sure of… if it is a Michael Bay film, it won’t be long before something gets blown up. His newest film, The Island, is about as subtle as a tsunami. It is complete with car chases, helicopter gunships, automatic weapons fire, all of which are mixed in with just enough plot to justify the full destructive power that Hollywood artists can muster.

Set in 2019, Lincoln Six Echo (Ewan McGregor) and Jordan Two Delta (Scarlett Johansson) live a quasi-idyllic life. O.K. so it is underground and their individual freedoms are next to non-existent. Their every need is provided for them. Their nutrition is monitored, their diet is regulated, and their laundry, which consists of multiple renditions of the same white jump suit, is regularly done for them.

Of course, free speech is not encouraged and just the idea of sex will cause security to descend upon the randy guy or girl and remove them from general population. This place has got proximity laws that are taken very seriously.

Jobs are rather mundane and life in general is on the dull side. The one bright spot is the regular lottery where a lucky winner is transported to the only uncontaminated place left in the world – “The Island.”

Naturally, it is all a lie. Lincoln uncovers the overly simplistic plot and taking Jordan in tow, tries to escape from the artificially structured environment to discover what life is truly about.

While watching, if you find yourself thinking about Logan’s Run or Soylent Green or A.I.: Artificial Intelligence or any number of other films, well… that can’t be helped. Michael Bay, God bless him, has never been accused of originality. What he does, better than most, is provide adrenalin-producing action scenes (and groan-worthy dialogue). The Island does not disappoint in either category.

Forget that the dialogue is cheesy or that the outcome is obvious. Go to see an incredible high-speed car chase. Enjoy the giant turbine engine blade demolition. Laugh at the comical one-liners that are simultaneously amusing and annoying.

It is an easy film to rip from a critical standpoint. But I’d be a liar if I said it was intolerable. It is entertaining despite the fact that it will never be confused for high art. The action scenes are exciting and, because of the talents of Michael Bay, Ewan McGregor, Scarlett Johansson, Sean Bean, Djimon Hounsou, and Steve Buscemi, they are also considerably enjoyable.

The film’s “drama” is dependent upon Lincoln’s growing curiosity. He questions his purpose and whether or not he has been told the truth during his life thus far. We are in a similar state but, unlike Lincoln, our Creator wants us to know the truth.

For this is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Saviour; who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. 1 Timothy 2:3-4 (KJV)

God has made truth available to us but if we are going to put ourselves in a position to hear it, we must be willing to consider that what we “know” might only be a fraction of what is available to know. By studying that which God has already given us, we can learn the rest. It is God’s will that we do.
