Author-verified transcription

It's been twelve hours since I walked out of The Sweetest Thing and I have to admit that I still have no idea what the title is supposed to reference. There is precious little about this film that could be considered "sweet."

It certainly isn't referring to Christina Walters (Cameron Diaz, Charlie's Angels), a party girl who shakes her booty as often as most people shake hands. Not interested in finding Mr. Right, she goes through a succession of Messrs. Right Now. There is a name for such a woman but "sweet" certainly isn't it.

Nor is Christina's best friend Courtney (Christina Applegate, Just Visiting) the titular character, unless by titular we mean her two "character traits" which are pushed up, padded and probed throughout the film. Employing a twisted brand of logic, we are told that the sure sign that Courtney is serious about a guy is if she isn't having sex with him. Sweet? I think not.

Neither is Jane (Selma Blair, Legally Blonde) deserving of the appellative as she bounces back from losing her boyfriend by entering into one disastrous sexual liaison after another. Since The Sweetest Thing is meant to be a comedy, these hapless and increasingly embarrassing encounters are played for laughs... cheap laughs.

The story is as simple as it needs to be to get these three actresses into various stages of undress. Christina meets Peter (Thomas Jane, 61) in a dance club where he manages not to go gaga at the sight of her (gasp!). His momentary indifference somehow acts as an aphrodisiac for Christina, who spends the rest of the movie trying to find him with her friend Courtney in tow. Their misadventures are either sexual or scatological in nature and are deserving of no further mention here. For an in-depth breakdown of potentially offensive elements, visit the good people of www.screenit.com. This film should have them working overtime.

Directed by Roger Kumble (Cruel Intentions) and written by Nancy M. Pimental (her first feature film screenplay), The Sweetest Thing makes a number of fatal errors, any one of which would have caused this movie to fail. Combined, they make it little more than a dressed-up 90 minute exercise in soft-core porn even though they stop short of showing any actual nudity. Still, for all its sexual posturing, they might as well have called it The Sweatiest Thing.

The biggest problem is one of honesty. The filmmakers aren't honest with their own characters. Sacrificing integrity to chase a joke (or what is meant to be a joke), they will have their characters say and do things that are inconsistent in keeping with their nature. Also, the filmmakers aren't honest with the topics of sex and relationships, minimizing these fundamental realities to the point of making them meaningless. Finally, the filmmakers aren't even honest with their craft. They abandon the basic tenets of storytelling for no good purpose and even manage to botch the resolution of their own muddled picture.

The sexual and romantic game playing employed by Christina and friends has nothing to do with love and serves as a terrible example for those who are of dating age. For a list of love's qualities provided by our God who IS love, we need only turn to 1 Corinthians 13. There we find the simple truth:

Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres. 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 (NIV)

I'm sure there's enough in that description to make a thousand movies. It is too bad that no one referred to it when this film was still on the drawing board.

All in all, the sweetest thing about The Sweetest Thing will only be experienced by those who choose not to see it. To be oblivious to the existence of this film would be very sweet indeed.
