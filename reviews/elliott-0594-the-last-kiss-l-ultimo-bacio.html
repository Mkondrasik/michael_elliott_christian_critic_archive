Author-verified transcription

Growing up and into mature relationships is not necessary a smooth and seamless process. There are challenges at every stage that one must face. In The Last Kiss, we follow a group of friends who are in their thirties as they encounter obstacles to their individual pursuits of happiness.

Carlo (Stefano Accorsi) is living with Guilia (Giovanna Mezzogiorno) with whom he is expecting a child but he knows that he isn’t ready for marriage. Perhaps he has observed his friend Adriano (Giorgio Pasotti) too closely. Married, with a six month old baby, Adriano is left wondering where the romance and excitement to life has gone.

Another friend, Paolo (Claudio Santamaria) has yet to recover from being dumped by the love of his life and is a bit bitter on the whole relationship scene. Alberto (Marco Cocci) is content to live the bachelor life, bedding a different woman each night in a “no-strings” kind of way so we won’t be hearing him extol the virtues of marriage. Even Guilia’s mother (Stefania Sandrelli) is reconsidering her life and what might have been had she not gotten married some twenty-nine years ago.

Disillusioned with the state of their love lives and fearful to what the future may bring, the friends make plans to leave it all behind, rent a camper van and travel across Africa.

Carlo puts those plans on hold when he meets the beautiful eighteen year old Francesca (Martina Stella) at yet another friend’s wedding. Seeing her, he finds himself wondering if life with Guilia is all that he wants or needs. As his temptation grows, so does our awareness of all that he would be risking.

Writer/director Gabriele Muccino has touched a universal nerve with his observation of the changing attitudes towards marriage and the fact that more and more singles are delaying entrance into a committed relationship.

Certainly a contributing factor, at least in terms of these characters, is fear. Commitment, to them, is seen as giving up individual freedom. This is something that they are reluctant to do.

What these characters fail to understand is that commitment is a key ingredient to assured success. Whether we are speaking of a relationship, a job or a task, unless we decide to commit to it, we will not be successful at it.

For he that wavereth is like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed. Let not that man think that he shall receive any thing of the Lord. James 1:6-7 (KJV)

The rewards of committing one’s self to another and working together to achieve a common goal far outstrips any pretense of “freedom” one receives from being alone.
