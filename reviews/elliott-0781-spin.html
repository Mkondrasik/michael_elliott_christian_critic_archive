Author-verified transcription

Spin, the directorial debut of James Redford (son of Robert) asks the question: what is a family? Set in the 1950s, a time when the nuclear family was king, it follows the life of a young man torn between his blood relations and the people who raised him.

Eddie (Ryan Merriman) lost his parents as a young boy when his father crashed a plane into a mountain near their home. He is placed into the care of his uncle Frank (Stanley Tucci) who soon accepts an engineering contract abroad. He leaves Eddie with his ranch manager Ernesto (Ruben Blades) and his wife (Dana Delany) with the understanding that they will become his surrogate parents.

Ten years later, Eddie is a high school student whose unresolved emotions and sense of abandonment sometimes surface in unhealthy and troubling ways. A young Mexican student (Paula Garces) reveals herself to be the same Franchesca who Eddie knew as a child and a possible romantic spark between them is kindled. When Frank suddenly returns to the farm to uphold a promise he once made to Eddie's father -- to teach him to fly -- Eddie finds that he is conflicted.

Based upon the book by Donald Everett Axinn, Spin is a simple coming-of-age story made complicated by elements like racial bigotry, incest and familial bonds.

There isn't anything grand about the story-telling in Spin. It is a small, intimate picture that, while it deals with mature themes and issues, keeps itself well within a PG rated, family-friendly environment.

Ryan Merriman, who was a featured actor in The Deep Side of the Ocean, shows a comfortable, natural presence as Eddie. The sweetness of his demeanor charms the audience but a harder edge to his character might have provided more dramatic tension in the film.

For three quarters of the film Paula Garces has little to do except serve as the romantic foil for the shy Eddie. When her character's troubled home life is revealed, she is able to add more dimensions to what had been a rather threadbare role.

Stanley Tucci, Ruben Blades, and Dana Delany provide mature and solid support. What is particularly nice is that no one in the film is a villain (at least not in the traditional sense). These are genuine, flawed individuals with their own regrets, desires and dreams. The pressures on Eddie come primarily from within himself as he searches for the clues to who he really is and what he really wants to do with his life.

How we define who we are goes far beyond the genetic makeup of our cells. Just as important to the men and women we will become is the way we were raised and the adult influences that had their impact upon us during those years.

It is true that Jesus Christ was God's immaculately conceived son but it would be a huge mistake to overlook the tremendous job Joseph did as his earthly father. Joseph's influence as a righteous man who loved God and aspired to live within his commandments cannot be overstated. It was he who taught Jesus the laws of God; who developed in him the respect and love for his heavenly Father; and who protected him, sometimes by receiving revelation, from those who would harm him.

Our "family" is comprised of more than just those who share a name or a bloodline. It includes all who, because they share a common love, share a common goal and vision.
