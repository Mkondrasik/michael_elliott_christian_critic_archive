BASEketball

Author-verified transcription

The concept is silly, the jokes are sophomoric, the acting (for the most part) is amateurish... and yet, BASEketball, as directed by David Zucker (Airplane!, The Naked Gun) will still attract its share of fans. Don't ask me why. I can't really explain the popularity of these films.

Granted, Airplane! was funny, especially for its time. When it first burst on the scene, the movie-going public was being inundated with airplane-in-distress films. By spoofing the genre, Airplane! helped to bring an end to them. The first Naked Gun was humorous, thanks largely to the brilliant deadpan skills of its star, Leslie Nielsen.

BASEketball (and there's no way around saying this) is simply stupid. It appears to be a spoof about sports in general but instead of wit or genuine satire, it resorts to sexual innuendo, insults, and gross-out humor. It centers around a lame made-up game that combines basketball and baseball rules. It's kind of like the basketball game of "horse" except instead of letters, the shooter gets hits or outs.

The movie stars Trey Parker and Matt Stone, best known for developing the popular TV show South Park. Some may find their irreverent brand of comedy refreshingly bold, I found it forced and largely unfunny. Of the trio of friends which "invent" the game of BASEketball, Dian Bachar (Orgazmo) as Squeak is the only actor who pulls off anything resembling a character.

Even more embarrassing are the many cameo appearances that pepper the story. Bob Costas and Al Michaels show no shame in lowering themselves to the grade school potty humor that BASEketball exalts. Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Dale Earnhardt, Steve Garvey, Reggie Jackson, and Robert Stack all appear before the camera presumably to collect a check and run to the bank. I'm sure they didn't agree to do this film for the advancement of their respective careers. (At least, I would hope they didn't.)

Also "acting" in the film is Baywatch babe, Yasmine Bleeth, Ernest Borgnine, Jenny McCarthy, and Robert Vaughn.

Not surprisingly, sports or athletics is a recurring analogy that God uses in His Word to emphasize certain spiritual truths:

Know ye not that they which run in a race run all, but one receiveth the prize? So run, that ye may obtain. (1 Corinthians 9:24) [RJV]

For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. (Ephesians 6:12) [KJV]

I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith. (2 Timothy 4:7) [KJV]

Athletic imagery found in the Word of God helps us to maintain the proper mindset needed to prevail in the spiritual competition of life. Just as an athlete trains diligently to keep himself in the best possible physical condition, so must we diligently keep ourselves in the best possible spiritual condition by training our minds to think God's truth.

Of course, none of this is evident in the film BASEketball. But then, BASEketball is not a true sport. Neither are Mr. Parker and Mr. Stone true athletes. In fact, based on their work in this film, there is little to suggest they are anything more than goofy personalities.
