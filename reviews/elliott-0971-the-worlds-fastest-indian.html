Author-verified transcription

“I feel the need... the need for speed,” was the catch phrase of Tom Cruise’s character in Top Gun. It could also be the mantra for a man from New Zealand named Burt Munro, whose life story is told in The World’s Fastest Indian.

Munro, played by Anthony Hopkins, was something of a local celebrity in the small New Zealand town of Invercargill. He comes across as sweet, charming, and somewhat eccentric, much like the film itself.

Having spent over forty years tinkering in his shed and making modifications to a 1920 Indian Scout motorcycle, Munro’s big dream is to travel to the Salt Flats of Bonneville Utah during Speed Week and run his antiquated bike with homemade parts through speed trials to see how fast he can get it to go.

Once the money is raised, Burt and his bike begin the long, adventure-filled journey from his sleepy little town to the West Coast city that hardly ever sleeps. Like Crocodile Dundee before him, Munro finds America a bit weird but takes the new experiences in stride as he focuses on his goal of getting to Bonneville.

Despite his lack of funds, lack of support, but no lack of obstacles standing in his way, Munro finally arrived at his final destination and given an opportunity to realize his life’s dream.

What happens next is an inspiration and testament to Burt’s perseverance, resiliency, and “can do” spirit.

Hopkins is simply brilliant as the lead character. Throwing himself into the role with 100% conviction, he is at once thoroughly convincing and delightfully entertaining, reminding us all of why he’s considered one of our greatest talents.

His supporting cast is relegated to not much more than cameo appearances as they play characters who flit in and out of Munro’s life as he travels throughout the world in pursuit of his dream.

Aaron Murphy gets the most screen time as Tom, Munro’s 10 year old next door neighbor who idolizes him and his unusual ways. Diane Ladd appears briefly as a lonely widow who befriends Munro on his long trek to Utah. Paul Rodriguez shows up as an LA used car salesman. Chris Williams is a cross dressing hotel clerk who becomes Munro’s first US fan.

But the crux of the picture remains on the capable shoulders of Hopkins who carries the film from beginning to end and makes it appear effortless.

The secret to Munro’s success can be found in the man’s mindset. To Burt, every problem had a solution; every obstacle could be overcome... one just had to keep working at it until it happened. His drive and dedication to making his dream a reality serves as an inspiration to anyone who has set a vision and desires a goal. It also, at an age where most others would consider retirement, kept him young.

Hope deferred maketh the heart sick: but when the desire cometh, it is a tree of life. Proverbs 13:12 (KJV)

Giving up on one’s dream or goal is akin to a death sentence. If we want to find a true “fountain of youth,” we should develop a passion, set a goal, and pursue it with a soul’s conviction that matches Burt Munro’s.
