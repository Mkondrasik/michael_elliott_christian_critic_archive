Ella Enchanted

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 9, 2004
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 John 1:6Psalms 119:30Joshua 24:15

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Free will; obedience; choice; love

Author-verified transcription

Once upon a time... blah, blah, blah. I’m sorry to say but that’s the feeling one gets while viewing Ella Enchanted. It’s a fairy tale that fails to capture our imagination. Tired, stale, and lame are not adjectives that you want to hear attached to a light comedy/fantasy film. Ella may be enchanted but the audience will soon find themselves disenchanted.

Ella (Anne Hathaway) is a young woman who, as a babe, was given the “gift” of obedience by her inept fairy godmother (Vivica A. Fox). This gift is immediately recognized as a curse because Ella is compelled to do whatever command someone else may give her. No wonder she doesn’t let anyone know of her unique condition.

But when her mother dies and her father remarries, she is saddled with a cruel stepmother and two obnoxious stepsisters who discover her secret and unfairly use it to their advantage.

Ella decides to set off on a quest to find her fairy godmother so she can take back the “gift” that was given. Along the way, she meets an elf who wants to be a lawyer, ogres who want to eat the elf, and giants who just want to be left alone.

She also falls in love with Prince Charmont (Hugh Dancy) who, in his naiveté, doesn’t understand what his evil uncle, Edgar (Cary Elwes) has done to pollute and corrupt the kingdom. But even as Prince Char and Ella grow emotionally closer, her “gift” threatens to pull them apart because people continue to abuse their control over her to achieve their own self-serving goals against her will.

It’s actually a cute idea, but in the hands of director Tommy O’Haver, it self-destructs and melts down into an incomprehensible mess. The single joke - that Ella is compelled to obey whatever command is given her - is flogged to near death and ceases to be funny within the first five minutes.

The actors probably are as happy as they can be in the story, but the failure of the film should not lie at their feet. The old adage reminds us that we can’t make a silk purse out of a sow’s ear. This particular script smells like it has been in the barn for a while.

It is a disappointment because the concept has a considerable amount of promise. The fairy tale it tells serves as a pretty good parable - reminding us of the free will that we’ve been given by our God.

As our Creator, God could have easily made each of us to be the kind of obedient puppet that Ella must be due to an ill-advised spell by a well-meaning fairy. But as a loving and just Creator, He instead blessed us with the wonderful quality of free will. He did not wish us to be compelled to obey. Rather, He wanted us to obey willingly - of our own volition - motivated by love.

And this is love, that we walk after his commandments. This is the commandment, That, as ye have heard from the beginning, ye should walk in it. 2 John 1:6 (KJV)

Unlike Ella, we do have choices to make. Obedience to the ways of God will lead to blessings and abundance, but part of the reward that awaits is the knowledge that we have freely chosen the course we take.
