Author-verified transcription

It's an old story. Boy meets girl. Boy loses girl. Boy enlists all of Chicago to help find girl. On The Line tells its story with all the subtlety of an "El" train which is appropriate since that is where much of the action is set.

N'Sync band member Lance Bass stars in his feature film debut as Kevin Gibbons, a "talented" though somewhat goofy adman who has trouble putting himself "on the line." Especially when it comes to women, Kevin is known as something of a "choke" artist. Thus, when he meets the girl of his dreams (Emmanuelle Chriqui, A.I.) on the "El," he simply lets her leave without getting her name or number.

After realizing that he's being a bit of a "wuss," Kevin risks public humiliation and engages in a citywide ad campaign to find her. Using posters and a series of newspaper articles, he prompts a flood of responses from lonely Chicago women, a result which thrills his extremely immature and opportunistic friends, but leaves him no closer to reuniting with his fantasy date.

Will Kevin and his mystery woman ever meet again? Will he ever take credit for the ideas which his coworkers steal as their own? These are questions that anyone familiar with trite writing will be able to answer without hesitation. The far more interesting questions are these: After Glitter and On the Line, who will be the next pop star to bring us a less than stellar film? How many more debut duds must we see before studios realize that pop star popularity does not a film actor make.

The most enjoyable moment of the film comes during the running of the credits when costars Lance and Joey are joined by other members of their boy band (in disguise) for some quick and moderately funny repartee. It showed, however briefly, a spark of life at the end of an otherwise dead and sparkless film. If any producer is game for another N'Sync film project, perhaps a faux reality film like A Hard Day's Night (The Beatles), Head (The Monkees), or ::shudder:: Spice World (The Spice Girls) might be something they could actually pull off.

In On The Line, characters are reduced to a one word descriptive: Lance is bland, which is an odd choice seeing as his character is often described as having great passion; Joey opts simply for crude which makes more sense as that is also how his character is described. As Lance's friends, rapper GQ (The Bomb-itty) is annoying and James Bulliard (Full Disclosure) went for either intelligent or artsy, I couldn't really tell. The one bright note in the film is Emmanuelle Chriqui, who is very cute in her one dimensional role.

Perhaps N'Sync fans will appreciate the film to a far greater extent than I but they would have to be pretty big and forgiving fans. The soundtrack does feature selections by Lance and the boys as well as Richie Sambora and Al Green both of whom appear in the film. The music may not actually produce nausea but it is almost as forgettable as the film in which it is featured.

First time screenwriters Eric Aronson and Paul Stanton do have a valid point to make. They just make it poorly. To succeed in life, one must participate in that life. Sitting on the sidelines due to a fear of failure or rejection is a sure way not to get what we most want. Most of the time the only thing preventing us from getting our heart's desire is our own inaction... even when there's a guarantee that we'll get it, we don't do the smallest thing that will make it ours.

And I say unto you, Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you. Luke 11:9 (KJV)

This scripture provides the key to getting what we want. Ask, seek, and knock are verbs. They indicate that action must be taken on our part. God promises that He will do His part and He is faithful to perform His promises. But He won't act unless we take the first step towards believing action.
