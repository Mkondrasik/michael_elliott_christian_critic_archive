The Call

Author-verified transcription

The life of a 911 operator gets the big screen treatment in the 2013 thriller The Call. Halle Berry plays Jordan, an experienced emergency call dispatcher, who must break all the rules of her trade in her attempts to save a young caller on the other end of the line.

Casey (Abigail Breslin) is the girl in distress which is quite an understatement seeing as she is making her 911 call from the trunk of a psychotic killer’s car. It is a killer Jordan knows only too well, having handled a call from his last victim some six months previous. That call did not have a happy ending.

As her cop boyfriend (Morris Chestnut) tries unsuccessfully to track and catch the kidnapper/killer, Jordan spends most of her time on the phone trying to calm Casey down so she might provide clues that will help the police find her. The tension is palpable and Halle Berry plays her character perfectly throughout most of the film. Near the end, the film takes a sharp and disconcerting turn that is inconsistent with everything that came before but everything that led up to that point was hitting all the right notes.

Michael Eklund is stereotypically creepy as the kidnapper. Abigail Breslin does what she can with a role that calls for her to be hysterical about 90% of her screen time.

Early in the film, Jordan quotes her policeman father regarding the pressure of living with the reality that you “might be the difference between somebody living and somebody dying.” As Christians, that concept is one that we should really take to heart and all too often don’t.

Because we have been blessed with the knowledge and awareness of our position in Christ, we are able to live with a confidence and a vibrant hope that exceeds human understanding. Not everyone else does. How many people do we walk by without expressing the joy and the truth we know to be available? How often do we stay silent when those around us are in real spiritual need?

Knowing that we might be the difference between somebody living and somebody dying is not something to take lightly. Add to this awareness the knowledge that the life we might save is an eternal life vs. an eternal death and it becomes very heavy indeed. God has given us the ministry of reconciliation so we might bring people back to Him. He exhorts us to be bold and speak His truth to all so that those who have the heart to receive His Word can be welcomed into His family – His kingdom – for all of eternity. How can we stay silent or stand idly by when the stakes are that high?
