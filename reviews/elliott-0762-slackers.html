Author-verified transcription

College. To some it is a time of study, intellectual development, and an opportunity to broaden one's horizons. To the main characters of Slackers, it's nothing more than a four year romp into the world of drugs, sex and moronic pranks. These guys "don't need no stinkin' classes." The learning stuff can be handled by anyone who knows the "scam."

If it is one thing that Dave (Devon Sawa, Final Destination), Sam (Jason Segel, Freaks & Geeks), and Jeff (Michael C. Maronna, Home Alone) have learned, it's how to work the scam. Dependent upon the nefarious acquisition of collegiate "blue books" used for class exams, their penchant for cheating has gotten them through the last four years of college and netted them an impressive grade point average even though they rarely attend the classes they take. Now on the eve of final exams, they are one scam away from graduation and one misstep closer to their high paying career goals.

Enter "Cool Ethan" (Jason Schwartzman, Rushmore), a self-nicknamed psychotic dweeb who stumbles upon their scam and threatens to expose the cheating trio. Fortunately for them, Ethan has a price. Her name is Angela (James King, Pearl Harbor). If the scam artists can work a miracle and hook Ethan up with his dream date, all transgressions will be forgotten. It's a task easier said than done... even to these artists of duplicity.

The reason is evident and twofold: One, Ethan is a major nutcase and therefore pretty much considered to be poor date material. Two, Angela is such a babe that Dave has fallen for her himself and it appears the feeling might be mutual. They should have known better to have doublecrossed a guy who has given himself a nickname. "Cool Ethan" loses his cool at this unexpected turn of events and it isn't a pretty sight.

While there are a number of amusing sequences to be found in this film, (many of them a result of the over the top quirkiness of Jason Schwartzman), the film's unrestrained offbeat humor leads to a breakdown of a moral framework within which the characters might have found some redemption. Without that framework, we find once again that the envelope of taste can be pushed only so far before it folds back over onto itself, smothering any point (comedic or otherwise) that it may have been trying to make. When, oh when, will filmmakers ever learn?

Famed fashion photographer Dewey Nicks, who directed the award winning commercial "Let's Light This Candle" for Ameritrade, makes his feature film directorial debut here. Showing an interesting visual style, he unfortunately has decided to take the gross-out humor approach to storytelling. The film does reflect a talent of some note, but its taste for "shock humor" will wear thin on all but those weaned on the comedy of Tom Green and The Farrelly Brothers.

The moral "lesson" given in the film (ie. it is better to tell the truth than to lie) is neither sincere nor fully represented. These slackers never really learn their lesson nor are their actions followed by appropriate consequences. The odd thing is that their "scam" took as much if not more effort than simply studying the material and taking the tests honestly. Had they done so, the end result (receiving an actual education) would have been far more favorable.

God's Word tells us that we will reap (or gather) what we sow (or plant). There are some paths for which short cuts simply do not work. The path of knowledge and wisdom is one of those.

He becometh poor that dealeth with a slack hand: but the hand of the diligent maketh rich. Proverbs 10:4 (KJV)

A slack hand might bring immediate gratification but the scriptures state that it will lead to poverty. A college education is too great an opportunity to throw away or treat as if it were meaningless. The diligent know better.
