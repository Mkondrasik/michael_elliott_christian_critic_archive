Author-verified transcription

The phrase "all that glitters is not gold" is going to be getting a lot of use this weekend as critics struggle to find words to describe just how bad Glitter is. The story of a young girl rising to the dizzying heights of stardom, surpassing her boyfriend/producer who is heading in the opposite direction, has been told many times before but never with this degree of ineptitude. Reminiscent of " A Star Is Born," Glitter should instead be subtitled "A Star is Boring."

Mariah Carey (The Bachelor) heads the cast as Billie Franklin, a young girl with a big voice, who is abandoned by her young mother at the age of eleven. With her gal pals Roxie (Tia Texada, Bait) and Louise (Da Brat, Kazaam), she grows up and becomes a dancing fixture at a club a la Studio 54.

It is there that she is "discovered" by disreputable producer Timothy Walker (Terrence Dashon Howard, Big Momma's House) who tries to exploit Billie's talent. Dice AKA Lucky Seven (Max Beesley, Kill Me Later), a DJ at the club, steps in to save the day. Casting Walker aside, Dice produces Billie's first song and it goes to the top of the charts.

Dice and Billie become a couple (in a series of courting sequences which are so clichéd and antiquated that they are laughable) but when the major players in the music industry take an interest in Billie, Dice starts to feel less and less important.

Director Vondie Curtis-Hall (Gridlock'd) tries to distract the audience from realizing the vapidity of his film by using various cinematic tricks and camera effects but it's like gluing glitter on garbage. All he's done is bring attention to the garbage.

I'm not even sure that fans of Mariah Carey will find something to appreciate here. The music simply isn't that memorable. And that's her long suit.

As an actress, she is limited in what she can do. Changing costumes more often than expressions, her biggest character choice seems to have been where to place a swash of silver body paint. It inexplicably travels from her chest to her arm to her back. It's as if the Nike "swoosh" gained a life of its own and decided to attack a recording artist.

Max Beesley offers a little more substance in his role as a New York DJ/musician turned producer but is severely hampered by the clichés of the script by Kate Lanier (The Mod Squad) and banality of the story. Tia Texada and Da Brat (sounds rather like a cartoon crime fighting team) merely add to the unintentional camp in their stereotypical doo-wop roles.

Perhaps the most poignant aspect of Glitter is the reminder of how quickly and unexpectedly our minds will return to the events of September 11th. Glitter is set in New York City and in its many establishing shots we catch glimpses of the twin towers of the World Trade Center still standing.

From the dialogue, we gather that Billie has trouble trusting people. We can sympathize. People often disappoint. We often disappoint those who put their trust in us. As humans we will sometimes exhibit weakness despite our best efforts to stand strong. We will lie, break promises, and act selfishly. Thankfully, when we honestly ask for forgiveness after these moments, God grants it to us and gives us encouragement to try again. There is one, however, who never disappoints. One who cannot lie and will never turn his back upon those who rely upon him.

It is better to trust in the LORD than to put confidence in man. Psalms 118:8 (KJV)

There is none so strong as our God. There is none so loving or giving as our God. And there is none in whom we can trust with our whole heart without fear or risk of disappointment as our God. His greatest joy, as is true of any father, is to have His children trust Him completely, receiving of Him all the goodness that is in Him to give.
