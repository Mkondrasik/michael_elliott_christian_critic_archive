Fracture

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 20, 2007
Rating
3 stars

Sermon / spiritual topic: Choices

Author-verified transcription

The game of cat and mouse can be so entertaining to watch that we often forget that it's a life or death struggle for one of the participants. In the poorly named Fracture, two cocky, self-assured men face off in a battle of wits. At stake: one man's reputation and another man's life.

Prosecutor Willy Beachum (Ryan Gosling) is cashing in on his undefeated status as a government paid lawyer by joining the ranks of his elite colleagues practicing corporate law. But before he makes the jump, he's handed one last case – a man named Ted Crawford (Anthony Hopkins) has confessed to killing his wife. Beachum simply needs to walk him through the process for another slam dunk conviction. Or so he thought.

It slowly dawns on Beachum that he and the system are being manipulated by a clever and conniving man who believes he's smarter than all of them. So smart, in fact, that he'll be able to get away with murder.

Fracture is a clever little murder mystery thriller that is enhanced by two strong performances. Hopkins eases into the role of Crawford like it is a comfortable suit. The odd combination of diffident arrogance he gives his character is so expertly managed that he can play the bad guy without alienating the audience. We know we are supposed to root for the lawyer but we do enjoy watching the master at work.

Gosling develops a nice arc or growth pattern for Beachum. I think he may have started off a bit too nonchalant but as the story progresses, so does the depth of his characterization.

The element that didn't particularly work was the romantic relationship between Beachum and his corporate boss, Nikki (Rosamund Pike). It was too convenient, too contrived, and not nearly enough developed. I could understand it from a plot perspective but that doesn't mean that I believed it for a second.

Without the work of Hopkins and Gosling, Fracture probably would have become just another mundane entry in an overpopulated genre. Their work is what sells the film and makes it something worth watching.

The biggest decision in the film is the one made by Beachum. After Crawford pulled one over on him (and the state) most everybody was advising him to lick his wounds, move onto his new job and forget it. It certainly would have made financial sense. But it would have meant letting Crawford get away with evil.

“All that is necessary for the triumph of evil is that good men do nothing.” (Edmund Burke)

Beachum had to make the decision of risking his future and his reputation as a lawyer to continue to pursue someone he knew to be guilty of a heinous crime. Our choices may not be as dramatic as the one presented in this film but they exist nonetheless. We are daily challenged to choose between good and evil in a world that often recognizes and rewards evil.

God asks us to be living sacrifices in this world by making choices that will glorify Him and be a witness to all He has called. Sometimes those choices will be hard. But they will never be in vain.

I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service. Romans 12:1 (KJV)
