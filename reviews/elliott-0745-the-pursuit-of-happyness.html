Author-verified transcription

Henry David Thoreau once observed that "most men live lives of quiet desperation." He must have imagined someone like Chris Gardner of The Pursuit of Happyness when he first wrote those words.

Gardner (Will Smith), like many of us, was living paycheck to paycheck... and not always successfully. Having sunk his life savings into a medical device that proved to be slow to catch on with medical professionals, he and his family are now struggling to make ends meet.

It proves too much for his wife (Thandie Newton), who bows out early in the film, leaving Chris to raise his six-year-old son (played by real life son Jaden Smith) on his own.

Gardner, looking for a new opportunity, interviews for and receives an internship at Dean Witter. What he didn't realize was that the six month experience was unpaid and held no guarantee of employment upon its conclusion. In fact, of the twenty interns, only one is offered a position with the firm.

Gardner starts the film in debt and in deep trouble. It gets worse from there. The movie is the equivalent of watching the book of Job... with the first chapter taking up the first 90 minutes of its nearly 2 hour running time. The filmmakers repeatedly throw Chris under the bus only to have him doggedly get up and keep plodding forward.

I'm sure the filmmakers wanted to present a "feel-good" inspirational movie to its audience but somewhere along the way it lost its balance. The burdens borne by Chris are deeply felt by us and his relief, when it comes, is too late to justify sitting through the agony of what he went through.

Will Smith is a fine actor and presents a mature and thoughtful performance. It unfortunately is wasted on a script and director who couldn't shape the film in a more palatable way.

Thandie Newton treads a fine line by playing Gardner's wife who abandons her husband and child when times get tough. She should consider the fact that we don't hate or judge her too harshly as a victory. She shows vulnerability in what could have been an unlikable character.

The Pursuit of Happyness was based on the life story of Chris Gardner and I certainly do not intend, through this review, to diminish his achievements. The film may not have worked as well as it might but his life story does illustrate the power that exists in God's design.

The law of believing, which God initiated, remains the key to having success in life. When we believe negatively, we receive negative results. When we believe positively, God promises us positive results. The challenge, as Chris demonstrated, is keeping our believing - and the actions that stem from that believing - positive even when our outward circumstances would lead us to nothing but negative thinking.

Scriptures state that "as a man thinks, so is he." If we allow negative thoughts to dominate our mind's activity, we shall receive the rotten fruit of those seeds that we have planted. Healthy fruit (or results) are grown from healthy, positive thinking. To paraphrase a simplistic observation: "Winners think like winners; Losers think like losers."

God tells us that by enduring in the face of opposition, we shall receive the victory and the glory that we desire. Perseverance is difficult but the rewards of it are beyond our understanding.

Behold, we count them happy which endure. Ye have heard of the patience of Job, and have seen the end of the Lord; that the Lord is very pitiful, and of tender mercy. James 5:11 (KJV)
