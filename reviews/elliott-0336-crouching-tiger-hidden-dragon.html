Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon

Original publication date

Author-verified transcription

Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon has been slowly opening across the USA for the past couple of months, receiving both critical and audience acclaim as well as award nominations by the bucketful. I'm happy to report that, for once, the hype has not exceeded the actual experience. This is a masterful picture of the highest caliber.

Directed by Ang Lee (Sense and Sensibility) and based on a Chinese story by Du Lu Wang, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon mixes the right proportion of romance, philosophy and martial arts action to come up with a stunning and enthralling cinematic experience.

Li Mu Bai (Yun-Fat Chow, Anna and the King) is a legendary Wuxian warrior renowned for his Wudan fighting skills. Such skills enable these experts to defy physics, literally floating on air and skipping over rooftops and up the face of 15 foot walls. Li has returned home to retire from Giang Hu (the martial arts life) perhaps to begin a long repressed romantic relationship with Shu Lien (Michelle Yeoh, Tomorrow Never Dies) who also is trained in the Wudan techniques.

When Li's sword, "The Green Destiny," is stolen, Li and Shu Lien become embroiled in a search for the notorious Jade Fox (Pei-pei Cheng, The Spirit of the Dragon) who poisoned Li's master many years previously. Along the way they come across Jade Fox's young student, Jen Yu who has an impressive fighting technique but lacks the necessary philosophical understanding which would complete her training. Jen is torn between her upcoming nuptials, her love for a desert bandit, and the irresistible pull of the free and rebellious life of a Wuxian warrior. Li, recognizing her potential, offers her the opportunity to become his disciple and receive the knowledge and wisdom he has developed over the years. It is an offer which she impetuously rejects leading to much of the conflict which gives the film its tension.

Ang Lee has not made a by-the-numbers "kung-fu, chop-sockey" film which is to be mindlessly enjoyed and then forgotten in the space of a few hours. Deftly crafted and beautifully filmed, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon is richly endowed with great characters, a cultural awareness and sense of history, and yes, fabulously choreographed fight scenes (Woo-ping Yuen, The Matrix) that are breathtaking in their scope and precision.

Added to that is a lyrically haunting score by Tan Dun which features the talented cellist, Yo Yo Ma, and some of the most cinematically stunning images seen in the past year. The treetop duel between Li and Jen is one of the most delicately filmed scenes I have ever witnessed, resembling a ballet more than a fight.

Because the Wudan training emphasizes inner strength over external strength, there are many spiritual aspects to the film to be seen... some of which parallel the biblical principles by which we lead our lives. An example: Jen learned the Wudan ways by reading a manual which Jade Fox had stolen. Li sees that she has mastered the moves, but lacks the mental discipline to master herself thus her training is incomplete. He tells her, "You've read the manual but you don't understand it. You need a master."

Reading the Bible is something that is important for all of us to do. But for a complete spiritual education, it isn't enough. We all need teachers, those who are wiser and more fully instructed, to help us in order that we may reach a full understanding.

And Philip ran thither to him, and heard him read the prophet Esaias, and said, Understandest thou what thou readest? And he said, How can I, except some man should guide me? And he desired Philip that he would come up and sit with him. Acts 8:30-31 . (KJV)

When we are self taught, we are limited by the knowledge, ability, and experience of our instructor.
