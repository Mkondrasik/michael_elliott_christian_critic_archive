Author-verified transcription

"Anything can happen on a snow day!" Anything, it would appear, except for the making of a decent movie about it. Nickelodeon's latest film, Snow Day, is a disappointing one for both young and old alike.

Teen Hal Branston (Mark Webber, Drive Me Crazy) is head over heels infatuated with school goddess Claire (Emmanuelle Chriqui, Detroit Rock City). Up to now, he's been invisible to her. But with the advent of a snow day, he feels that love is in the air. He is just too snow-blind to realize that the love he feels is coming from the direction of his gal-pal, Lane (Schuyler Fisk, The Babysitter's Club).

His kid sister (Zena Grey, The Bone Collector) isn't too interested in her brother's love life. She has bigger fish to fry. This is the year that Syracuse kids will have their revenge on their age-old nemesis, "Mr. Snowplow Man" (Chris Elliott, Cabin Boy) who lives only to clear streets with his monster rig (named Clementine) so schools can reopen.

Meanwhile, Dad (Chevy Chase, Vegas Vacation) is just trying to boost his ratings. As the third ranked weatherman in a three station market, he is forced to wear hula skirts and snowman outfits in a desperate plea for viewers. Mom (Jean Smart, Guinevere) isn't much support as she's in the middle of a global business deal which causes her to neglect the family that needs her, especially 4 year old Randy (Connor Matheus in his film debut).

There's the basic framework of the movie. Throw in a few conflicts such as Claire's ex-boyfriend (David Paetkau, Disturbing Behavior) and an obnoxious weatherman on a competing station (John Schneider (The Dukes of Hazzard). Add a couple of running jokes like the principal getting pelted with snowballs in every scene and the skating rink guy playing only Al Martino records. Mix them together and we have the basic formula for a movie targeted for the preteen set. Frankly, I think that the filmmakers have missed their target by aiming too low.

A few of the actors manage to turn in likable performances. Most notably, Mark Webber as a stereotypically nice guy, Jean Smart as a stereotypically career-driven Mom, and John Schneider as a stereotypically shallow TV personality. Chevy Chase shows little of the bemusing comedic style which has earmarked his performances of years past. Chris Elliott is merely difficult to watch as the hygienically challenged snowplow driver who is bereft of even the most basic of social skills.

Nickelodeon has entrusted the film to a novice director (Chris Koch) and two novice screenwriters (Will McRobb and Chris Viscardi). The promising premise of how the spontaneous freedom of a "snow day" affects a group of people lays unrealized at their feet. Too often they have settled for caricatures and comedy punctuated by flatulence instead of developing real characters and more substantial humor.

But the premise is a good one, isn't it? Who doesn't look back fondly on the days when, as kids, we would awaken to find a white blanket of snow on the ground and the radio announcer running down the list of school closings. When our school's name was finally given, our giddiness knew no bounds. Even for those of us who actually liked school, the idea of a surprise day off simply felt too good to be denied.

If only the filmmakers could have captured that same school-age joy. Instead they give us plots of revenge and felonious mischief. In the film, children continually show their disrespect to adults while the adults, quite frankly, give the children little reason to respect them.

A snow day is representative of a certain kind of freedom... But just as with the freedom we are given by God, we should not take advantage of it by purposely doing wrong.

For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another. Galatians 5:13 (KJV)

A day off from school does not mean a day with no rules. No more than a day away from a church building means a day without God.
