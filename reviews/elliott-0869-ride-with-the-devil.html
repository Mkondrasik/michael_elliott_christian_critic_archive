Ride With The Devil

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 24, 1999
Rating
3 stars

2 hr: 18 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Habit; Faithfulness; Conduct

Author-verified transcription

As we near our headlong plunge into the 21st century, what better way to grasp the significance of where we are going than to look back to the 19th century and be reminded as to how far we’ve come? Director Ang Lee (Sense and Sensibility) has done a magnificent job in capturing the look and feel of the Civil War era in Midwestern America.

Be advised that this is not a typical Blue vs. Grey/Battle of Gettysburg reenactment. Based upon Daniel Woodrell’s novel “Woe to Live On,” Ride With The Devil focuses upon the little known bands of confederate “bushwhackers” who would, under cover of night and bushes, raid Union camps and homes of Union sympathizers in and around the Kansas and Missouri territories.

The central characters are boyhood pals, Jack Bull Chiles (Skeet Ulrich, The Chill Factor) and Jake “Dutchy” Roedel (Tobey Maguire, Pleasantville) who, after seeing one of their fathers gunned down by the northern aligned “jayhawkers,” decide to actively resist the Yankee aggression and fight for the only life that they’ve known or desired. The war, to them and many like them, is not politically or ideologically motivated. It is personal.

Becoming part of the “irregular army” by joining with others of similar backgrounds, they move from place to place looking for the ultimate battle which will have a great impact and give a substantial meaning to the choices they’ve made. The problem is, all the “meaningful” battles are being waged back east.

In addition to the two friends, we also meet George Clyde (Simon Baker-Denny, Judas Kiss), an erudite Southern gentleman with a sharp wit; Daniel Holt (Jeffrey Wright, Celebrity), a ex-slave freed by George and has slavishly remained by his side; and Sue Lee Shelly (Jewel in her feature film debut), a young Southern widow who befriends the rebels during a harsh winter lull in hostilities.

The acting, across the board, is exceptional. As the central figure, Mr. Maguire plays a foreign born Missouran who is determined to fight alongside his friends and neighbors. His determination, however, does not blind him to the atrocities and the infinite sadness which has affected every citizen of this divided country.

As the only bushwhacker in his group to have learned his letters, Jake is able to read the purloined correspondence written by northern families which express, in simple terms, such universal feelings as loss, hope, and love.

James Schamus (The Ice Storm), a frequent collaborator with Mr. Lee, has written a script which rings remarkably true to the period in which the action takes place. The intelligent and thoughtful dialogue he has penned does more to communicate the character and heart of the men and women on screen than any other element of the film. It is an achievement not to be understated.

The violence depicted in Ride With The Devil is appropriate to its wartime setting. There are scenes which may prove to be unsuitable for young or impressionable viewers.

I was taken by how the civility and gallantry of most of the Southern men was not superseded by the enmity they felt toward their enemy. In the midst of a fierce gunfight where the bushwhackers were pinned down in a house surrounded by opposing troops, both sides ceased fire because two women were still inside the house. After the women were given their leave, the barrage of bullets immediately resumed.

The spiritual point to be made is that even in the midst of the most horrendous conditions, the habitual thought or practice developed over a lifetime of faithful adherence is not easily forgotten or dismissed. As Christians, we should continue to build the habits of a godly life now, so when the pressure does hit, our thoughts and actions will automatically turn to God.

Ephesians 2:2 reminds us that we’ve all, at one time or another, ridden with the devil. But our habit should be to put the past behind us and instead ride with God.
