Author-verified transcription

How times have changed and how we have grown as a people. Interracial relationships just don't have the shock value that they used to have. In Guess Who, the filmmakers don't even attempt to take the film down the same preachy path of tolerance as Guess Who's Coming to Dinner, the 1967 film that may share the same premise but very little else.

Percy and Marilyn Jones (Bernie Mac and Judith Scott) are preparing to celebrate their 25th anniversary by renewing their vows in front of friends and family. Daughter Theresa (Zoe Saldana) will be in attendance along with her current boyfriend, Simon. Daddy couldn't be happier because he has run the boy's credit history and found that he's smart and successfully employed. There's just one little detail that Theresa has failed to mention to anyone - Simon (Ashton Kutcher) happens to be white.

It's a detail that is hard to hide in a room full of black people and it accounts for some awkward moments when he is finally introduced to Theresa's close knit family. But as the story progresses, the race issue all but goes away. Instead the film focuses on more traditional romantic comedy fare - trust, honesty, and the difficulties faced by two individuals trying to merge their separate lives into one.

The two featured couples (Percy Marilyn and Theresa Simon) show that forming and keeping a loving relationship is a process that does not necessarily get easier with time - be it black, white, or mixed. The film hits its stride when it settles down and shows how the characters work to keep their love alive and their relationship strong.

Kutcher and Mac have decent chemistry that could have been more full exploited. Instead, the filmmakers continue to fall back upon time tested slapstick and superficial situational humor: Percy and Simon in a hard core go-cart competition: Percy and Simon sharing a bed: Percy catching Simon wearing his daughter's lingerie... such silly hijinks do not begin to tap into the richness that could have been mined from what is essentially a sweet and sentimental story.

The strongest moment of the film comes when the male characters try to describe what the women in their lives mean to them. What they eventually come to describe falls into lockstep with God's original design.

For this cause (marriage› shall a man leave his father and mother. and cleave to his wife: And they twain shall be one flesh: so then they are no more twain. but one flesh. Mark 10: -8 (KJV;

I have been single and I have been married. I know from experience that I have become a far better man due to the support I receive from my wife. She adds strength where I am weak: sheds light at times when I might be blind: and completes me in every other area of life. A man who has experienced being part of a successful marriage relationship, recognizes that without his partner he is only a fraction of the person he knows he can be. It is a lesson that both Percy and Simon come to learn - a lesson that knows no color barriers or cultural divides.
