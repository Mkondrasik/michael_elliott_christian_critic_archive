Toy Story 2

Sermon / spiritual topic: Purpose

Author-verified transcription

The toys are back in town. And none too soon. The poor artwork and weak story of Pokemon, last week's animated offering, was enough to sour anyone off animated films. Leave it to Disney and Pixar Animation Studios to restore faith in the genre. Toy Story 2 is not only every bit as good as the original, it is, in many ways, better.

The cast of characters are pretty much the same. Woody, Buzz Lightyear, Mr. Potato Head, Slinky the dog, Bo Peep, Hamm the piggy bank, Rex the dinosaur and the other toys are back with the same voices giving them dimension.

As the film opens, Buzz (voice of Tim Allen, Home Improvement) is battling an intergalactic war (on a video game) as Woody (voice of Tom Hanks, Saving Private Ryan) searches for his hat, preparing to accompany his owner, Andy (voice of John Morris, The Nightmare Before Christmas), on their annual trek to "cowboy camp." Due to an accident, Woody is left behind and mistakenly ends up in the bane of any toy's existence... the dreaded yard sale. It is up to Buzz and the rest of the gang to save Woody and return him to his rightful home before Andy gets back from camp.

The movie follows two main story lines: the rescue of Woody, and Woody's discovery of his past which brings with it a sense of responsibility which may affect his future.

The computer generated animation techniques used in Toy Story are seen to have been greatly improved. The depiction of Andy's family pet, Buster (a playful dachshund), as well as toy store owner (and rare toy collector) Al McWhiggin (voice of Wayne Knight, Seinfeld) are merely two examples of how sophisticated the process has become.

As accomplished as the technically precise artistic skill is, the success of the film is due mainly to the superb writing. Screenwriters Andrew Stanton, Rita Hsiao, Doug Chamberlin and Chris Webb have filled the screenplay with heart. The characters are warm, funny and identifiable.

Randy Newman's (A Bug's Life) soundtrack also helps to enhance the emotional texture of the film. His song, "When She Loved Me," sung by Jessie the cowgirl (voice of Joan Cusack, Arlington Road), a toy from Woody's past, is a poignant ballad which tells the tale of a child outgrowing her toys. It is one of the major themes of the film, the other being a pet peeve of mine... Collectibles.

Woody finds out that he is a rare collectible Sheriff Woody doll, the featured part of a set of toys called Woody's Round-up. When Al finds him in the yard sale, he completes the set which can be sold for thousands. Woody will be preserved for many generations, but he will never be played with again. An examination of the true purpose of toys follows. Favoring a "life" behind museum glass is Prospector Stinky Pete (Kelsey Grammar, Frasier), a member of Woody's Round-Up and a toy who has never been out of his box.

We can take this cue to examine our own lives. Are we fulfilling the purpose for which we were created? Or are we living our lives inside a box of our own making, afraid or unwilling to step outside and function as we were designed to function?

As with the toys in Toy Story 2, we have a limited time in which we may serve the will of our Creator. Therefore, each moment counts. Each minute is precious. As the scripture says:

See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Ephesians 5:15-16 [KJV]

We have all eternity (or infinity and beyond) to carry as a hope in our heart. But for today, we have a God-given purpose - to live lives which are pleasing to Him by keeping His commandments. After all, the true joy of life cannot be experienced by staying inside a box and simply watching it go by.
