Blood Work

1 hr: 40 min

Author-verified transcription

There was a time when the squinty, steely-eyed gaze of Clint Eastwood (Unforgiven) was used to register the fact that violent justice was about to be administered. Now, in Blood Work, we just worry that the 72 year old actor might have been given the wrong eyeglass prescription.

By playing a retired FBI agent recovering from a heart transplant, it would appear at first that Mr. Eastwood is wisely choosing projects that would fit his advancing years. But this particular retiree chases down bad guys, beds women who are less than half his age, and performs physical feats so difficult that there is no serious attempt to mask the fact that a stunt double is being used.

Eastwood plays Terry McCaleb who is drawn out of retirement by Graciela Rivers (Wanda De Jesus, Ghosts of Mars), the sister of the organ donor whose heart was used to save the retired agent. She figures that since it is her sister's heart that is now beating inside of McCaleb, he "owes her one" and should do what he can to find her killer.

Already feeling guilty about receiving a heart when so many other people were still waiting, McCaleb agrees to look into the case. This delights his daffy houseboat neighbor Buddy (Jeff Daniels, Pleasantville) who volunteers to be his partner. However, it infuriates both the lead officer working the case (Paul Rodriguez, Rat Race) who believes he is simply seeking headlines and McCaleb's attending physician (Anjelica Huston, The Royal Tenebaums) who believes him to have a death wish.

The screenplay by Brian Helgeland (Payback), which was based upon the novel by Michael Connelly, is written with all the subtlety of a root canal performed without anesthesia. This whodunit fumbles the introduction of its characters so badly that it is evident from the first who the killer is. A whodunit that can't stop audiences from immediately connecting the who from the dunit is a tedious affair.

Eastwood hasn't lost an iota of his screen persona which he has honed over the last three or four decades. With a gravely voice and weather-beaten skin, he's every bit as "dirty" as Harry ever was. In Blood Work, it is rather in his role as director that he seems to have lost some of his energy.

Slow, plodding, and often misfiring, Eastwood forces the story forward with little show of grace or finesse. The supporting cast, which includes Wanda De Jesus, Jeff Daniels, Anjelica Huston, and Paul Rodriguez, all give acceptable if somewhat predictable performances.

The fact that McCaleb was a recipient of a new heart is an important plot device, not least of all because it acts as his motivation for getting involved in this case. We can understand why he feels such a sense of responsibility because we have a kind of figurative kinship with McCaleb. It turns out that God has been in the heart transplant business far longer than man.

"A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh." Ezekiel 36:26 (KJV)

There is no heart so diseased or damaged that it cannot be healed by God. Every person who has ever been born again has undergone this spiritual heart transplant. Like McCaleb, we have received a gift of life and hope. Like McCaleb, we are asked to take action out of a sense of responsibility and thankfulness to the One who provided us with that heart.
