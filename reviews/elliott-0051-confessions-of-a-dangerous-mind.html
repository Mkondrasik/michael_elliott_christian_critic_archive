Confessions of a Dangerous Mind

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 24, 2003
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Timothy 2:20-21Ephesians 4:29Luke 6:45

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Purpose; usefulness; meaningful contribution; entertainment and responsibility

Author-verified transcription

Anyone who has ever seen an episode of The Gong Show knows what a goofball Chuck Barris was as its host. What we didn’t recognize was that he was also deadly. In Confessions of a Dangerous Mind, a new film based upon Barris’ “unauthorized autobiography”, we are told that while he was producing such erstwhile TV hits as The Dating Game and The Newlywed Game, he was allegedly performing different kinds of “hits” as a CIA assassin.

The film follows Barris (Sam Rockwell, Galaxy Quest) from the time he was a lonely young man working as an NBC page; through his years as the king of TV game shows; to finally show him as a depressed middle aged man whose time in the brilliant spotlight of fame has come to a close.

In between, we see him being recruited, trained, and employed as an independent contract killer for the CIA. It is obviously just one more goof from the man who was often blamed for the decline of civilized entertainment although this particular gag may have some psychological teeth to it.

If we are to believe the film, Barris took the criticism of his TV contributions seriously. It apparently troubled him that he be remembered solely for the mind-numbing pabulum which he foisted on the general public. Never mind that he was merely giving the people what they wanted to see. The question must be posed: Did he fabricate this CIA persona, a patriot who kills for his country to squelch the spread of communism, as an expression of his subconscious and unrealized desire to make a meaningful contribution to the world? Who knows?

Whatever the case may be, it makes for an offbeat tale adapted for the screen by the off-centered Charlie Kauffman (Adaptation).

George Clooney works both sides of the camera for the first time, not only serving as director but also performing in a supporting role as the stone faced, enigmatic CIA recruiter. While his acting is superior to his directorial eye, he does show promise. This effort just contains a number of overambitious freshman mistakes.

Being on the Hollywood A-list certainly has helped Clooney attract some big names to fill out his cast list. Drew Barrymore is featured as Chuck Barris’ on-again, off-again hippie girlfriend; Julia Roberts shows up as a CIA femme fatale; Rutger Hauer appears as a fellow hitman; and both Brad Pitt and Matt Damon provide cameos in non-speaking roles which could have been much funnier.

Despite the high profile supporting cast, Confessions is Sam Rockwell’s moment to shine after spending years languishing in supporting roles and unseen independent films. As someone who grew up during the Chuck Barris years of titillating TV innuendo, I found his depiction uncannily accurate.

On the surface, we would think that Barris should have been happy with his success. After all, he accomplished his goal of producing TV that people wanted to watch. But it is clear that his victories were shallow ones and his success was less than satisfying. Perhaps this is what comes of giving people what they want instead of giving people what they need. The former caters to the weaknesses of others for one’s personal gain. The latter builds strength to the betterment of all. It isn’t difficult to see which would be more personally rewarding.

In a large house there are articles not only of gold and silver, but also of wood and clay; some are for noble purposes and some for ignoble. If a man cleanses himself from the latter, he will be an instrument for noble purposes, made holy, useful to the Master and prepared to do any good work. 2 Timothy 2:20-21 (NIV)

Television often gets a bum rap as being the gutter of cultural entertainment. It need not be so. The truth of the matter is that everyone working in television has a choice. Just as everyone living life has a choice. Are we to simply succumb to the lusts and baser desires of man because they exist? Or is it possible to aspire to a better way? It is a question that has relevance as more and more “reality TV” projects find their way to the airwaves.
