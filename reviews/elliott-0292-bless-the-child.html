Bless the Child

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 11, 2000
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 47 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual warfare; good and evil; discernment; power of God

Author-verified transcription

In the production notes for BLESS THE CHILD, director Chuck Russell (Eraser) is quoted as saying, "What is so unique about this movie is that most supernatural thrillers tend to empower evil only, which always seemed odd to me. Why are we in Hollywood frightened to empower good as well?" Amen, Mr. Russell. Why indeed?

And it is a unique film. A secular movie that does not shy away from incorporating the power of God into its narrative does not come along every day.

In BLESS THE CHILD, Kim Basinger (I Dreamed of Africa) stars as Maggie O'Connell, a psychiatric nurse who receives an unusual package on her doorstep... a nine day old baby girl abandoned by Maggie's sister Jenna (Angela Bettis, Girl Interrupted) a strung-out drug addict unable to take care of herself let alone a daughter.

Maggie raises Cody as her own, quickly realizing that she is different than other children her age. Diagnosed as borderline autistic, she acts as though she sees or hears things that others don't.

In her sixth year, Cody's uniqueness gets the attention of others, including the self-help guru cum Satanic cult leader Eric Stark (Rufus Sewall, Dangerous Beauty). Stark has been kidnapping children who meet the birth date requirements of a prophesied spiritual leader, after which he puts each child to a monstrous test to determine if he or she is the "one." Failure of the test leads to death, aptly labelled "the slaughter of the innocents" by Stark.

If Cody is proved to be the "chosen one," Stark's plan is to turn her, convincing her to worship Satan or failing that, kill her before she comes of age.

Maggie, with the help of God and a sympathetic FBI agent, must find and rescue her before it's too late.

Ms. Basinger has yet to repeat the caliber of her Academy Award winning performance seen in LA Confidential. Here, as in her turn in I Dreamed of Africa, she remains a bit wooden or stiff, although there are some moments between her and the child (played by a precious Holliston Coleman) that are genuinely sweet and rewarding.

Jimmy Smits (Price of Glory), gives a nice performance as the seminary student turned FBI special agent who is an expert on cult-related crime. When a cop comments on his unusual background he replies enigmatically, "I just found another way to fight him."

Mr. Smits' character recognizes the spiritual battle being waged and understands that the struggle between good and evil extends beyond our five senses.

Rufus Sewell is distastefully effective as the Satanic cult figurehead and Angela Bettis makes full use of her screen time as the weak and drug dependent Jenna.

As a supernatural thriller, BLESS THE CHILD is very violent, including images of the victims of ritual mutilation as well as a decapitation. These scenes and images are designed to show the uncompromising evil of those who have turned their lives over to a merciless devilish force.

Oddly enough, the film's spiritual nature is both its blessing and its curse. By vividly depicting the evil and depravity of the devil spirit world, the filmmakers may alienate a large section of the Christian audience who choose not to subject themselves to such fare.

At the same time, by adhering to a belief that God is present and His power can be manifested to repel the spiritual darkness of this world, the filmmakers risk the ridicule of those who cannot see spiritual realities.

But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him: neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned. 1 Corinthians 2:14 (KJV)

To students of scripture who have had their spiritual "eyes" opened, angelic intervention, discerning of spirits via revealed visions, the power of prayer and energized believing, all of which are integral to the telling of this story, are as valid as any of the more traditional plot devices typically concocted by the spiritually uninitiated minds of Hollywood. God lives... And He can still kick the devil's butt.
