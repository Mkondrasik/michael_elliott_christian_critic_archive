Win a Date with Tad Hamilton!

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 23, 2004
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Genesis 6:2-51 Peter 3:1-51 Timothy 2:9-10

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love; marriage; appearances; choosing a spouse; godly character

Author-verified transcription

If there is a fresher face in Hollywood than Kate Bosworth's, I've yet to see it. She stars in the adorable new teen comedy romance, Win a Date with Tad Hamilton!

There's not many opportunities to meet movie stars when one is a check out girl in Frazier's Bottom, West Virginia. But when a Hollywood bad boy (Josh Duhamel) needs to polish his tarnished reputation, his agent and manager come up with a plan. Their plan puts Piggly Wiggly employee, Rosalee Futch (Kate Bosworth), on equal footing with every other girl in America.

The Win a Date with Tad Hamilton contest was devised to raise money for a children's charity thus improving Tad's image. But they didn't expect someone like Rosie to win it. Her beauty and genuine wholesomeness overwhelms Tad and he decides to follow her back to West Virginia in order to win her heart.

That's bad news for Pete Monash (Topher Grace), Rosie's boss and longtime friend who has secretly loved her for years. The question "Can a small town girl attract a big city star?" turns into "Can a small town guy attract a small town girl away from a big city star?"

Director Robert Luketic, who last directed the smash hit Legally Blonde, keeps the mood light and frothy and has cast actors able to give him what he needs to accomplish it.

Kate Bosworth has the California girl next door good looks and a bright and bubbly personality to go with it. She is delightful in her youthful exuberance as well as in her small town naiveté. It is by no means a stretch for the young actress but it is nonetheless totally enjoyable.

Topher Grace, from The 70s Show, costars as Pete and gives a humorously sarcastic edge to the saccharine sweet nicety of the film. Josh Duhamel as Tad is every inch the matinee idol he is portraying. It is easy to see why women might get weak in the knees in his presence and yet he gives far more than just a pretty face performance.

Ginnifer Goodwin and Kathryn Hahn, as Rosie's best friend and coworker from Frazier's Bottom, give amusing and highly supportive performances. Nathan Lane and Sean Hayes went a bit over the top in their roles as Tad's agent and manager but their screen time is limited and they do have some good comedic moments.

At the heart of the film is love. More specifically, why we love the people we do. Rosie was enamored with the screen persona of Tad Hamilton but did she love him? Tad was enamored with Rosie's small town goodness which was a refreshing change from the ambition-driven starlets he dated in Hollywood but did he love her? Certainly in both cases, the possibility for love existed but neither knew the other one very well.

Pete loved Rosie for who she was. Knowing her for over a decade and being her friend first, Pete knew her flaws as well as her strengths and he loved her because of and in spite of them all.

Committing to another individual is a momentous step. We should make sure that our decision is based upon more than a superficial appreciation of how a person looks or appears.

That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose. Genesis 6:2 (KJV)

By selecting spouses solely by their appearances, Old Testament believers selected wives who did not love God nor follow his ways. Unequally yoked, they allowed themselves to be persuaded away from their righteousness and evil began to multiply on the earth.

God's primary criteria for a wife is that she be a help, not a hindrance, to her husband's believing.
