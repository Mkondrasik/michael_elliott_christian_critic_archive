Meet The Parents

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 6, 2000
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Genesis 3:8-10Hebrews 12:11Proverbs 10:17

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Honesty; consequences; correction; humility; character

Author-verified transcription

Meeting one's prospective in-laws for the first time can be an anxiety-ridden, stressful and potentially embarrassing event. Especially if one of those in-laws-to-be is a retired CIA specialist renown for being "a human lie detector." Such is the case in the hilarious new comedy from Universal Pictures, "Meet the Parents."

Ben Stiller ("Keeping the Faith") stars as a man with a much maligned profession (male nurse) and an even more maligned name (Greg Focker.) By the time this movie has ended, both will have been played vigorously for all the laughs they might muster.

Just before Greg pops the question to Pam (Teri Polo, "Arrival") he finds out that her father, Jack, is the "old-fashioned" kind who would want any man to first ask him for his daughter's hand in marriage. So, he postpones his elaborate proposal plan until he can obtain her father's blessing during a weekend visit to the family homestead in upstate New York. It's a great plan with only one fly in the ointment. He doesn't know Jack (Robert Deniro, "Flawless").

Everything that could go wrong does. The harder Greg tries to make a good impression the worse the impression gets. As the weekend goes from bad to worse to disastrous, the humor also builds proportionately. The filmmakers and Mr. Stiller make Greg a sympathetic character so we do feel for him and will cringe at his embarrassment even as we are laughing at his predicament.

Robert Deniro is turning into quite the comic actor, following up on his successful comedic turn in "Analyze This." His Jack has an intimidating, larger-than-life quality about him which helps us to believe why Greg becomes nervous to be in his presence and terrified to ask anything of him.

Mr. Stiller, who served as the butt for most of the jokes in this movie, had plenty of opportunity to practice his pained expressions as his character was responsible for many a crisis, from overflowing the septic tank to losing the adored family pet.

Owen Wilson ("Shanghai Noon") has a small but very well-drawn role as Kevin, the apparently perfect ex-fiancé of Greg's intended. Wealthy, handsome and benevolent, he even attributes his interest in woodworking to Jesus (or JC as he familiarly calls him), who was, after all, a carpenter. Of course, Greg sees reminders of Kevin wherever he turns, which adds even more pressure as his own accomplishments pale in comparison.

Although there are innuendoes and references of a sexual, pharmaceutical, and profane nature abounding throughout the film, the filmmakers showed surprising restraint by not following the recent trend of overt outrageousness and blatant displays of gross-out humor. Director Jay Roach ("Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me") chose not to push the envelope of taste any further than this summer's offerings have already proven available, even managing to raise the bar back up at least a notch. It is by no means a G rated film, but the restraint shown by Mr. Roach and team made "Meet the Parents" a more enjoyable film experience than we might have otherwise expected.

The film's humor comes from Greg's frustratingly futile attempts not to look bad in the eyes of Debbie's parents. He will lie to cover up a small mistake and that lie will result in a much larger and much more embarrassing situation for him down the road. It is always better to live honestly, asking for forgiveness when one errs rather than to err twice by covering up a mistake or fault. It may not be pleasant to own up to mistakes or lapses in judgment, but doing so will not only build strength of character, it will keep one's heart at peace.

"Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby." Hebrews 12:11 (KJV)

There are indeed consequences to our actions. If we stay meek and humble, willing to accept reproof when we do wrong, and change our behavior so as not to repeat our errors, even our mistakes can work to make us better or stronger men and women.
