Heartbreakers

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
March 23, 2001
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Proverbs 5:3-4 Proverbs 6:24-25 Proverbs 7:21-22

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Sexual temptation; physical attraction; relationships; discernment

Author-verified transcription

Beware of women "baring" gifts. Especially when those "gifts" are the ample chests and shapely legs belonging to Jennifer Love Hewitt (Party of Five) and Sigourney Weaver (Alien) who star in the comedy, Heartbreakers.

Weaver and Hewitt play Max and Page, mother and daughter con artists whose main "scam" involves having Max seduce men into marriage after which Page seduces them to cheat on her. They then split the divorce settlement.

After successfully divorcing themselves from victim number 13 (Ray Liotta, Hannibal), a Jersey chop shop owner, they discover that the IRS has apparently confiscated their financial assets. So they drag their remaining assets down to Palm Beach, FL in search of the one big score that will set them up for life. Afterwards, Page, who has been getting too big for her micro-mini britches, plans to leave mom and start her solo scamming career.

The big fish who has taken the bait is William B. Tensy (Gene Hackman, The Mexican), a tobacco mogul worth 3 billion dollars who appears to have spent much of it by sampling his own product. Max, calling herself Olga, adopts a Russian accent and begins to work her considerable charms on the nicotine stained billionaire. Meanwhile, Page starts to work her own ill-conceived scam on a sweet and simple bar owner named Jack (Jason Lee, Almost Famous).

Complications arise when Page commits the number one taboo for a con artist... she falls in love with the mark.

Sigourney Weaver and Jennifer Love Hewitt both convincingly play women who rely on their physical beauty and sexual allure to get men to do their bidding. They are also not afraid to let themselves look a little ridiculous. Hewitt takes a number of pratfalls during the course of the film and Weaver, as Olga, bluffs her way through an amusing rendition of "Back in the USSR."

The comedy however comes primarily from the supporting cast. Ray Liotta executes a spot-on Jersey accent in his role of a disreputable body shop owner and Jason Lee has a some sweetly sarcastic lines built within the context of his romantic lead character. But it is Gene Hackman who steals the show, wheezing and hacking his way through his smoke-filled scenes as the nicotine addicted Tensy.

Director David Mirkin (Romy and Michele's High School Reunion) keeps the focus upon sexuality rather than the intelligence of the protagonists. Indeed, many of the problems facing the double-dealing duo are a direct result of poor planning or thinking. As a result, the film is heavily laden with sexual dialogue and situations which should give parents pause despite the PG-13 rating.

The idea that some men will follow a part of their anatomy other than their brain should not come as a surprise to anyone who lives in this world. The scriptures are full of exhortations meant to provide words of warning regarding the dangers of a purely carnal response in the face of a temptation.

For the lips of a strange woman drop as an honeycomb, and her mouth is smoother than oil. But her end is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a twoedged sword. Proverbs 5:3-4 (KJV)

Temptations come in all shapes, sizes, and forms. Sexual temptation continues to be one of the most effective weapons in the devil's arsenal. As with most of his weapons, he has perverted something true and vital... the natural attraction God built into the genders. A true godly love between a man and a woman will most certainly include a physical connection. But there are so many more elements or qualities to appreciate in a woman or man than merely the physical appearance. What Heartbreakers does is to elevate physical attraction to an almost solitary position of importance.. and that truly is heartbreaking.
