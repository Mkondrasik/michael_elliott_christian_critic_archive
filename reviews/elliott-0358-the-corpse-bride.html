The Corpse Bride

Author-verified transcription

Tim Burton's films are not suited to everyone's taste.. my own included. I've always found his work to be too dark and/or offbeat to be appealing. His newest stop-motion animated movie, Corpse Bride, is no exception.

The film features Victor Van Dort (Johnny Depp), a shy and awkward young man of 19th century Victorian upbringing. His nouveau-riche parents have betrothed him to Victoria (Emily Watson), a young woman whose family comes from "old money" that has long since vanished.

The poor boy is so nervous about his nuptials that he completely botches his vows during the wedding rehearsal. Off he goes into the woods to practice. There he proposes to a tree limb which turns out to be the bony finger of a shapely corpse. Emily (Helena Bonham Carter) accepts Victor's inadvertent offer of marriage and pulls him into the land of the dead where skeletons and maggots alike celebrate the new couple's union.

Meanwhile, due to Victor's disappearance, Victoria's parents hastily arrange for a substitute groom. The unscrupulous Barkis Bittern (Richard E. Grant) suspiciously offers to take Victoria as his own -- till death do they part.. a state which he has every intention of helping along.

Corpse Bride is a strangely macabre tale for a PG rated film that, subject matter aside, feels like it has been made for children. The Land of the Dead is filled with skeletons, partially decomposed bodies (beautified for aesthetic reasons), and maggots - one of which is a supporting character that keeps popping out of Emily's eye socket with what I assume are supposed to be humorous commentaries.

It is appropriate that, for a movie so immersed in death, Corpse Bride shows little sign of life. The animation is interesting to a degree and the artwork is skillfully done but the story and characters are rather flat and the overall 75 minute experience quickly becomes dull.

Even the musical score by Danny Elfman, which includes a number of original songs, is unremarkable and instantly forgettable.

To give credit where due, Johnny Depp does to a nice job conveying the unease and discomfort of his puppet character. Both Helena Bonham Carter and Emily Watson play their respective ingénue parts with aplomb. Most of the rest of the cast, in their vocal performances as well as in the physical representation of their characters, can best be described as spookily quirky.

The question of whether marriage and how it functions after "death do us part" is a question that is handled by Jesus Christ in the Scriptures. When asked by the Sadducees to explain what happens if a woman marries, becomes widowed, remarries, and repeats the cycle seven times.. whose wife is she at the time of the resurrection?

And Jesus answering said unto them, Do ye not therefore err, because ye know not the scriptures, neither the power of God? For when they shall rise from the dead, they neither marry, nor are given in marriage; but are as the angels which are in heaven. Mark 12:24-25 (KJV)

We need not worry about ever finding ourselves in Victor's predicament. First, the dead and the living do not co-mingle. Secondly, marriage was designed by God to help strengthen us while we live in this earthly plane.

When we reach our spiritual plane, the institution of marriage will no longer be needed.
