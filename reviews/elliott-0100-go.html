Go

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 9, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Accountability; consequences; judgment; responsibility

Author-verified transcription

“Sex, drugs, and rock ’n roll...” That may very well be the predominant litany of the latter half of this century. It is also the central force behind the new film Go.

Go is actually three stories in one, loosely tied together but told independently one from the other. Each of them begins at the same shift change at a local LA supermarket. They then branch off from there as they follow different characters.

In the first story, Ronna (Sarah Polley, The Sweet Hereafter), in order to earn enough money to pay her rent, agrees to broker a drug deal. Using friend Claire (Katie Holmes, Disturbing Behavior) as collateral, Ronna scores the drugs but has to dispose of them when the deal goes sour. Now all she has to figure out is how to get Claire back from the drug dealer (Timothy Olyphant, Scream 2), having neither the money nor the drugs to give him.

In the second story, Simon (Desmond Askew, Island) and friends take a trip from LA to Las Vegas where they are anticipating a wild time of gambling and sex. Even though they achieve both, they did not count on being chased by lunatic mobsters intent on killing them or running them off the road, whichever came first.

In the third story which valiantly attempts to tie the previous two tales together, we meet Zack (Jay Mohr, Playing by Heart) and Adam (Scott Wolf, White Squall), two actors charged with possession of drugs.

In order to escape punishment, they make a deal with the police to entrap their dealer, Simon (who happens to be on vacation in Vegas). So instead, they entrap his replacement at the grocery where he works, the aforementioned Ronna.

The movie is actually easier to follow than this review may indicate. Characterizations are clear and the linear plot(s) are easily entreated.

Standouts in the cast include Sarah Polley as the opportunistic Ronna, Taye Diggs (How Stella Got Her Groove Back) as Marcus, Jay Mohr and Scott Wolf as the actors who get busted for drugs, and Timothy Olyphant as the drug dealer with a heart of gold, tarnished though it may be.

This young and exciting cast is a definite selling point for the movie. As is the driving GenX music which is a constant presence in the film. Director Doug Liman (Swingers) knows his audience well and caters to their tastes.

This happens to be the major drawback to the film. As well crafted as the film might be. As well acted, well directed, well written... there is still no escaping the fact that there is no moral center to this film. All the characters are self-involved, seeking immediate self-gratification. When their particular plan fails, they immediately scheme to escape punishment or retribution. In other words, they take no responsibility for their actions.

Whether it is selling (or taking) drugs, gambling and losing everything, visiting strip clubs, torching a hotel, driving a hit-and-run vehicle, or any of a number of other deviant behaviors, these characters show no remorse or indication that their lives will change. Is this the message we want to give to our young people today?

The truth is this: the actions that we take today do have an impact and we will answer for them.

For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad. 2 Corinthians 5:10 (KJV)

These GenX’ers of Go may appear to have gotten off relatively easy for all the damage they caused. But we must remember that this movie ended before the judgment of Christ. “Sex, drugs and rock ’n roll” is not his litany. Nor is it ours. My recommendation? Even though the film rates three stars artistically, it rates 0 stars spiritually. Do not go to Go. Go to God instead.
