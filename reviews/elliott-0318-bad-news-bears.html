Bad News Bears

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 22, 2005

2½ stars

1 hr: 55 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Winning; integrity; values; what truly matters

Author-verified transcription

The potty-mouthed tykes of baseball return in a remake of the 1976 classic sports comedy, Bad News Bears. This version stays faithful to the original but does increase (by a considerable amount) the off-color language used by the pint-sized players and their coach.

The profanity is enough to warrant a PG-13 rating which means that the 12 year old characters are saying and hearing things that we probably wouldn't want our 12 year old children to say or hear.

Billy Bob Thornton is in full Bad Santa mode as Morris Buttermaker, a drunken, womanizing exterminator who, for a brief moment (2/3 of an inning), played in the major leagues. He is hired by Liz Whitewood (Marcia Gay Harden), the mother of a young boy whose baseball abilities are deemed insufficient for the highly competitive little league teams of his area.

Whitewood sues the league which is reluctantly forced to field another team - one that is comprised of the rejects and little league losers that no other team wanted. Buttermaker, despite his initial disinterest in doing anything but drinking himself into a stupor, eventually warms to the idea of teaching these kids the fundamentals of the game. Of course, he does so in an inebriated, foul-mouthed way.

Director Richard Linklater, no stranger to directing children after scoring big with School of Rock, keeps things simple. The movie is centered on Buttermaker. The kids are merely the catalyst for his change and a source of the film's humor.

Billy Bob Thornton provides enough star power weight to keep the movie grounded. His nonchalant delivery humorously contrasts the extremely inappropriate behavior of his character.

The film contains a second Oscar winner (Thornton won previously for writing Sling Blade) with Marcia Gay Harden (2001 winner for Pollock), playing a rather insignificant supporting role as the woman responsible for bringing the Bad News Bears to the league. Greg Kinnear (Oscar nominated for As Good As It Gets) adds his talent in the role of Buttermaker's nemesis, the egocentric coach of the league's perennial trophy winners, so the movie has a bit of a pedigree cast.

The younger members of the cast aren't nearly as experienced with many of them appearing in their first film. Of course, they also aren't expected to do more than fill their assigned stereotypes. Jeffrey Davies is the cool rebel with unbelievable talent; Sammi Kane Kraft is the girl with a power arm who has a history with Buttermaker; Timmy Deters is the diminutive, hotheaded, mitt-throwing Tanner; Brandon Craggs is the overweight loudmouth Engleberg; Tyler Patrick Jones is Lupus, the shy and awkward kid.

Added to the Bears' roster in this remake are Matthew Hooper (Troy Gentile), a paraplegic in a motorized wheelchair, and Garo Daragebrigadian (Jeffrey Tedmori), an Armenian youngster looking to impress his non-English-speaking father with his unimpressive athletic prowess.

What the Bears "learn" is that winning, while enjoyable, isn't everything. There are plenty of victories to be had that will not appear on the scoreboard's final tally. I would rather "lose" a valiantly fought contest than "win" without integrity or meaning.
