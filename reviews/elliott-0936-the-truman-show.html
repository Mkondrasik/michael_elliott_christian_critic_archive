The Truman Show

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 5, 1998
Rating
4 stars

1 hr: 43 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Deception; Discernment; Facades

Author-verified transcription

Andy Warhol once said that everybody in the world would eventually experience fifteen minutes of fame. In the case of Truman Burbank, he may have been underestimating it by about thirty-five years.

Truman Burbank is the star of The Truman Show, brainchild invention of a man known only as Christof. Filming Truman since birth, this twenty-four hour, seven day a week television project has managed to capture the attention of the entire world. The only one not watching is apparently Truman himself. The reason: He doesn't know he's being televised. Unlike Peter Sellers' character in Being There, whose life was shaped by the television he watched, Truman's life has been shaped FOR television to air. For thirty five years, he has been completely unaware that what he calls life is, in fact, a product being produced and distributed to a global market. That is, he's been unaware up until now.

A series of accidents has Truman beginning to question the only reality he has ever known. A reality that, unbeknownst to him, has been completely manufactured and manipulated by Christof, the "creator" and director of Truman's life.

Screenwriter Andrew Niccol (Gattaca) and director Peter Weir (Dead Poets' Society) have woven an interesting and original tale of discovery and coming of age, combining it with a blistering commentary on the viewing habits of our television-crazed society. Of all the accomplishments which Mr. Weir managed to achieve with this film, perhaps the most beneficial was his ability to pull back his star from giving another one of his trademark over-the-top performances.

Jim Carrey (Liar, Liar) plays the sweet and childlike Truman Burbank with a degree of self-control we've not seen from him before. Instead of pushing the envelope of credibility to the breaking point, Mr. Carrey keeps his character in check. Even within the unbelievable context of this film's unusual premise, Mr. Carrey's characterization is as sure as... uh... well, after seeing this film, where even the weather is customized, none of the regular "as sure as..." clichés communicate.

The film is further served by the stellar work of Ed Harris (Stepmom). As the beret-wearing, dictatorial Christof, Mr. Harris is riveting. There's a scene where Truman is reunited with his long-lost and presumed dead "father". Christof, working behind the scenes, orchestrates it perfectly, manipulating camera angles, adding a musical background, whispering lines to the actors via hidden earpieces, all to bring this dramatic moment to its emotional peak. It is a very funny scene because no one is playing it for laughs. To Truman, it's his life. To Christof and his cast and crew, it's their work. To the television audience, it's a four handkerchief cry-fest.

Laura Linney (Absolute Power) plays actress Hannah Gill, who is cast in The Truman Show as Truman's wife, Meryl. Natascha McElhone (Ronin) plays a character who falls in love with Truman and wants to tell him the truth. She is written out of the show more quickly than Christof can say "Cue the sun."

When asked how he thought he could manipulate events to the degree that he could keep Truman from finding out about the "real world", Christof makes this insightful comment: "We accept the reality with which we're presented." That statement is exactly what the devil wants us to believe. His success is dependent upon our perception of life going no further than where our five senses take us.

While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal. 2 Corinthians 4:18 [KJV]

Just as Truman had to learn to look past the deceptions and lies that his manipulators presented to him in order to find the truth, so must we, as we live in this world, look for the spiritual truths behind the senses reality that we can see, hear, smell, taste and touch.

For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. Ephesians 6:2 [KJV]

It took a great deal of courage for Truman to question all that he had ever known to search for the truth that he could not see. The same amount of courage is required of us to recognize and accept that there is an unseen spiritual power behind all of life. But when we do, the spiritual blessings go far beyond anything that this physical reality can offer us.
