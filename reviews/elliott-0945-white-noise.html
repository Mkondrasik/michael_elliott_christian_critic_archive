White Noise

Michael Elliott

January 7, 2005
Rating
2 stars

1 hr: 41 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Death; Lying Spirits

Author-verified transcription

"I hear dead people." That's the premise behind White Noise, the new supernatural thriller that is heavy on the supernatural part and rather light on the thrills.

The film is based upon the unusual and highly suspect belief that the dead are able to communicate with the living. Skeptical at first, Jonathan Rivers (Michael Keaton) becomes an ardent believer in EVP or electronic voice phenomenon. Six months after his wife (Chandra West) dies in a freakish accident, he begins to receive messages from the dead - or so he thinks.

He soon finds himself obsessed with listening to the pops and crackle of recorded static in the desperate hope that his wife will try to send him a message.

The messages he thinks he hears in the "white noise" of radio and TV signals further strengthens his belief that such phenomenon is possible. But there's something wrong with the timing. Some of the messages he's receiving are coming from people a day or two before they die and they're spurring him to take action.

Who is behind the phenomenon he believes he is experiencing and why is it happening in the first place becomes the central question in the film. Unfortunately, this question isn't posed in a way that holds our interest or keeps us on the edge of our seats.

Keaton does as well as can be expected, playing a role that requires incredulity that morphs into an obsessive belief. As he travels from one extreme to another, we should just be grateful that he keeps his character from flying off into the stratosphere of overacting.

White Noise as a thriller is disappointing simply because it doesn't generate the anxiety or scares that one expects from the genre. Director Geoffrey Sax seems to think that showing the snowy, electrically charged static screens of a TV or computer monitor provides us with the expectation that something is about to happen. He's must have forgotten that one's first response to static is pretty much universal - annoyance.

Although White Noise isn't much of a film it does provide us with an opportunity to consider what the scriptures tell us about communicating with the dead. And they are crystal clear on the matter. The dead cannot communicate.

His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish. Psalms 146:4 (KJV)
Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might; for there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest. Ecclesiastes 9:10 (KJV)

God does promise us that the dead will rise again when Jesus Christ returns for his church but until that day they remain dead - unable to act or think and unaware of the passage of time.

Despite this biblically documented truth, an entire industry exists in our culture that promises to do the impossible - connect the living to the dead. Such claims are lies. They are either the lies of men who are greedy for financial gain or else the lies of another spiritual power that some men have believed and foolishly hold to be true.

It wouldn't be the first time that men have been deceived by lying spirits. King Saul once sought out a woman who was possessed with evil spirits. Through her he was deceived into thinking he was speaking with the dead prophet Samuel.

Death is no mystery. It is certainly not a passageway into another reality. Scriptures call it an enemy. It is an enemy that will one day be defeated but until it is, we should focus our minds and energies on the life that we have.
