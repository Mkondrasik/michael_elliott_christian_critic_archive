Drumline

Author-verified transcription

Many people believe that the most exciting thing that can take place on a football field happens at half time. Drumline is a story which features the precision and dedication required of musicians who make up a Southern University's show style marching band.

Nick Cannon (The Nick Cannon Show) stars as Devon Miles, a talented and arrogant street performer who learned his drumming skills on the sidewalks of Harlem. When band director Dr. Lee (Orlando Jones, Evolution) offers him a band scholarship, he arrives at Atlanta A&T University with the idea that he'll be the star of the show.

He quickly learns that the point of being in a band is not to stand out but to blend in. "One band, one sound" is the operative phrase. Devon will have to undergo a bit of ego adjustment if he's going to find himself a part of the precision oriented team of the drumline.

Devon is his own worst enemy, challenging and alienating those whom he should embrace. Sean Taylor (Leonard Roberts, Buffy the Vampire Slayer), the senior band member who oversees the drumline, is as personally threatened by Devon's superior skills as he is worried about his showboating tendencies and what it will do to the sound of his band.

On Devon's side is Laila (Zoe Saldana, Center Stage), an upperclassman who responds to Devon's limited charms and Jayson (GQ, On The Line), the token white guy in a predominantly black school who struggles to keep his place on the field.

The best thing this film has going for it is the many scenes of drumline competition. The practiced skill of these performers is indeed something of which to marvel. The rest of the film is rather inadequate fish-out-of-water, underdog given a shot at the title kind of story.

Charles Stone, perhaps best known for directing the "Whassup" beer commercials, does his best to dress up the film and to his credit he manages to overcome many of the script's weaknesses. He has some personable performers with which to work and, of course, the music which drives the story forward with a distinctive beat. We may never think of "band camp" as a geeky or nerdy thing again.

Yet, the script is weak and not even the brash showmanship of these band performers can disguise it. Screenwriters Shawn Schepps (The Closet) and newcomer Tina Gordan Chism employ a wide variety of clichés and stereotypical characters but they miss opportunities to develop back stories and character arcs which might have made the film more meaningful or substantial.

Though weak, the primary message of the script is clear: Marching to the beat of our own drum will only take us so far. The more mature and disciplined we are, the more we are able to match the beat of those around us and add our sound and strength to theirs.

Just as a band, which will sound discordant and confused if even one member is playing out of concert with the rest, the body of Christ is exhorted to be in one accord with each other so as not to weaken or distort the message that we are to be sounding out.

Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment. 1 Corinthians 1:10 (KJV)

Achieving that level of like-minded functionality requires a great deal of discipline, humility, and practice. If band members can demonstrate those qualities so they can attain the "one band, one sound" goal, shouldn't we be able to do the same as ambassadors for Christ?
