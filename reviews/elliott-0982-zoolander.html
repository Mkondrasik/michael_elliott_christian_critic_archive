Zoolander

1 hr: 30 min

Sermon / spiritual topic: Laughter

Author-verified transcription

How stupid is Zoolander? When placed on a comparative scale of imbecilic movie characters, he’s off the chart. He’d have to go to school in order to join the cast of Dumb and Dumber. He’s so dumb, Forrest Gump would be embarrassed to hang with him. The only reason Freddy Got Fingered is because Zoolander wasn’t there to take the blame.

Thankfully, his lack of brain activity doesn’t seem to stand in the way of a successful career. Who needs to be smart when it is available to be professionally good looking? Derek Zoolander (Ben Stiller, Meet the Parents), three time Male Model of the Year, has the most recognizable face in the world, thanks to his patented “Blue Steel” look. (Think deer caught in headlights after said deer has sucked on a sour lemon.)

His popularity may be waning due to the explosive rise of newcomer Hansel (Owen Wilson, Shanghai Noon) with his surfer dude/extreme sports persona. Suddenly out of the limelight, life doesn’t hold the same meaning for poor Derek.

Little does he know the Machiavellian reality behind the fashion industry, which we learn is responsible for every political assassination since Lincoln was killed by that actor “slash” model, John Wilkes Booth. In a plot lifted from the pages of The Manchurian Candidate (or closer still, a really bad episode of Gilligan’s Island), Derek is brainwashed to kill the prime minister of Indonesia where the lack of child labor laws allow the fashion moguls to make huge profits.

Ben Stiller wears a number of hats for Zoolander. In addition to his starring role, Stiller also serves as director, co-writer, and co-producer. As the titular character, Stiller poses and prances with sunken cheeks, perpetually pursed lips, over-moussed hair, and a ridiculously affected accent. It is typical stereotypical campiness that is most often seen in sketch comedy.

Stiller has turned Zoolander into a family affair, casting his father, Jerry Stiller (Seinfeld), as Zoolander’s NY agent, and his wife, Christine Taylor (The Brady Bunch Movie), as a Times Magazine reporter who catches wind of the nefarious plot and tries to warn an infuriatingly dense Zoolander. His mother and sister appear in bit roles as well.

Owen Wilson is pretty funny as a vacuous pretty boy living large with his entourage of hangers on. The scene where he and Stiller compete in a “Rocky”-like “walk-off” on a fashion runway is so silly it can’t help but elicit smiles from us. Milla Jovovich (The Messenger) is also in the cast, doing a pretty good Natasha (of Rocky and Bulwinkle fame) imitation in her role as a leather clad villainess.

Will Ferrell (Jay and Silent Bob Strike Back) steals most scenes he’s in as the evil but color-coordinated and tightly corseted fashion designer Mugatu. His outrageously funny portrayal may be politically incorrect, but it is nonetheless effective.

In case the tenor of this review is vague, let me say clearly that Zoolander is a ridiculously silly, nonsensical piece of fluff. There is little of substance to hold onto and certainly no spiritual enlightenment to be had from the experience. The film also contains some sexual references and innuendoes as well as some potentially offensive behavior on the part of many of its characters. But after the last few weeks through which we’ve all lived, there’s something to be said for the humorous distraction of something so non-threateningly stupid. Quite frankly, it was good to hear people laugh.

A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones. Proverbs 17:12 (KJV)

Certainly Zoolander is not going to be the cure-all for what ails us. But if it can help us on our road to recovery by giving us a silly little reason to smile, if even for just 90 minutes, then I’m more than willing to give it a thumbs up.
