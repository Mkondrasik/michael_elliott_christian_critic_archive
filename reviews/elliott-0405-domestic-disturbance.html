Domestic Disturbance

Michael Elliott

November 2, 2001

Sermon / spiritual topic: Truth; lying; integrity; credibility

Author-verified transcription

"The Boy Who Cried Wolf" fable takes on a modern twist in the new action/thriller Domestic Disturbance. Twelve year old Danny is not thrilled that his divorced mom has got a new boyfriend and will say just about anything in his attempts to cause that relationship to fail. Little did he know that there was no need to make up stories. It turns out that the guy really is a murderous scumbucket.

Danny (Matt O'Leary in his film debut) is far from being a perfect kid. Troubled by his parents' divorce, he's been acting bad in what most recognize as a cry for attention. His father Frank (John Travolta, Swordfish) and mother Susan (Teri Polo, Meet the Parents) patiently work with him and lovingly encourage him to honestly address these issues openly.

Life goes shakily forward and Susan decides to marry Rick Barnes (Vince Vaughn, Psycho), a wealthy philanthropist. Such a change sets Danny off again, only this time he finds himself in a position to witness Rick as he commits a murder. The problem is that because of his lying past, no one will believe him. No one that is except for Frank and, of course, Rick.

After Frank's bid for custody backfires, he must prove Rick's guilt in order to save his son.

Domestic Disturbance is an average potboiler of a thriller. It's a by-the-numbers film that contains no surprises and few memorable scenes. Still, it moves briskly along and manages to elicit the appropriate amount of tension and action. While thoroughly predictable, it maintains a certain level of entertainment.

John Travolta plays an average Joe. a guy who likes boats and loves his kid. It is a relaxed and uncomplicated performance. Vince Vaughn could probably have used a bit more shading to his character in order to make him a bit more interesting.

The other cast members acquit themselves with somewhat perfunctory performances. Steve Buscemi (Ghost World) is especially restrained in his role as Rick's shady "business partner" who mysteriously appears on the day of the wedding.

Screenwriter Lewis Colick (October Sky) chose a film formula and never once departed from it. He does make a few missteps. Late in the film, Rick commits an error which is so stupid it contradicts the intelligence of the character which had thus far been established. Competent direction by Harold Becker (City Hall) and an above average cast is what keeps the movie afloat.

Domestic Disturbance does illustrate the age old conundrum that comes with lying. Had Danny built a reputation for telling the truth, people would have been far more likely to give him the benefit of the doubt at a time when he really needed someone to believe him.

Scripture tells us that we can know a man by the fruit he bears. Being known as a liar will cause people to mistrust all the words that one speaks.

Can the fig tree, my brethren, bear olive berries? either a vine, figs? so can no fountain both yield salt water and fresh. James 3:12 (KJV)

It is a reality of life of which the apostles were most keenly aware. Because of the importance of the message they were carrying to the known world of the first century AD, it was crucial that their integrity of speech be unchallenged.

But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God. 2 Corinthians 4:2 (K.JV)

Because of their commitment and faithfulness to speak the truth always to all men, the impact of their words had added value. So will ours as we are faithful to keep the same commitment.
