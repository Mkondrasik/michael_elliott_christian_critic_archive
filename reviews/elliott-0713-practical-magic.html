Author-verified transcription

Bubble, bubble, toil and trouble. Run for the exits on the double! Oh, how I wish I had heard and heeded that warning. I may have been spared sitting through the illusion of a romantic comedy called Practical Magic.

Practical Magic is based on the novel by Alice Hoffman and directed by Griffin Dunne (Addicted To Love). It tells the story of Sally and Gillian Owen, sisters who are descended from a long line of witches and saddled with a family curse that dooms any man who loves them to death. The rest of us just have to watch this movie.

Sandra Bullock (Hope Floats) is Sally, the more reserved witch of the two. She limits her exercised powers to merely lighting candles with her breath and stirring her tea without actually touching the spoon.

Nicole Kidman (The Peacemaker) is Gillian, the wilder and more sexually promiscuous one. Her powers are not as clearly defined as Sally's, but that matches her character which is vaguely drawn. Miss Kidman is not successful in fleshing out what the screenwriters failed to include.

Stockard Channing (Twilight) and Dianne Wiest (The Horse Whisperer) play the girls' aunts who raise them after their father is tragically killed (by the curse, remember?). As they grow up, the sisters begin to drift apart. Sally tries to lead the normal life of a widowed housewife (that darn curse again) and mother of two (girls, of course.)

Gillian, on the other hand, jumps from man to man (and presumably bed to bed) until she finally meets one whose "spirit" is stronger than hers. When he becomes physically abusive, she turns to her sister for help. Together, they manage to kill the guy, bring him back to life, kill him again, and still have to deal with his lingering spirit. In other words, a movie that is "fun" for the whole family... if you happen to be a Manson.

Both Miss Bullock and Miss Kidman show themselves in a less than attractive light. Miss Kidman is particularly unappealing as the wanton witch with terrible taste in men. Ms. Channing and Ms. Wiest, as the spinster aunts (gee, that curse just won't quit) are able to salvage a little dignity within their roles, but not enough to redeem the picture.

Director Griffen Dunne's biggest mistake was in accepting this improbable, unrealistic, and thoroughly godless script as the foundation for what he shows on the screen.

Magic is a tough call for parents these days because there is so much innocuous "fun" associated with it. Ouiji boards, illusionists (like David Copperfield), palm readers, and most Disney products (isn't their central location called The "Magic" Kingdom?) may appear to be innocent and entertaining, but they all serve to entice our young into a frame of mind conducive to devil spirit possession.

Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness, Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies, Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revellings, and such like: of the which I tell you before, as I have also told you in time past, that they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God. Galatians 5:19-21 [KJV]

If we, as parents, allow our children to be freely exposed to such blatant propaganda for our spiritual enemy, we should expect to see two sets of consequences: First, on an earthly plane, our children will not grow to respect the one true God or His Word; and secondly, on a spiritual plane, we will not receive the heavenly rewards for raising our children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.

Jesus Christ did not perform "magic tricks." He manifested the power of God. Magic is the devil's handiwork. After all, it is based on deception and he is the greatest deceiver this world has ever known. Magic has no place in a believer's life or mind. Speaking biblically, it is NOT practical.
