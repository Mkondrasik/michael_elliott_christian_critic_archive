Author-verified transcription

Olympus Has Fallen. Your expectations of this film should quickly follow suit. This action adventure movie from director Antoine Fuqua somehow makes a siege on the White House look downright mundane.

Gerard Butler stars as Mike Banning, former Secret Service agent transferred from his elite status as the lead for the Presidential guard detail after he allowed the First Lady (Ashley Judd) to die so that he might save the President (Aaron Eckhart). He’s now holding down a desk job at the Treasury Department… pushing papers and looking for redemption.

When North Korean terrorists attack and quickly overrun the White House, Banning, who has somehow made it back inside the 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue residence, becomes the sole hope for the hostages, (the president and his son among them), who are being held by the ruthless Kang (Rick Yune). In true Die Hard fashion, Banning becomes a one-man wrecking crew, as he decimates the terrorist army one evil-doer at a time.

Early in the film, Banning was placed in an unwinable situation. He was forced to make a life or death decision where, regardless of his choice, both life and death were to be certain outcomes. All that he got to choose was who would live and who would die. Ask Sophie… that is not a choice one ever wants to make.

Thankfully, God never asks us to make that choice. Deciding who gets saved is not something that is up to us. It isn’t really up to God … because He has already ordained that His salvation is something that is available to all men.

“Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.” 1 Timothy 2:4 (KJV)

The choice of salvation is, and always has been, up to the individual in need of saving. God does not force Himself or His promises onto people. All He does is offer eternal life to all. Once offered, it is up to the individual to accept or not.
