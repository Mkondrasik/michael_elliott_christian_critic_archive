Author-verified transcription

The live action/animated feature Osmosis Jones may boast the most inventive, original concept of any film this summer. The thing is... I would have enjoyed it even more if the concept hadn't fallen into the hands of those who think gross is good.

Zoo keeper Frank Detorri (Bill Murray, Charlies' Angels) is a mess. His health, hygiene, and dietary habits are beyond disgusting... they are even noticeable to his exceedingly patient young daughter, Shane (Elena Franklin in her film debut) who tries to get him to clean up his act.

When he eats an egg which has fallen on the ground, we follow the egg down his gullet and enter The City of Frank, an animated, vibrant metropolis consisting of cellular organisms each with a job to do. A job that Frank makes extremely difficult.

It is here we meet Osmosis Jones (voice by Chris Rock, Down to Earth), a white blood cell who grew up on the wrong side of the digestive tract but rose above it to become a member of the FrankPD. Jones has recently been demoted from the prestigious Immunity Division and assigned instead to the menial task of tartar control due to his past overzealous behavior which resulted in a devastating embarrassment for Frank and Shane. Yearning to redeem himself, Jones may get his chance because a particularly nasty virus has just ridden into town on the back of the egg.

Thrax (voice by Laurence Fishburne, The Matrix) has got fatal plans for Frank and will surely succeed unless Jones and his new partner Drix (voice by David Hyde Pierce, Frasier), a visiting time release cold capsule, can uncover and foil his dastardly plot.

The animated sections of the film (directed by Tom Sito and Piet Kroon) are lively, colorful, and entertaining as we follow Jones and Drix who are looking for Thrax in every nook and cranny of Frank. The vocal artists, particularly David Hyde Pierce and Laurence Fishburne, bring a full and varied texture to their characters.

The live action sequences, directed by the Farrelly Brothers, is colored by the pair's predilection for things gross and outrageous. What better setting for gross-out humor than a film featuring the human body with all of its mucous, fluids, sounds, and smells? Osmosis Jones could have been a delightful, engaging, and relatively decent tale of microscopic derring-do but it is tainted by sight gags involving vomit, festering pimples, gas emissions, and infected ingrown toenails. Come to think of it, the kids (boys mainly) will probably love it. I don't share that enthusiasm.

A great deal of original thought went into the construction of this film, starting with the screenplay by newcomer Marc Hyman. Thinking of the body as a self-contained city led Hyman to match familiar civic functions with existing body parts. Therefore, the stomach becomes the arrival terminal for incoming visitors. The bowels, of course, serve as the departure gate. The mayor's office is up by the brain while the more undesirable denizens of Frank hang out in the spa-like conditions of the sweat glands. The sinus wall is depicted as a dam and the nervous system looks remarkably similar to high voltage wiring. Of course, to hear the Stones play in concert one need only travel to the kidneys.

Even with all of its gleeful playfulness, the film nevertheless reminds us of the awesome intricacy of the human body which God designed.

For the body is not one member, but many, 1 Corinthians 12:14 (KJV)

There are so many components to the human body which must work together with such precision that it should be clear we are of a godly design. In fact, in Psalms 139, God tells us that we are fearfully and wonderfully made... that our bodies are "curiously wrought" or embroidered.

Part of what God has woven into the fabric of the human being is our ability to heal. As strange as it may seem, the animated detective story of Osmosis Jones is based upon truth. We may require outside medical assistance at times to remove the cause of an illness or reduce the effects of its symptoms, but any doctor will tell you that the actual healing process is built into God's wonderful design known as the human body.
