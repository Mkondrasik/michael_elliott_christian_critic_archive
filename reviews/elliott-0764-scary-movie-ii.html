It is an odd feeling to be the only one not laughing in the theater. It is an experience I am getting used to. The last time it happened, the horrific Scary Movie was playing. Tonight, Scary Movie II had people laughing uproariously... for about 15 minutes. I was very gratified to see that the laughter died out rather quickly. Perhaps we, as a people, are tiring of the crude shock humor which Scary Movie II, like its predecessor, use almost exclusively.

Since the sequel so closely parallels the original, I pulled my original review of the first. I find that it still applies to this new film. So, if they can plagiarize themselves, so can I. Here's the original review (with only a few edits needed).

As a spoof of the horror genre, Scary Movie II fails miserably. The film makes obvious references to The Exorcist, The House on Haunted Hill, Poltergeist, Hannibal, and even Charlie's Angels, but instead of offering an intelligent satire, it exploits these types of films in order to employ a brand of sexual and scatological humor that out-Farrellys the brothers that made Me, Myself and Irene. As a comedy, Scary Movie II doesn't just border on obscenity, it bathes in it.

There is really no plot to describe because the film doesn't pretend to follow a logical narrative. It comes across as a series of sketches tied together by an inept ghost who is inexplicably pursuing a not-so-young group of teenagers.

The film boasts no less than seven credited screenwriters, once again giving proof to the adage that more is not necessarily better. The dialogue is simply awful, and the characters are transparent stereotypes whose sole aim, presumably is to offend whichever classification of human being they so shallowly represent.

Director Keenen Ivory Wayans (In Living Color) clumsily directs each scene, telegraphing any "jokes" long before they are played. As a result, we have a poorly structured and poorly filmed movie whose humor is based on shocking the audience with an forced audacity, pushing the limits of taste and propriety well beyond any reasonable definition of decency.

Scary Movie II is only scary when we consider what it takes for any film to reach the point of a wide theatrical release. That people in the film industry believe this to be the kind of film and kind of humor which will have enough of a mainstream appeal to earn a profit and thus justify a reported who knows how many millions of dollars budget, is more than scary. It is an indictment of the culture that we, as a people, have helped to build.

Anyone who has been a regular reader of my reviews will know that I use secular films to make spiritual points. For that reason, I do not condemn films that do not adhere to a strict biblical doctrine, but rather use them as an illustration to help explain what biblical doctrine is. That said, to the best of my recollection, this film is the first I've seen since I've started reviewing that is so void of content or substance that I am feel justified in using the following scripture:

But shun profane and vain babblings: for they will increase unto more ungodliness. And their word will eat as doth a canker: 2 Timothy 2:16-17 (KJV)

Some things are so unedifying that they should be avoided. Everyone must make up their own minds, of course. For me, would that I had those 90 minutes back that I spent watching this film so as to put them to better use.

Whenever something is repeated in the scriptures, it indicates that the thing repeated has been "established." It is solidified. In the secular realm, and in this particular example, this spiritual example holds true. Scary Movie II is just as much of a waste of time and talent as was the first one. We can only pray that this time around, the box office will be poor enough to discourage a Scary Movie III.
