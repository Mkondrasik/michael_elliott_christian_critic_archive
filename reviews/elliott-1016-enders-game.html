Author-verified transcription

Based on the popular sci-fi series by Orson Scott Card, Ender’s Game is set a half century after the earth barely squeaked out a victory against an alien force. Since then, they have been preparing for the inevitable rematch by enlisting the most adept video game players from amongst their children in the hopes to find a leader that can deliver a lasting victory against what appears to be a stronger and superior foe.

Ender Wiggin (Asa Butterfield) is counted among the best and brightest. An unlikely hero, he presents a slight and unimposing figure. But he does possess an innate ability to read his opponent or oppressor and find the best and most effective response to him. He appears to be the Earth’s best chance for survival but is he someone that an army will follow?

Colonel Graff (Harrison Ford) certainly hopes so. He has been looking for a leader to stand in the place of the legendary Mazer Rackham (Ben Kingsley) who single-handedly defeated the aliens in the first battle by means of an unexpected and brave sacrifice. In Ender, Graff believes he has found someone with the right combination of empathy and strategic skills that will lead Earth to its final victory.

Most of the film follows the training exercises that are designed to give the “launchies” (recruits) experience in the kind of combat strategy they will need in a real battle. It is through these training exercises that Ender must go from raw recruit to respected leader with little to no help from his superiors.

What makes Ender “special” is his ability and willingness to get to know his enemy as well as his enemy knows his own self. By taking the time to understand his enemy’s needs, desires, motivations, fears, strengths and weaknesses, Ender is able to quickly strategize an approach to ensure a victory against him.

Jesus Christ came to give us the same advantage. There is relatively very little mentioned in the Old Testament regarding our spiritual adversary. The reason is simple. There wasn’t much known about him prior to the arrival of Jesus Christ, the Messiah, who came to reveal and destroy the works of the devil.

"He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil." 1 John 3:8 (KJV)

One cannot destroy the works of the devil unless one recognizes and understands how the devil works. It is this knowledge that Jesus imparted, coupled with the spiritual strength embodied in the new birth, that enables us to withstand and defeat the adversarial attacks in our own lives. Without the lessons that Jesus came to teach, we would be resigned to live in spiritual ignorance. But we have been given the blessed advantage of knowing how and why our enemy does what he does. Because of this, we can build a defense against him.

“Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.” 2 Corinthians 2:11 (KJV)
