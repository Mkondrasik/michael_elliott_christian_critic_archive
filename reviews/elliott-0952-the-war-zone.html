The War Zone

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 10, 1999
Rating
0 stars
Running time
1 hr: 38 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 5:1Ephesians 6:12Romans 6:12-16

Sermon / spiritual topic: Incest

Author-verified transcription

The War Zone is appropriately named not because it tells a story of military combat but rather because it tackles the internal struggle of a family dealing with the most taboo of subjects... Incest. This family is living in a private and personal "war zone" of unfathomable proportions. The carnage the film depicts is less physical and more emotional in nature, but one can only imagine the depth of psychological scarring its characters would have experienced.

This uncompromisingly frank movie does not shy away from its subject. Neither does it try to explain how such things came to be. Because of the approach taken, our focus is directed instead to the devastating effects the incestuous relationship has on the various family members.

Tim Roth, in his directorial debut has assembled a remarkable cast with which to lay bare (literally) a family in distress. Ray Winstone (Nil by Mouth) plays the Dad who presents a quiet, gentle demeanor which disguises the fact that he is the worst kind of abuser. Tilda Swinton (The Beach) is the Mum who is blind to the sexual misconduct of her husband and the harm being done to her eighteen year old daughter, Jessie.

It is fifteen year old Tom (Freddie Cunliffe, in his film debut) who first discovers the horrific secret of his family, catching a glimpse of an exchange between father and daughter during bath time. Spying further, his suspicions are confirmed as he (and we) see Dad lead Jessie to an abandoned bunker on the unforgiving coastline of Devon where he proceeds to take her sexually.

Lara Belmont gives a brave and heart wrenching performance as Jessie. It is difficult to recall a character as emotionally raw as this one and many of her scenes are uncomfortably realistic. The War Zone marks her first time in front of a camera (as it does for young Mr. Cunliffe) which makes their performances all the more noteworthy.

Mr. Roth sets the film in the bleak and dreary coastal town of Devon, England. The overcast skies and gray landscape help set the proper atmosphere for this humorless film. The score by Simon Boswell (A Midsummer Night's Dream) also adds a musical tension which underscores the sense of impending discovery coupled with sadness as young Tom draws nearer to the truth.

This is one depressing film and will leave its mark on those who choose to watch it. Effective and accurate, not only in its portrayal of human depravity but also the guilt and shame that follows, it contains many disturbing scenes and nauseating implications. Normally, the film would be awarded between three and four stars for its artistic excellence. However, I want to be clear that, due to its harsh and emotionally draining subject matter, I am not going on record as recommending this film. Everything gives off something and The War Zone is no different. A feeling of depression and an overall heaviness followed me out of the theater at the end of the film and that I wouldn't wish on anyone.

As believing Christians, we live every day in "The War Zone." Our battles are spiritual ones and our opponent uses everything in his arsenal to try to defeat us. Incest is nothing new to our generation. The apostle Paul confronted the Corinthian believers on this very subject:

It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you, impurity of a sort that is condemned and does not occur even among the heathen; for a man has his own father's wife. 1 Corinthians 5:1 [Amplified]

No one is immune to any of the temptations of this world, regardless of how distasteful they may appear to us today. We have a spiritual enemy who excels in deception and in blinding the eyes of people. He has done so for centuries. All the more reason for us to arm ourselves with the truth of the Word which will stand for all time. It will guide us through the temptations, strengthening us so we may stand against the lusts of the flesh and the pride of life. Instead of suffering through the anguish of The War Zone, we can live as victors in the spiritual war zone of life.
