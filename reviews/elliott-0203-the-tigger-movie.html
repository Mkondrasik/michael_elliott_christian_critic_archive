The Tigger Movie

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 11, 2000
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Family; belonging; children of God; fellowship; spiritual family

Author-verified transcription

The year 2000 has been very good for the popular Disney characters of earlier generations. First Mickey Mouse and Donald Duck were both super-sized on the IMAX screen in Fantasia 2000, and now, for the first time in seventeen years, we get a cinematic visit from Winnie the Pooh and his friends from the Hundred Acre Wood.

All the main characters are on hand: Winnie, Piglet, Eeyore, Rabbit, Owl, Kanga, Roo, and Christopher Robin. But as the title suggests, The Tigger Movie features the rubbery, springy, ball of striped furry energy called Tigger (spelled T-I-double grrr-r). The movie opens with the irrepressible Tigger looking for someone to bounce with, for, as we all know, "bouncin' is what Tiggers do best." When no one else has the time or inclination to bounce, he figures that being the "onliest" Tigger might not be such a wonderful thing after all.

His friends, led by the young and irresistible Roo, try to make Tigger feel better by leading him to believe that he must have a family somewhere. Thus begins Tigger's quest for his family tree. And since it's his family, he figures it would have to be the biggest, most tiggerific tree in the whole Hundred Acre Wood.

As his search grows longer and longer with nary a single Tigger sighting, he becomes sadder and sadder. Nothing cheers him up for long, not even teaching Roo the special "whoop-de-dooper, loop-de-looper, alli-ooper" bounce, which is the most difficult bounce in Tigger's repertoire. It soon becomes obvious that it will be up to his friends to figure out some way to put the spring back into Tigger's tail.

The filmmakers, led by director Jun Falkenstein, took great pains to capture the feel and look of the earlier Pooh features. There's certainly a sentimentality to the picture that adult audiences will appreciate. The story line is reminiscent to Gonzo's search for his family in the 1999 disappointing release Muppets From Space. Thankfully, the Disney team was able to incorporate the same thematic elements into a stronger, more rewarding picture.

The accomplished song writing team of brothers Richard M. Sherman and Robert B. Sherman (Mary Poppins, Chitty Chitty Bang Bang) contribute six new songs to the effort. The songs are pleasant enough, though not particularly memorable, a point perhaps inadvertently driven home as soon as we hear Tigger's theme song, written by the same Sherman Brothers in 1968 and, in contrast, even today, immediately recognizable.

At the screening I attended, the youngest members of the audience were getting a bit restless two-thirds into the film, indicating that perhaps the running time and lack of constant action may try the attention span of preschoolers. However older children as well as their grateful parents should find The Tigger Movie very satisfying. The final sequence, which puts the beloved characters in life-threatening peril, may be considered a bit intense by Pooh standards, but nothing which isn't standard Saturday morning television fare.

Tigger's quest for his family was born out of a feeling of sadness and loneliness as well as out of a growing desire to belong to someone or something else. These are thoughts and emotions to which perhaps most of us can relate. However, as Christians, we know that we belong to a family that spans time and generational barriers. There is never a need for us to feel alone or unwanted.

The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God: And if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; Romans 8:16-17a [KJV]

God called us to be His family; His sons and daughters. Regardless of the relationship which we may share with our earthly families, be it good or bad, the spiritual relationship we have with one another, which is knit together in love, is what reminds us that we shall never be truly alone. To use Tigger's words, that's one of the "bestest" things about being a Christian.
