Author-verified transcription

Our lives are comprised of little more than a series of events that we experience. One event cascades into another which leads to still another and another. As the domino effect takes over, it is natural for one to look back at some point in one's life and wonder, "What if?"

Such is the premise of Sliding Doors starring Gwyneth Paltrow (A Perfect Murder). Miss Paltrow plays Helen, a woman who may or may not discover her boyfriend in bed with another woman. Whether she makes this discovery or not hinges upon her catching a particular subway train.

As the movie reaches this pivotal scene, it suddenly reverses and plays the scene again with a different outcome. From that moment on, we find ourselves watching two different movies...simultaneously: One that has Helen discovering her boyfriend's infidelity; the other that has her remaining in the dark.

Working from his own screenplay, director Peter Howitt, in his directorial debut, brilliantly keeps the parallel stories distinct yet familiar as each movie shares the same characters and events. In fact, the movies become easier to distinguish as Helen's "lives" grow farther and farther apart.

Miss Paltrow is marvelous in this unusual dual role. Her beauty and charm make it difficult to imagine any man stupid enough to cheat on her. Perhaps that is why John Lynch (Moll Flanders) plays Jerry, the unfaithful boyfriend, as a bumbling liar who, after getting caught with his pants down, adopts that telltale deer in the headlights look about him. Here, we think, is a man that stupid.

John Hanna (Four Weddings and a Funeral), as James, the Monty Python fan who befriends the miserable Helen in her time of need, is both amusing and endearing.

Jeanne Tripplehorn (Waterworld) rounds out the cast as Lydia, the other woman in both movies. She plays the role with great relish and zeal. This is clearly not a woman to be scorned.

Sliding Doors is an inventive and entertaining movie which poses some interesting questions, although a surprise ending may prompt some to draw improper conclusions regarding the role of "fate" or "destiny" as if these were tangible realities. That would be a mistake. The only thing controlling our future is the decisions we make on a day to day basis. It is part of the free will God has designed and freely given to us.

That being said, each of us will face crossroads in life. There are events that occur that demand from us a response. How we respond at such times will determine the course our life will take.

As the prophet Moses said in Deuteronomy 29:12 [KJV],

"I set before thee this day life and death. Therefore, choose life."

Each day that we live.. indeed each moment of each day...gives us the opportunity to choose a righteous path that leads to a full and fulfilling life.

"What if?" is the wrong question to be asking at the end of our lives. There is no profit in knowing the answer even if it were available to know.

If we choose the godly path when we find ourselves at those crossroads of life, the scriptures tell us that we have chosen correctly. There is no reason to wonder "What if?"

As the Apostle Paul said in 2 Timothy 4:7 [KJV] as he neared the end of his life,

"I have fought a good fight. I have finished my course."

He wasn't asking "What if I had done this?" or "What if I had said this?" Once the truth was made known to him, he lived his life according to the scriptures. It wasn't fate or destiny that made him a great man of God. It was his daily commitment to the Word of God that he loved.
