Anger Management

Michael Elliott

April 11, 2003
Rating
2 stars

Author-verified transcription

The biggest problem with Anger Management is the fact that it is an Adam Sandler film featuring Jack Nicholson instead of being a Jack Nicholson film featuring Adam Sandler. A brilliant premise treated sophomorically by Sandler's Happy Gilmore Productions will naturally result in a sophomoric film. The effort is far beneath Nicholson's talents. It is however par for the course where Sandler is concerned.

Sandler (Punch Drunk Love) stars as Dave Buznik, an emotionally repressed executive assistant, who is ironically accused of an in-flight emotional outburst. He is sentenced to sit through 20 hours of "anger management" with the controversial therapist Buddy Rydell (Jack Nicholson, About Schmidt).

Buddy's unorthodox "treatment" drives Busnik to the brink of rage which the court interprets as need for more intensive therapy. So they assign Buddy to move in with Dave and become Busnik's constant and continually irritating companion during a 30 day trial.

The film pretty much consists of Buddy's therapy and the effect it has on his reluctant patient. It isn't pretty. Standing in the wings is Linda (Marisa Tomei, In The Bedroom), Dave's understanding girlfriend, who is simply waiting for Dave to make a commitment.

While it is clear that Dave has issues - a fear of intimacy, feelings of inadequacy, etc. - they are minor when compared to the long list of problems which Buddy brings to his life.

Anger Management could have been a brilliantly funny film. It could have provided insight regarding the human psyche and the strange foibles and peccadilloes which make us all unique individuals. It could have been as enjoyable for the audience to watch as it apparently was for the actors to make. It could have been all these things. But it isn't. It is the biggest cinematic disappointment of the year thus far.

Besides the premise itself, the only enjoyable things about Anger Management are the odd celebrity cameo appearances that keep popping up. But even these, as numerous as they are, cannot disguise the fact that we're looking at a train wreck. Poorly written, directed and edited, the film wastes more opportunities for humor than it uses.

Sandler simply reverts to his stock character of introvert-boy while Nicholson offers a caricature of his on screen persona. Neither satisfies.

Naturally, the film has to do with anger and how that particular emotion affects us. As far as Dave is concerned, we see immediately that his problem is a result of suppressed anger. While there are many scriptures which describe the benefits of controlling one's emotions, complete suppression of emotion is never a healthy thing. Anger can be justified and is sometimes spiritually required.

Perhaps the best scriptural example of righteous anger might be found in Matthew 21:12-13.

And Jesus went into the temple of God, and cast out all them that sold and bought in the temple, and overthrew the tables of the moneychangers, and the seats of them that sold doves, And said unto them, It is written, My house shall be called the house of prayer; but ye have made it a den of thieves. Matthew 21:12-13 (KJV)

Jesus acted with deliberate care. Yes, he was angry but his anger did not control him. This is the key to how we should manage our emotions. Emotions are a good and positive aspect of being human. They add spice and variety to life. But they should never overwhelm our ability to reason. And they should never control our actions.

He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city. Proverbs 16:32 (KJV)
