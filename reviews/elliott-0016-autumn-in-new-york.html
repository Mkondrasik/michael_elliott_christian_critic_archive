Autumn in New York

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 11, 2000
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

John 6:35 Ezekiel 3:1-3 Job 23:12

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual nourishment; God's Word; hunger and thirst

Author-verified transcription

In AUTUMN IN NEW YORK, one of the characters states definitively, "There are only two kinds of love stories: Boy loses girl; or girl loses boy." With that cheery philosophy guiding us, we are then treated to a developing love relationship slated for heartbreak.

Richard Gere (Runaway Bride) plays Will Keane, 48 year old restaurateur and unabashed womanizer. Excusing himself from his current girlfriend he keeps his future girlfriend waiting at the bar while he flirts with two more possible girlfriends who have come to his restaurant. His friend and maitre d' (Anthony LaPaglia, Summer of Sam) is beginning to feel like a pimp.

During this odd female juggling act, he spies 22 year old Charlotte (Winona Ryder, Girl Interrupted) celebrating her birthday at his restaurant. Despite the fact that this young girl is the daughter of a woman with whom he was once friendly, he manages to get and use her number. Her boozy grandmother (Elaine Stritch, Small Time Crooks), after unwisely introducing them, is unable to stop the inevitable.

They date. They do more than date. He is honest with her by telling her that their romantic interlude cannot lead to anything else. She is honest with him as well. She knows it can't lead to anything else because she'll be dead within the year. Whoa.... Bummer.

It turns out that Charlotte has been diagnosed with a untreatable tumor growing in her chest cavity. She simply wants to live out the time she has left in a normal and uncomplicated manner. And so she begins a complicated relationship with a not-so-normal man. Go figure.

Meanwhile, Will is having some problems. Never before able to commit to a serious relationship, he finds himself unable to think of anyone except Charlotte and that scares him. The movie is really about his despicable character and how this young girl manages to change him from a skirt chasing charming cad to a loving, caring, monogamous individual.

The film is attractively shot by director Joan Chen (Xiu Xiu: The Sent-Down Girl) and cinematographer Changwei Gu (Hurlyburly). New York, as a setting, has rarely looked better and the two stars look radiantly appealing. It is the story, written by Allison Burnett (Red Meat), which is, much like young Charlotte, dying on the vine. Perhaps this version of Love Story may strike a chord with a female audience, but I found the film to be ineffective in the emotional manipulation category. If one is going to make a weepy romance, it would help to add some scenes that could trigger tears.

The biggest hurdle the film must overcome is the fact that although Mr. Gere and Ms. Ryder sparkle in their individual roles, there are no sparks being generated from the relationship between them. What we are left with is a passionless romantic drama which, while cosmetically attractive, has no soul to sustain it.

As a chef/restaurateur, Keane has an opinion on food. He calls it "the only beautiful thing that truly nourishes." Perhaps from a secular point of view, he's right. Spiritually, of course, we recognize that God's Word nourishes our soul as completely and as fulfilling as a three course meal might nourish our body. Perhaps that is why God frequently uses food to make a spiritual analogy.

And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst. John 6:35 (KJV)

We can appreciate the satisfaction we physically have when we finish a well-balanced, well-presented meal. That satisfaction pales in comparison to the nourishment God gives us spiritually whenever we feast upon His Word.

We have the means and responsibility to present the Word to people who have a hunger and thirst but no knowledge how to satisfy that hunger or quench that thirst. Better than a five-star gourmet restaurant, the Word of God provides a "meal" that will nourish beyond a lifetime.
