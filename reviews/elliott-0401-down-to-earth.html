Down To Earth

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 16, 2001
Rating
1½ stars

1 hr: 27 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Death; resurrection; Christ's return; hope

Author-verified transcription

As any combat veteran will attest, trying to light three cigarettes on a single match is an ill-advised idea. Lighting the first cigarette alerts the enemy; lighting the second gives them time to aim; the poor soul who tries for the third time gets the bullet. Down To Earth falls victim to the "three on a match" curse and will be sure to draw fire from the nation's critics.

Harry Segall's play, Heaven Can Wait has now had three film incarnations. In 1941, Robert Montgomery starred in the Academy Award winning Here Comes Mr. Jordan. In 1978, Warren Beatty starred in the Academy Award nominated Heaven Can Wait. Now in 2001, Chris Rock stars in Down to Earth but it is highly doubtful that awards are anywhere in its future.

The premise is the same in all three films. A quick-on-the-trigger angel snatches a man's soul seconds before a terrible accident which surely would have been fatal. Upon arriving in heaven, it is discovered that the man wasn't scheduled to die. Because his old body was cremated, the angel must find him a new body to house his soul.

The previous films dealt with a boxer working his way to the top and an NFL quarterback on his way to the Super Bowl. In Down to Earth, Chris Rock plays Lance Barton, a struggling comedian whose act generates little enthusiasm. Still, he is determined to make it and has chosen Amateur Night at the Apollo Theater in Harlem as his proving ground.

After he and his body part ways, he winds up inside the body of middle-aged, overweight, white billionaire Charles Wellington. This doesn't deter his dream of bringing his street-smart, hip-hop act to the Apollo...even if he has to do it as a fat and happy white Republican.

The racial twist is by far the most interesting element to this remake but the filmmakers simply don't employ it in ways to make any kind of social statement. It is a wasted opportunity. As a pure comedy, apart from Rock's closing standup routine, there is nothing that gives this film any distinction.

Rock can be blisteringly funny on stage but is given little opportunity to shine in this vehicle. Regina King (Jerry Maguire) is merely adequate as Rock's love interest, Sontee. (And we never accept that her character would be attracted to Wellington.) Chazz Palminteri (Hurlyburly) and Eugene Levy (American Pie) walk miserably through their roles as the angels who are keeping an eye on Lance's situation.

Clearly, directors Chris Weitz and Paul Weitz (American Pie) never managed to get a handle on this project. This is unusual seeing as they had two other award caliber templates from which to copy.

Of course, the story, as was true of its other two incarnations, is pure fantasy. There is absolutely no scriptural support which tells us that angels snatch souls and take them to heaven; or that heaven resembles a popular nightclub where, if your name isn't on the guest list, you're told to go to hell... literally; or that dead people can be reincarnated and come back down to earth.

The scriptures are quite clear regarding what happens after death if we but take the time to study them. All verses agree that nothing regarding the dead will take place until Christ returns.

"For this we declare to you by the word of the Lord, that we who are alive, who are left until the coming of the Lord, will by no means precede those who have died. For the Lord himself, with a cry of command, with the archangel's call and with the sound of God's trumpet, will descend from heaven, and the dead in Christ will rise first. Then we who are alive, who are left, will be caught up in the clouds together with them to meet the Lord in the air; and so we will be with the Lord forever." 1 Thessalonians 4:15-17 (NRSV)

This great promise of God has been made to comfort us and give us hope which is more than I can say for this uncomfortable and hopeless film.
