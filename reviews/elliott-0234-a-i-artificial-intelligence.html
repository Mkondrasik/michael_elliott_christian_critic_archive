A.I. Artificial Intelligence

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 29, 2001
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 45:18Ephesians 1:4-5Psalms 115:16

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Creation; purpose; existence; family; fellowship; love

Author-verified transcription

The story behind the making of A.I. is an interesting one. Stanley Kubrick (Eyes Wide Shut) had dreamed of making this film for almost two decades, dying before he had the opportunity to see it realized. His friend, Steven Spielberg (E.T.), honored the late director's vision by taking up the reins and bringing A.I. through production. If only the finished product had proved to be worthy of such an impressive high-caliber "collaboration."

The story is a simple one. Basically, it's an adult version of Pinocchio, 21st century style. The ice caps have melted. The world's coastal cities are submerged. Millions of people have been displaced. Procreation, once such a simple process, has become more difficult as the decision to allow people to have children is weighed against the diminishing resources that must be divided amongst all human life.

The solution? Robot children. Ones that have the added capacity to love. "Mechas" (short for mechanicals) have been used for some time to perform menial or sexual tasks. But David (Haley Joel Osment, The Sixth Sense) is a prototype of a mecha that has been equipped with emotional sensors.

The scientist who developed David (William Hurt, The Big Chill) can answer all technical questions posed to him. Where he stumbles is over the moral questions such as "what responsibility do the owners have towards the robot who has been programmed to love them?"

For the first third (and most effective part of the film), it is exactly this question which Monica (Frances O'Connor, Bedazzled), her husband and son must face as they try unsuccessfully to accept David into their family. Had the movie stayed in this family-oriented setting, Spielberg's commercial instincts may have prevailed and the end result might have been far more pleasing.

Instead, the second section of the film follows an abandoned David, his super toy Teddy, and a "love" mecha named Gigolo Joe (Jude Law, Enemies at the Gate) as they set out in search of a way to turn David into a real boy. Their journey takes them through such futuristic horrors as "Flesh Fairs," where robots are gleefully destroyed in a circus like atmosphere, and Rouge City where women are apparently the featured attraction (though we don't see many).

Here, the film loses much of its steam and by the time we hit the third section, the film has long overstayed its welcome. The final half hour of the film can only be described as disappointing. What could have been an interesting and compelling examination into the nature of life and love (i.e. the meaning of existence), becomes a mediocre and maudlin sci-fi soap opera. The production values may be high and the gadgets amazing, but the story itself is not captivating even though the subject matter is.

Haley Joel Osment does his best to carry the film through his portrayal of a machine who longs to be human, but even his uncanny talent (unusual in one so young) is overwhelmed by the overly ambitious scope of the project.

A.I. seems to want to be an edgy, intellectual drama set in a futuristic society where advanced technology has outpaced man's ability to morally apply it. The problem is that Spielberg's touch lacks the hard "edge" that is needed to truly unsettle an audience and involve us in his "alternate reality."

A character near the end of the film comments that, for a while, humans were considered to be the key to understanding the meaning behind existence itself. He then dismissed that idea as a disproved theory. Obviously, that character didn't read his Bible with enough care.

For thus saith the LORD that created the heavens; God himself that formed the earth and made it; he hath established it, he created it not in vain, he formed it to be inhabited: I am the LORD; and there is none else. Isaiah 45:18 (KJV)

The reason for our existence has been revealed to us already. God wanted a family. Fellowship. Love. And there is nothing artificial about that.
