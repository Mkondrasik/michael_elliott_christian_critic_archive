Around the Bend

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 8, 2004
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Luke 15:21-24 Jonah 4:10-11 Matthew 18:21-22

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Forgiveness; prodigal son; reconciliation; letting go of the past

Author-verified transcription

The parable of the prodigal son is given an odd twist in Around the Bend. A man, in order to fulfill a dying man’s last wish, returns to the family he abandoned thirty years earlier. It is his son who has the most difficulty accepting him back into the fold.

One can hardly blame him. Jason Lair (Josh Lucas) never really knew his father. He was told that, after his mother died in the same car accident that crippled him, his father just up and left. He was raised by Henry, his archeologist grandfather. He was two years old at the time. Now a young man with a child of his own and a failing marriage, he has long since resigned himself to the loss of both his parents.

With no advance warning or explanation, his father, whom he calls Turner (Christopher Walken), shows up on his doorstep. Henry (Michael Caine) is delighted and insists on taking everyone out to the local KFC, his favorite restaurant, to celebrate the return of his son. Seven-year-old Zach (Jonah Bobo) is just curious why Grandpa Turner, whom he was told was dead, is now suddenly alive.

The awkwardness of the reunion is extended once Henry dies and leaves behind an unusual request in his will. He wants Turner, Jason and Zach to take a mysterious road trip and spoon his ashes at designated spots which he has sealed in various KFC takeout bags. It is clear that the convoluted ritual, which of course involves dining at various KFC restaurants along the way, is designed to give the family time to bond and to accept and forgive one another. It also appears to be leading up to the revelation of a deep, dark family secret.

Around the Bend serves as a showcase for a trio of actors who take full advantage of the opportunity to ply their craft. Michael Caine is, once again, wonderful in a role which, to our disappointment, has abbreviated screen time. Even so, we feel his presence in the film long after his character has died.

Christopher Walken is an actor who has almost made quirkiness an art form unto itself. Turner is full of secrets and contradictions which is a perfect match with the screen persona that Walken has developed over his career.

Josh Lucas may not be in the upper tier of box office draws yet but it is only a matter of time. He has an easy going manner and the leading man good looks which should catapult him into the A-list in no time at all.

Writer/director Jordan Roberts doesn’t really have anything new to say with Around the Bend but he says it in such an earnest way that we don’t mind hearing it again. Basically, the movie is just reminding us that we’re all human. We all make mistakes in life. The lesson of the prodigal son (or in the movie’s case, the prodigal father) is ultimately one of forgiveness.

And the son said unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in thy sight, and am no more worthy to be called thy son. But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet: And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it; and let us eat, and be merry: For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry. Luke 15:21-24 (KJV)

Instead of harboring resentment or building bitterness over the time lost and the pain caused by Turner’s disappearing act, the parable teaches us that Jason should rejoice in the fact that Turner has finally found the courage to come home again.

The bottom line is that no matter how hard we try, we can never change the past. We can however change the future by not allowing the past to dictate what we do today.
