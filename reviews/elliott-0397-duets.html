Author-verified transcription

If karate means "empty or open hand," could it be that karaoke means "open mike?" To the devotees of this growing cultural phenomenon, karaoke is not just a passing entertainment fad, it is a way of life. It provides an adrenaline rush by granting an average person the opportunity to stand before an audience as a star for three and a half minutes. Duets uses the subculture which is developing within the ranks of the rapidly spreading karaoke clubs as a foundation from which to explore deeper societal issues.

Director Bruce Paltrow (TV's St. Elsewhere) and screenwriter John Byrum (The Razor's Edge) combine three separate stories involving three unlikely couples who are brought together by the "big" money karaoke competition taking place in Omaha, Nebraska.

Ricky Dean (Huey Lewis, Short Cuts) is a hustler; a karaoke con man who travels from bar to bar, entering contests to collect on small first place purses as well as the larger side bets he places with the home town favorites. His wandering lifestyle is interrupted when he meets up with his Vegas showgirl daughter, Liv (Gwyneth Paltrow, Shakespeare in Love). She was the result of a brief fling and someone whom he has never let into his life. With her mother dead, Liv decides, much to Ricky's discomfort, that now is the time to get to know her other parent.

Billy Hannon (Scott Speedman, TV's Felicity) is a genuinely nice guy; a down on his luck taxi driver/ex-seminary student who hooks up with Suzi Loomis (Maria Bello, Coyote Ugly), a karaoke singer who is not above performing a sexual favor or three in order to advance her career. Unhappy with where he is in life, he agrees to drive her to LA as she searches for her fame and fortune.

The most incongruous pairing involves the burnt out, disillusioned, and pill-popping traveling salesman Todd Woods (Paul Giamatti, Big Momma's House), who forsakes family, friends, and career to pursue the karaoke lifestyle. He meets the career criminal Reggie Kane (Andre Braugher, Frequency) who has only two talents in life, singing and armed robbery. Of the three relationships, theirs is the most interesting and the most frustrating of all, as it appears to be on a track destined for unhappiness for no good reason other than sheer ignorance. The karaoke rendition of "Try A Little Tenderness" performed by these two characters is a wonderful moment and for me, the highlight of this film.

It is with these two characters that the script takes a sharp "dramatic" turn which stretches the credibility of the story beyond the audience's ability to suspend their disbelief. This plot twist lessens the sweet impact the film could have made with the various characters and their stories and cheapens the overall production. Duets also unnecessarily uses heavy sexuality and profanity to present its tale. By doing so, the filmmakers have cut their potential audience substantially. One would think that the studios would eventually clue into the fact that good stories do not need to include material inappropriate for family consumption in order to gain the adult audience. Quite often, the opposite is true.

The common element shared by these six characters is their need to break beyond the shackles of their current existence to fill a long standing void in their life. Karaoke merely serves as a metaphor for the risks that they must take in order to break free from whatever is binding them to a life of mediocrity and quiet resignation.

Using the same analogy, many of us were bound by invisible shackles which held us back from achieving all that God would have us do. As we grow in our spiritual maturity, may we be more willing to risk the ridicule of an unbelieving world and sing out for the glory of our God.

Behold, the former things are come to pass, and new things do I declare: before they spring forth I tell you of them. Sing unto the LORD a new song, and his praise from the end of the earth, ye that go down to the sea, and all that is therein; the isles, and the inhabitants thereof. Isaiah 42:9-10 (KJV)
