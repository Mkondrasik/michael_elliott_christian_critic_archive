Author-verified transcription

For some, college is an opportunity to grow, explore new vistas, and chart the course of one's future. Attending college can and should be a life-building experience. Of course we know that every opportunity can also be wasted. In the case of Van Wilder, waste would be putting it kindly.

Wilder (Ryan Reynolds, Buying the Cow) has chosen to matriculate at a snail's pace, evident by the fact that he is in the middle of his seventh year of undergraduate work. It is not that he's lazy or unintelligent. He just likes college so much that he never intends to graduate. The reason he likes college is simple: he excels at partying.

After his father (Tim Matheson, Animal House) decides to stop pumping money into a losing enterprise, Wilder must find other ways to pay for his tuition. Among his various enterprises, he starts "Topless Tutors" and hires himself out as a "party liaison." Both services prove to be widely popular.

Wilder has a potential girlfriend in Gwen (Tara Reid, American Pie), a journalism major assigned against her wishes to do a story on the self-proclaimed BMOC, a potential rival in Richard Bagg (Daniel Cosgrove, Valentine), a stereotypical frat boy whose ego and lack of intelligence makes him the butt of the many gross jokes to which we are subjected, and a potential sidekick in Taj (Kal Penn, American Desi) a foreign exchange student who hopes to learn from Wilder's debauchery.

Director Walt Becker (Buying the Cow) and the screen writing team of Brent Goldberg and David Wagner (whose first effort together was a seven minute parody called Saving Ryan's Privates) are responsible for bringing this lame and sorry excuse for a comedy to fruition. They obviously hope to tap into a market clamoring for gross humor and sexually immature material. We can only pray that such a market will one day cease to exist.

Ryan Reynolds is affable enough as the slacker with a heart of gold. It is however a one note performance which, when spread over 90 minutes, tends to become bland and uninteresting. Tara Reid continues her run in a long string of forgettable and emotionally shallow projects.

Van Wilder is a National Lampoon production but doesn't come close to the energy of an Animal House or a laugh count of their Vacation series with Chevy Chase. I would suggest waiting until the video of Van Wilder comes out and then continue to wait until it is shown as a midnight offering on some obscure network. Even then, if you have something better to do, like re-grout the shower tiles, opt for the grout.

The filmmakers paint Wilder as being something of a campus legend, a person to emulate and idolize along the lines of a collegiate Ferris Bueller. Should such a person actually exist, emulation would not be a wise course of action. Only in the movies is a wasteful slacker with a major lack of ambition held in such esteem. In life, such an individual is generally thought of in much less complimentary ways.

A foolish son is a grief to his father, and bitterness to her that bare him. Proverbs 17:25 (KJV)

Even though the film ends with by employing flawed rationalizations in an attempt to somehow try and redeem the central character in the final act, it is a case of too little, too late. Van Wilder shows himself to be a first class loser. The same can be said of the film which bears his name.
