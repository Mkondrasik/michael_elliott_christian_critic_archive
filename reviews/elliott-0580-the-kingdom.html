From whence come wars and fightings among you? Come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members? (James 4:1 KJV)

War or (better said) the hatred that fuels the continuation of war is antithetical to the proper Christian mindset. Trying to rationalize or justify war from a Christian perspective is (or should be) as futile an exercise as there is. We certainly feel that way while watching The Kingdom.

When an unnamed band of terrorists attack an American compound in Saudi Arabia, killing over 100 innocents, an FBI team forces its way past the geo-political obstacles and are granted 5 days to investigate the horrific incident (under the watchful eye of Saudi police colonel Faris Al Ghazi (Ashraf Barhom).

Under duress and without much cooperation from the local government, FBI agent Ronald Fleury (Jamie Foxx) and his team (Jennifer Garner, Chris Cooper, and Jason Bateman) begin their in-depth and meticulous investigation to find the clues that might lead to those responsible.

The film itself never quite comes together. Surprisingly enough, despite the A-list award winning cast, there just isn’t enough substance to the story to justify the two hour running time. It has the feel of an episode of one of those CSI TV shows extended an hour past its audience’s attention span.

The opening moments which give an overview look at US/Saudi relations are riveting as are the final 20 minutes of the film. Unfortunately, it is the stuff in between that takes up the lion’s share of the film and does so in a way that isn’t particularly captivating.

The acting across the board isn’t the problem. Fine performances are given by all four of the American leads as well as a sensitive portrayal from Ashraf Barhom as a Saudi investigator. But the script by Matthew Michael Carnahan never clarifies the confusion or adds insight to the insanity of the Mid-East conflict.

We know “war is hell” so seeing the maimed bodies and bloodshed doesn’t add to our perception or understanding. And outside of a gratuitous “we are all people under the same sun” kind of message, The Kingdom doesn’t particularly add anything to the very real problem that exists in the Middle East.

In The Kingdom, people kill out of a hatred that is never explored or explained. That’s not to say it doesn’t exist. It is only to say that we are no closer to understanding it after watching the film. The conflict continues and young men and women continue to die for a cause few of us can truly understand.

The scriptures tell us that war and violence will always be a part of this world. The “world peace” that some preach is a fallacy for it is not within man to achieve on his own. But there will come a time when wars will cease and peace will reign.

And he shall judge among the nations, and shall rebuke many people: and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks: nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more. Isaiah 2:4 (KJV)

As we strive to live in this world peaceably with all men, we can find comfort in the knowledge that one day, it won’t require as great an effort. It will be the natural course of our eternal lives.
