We’re The Millers

Author-verified transcription

What constitutes a family? In the case of the profanity-laden and sexually explicit comedy, We’re The Millers, the family in question is comprised of a small time drug dealer, a jaded stripper, a rebellious teen runaway, and a virginal nerd. Of course, these characters are all whitewashed, Hollywood-cleansed comedic versions of their stereotypes.

When dime bag drug dealer David Clark (Jason Sudeikis) is robbed of his cash and stash, his supplier gives him an opportunity to make up the loss with one quick trip into Mexico to pick up a “smidge” of marijuana. Knowing the penalty for drug smuggling, David has the bright idea to camouflage his activities by pretending to be on a family vacation. He rents an RV and hires a family. Being a lone loser type, the only people he knows can be similarly described. His neighbor Rose (Jennifer Aniston) is a stripper/lap dancer who has just gotten evicted from her apartment; Casey (Emma Roberts) is a homeless teen runaway scamming her way on the streets; and Kenny (Will Poulter) is a clueless, naïve geek whose parents have apparently abandoned him.

There is a great deal of humor in the film but it is of a highly sexual and profane nature. Much of the humor is derived from the way these low class individuals try to blend in with middle-American families and their white-bread values. There is a great deal of “shock” comedy used including a highly publicized scene involving a prosthetic sex organ worn by one of the teen characters.

The language and sexual references aside, the film does ask a pertinent question: what exactly is a family? Is it merely defined by the genetic cocktail in our DNA or is there something deeper involved? Here’s an answer Jesus once gave in a particular situation:

“Then Jesus’ mother and brothers arrived. Standing outside, they sent someone in to call him. A crowd was sitting around him, and they told him, ‘Your mother and brothers are outside looking for you.’ ‘Who are my mother and my brothers?’ he asked. Then he looked at those seated in a circle around him and said, ‘Here are my mother and my brothers! Whoever does God’s will is my brother and sister and mother.’” Mark 3:31-35 (NIV)

Jesus’ vision was set on a far higher plane than his human ties. It is not that he dismissed his mother and his siblings; it is that he saw his family to be inclusive of all those who would one day become his brothers and sisters because of the truth he was teaching. When we are born again of God’s spirit, we are spiritually related to Jesus Christ as our brother and fellow heir. We are also spiritually related to every man, woman and child who has similarly accepted Jesus as their Lord and Savior.

As a result, our spiritual family comes from all walks of life – and many may have come from backgrounds similar to or worse than what is depicted in the film. But as the film demonstrates, none of that really matters. Where we come from is immaterial. The question is where do we want to go? Wherever that may be, it is nice to know that we can travel with a family who can help us along the way.
