Author-verified transcription

C.O.G., based on an essay by humorist David Sedaris, tells the tale of a young man figuratively lost in this world who keeps finding the wrong people who try to guide him.

Yale graduate David (Jonathan Groff) is so disillusioned or confused with life that he decides to take a one hundred eighty degree turn to see if he can find any answers from the heartland of America. Calling himself Samuel, he endures an excruciating bus ride to Oregon and takes a job as an apple picker, working alongside itinerant Mexicans. Any idyllic fantasy he may have had of finding answers to life is quickly dashed.

And yet he sticks with it – even when the girl whose idea it was to join the “working class” bails on him. He jumps at an opportunity to escape the farm to go to a factory, replacing one tedious job for another. There he meets a semi-intelligent man (Corey Stoll) who provides the potential of friendship until he mistakes David’s desire for companionship for a sexual invitation.

David finds another escape, calling upon an evangelical Christian (Denis O’Hare) to offer him a safe haven. Unfortunately, this Christian’s hypocritical walk offers little solace, few answers, and no example for David to follow. Even David’s “conversion” to Christ is born of desperation and fear rather than hope.

The sad and meandering film ends rather abruptly and disappointedly with David no better off than when he began. What sense he will make of his experiences is left to our imagination but the impression we are left with is that Christianity is not going to be held in much esteem.

The representation of the majority of Christian characters leaves a lot to be desired. The hypocrisy, limited charity, and self-serving interests displayed in this film don’t cast a positive light on the religion or those who practice it. But it is a dangerous thing to negatively judge an institution or belief system based upon the failings of those who profess to follow it. To do so is to miss the point.

The one thing that can be said of all Christians is that we are flawed individuals. Even those of us who honestly strive for perfection will continually miss the mark. Some of us may not even try. Many of us may call ourselves Christian but show no other outward sign of following the example of Jesus Christ. All that is true but does not tell the whole story.

We cannot judge Christianity by those who fail to practice it or fail at practicing it. Christ is to be our example – not those who merely claim his name without adopting his mindset.

“He answered and said unto them, Well hath Esaias prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.” Mark 7:6 (KJV)
