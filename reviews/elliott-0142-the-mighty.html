The Mighty

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 30, 1998
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ecclesiastes 4:9-10Matthew 18:19-20Galatians 6:9-10

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Friendship; fellowship; mutual support; strength; household of God

Author-verified transcription

"A knight is known by his deeds." It is a simple statement with much wisdom behind it... much like the movie from which the quote originates. Based on the children's story, Freak the Mighty, by Rodman Philbrick, The Mighty tells a warm and touching cinematic tale of an unusual partnership turned friendship.

To say that Maxwell Kane (Elden Ratliff, The Mighty Ducks) is large for his age would be to indulge in an understatement. Repeating the 7th grade hasn't helped matters. But Max Kane has a few other social strikes against him. He's got some kind of learning disability and he's got a convicted murderer for a father who is thankfully serving time in prison. All in all, not a shoo-in for most popular classmate. In fact, he is scorned and mocked most every day.

Max's life takes a turn for the better with the entrance of his newly arrived next door neighbor. Kevin Dillon (Kieran Culkin, Father of the Bride II) is a brilliant, articulate, inquisitive boy with a pureness of heart and vivid imagination. He has also been diagnosed as having Morquiro disease, which stunts the growth of the body's skeletal structure but still allows the internal organs to grow. As a result, Kevin is weaker than most and can only walk with the assistance of crutches and leg braces. But his mind roams free and unshackled.

Kevin begins tutoring Max, who is having trouble reading. His advice: "Each word is part of a picture. Every sentence is a picture. All you do is let your imagination connect them together. If you have an imagination that is." He then gives Max a book on King Arthur's Court and a dynamic relationship is born.

Independently, each boy is hampered or "crippled" by his own shortcomings. Together, they provide the strength where the other is weak. "I need legs, you need a brain," Kevin explains. They combine to form the noble invention, "Freak the Mighty," valiant pursuer of truth and honor. Their rundown neighborhood becomes Camelot and its denizens either knights or damsels in distress.

But their lives cannot stay in this kind of Fantasy Land. Kevin's disease is real, as is Max's traumatic past which results in emotional withdrawal. As Kevin's physical condition gets worse and Max's murderous father is released from prison, the two must team up for a real adventure which could change or end their very lives.

We have some real heavyweights in the supporting cast. Gena Rowlands (Playing by Heart) and Harry Dean Stanton (Fire Down Below) admirably play Max's grandparents unto whom Max's care has been given. Sharon Stone (Diabolique) is luminescent as Kevin's loving and suffering mother. James Gandolfini (She's So Lovely) is menacing as Max's father and Gillian Anderson sheds all vestiges of her X-Files character to play a girl from the wrong side of the tracks with the right kind of heart.

But the main reason to see the film is the work done by its youngest cast members. Kieran Culkin is the younger brother of Macaulay, but don't hold that against him. His work in this film is the work of a real actor. It is touching, deeply moving, and substantial in terms of character development.

In many ways, Elden Ratliff as Max, has a harder time of it. While Kieran's character has a physical deformity that is evident for all to see, Max's problems are psychological in nature. Mr. Ratliff presents a complex character worthy of our notice.

It is the relationship of these boys that makes the film worth watching. Scripture says:

Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: Ecclesiastes 4:9-10a (KJV)

Whether it be a friend, a spouse, or a brother or sister, having someone upon whom one may lean for support is vitally important. No one need be alone in this world. God Himself called us to be a part of a functioning household. A household in which we may draw strength as well as impart strength. It's not just idle talk... The household of God is known by its deeds.
