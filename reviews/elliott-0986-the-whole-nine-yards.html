The Whole Nine Yards

Author-verified transcription

Nicholas “Oz” Oseransky is not just a fish out of water in the new movie The Whole Nine Yards... He’s a fish that is soon to be filleted, seasoned, and on its way to the skillet.

A wholesome Chicago-born dentist, Oz is leading a relatively normal life in Montreal, Quebec. Oh, he may have a few unresolved issues: His hateful French-Canadian wife wants to kill him, his dead father-in-law/partner saddled him with an enormous debt, and now, to top it off, Jimmy “The Tulip” Tudeski (number one hit man for the mob and government informant) has moved in next door to escape the mob contract that has been placed on his life.

Jimmy’s life isn’t the only one in jeopardy. Our mild mannered dentist is soon on everybody’s “hit” parade. The mob wants Oz to lead them to Jimmy so they can kill him. Jimmy wants Oz to set the mob up so he can kill them. Oz’s own wife doesn’t care which way it goes, as long as Oz is dead when it is all over. And Jimmy’s beautiful wife just wants Oz for herself... a fact which, for no other reason, may be enough to make him Jimmy’s next victim.

This slight but amusing comedy is directed by Jonathan Lynn (My Cousin Vinny) and written by newcomer Mitchell Kapner. While both have performed their respective duties adequately, if the film can be considered successful, it is primarily due to the actors’ execution, especially that of Matthew Perry (Three to Tango) as the “Nervous Nellie” dentist trying to keep his wits (usually unsuccessfully) amid the hardened killers who have invaded his life. His pratfalls and overt physical humor are indeed funny yet they have been reigned in enough to keep the character appealing and believable. He is the center around which the film revolves.

Bruce Willis (The Sixth Sense) uses his immediately recognizable smirk to great effect as a hit man with a heart of gold. A man with principles, (who just happens to kill people for a living), he believes in the sanctity of marriage which is why he won’t give his wife a divorce. Of course, he has no second thoughts about arranging for her death in order to collect a ten million dollar nest egg (the whole nine yards of the title).

Natasha Henstridge (Species) is the wife in question, and she does exude an air of beauty, sophistication and class that is immediately appealing to Oz. She is quite unlike his own murderously duplicitous wife, Sophie (Rosanna Arquette, Buffalo 66), who is a shrill, complaining shrew with an irritatingly bad French accent.

Kevin Pollack (She’s All That) has some amusing moments as the consonant challenged Janni Goglak, mob kingpin. So does Amanda Peet (Isn’t She Great) as Oz’s receptionist and hit man aficionado, Jill. Michael Clarke Duncan (The Green Mile) lends his considerable support to the film as Frankie Figgs, one of Goglak’s henchman.

Obviously, it is not our intention to condone or glorify the murders or other criminal actions of the characters in the film. Be advised that the movie indeed deserves its “R” rating. But even with all the “cartoon violence,” killings, and socially unacceptable practices going on, The Whole Nine Yards retains a certain degree of sweetness because at the center of it all is a genuinely nice guy who is motivated... Not by greed... Not by vengeance... But by love.

Love, as God defines it, remains the greatest motivating factor in the world. When we operate out of a love for God, we are strengthened by His might and all things become possible. The hit men hired to “cap” Oz, couldn’t seem to bring themselves to do it because of his good and genuinely sweet nature.

When a man’s ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him. Proverbs 16:7 [KJV]

A man’s ways cannot please the Lord unless he first loves God with all his heart, soul, mind, and strength, keeping His commandments and loving his neighbor as himself. That last part is sometimes hard. But who knows? If a neighbor like Jimmy the Tulip can recognize and respond to love, maybe so can yours.
