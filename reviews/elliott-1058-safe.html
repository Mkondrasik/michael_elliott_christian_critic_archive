Safe

Author-verified transcription

“Are we safe?” asks a character in the carnage-filled film by Boaz Yakin called Safe. The character might as well ask, “Is there anyone we haven’t killed yet?” A direct answer is never given but from what we see leading up to the question, we would be inclined to say no.

Jason Statham stars as Luke Wright, an ex-mercenary turned ex-cop turned B-level cage fighter who gets on the wrong side of some very bad people. And then he sees Mei (Catherine Chan) in a subway. This 12-year-old gifted girl is being abused, controlled and manipulated because of her photographic memory by a different group of very bad people. Intuitively, Luke has a new found purpose. To protect Mei and kill as many very bad people as central casting can throw up on the screen. As always, central casting seems to have an unending supply at the ready.

And that, in a nutshell, is the plot. Admittedly, it is sparse. In fact, it is almost embarrassingly non-existent. And yet, it is enough to lay the foundation for scene after scene of gun blazing, neck-cracking, mass killing action. If that is the cup of tea you desire, you’ll be steeped in it, pun intended.

It was Mei who asked the question that opened this review. And she asked it of Luke. Perhaps that is why he did not provide a direct answer. No man can guarantee safety for himself or for any other person. All he can do is prepare as best he can to fight any enemy to the best of his abilities. But no one can claim a victory before the battle is fought… no one except God.

“The horse is prepared against the day of battle: but safety is of the LORD.” Proverbs 21:31 (KJV)

In this figure of speech, the horse symbolizes the physicality of man. Man can prepare, plan, and fight, but he cannot promise victory or a safe outcome to the fight. God can. God, who knows all and sees all, can promise victory and salvation to all who turn to him. If we really want to know and receive safety, we first must know and receive God into our hearts. When we do, we will never again have to ask, “Are we safe?” We will already know it to be true.
