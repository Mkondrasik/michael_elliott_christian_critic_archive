Author-verified transcription

State and Main... the American epitome of a small town intersection. For the cast and crew of "This Old Mill," a fictional period film about the quest for purity, this intersection in the sleepy town of Waterford, VT represents just the setting they so desperately need. A need stemming from the fact that the last small town they were in has banned them from ever coming back.

Writer/director David Mamet (The Winslow Boy) uses his ear for dialogue to great advantage in this comedic clash of two cultures. On the one hand, you have the morally loose, self-important Hollywood crowd headed by director Walt Price (William H. Macy, Magnolia), whose bald-faced lies are excused as a "gift for fiction." Mix that with the apple pie and ice cream social set of a typical "Home Town, USA" and you have the basic ingredients for this film which brings to mind the screwball comedies of days gone by.

Straddling the two opposing lifestyles is novice screenwriter Joseph Turner White (Philip Seymour Hoffman, Almost Famous) who is learning all about the need to compromise in the motion picture industry. Example: His script, "This Old Mill" has to be rewritten because Waterford no longer has a mill (it burnt down in a rash of mysterious fires) and the studio can't afford to build one.

He is befriended by the quick-witted local bookstore owner and community theater director Ann Black (Rebecca Pidgeon, The Winslow Boy), which does not please her fiancé (Clark Gregg, The Adventures of Sebastian Cole), an ambitious local politician looking for something to hold against the movie people.

He doesn't have to look far. The film's leading man, Bob Barrenger (Alec Baldwin, Hunt for Red October), has a weakness for underage girls and Carla Taylor (Julia Stiles, Ten Things I Hate About You), a very willing local waitress, has become his latest "hobby." This relationship is witnessed by the young screenwriter who must then decide which is more important to him: his moral integrity or his career.

The film features an extremely talented ensemble of players. In addition to the actors already mentioned, all of whom are wonderful in their roles, David Paymer (Mr. Saturday Night) plays an acerbic producer who never travels without his law books covering the statutes on statutory rape; Sarah Jessica Parker (TV's Sex in the City) plays the film's leading lady who childishly has fits when anyone treats her like a child; and Charles Durning (TV's Evening Shade) plays the town's star struck mayor to perfection.

Mamet has filled his script with inside industry jokes and insightful observations regarding movies, the people who make them, and the fans who are all too willing to forsake their small town values for a taste of Hollywood glamour. But even though the behavior and attitudes of the players are both immoral and unsavory, Mamet does not handle the material in a salacious manner.

Just like the fictitious screenwriter realizes about his film, "This Old Mill," State and Main is about second chances. Some people, like Barrenger, never seem to learn and keep making the same mistakes. Others, like White or Ann, see the opportunity they have been given to try to correct past mistakes and reset their lives on a proper, more favorable course.

This is a theme that should be near and dear to all of our hearts. God is all about second chances.

Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord; Acts 3:19 (KJV)

"Repent" comes from a Greek word that literally means "think differently." As we condition and train our minds from their being world-oriented to being more Word-oriented, we literally change the direction of our thoughts and, eventually, of our actions. Regardless of who or where we are, God promises all of us the opportunity to change as we adopt the meekness to hear and heed His Word.
