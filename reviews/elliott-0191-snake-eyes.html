Snake Eyes

Sermon / spiritual topic: Truth; renewing the mind; humility; changing habits; personal transformation

Author-verified transcription

When is a championship title fight not a fight? When it is both the setting and plot device for the newest Brian DePalma action thriller.

Snake Eyes takes place in an Atlantic City hotel/casino/boxing arena complex where 14,000 fight fans have gathered to watch the title bout between... well, it doesn't really matter. What does matter is that those 14,000 are about to become both witnesses and suspects in an assassination attempt.

Among them is homicide detective Rick Santoro (Nicholas Cage, Face Off), a brash, outspoken cop who is not opposed to shaking down a few criminals to pocket a little extra spending money. He's there as the invited guest of his best friend, Navy Commander Kevin Dunne (Gary Sinise, Apollo 13), who is handling security for the arena in order to protect the Secretary of Defense who is seated just behind Rick.

As Hurricane Jezebel rages outside, the fight reaches a frenzied crescendo, bringing the fans to their feet... all except the Secretary, who has just taken a bullet in the throat. Following the ensuing panic, Rick takes command of the investigation, shutting down the complex, confining all 14,000 witnesses/suspects until he can figure out what to do next.

Mr. DePalma (Mission Impossible) opens the movie with an incredibly difficult, seemingly endless shot as the camera follows Nick as he meets and greets everyone that he knows (which appears to be just about everyone in the complex).

Mr. Cage overplays his character in that long, extended shot, keeping his energy level a bit past the red line. After the assassination, things settle down a bit and we can begin to see some nuances in his acting.

Mr. Sinise is very effective in his role as the Navy man whose charge is shot at the same time he is suspiciously drawn away from his post. Concerned about the possible career ramifications, he turns to Nick for advice who concedes, "If there's one thing I know, it's how to cover my ass."

Carla Gugino (The Way At Home) is cast as a mysterious woman who was seen talking to the Secretary just seconds before he was killed.

Mr. DePalma has directed half of an intriguing and involving mystery. As the layers start peeling away and the pieces start falling into place, he shows us the same scenes played from different perspectives. It is a technique that we of course have seen many times before, but Mr. DePalma still manages to use it quite effectively.

However, the killer is revealed early on and in so doing, Mr. DePalma diffuses much of the tension he has helped to build. At that point, the film slowly begins to fall apart until it reaches a lame and totally dissatisfying conclusion.

With 14,000 suspects, it is difficult to know where to start. Or who to trust. Detective Santoro quickly realizes that not all is as it seems. After utilizing the casino's hidden cameras and interviewing key participants, he begins to recognize that the investigation is headed into an area from which he'd rather stay away. "I don't want to do this!" he screams on the stairwell as a new piece of information is revealed. Of course, he does it anyway. Why? Simply because, as a detective, he seeks the truth.

Who can't associate with that? Within our lifetime we will all be called to do things that we don't want to do and to believe things that we don't want to believe. It takes meekness and humility to place our own desires in subjection to the truth.

That ye put off concerning the former conversation the old man, which is corrupt according to the deceitful lusts; And be renewed in the spirit of your mind; Ephesians 4:22-23 [KJV]

Each of us have developed habits or a mindset on various issues. When it is revealed to us that those habits or mindset are contrary to the truth of God's Word, the onus is on us to change. We can't adapt truth to fit our own personal desires. But we can change our thinking and our behavior to fit with the known truth.

Truth doesn't always match our preconceptions, but that doesn't make it less true.
