Author-verified transcription

It has been rumored that this year will mark the final season of the hit NBC series Friends. We can only assume that the cast will then separate and begin individually churning out feature films. If those films are anything like Serving Sara, look for a Friends reunion show in the very near future. They'll need the work.

Matthew Perry (The Whole Nine Yards) dumps his smart aleck Friends persona to play a smart aleck process server named Joe Tyler. He hates his job but sticks with it because... We don't know. The filmmakers don't tell us.

He gets a job to serve divorce papers to Sara Moore (Elizabeth Hurley, Bedazzled) who is clueless to the fact that her cow punching husband is a jerk. Is she just stupid or does she really love the guy? We don't know. The filmmakers don't tell us.

Figuring that she'd receive a better divorce settlement in the NY courts than the Texas courts, Sara promises to give Joe a million dollars to serve her husband first. When Joe accepts, his boss (Cedric The Entertainer, Big Momma's House) calls for Tony (Vincent Pastore, The Sopranos), a Brooklyn behemoth with bologna for brains, to serve Sara before Joe can serve Cowboy Gordon (Bruce Campbell, The Evil Dead). Whoever serves first wins.

The race is on and before it is over Sara's pants are ripped off, Tony makes a few dozen references to his Brooklynese demeanor, Cedric The Entertainer tries to entertain us with superfluous dancing, and Joe finds himself up to his armpit groping around for a bull's prostate. We haven't seen such hilarity since Say It Isn't So! (which I believe lasted a week or so in theaters last year).

Matthew Perry, whose work in Friends is exemplary, doesn't quite connect with his character Joe. It probably doesn't help that the script (by Spin City writers Jay Scherick and David Ronn) gives him precious little with which to work. He's got a nice 3 day old stubble on his face but we're not sure if it is because his character has forgotten to shave or if he's just trying to disguise himself.

There's no disguising Elizabeth Hurley or the fact that she really isn't much of an actress. She's certainly attractive and has a pleasant personality but doesn't communicate much beyond a smirk that serves to telegraph the laughs which never manage to come.

To be fair, the problem with Serving Sara isn't really with the actors. The blame for this lies with the director (Reginald Hudlin, The Ladies Man) and screenwriters. They've tried to make a formula comedy but have chosen to use ingredients that have only produced weak comedies in the past.

So, how might we use this filmgoing experience for good? We could look at the scriptures that communicate God's heart regarding marriage and divorce. That would be edifying but it doesn't really address much in this film. There's no godly marriage represented in Serving Sara.

We might point out that love of money is what drives most of the characters in this film but again, there is no counterpart to represent the godly alternative.

Perhaps we should concentrate on the legal actions at the heart of the film. Gordon wants to sue for divorce in Texas so he can avoid paying half of his estate to Sara. Sara opts to countersue in NY so she might get half his estate in the divorce proceedings. What does the Word of God say about it?

"And if any man will sue thee at the law, and take away thy coat, let him have thy cloak also." Matthew 5:40 (KJV)

We need not serve our fellow man with the threat of legal action when we could be serving God in love. The point of the referenced scripture is that material things are simply not worth disturbing the peace that we can have in our heart.

The main problem with Serving Sara is that there is no love being manifested by any of the characters. Without love, life doesn't make much sense. Neither does this film.
