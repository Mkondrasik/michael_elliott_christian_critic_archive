Author-verified transcription

Basic, a new military thriller/mystery throws out so many red herrings that the audience can be forgiven if they think they're in a bait shop rather than a theater. At least it explains that fishy smell coming from the plot.

John Travolta (Domestic Disturbance) stars as Tom Hardy, an ex-Army Ranger turned disgraced DEA agent, who has been summoned by an old friend to investigate the disappearance of a small team of Special Forces trainees. Most of those soldiers failed to return from their latest exercise which was being led by the now missing Sgt. Nathan West (Samuel L. Jackson, Changing Lanes). The ones who made it back are either dead or not talking.

Bringing in outside help is a thorn in the side and a slap in the face to the acting investigator, Capt. Julia Osborne (Connie Nielsen, The Hunted), who must reluctantly agree to assist the unconventional and decidedly nonmilitary Hardy. He eventually succeeds in getting the survivors to talk, but their stories don't match.

Digging deeper, Hardy and Osborne soon find themselves bogged down in a sea of fabrications, accusations, and bold faced lies. Who to believe? Who to arrest? Who to blame for roping them into this mess of a picture in the first place?

Director John McTiernan, who last directed the critically lambasted Rollerball, once again drops the ball here. Many of the scenes simply don't work as directed (case in point is the "fight/seduction scene" between Hardy and Osborne.)

The actors make valiant efforts to save this film from itself but the script by James Vanderbilt (Darkness Falls) ultimately proves too convoluted and twisted for even the charm of Travolta and the skills of Jackson to overcome. Danish actress Connie Nielsen is miscast as a Southern officer with pouty lips and an accent which defies geographic placement.

Also in the cast are Giovanni Ribisi (Heaven) as a quirky soldier, Taye Diggs (Chicago) as a black soldier, Brian Van Holt (Windtalkers) as a hunky soldier, Roselyn Sanchez (Rush Hour 2) as a female soldier, and Harry Connick Jr. (Hope Floats) as a medical soldier. I realize that these aren't much in the way of descriptions but I can only work with what I'm given. We don't really get to know much about any of these characters.

The cast is further hindered by the fact that most of the film takes place in the jungles of Panama during a hurricane. If there were gems of dialogue to be found in the script, they were lost to the sound of the rain and wind effects.

During a training exercise, Sgt. West had to drill into his recruits the importance of their greatest weapon - their brains. We often overlook the obvious, especially when the warfare that we are engaged in is a spiritual, unseen one. Our mind and what we choose to do with it can be the most important "weapon" in our arsenal.

For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds; Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ. 2 Corinthians 10:4-5 (KJV)

Armed with a sound mind and the power and love of God, we are fully equipped to handle any situation that may arise.
