It wasn’t all just a bad dream. Left Behind II: Tribulation Force picks up threads of the popular Christian story and continues to weave the tapestry of apocalyptic woe mixed with evangelical hope.

It has been a week since millions of people suddenly disappeared from the planet. A small band of newly born-again Christians have come to realize that the event is just one of many that were prophesied in the Bible. As the Scriptures state in 1 Thessalonians 4, Jesus Christ returned for his church and anyone who, at that time, had not yet believed on him were left behind on earth. Using this second chance to believe and serve God, the small group forms the Tribulation Force to warn and teach others the truth.

Meanwhile, UN president Nicolae Carpathia (Gordon Currie) increases in strength and power, offering the leadership that the world seeks during this confusing and emotionally tumultuous time. What no one in the world realizes, except for the Tribulation Force, is the fact that Carpathia is the prophesied antichrist whose world domination will not be the saving grace they desire.

In Part II, the relationship between ace television reporter Buck Williams (Kirk Cameron) and Chloe (Janaya Stephens) turns romantic. Both Buck and Rayford Steele (Brad Johnson) gain positions within Carpathia’s camp in order to obtain inside information. Buck is especially interested in two mystery men clad in Old Testament clothing who quote scripture and breathe fire upon any who attack them. They dwell by the Wailing Wall in Jerusalem which has been deemed off limits by Carpathia. Also in Jerusalem is Rabbi Tsion Ben Judah (Lubomir Mykytiuk, K-19: The Widowmaker) who is preparing to make a worldwide announcement of earthshaking proportions.

The flaws of Left Behind II: Tribulation Force are most noticeable when the film tries to depict the global impact of a worldwide crisis. As apocalypses go, this one is rather neat and orderly. However, the filmmakers are more successful when they narrows their scope to concentrate on the personal stories of the characters. In two of the most effective scenes, the spiritual conversions of a suicidal man and a skeptical woman are poignant and real.

Overall, Left Behind II has the look and feel of an average made-for-TV movie which is appropriate, since it is being released direct to video. This might be attributed to the project’s director, Bill Corcoran, whose filmography is thus far limited to TV series or mini-series shown on TV.

The main characters from the first film have returned for the sequel and have settled comfortably into their respective roles. Kirk Cameron still has his boyish looks which makes it difficult to accept him as a seasoned and well-respected news reporter but he’s earnest and affable as Buck. Gordon Currie’s accent seems to have gotten a bit thicker but he still conveys the oily duplicitousness of his character.

The events depicted in Left Behind II: Tribulation Force are at best speculation - the author’s private interpretation of the figurative language in the book of Revelation. We don’t know much about the details of what life will be like on earth once Christ returns for his church. God’s heart and desire is for nobody to be left behind. The focus of His Word is for people to come to an understanding of who He is and what He has made available for us.

This is good, and pleases God our Savior, who wants all men to be saved and to come to a knowledge of the truth. 1 Timothy 2:3-4 (KJV)

The reality, of course, is that many will choose not to believe and will experience something after the believers have been “gathered together” to join their savior in the air. The specific events of that time cannot be definitively known but one thing is certain. It will be a poor substitute for what the rest of us will be doing.
