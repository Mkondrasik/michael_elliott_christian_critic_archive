Chasing Liberty

Author-verified transcription

Life isn't easy for those in the Oval Office and I'm not referring to the President of the United States. In Chasing Liberty, the one with the problem is the president's eighteen year old daughter. She can't get any... privacy that is.

For a red-blooded American girl, there's no worse fate than to have a father who has the resources of the world's foremost superpower at his disposal. Even when a guy works up the courage to ask her out, it has to be a bit intimidating to strike up a casual conversation when twenty Secret Service agents are focused on every movement.

Anna Foster (Mandy Moore), codename: Liberty, decides to take matters into her own hands by slipping away one night in Prague while her father is on some diplomatic mission. Hopping on the back of a stranger's motor scooter, she manages to evade the pursuing protective detail and finds herself to be truly alone for the first time in fourteen years.

Except that she's not truly alone. She is in the company of the handsome stranger (Matthew Goode) who drove the getaway scooter. Little does she know that he is a Secret Service operative who happened to be in the right place at the right time. He has been instructed to stay with her and not reveal his true identity. Thus Anna will be protected without feeling restricted. What the president didn't consider is that these two young people might fall in love. But how will Anna feel when she learns the truth?

Chasing Liberty is a teen romance/comedy that is as derivative and formulaic as they come. The settings (Prague, Venice, and Berlin) are attractive, as is Ms. Moore, but there just isn't much else going on behind the pretty facade.

The most amusing sequences in the film belong to Jeremy Piven and Annabella Sciorra who, as Liberty's primary security detail, build a nice, easy chemistry between them. Their slow burning, "opposites attract" kind of romance is a nice counterpoint to Anna's headstrong approach to love.

Mark Harmon, who plays the president, makes a perfectly patently unremarkable figure. Caroline Goodall, who plays his wife, fares no better.

Mandy Moore does what she can to inject some energy into what appears to be a tired script and concept but after her character bounces from being pouty to flirtatious a few dozen times there's nothing left in her bag of tricks. All in all, Chasing Liberty is a momentary January distraction - a completely average piece of fluff.

Of course, the purpose for the Secret Service detail is not to ruin a teenaged girl's love life but to provide protection from those who would cause her harm. In her desire to be independent, she willfully steps outside of the "hedge of protection" which has been provided for her.

Living in the light of God's love offers us protection as well. Some may find it oppressive or restrictive to adhere to God's standard of truth. Others find themselves being tempted by the excitement or lure of a "forbidden fruit." Whatever the reason, stepping outside of God's "hedge of protection" makes one vulnerable to every kind of attack and pain while staying there provides safety.

Wherefore ye shall do my statutes, and keep my judgments, and do them; and ye shall dwell in the land in safety. And the land shall yield her fruit, and ye shall eat your fill, and dwell therein in safety. Leviticus 25:18-19 (KJV)
