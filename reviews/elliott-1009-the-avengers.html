Author-verified transcription

It is so easy to make a bad film based on a comic book character. So when a great film based on a comic book character comes along, it tends to stand out. The Avengers stands out above them all because it collects the greatest Marvel comic book heroes and puts them into a single story. And it does so flawlessly.

Loki (Tom Hiddleston), brother of Thor (Chris Hemsworth), comes to Earth with conquest on his mind. He has the army and the power to force the planet’s citizens to their knees. Nick Fury (Samuel L. Jackson) of S.H.I.E.L.D. has other plans. His plan of defense will require the world’s most unique individuals to unite as one to battle a common, unbeatable foe. Easier said than done.

Iron Man (Robert Downey Jr.), Captain America (Chris Evans), The Hulk (Mark Ruffalo), Thor (Chris Hemsworth), Black Widow (Scarlett Johansson), and Hawkeye (Jeremy Renner) form a reluctant force that is the world’s last chance for salvation. All they need to do is agree to work together. It is a decision not easily reached.

Director/screenwriter Joss Whedon has crafted one of the finest films of this genre in movie history… if not the finest. Mixing action with humor; character development with a strong story line, he has made a nearly two and a half hour film seem brief. No missteps, no weak performances, no cringe worthy special effects… The Avengers is everything one might want in a big budget, high profile, special effects extravaganza.

The individual lead characters are certainly impressive in their own right. Possessing super-human qualities and abilities, any one of them would be able to hold their own in a fair fight. But against an alien army with advanced technology and power? Not one of them has a chance of defeating the invaders.

That was exactly Nick Fury’s point. They didn’t have to stand alone. Collectively, they would be a stronger and more effective force able to combat a far superior adversary. This is a truth that directly relates to our spiritual situation. We do not have to face our spiritual adversary alone. We were called to be a part of something greater - a body of believers. Together, with God’s help, we can form a force our spiritual adversary cannot defeat.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up. Again, if two lie together, then they have heat: but how can one be warm alone? And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.” Ecclesiastes 4:9-12 (KJV)
