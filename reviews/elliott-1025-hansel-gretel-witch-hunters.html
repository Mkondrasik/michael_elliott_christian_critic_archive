Hansel & Gretel: Witch Hunters

Author-verified transcription

In the 19th century, the Brothers Grimm were originally criticized for their collection of children’s fairy tales as the content and subject matter were deemed to be unsuitable for little ears. The makers of Hansel & Gretel: Witch Hunters make no such mistake. This is an R-rated film and as such was never intended for children.

The film takes the Grimms’ fairy tale and uses it as its background story. It then carries the legend forward to the titular siblings’ adult years. Having defeated the cannibalistic witch in the candy house, the children (now orphaned) grow up to make their fame and fortune out of killing these evil and powerful creatures. Apparently there is no dearth of them in the German woods.

Called to the village Augsburg to help locate those responsible for the disappearance of a dozen small children, Hansel and Gretel uncover a plot involving a blood moon and a large assembly of witches that can only mean something very big and very bad is brewing on the horizon. Armed to the teeth, Hansel (Jeremy Renner) and Gretel (Gemma Arterton) vow to rid the countryside from the plague of these practitioners of the dark arts.

Hansel & Gretel: Witch Hunters is an exceedingly violent film with buckets of stylized gore and guts thrown up on the screen. Not to say there isn’t a fair amount of humor to be had. The ironic asides and one-liners abound. But the film is a fantasy action movie with a heavy-handed dose of both fantasy and action.

A young man who, having had followed the adventures of Hansel and Gretel throughout the years confesses to Gretel that his life ambition is to be a witch hunter like them when he grows up. She quickly sets the record straight by saying, “We did not choose this life.”

Hansel and Gretel, because of what happened to them as children, received a level of immunity to the witches’ spells thus making it easier for them to defeat them. Because of this they feel a sense of obligation to protect the world’s children from experiencing the evil they faced. To draw a biblical analogy to this unlikely fantasy tale, we only need look to how God empowers those who believe in Him. To believers, He grants the gift of holy spirit which contains within it nine unique and distinct manifestations including, among others, word of wisdom, word of knowledge and the discerning of spirits. Armed with this gift, believers have the ability to recognize and defend themselves against the devilish powers at work in this world.

Unlike Hansel and Gretel, we do get to choose this life. God has provided us free will ability. First, He allows us to choose to accept His gift of holy spirit; and second, we can choose to manifest its power in this world. But having accepted it, there is a sense of responsibility that is inferred. Those who receive holy spirit are God’s ambassadors in this world and the ones to whom the word of reconciliation is entrusted. We have the privilege and divine responsibility to do the works of Jesus Christ.
