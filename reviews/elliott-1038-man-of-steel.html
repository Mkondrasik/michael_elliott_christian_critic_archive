Author-verified transcription

The oft-told story of Superman has long been used as an allegory for God’s plan of redemption embodied by Jesus Christ. The most recent cinematic example of its telling (Man of Steel) allows us to once again make that obvious connection.

After visiting Krypton to see how Jor-El (Russell Crowe) manages to save his newborn son from the fate of his doomed planet, the story moves to Earth where the infant, now a boy, is discovering that he is not like other kids. Thankfully, he has a kind and supportive father figure in Jonathan Kent (Kevin Costner) who helps him through the unusual growing pains unique to his situation.

Jumping ahead a number of years we find the young man drifting through life - not yet sure of his purpose or role. The surprising arrival of another survivor from his home planet forces him to choose which direction his life will take.

Director Zack Snyder has thrown a lot of frenetic action upon the screen and has made some different choices that manage to put an unexpected spin on a familiar tale. Henry Cavill, though he definitely looks the part, is not as charismatic as others who have donned the cape. Amy Adams, as Lois, is less love interest than confidante although there is an emotional undercurrent that begins to surface as the film progresses. Michael Shannon plays the villainous Commander Zod bent on recovering the essence of Krypton that resides within the DNA of the Man of Steel.

We learn, from Jor-El, the meaning of the big “S” that adorns Superman’s costume. It is not an “S” at all but rather a symbol that, on Krypton, means hope. That is the underlying theme of the Superman story – hope for the continuation of Krypton’s race and hope for the salvation of those who live on Earth because of a champion who will protect them from harm.

Jesus Christ is our Superman. He is our only hope for salvation. He is the one remaining link we have to a presence that was separated from us. Without Christ, we have no hope; no possibility for redemption or salvation; no protection from those whose only goals are to steal, kill, and destroy.

“[Remember] that you were at that time separated (living apart) from Christ [excluded from all part in Him], utterly estranged and outlawed from the rights of Israel as a nation, and strangers with no share in the sacred compacts of the [Messianic] promise [with no knowledge of or right in God's agreements, His covenants]. And you had no hope (no promise); you were in the world without God.” Ephesians 2:12 (Amplified)
