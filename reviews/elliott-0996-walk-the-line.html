Author-verified transcription

Walk the Line is a biopic of country singer Johnny Cash and tells an all too familiar story of a rising star with a troubled childhood; the many pitfalls that are found along the road to success; and the deliverance of a redeeming love. It is the latter aspect accompanied by two near flawless performances that lifts the film above the mundane.

Similar in structure to Ray, last year's Oscar-nominated biography, Walk the Line takes us inside the life of a musical legend to show us the man. Joaquin Phoenix nails the persona of the "man in black," and strips him bare to show us both his strengths and his weaknesses.

After a brief prologue as Cash waits "backstage" before his landmark concert in Folsom Prison, the movie flashes back to his Arkansas childhood, moves to his time spent in Germany with the army (where he taught himself how to play the guitar), then back to Nashville where he married his first wife and eventually got the courage to audition for Sam Phillips of Sun Records.

From there, the film tracks Cash's rising star, his fall into addiction and his long climb out of it. But nothing is more important to the story or to Cash's life than the relationship he had with June Carter (Reese Witherspoon), his partner, his best friend, and his deepest love.

Like her costar, Witherspoon is brilliant in the film, showing both sides of Carter's personality: the bubbly, vivacious public image and the quiet strength and rock-solid foundation of a woman of faith. While Joaquin Phoenix gives the film its legs, Reese Witherspoon provides its heart. Watching them connect onscreen is pure joy.

As befitting a story about a musical icon, the soundtrack is priceless. T-Bone Burnett (O Brother Where Art Thou?) worked on the score and worked with Phoenix and Witherspoon to enable them to capture the vocal essence of Cash and Carter. All succeeded marvelously and the musical sequences provide highlights too numerous to count.

Director James Mangold keeps a steady hand on the camera and wisely gives his actors plenty of space. The end result is a smart and poignant homage to a musician who at one time outsold the Beatles, toured with Elvis Presley and Jerry Lee Lewis, and was the youngest person ever chosen for the Country Music Hall of Fame and the first to be selected to be included in both the Country and Rock Music Hall of Fame.

He was also a man all too familiar with the temptations of this world and the spiritual darkness associated with them. He very well could have been lost forever in the darkness had it not been for the love and friendship of June Carter.

To paraphrase Jack Nicholson's character in As Good As It Gets, "She made him want to be a better man."

We too have someone who recognizes that we aren't perfect; that we sometimes succumb to the temptations of this world; but who loves us despite it all. That love can give us the strength and conviction to "walk the line," keeping us on the straight path of righteousness. The love of God will lift us out of the bleakest and darkest depths and into His glorious light if we but accept and respond to His love.

For we ourselves also were sometimes foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another. But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared. Titus 3:3-4 (KJV)
