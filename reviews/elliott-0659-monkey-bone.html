Author-verified transcription

The sexual innuendoes run wild... kind of like a pack of wild monkeys... in this visually adventurous but narratively inane story by the director of The Nightmare Before Christmas and the screenwriter for Batman. Both of those popular film hits had Tim Burton in common and the absence of his name on the credits of Monkey Bone may explain why this film lacks the ingenuity and fresh originality of the others.

Brendan Fraser (The Mummy) stars as Stu Miley, a recalcitrant cartoonist uncomfortable with his success. Monkey Bone, a character which he drew as part of his nightmare therapy, has become a cultural icon. As merchandising prospects begin to surround him, all Stu can think about is proposing to the girl of his dreams, Julie (Bridget Fonda, Single White Female). Before he can, he is severely injured in a freak auto accident.

As he lies comatose in a hospital bed, with faithful Julie at his side, his thoughts are transported to a nightmarish carnival-like world called Downtown. Fueled by the nightmares of those living, the Downtown denizens have long been fed by Stu's fertile imagination, the "figments" of which now live here among other bizarre and frightful looking monsters derived from other nightmares. Monkey Bone is also present and, as voiced by John Turturro (O Brother Where Art Thou?) is as annoying as any sex-crazed, foul-mouthed monkey could be.

All Stu wants is one last opportunity to tell Julie that he loves her. All Monkey Bone wants is to stop being a figment and become flesh. And since he figures that Stu, being in a coma, isn't using his body right now, there would be no harm in his taking it for a spin. Stu must face Death (played with annoying nonchalance by Whoopi Goldberg of Hollywood Squares) in order to regain his girl, his body, and his life... although not necessarily in that order.

Brendan Fraser has a history of playing offbeat, off-centered characters and Stu, the man behind the monkey, is no exception. At first shy, reserved, and haunted by intensely perverse dreams, he later gets an opportunity to play the polar opposite as "Monkey Bone" takes possession of his body and Stu becomes loud, crude, and obnoxious.

Bridget Fonda looks beautiful in the thankless and senseless role of Julie, the kind-hearted girlfriend who just happens to specialize in a radical form of sleep therapy. She, inconceivably, is slow to notice Stu's all-too-noticeable simian ways.

Chris Kattan (Saturday Night Live), in a bizarre sequence, plays an organ-tossing corpse whose body Stu is forced to use in order to stop his monkey from ruining what's left of his life.

Director Henry Selick uses a blend of makeup, costuming, and stop action animation to manufacture the surreal environment in which the story takes place. Surreal is an understatement. Downtown is a fantasy world set in Stu's warped mind, therefore there are no boundaries or limitations which must be obeyed and Selick demonstrates his inventiveness in the types of creatures introduced on screen. But their uniqueness is only of passing interest as the story quickly becomes tiresome, failing to draw us in.

We can certainly sympathize with Stu's problem as most of us have had nightmares of one form or another. Perhaps ours have not been as outlandish as his, but nevertheless there is no mystery as to their origin. Fear. We need not tolerate our sleep being disturbed because of some abiding fear that continues to reside in our subconscious mind. God promises us that we can drive fear out of our lives by trusting in His perfect love.

Be not afraid of sudden fear, neither of the desolation of the wicked, when it cometh. For the LORD shall be thy confidence, and shall keep thy foot from being taken. Proverbs 3:24-26 (KJV)

A sweet sleep is a worthy and attainable goal. What's more, it is a promise from God to those whom He loves.
