American Gangster

Author-verified transcription

Within the first ten seconds of American Gangster, we know exactly what kind of man Frank Lucas is as we watch him coldly torture and murder a man. Within the next ten minutes we discover what kind of a man Detective Ritchie Roberts is as we watch him turn in a million dollars in unmarked bills that he easily could have pocketed.

But over the two and a half hours of American Gangster's running time we are taught much more about these men who might be described as being the opposite sides of the same coin. Based on a true story, American Gangster focuses upon a pair of men who chose different paths which were destined one day to cross.

Lucas (Denzel Washington), as coldhearted as he may be, is the consummate family man. Schooled in the Italian way of doing shady business (family first), he keeps a low but profitable profile as a heroin kingpin. He is ruthless and unforgiving in his business dealings but softens whenever his family is around.

Roberts (Russell Crowe), as ethical as the day is long, ignores his parental and spousal responsibilities to the point that his marriage is ruined. He may be ruthless and relentless in his police work but he is apathetically impotent in his personal relationships.

These are two men - both with strong positive values; both with seriously flawed perspectives. We can simultaneously admire and despise qualities in both characters.

Director Ridley Scott films American Gangster in a dark, shadowy, gritty style befitting of the dirty nature of the world his two lead characters inhabit. There is nothing glamorous about drug trafficking and drug addiction and Scott captures this ugliness and puts it on the screen.

Washington is intense and controlled as Lucas. His good guy persona helps make this horrific killer and importer of drugs somewhat tolerable so we might see a glimmer of humanity behind his inhumane ways. Crowe explores the depth of his character and fully brings him to life, showing the character's flaws as clearly as he does the positive traits. Because of their celebrity status, it is difficult to say that Crowe and Washington "disappear" into their characters but they do serve the characters well and don't distract from the telling of an interesting story.

The rest of the cast perform admirably in an ensemble spirit (meaning that no one draws undue attention or stands apart from the crowd). They are simply there to serve the movie and they do it well.

My only criticism of the film is one of endurance. Two and a half hours seems a bit overlong to tell a story that is essentially about cops and robbers. There are scenes that are stellar but there are some that are not so celestial. The most disappointing element is that the lack of interplay between the two stars. It is only when they have their one big scene together that we realize what we've been missing all along.

Lucas and Roberts both have qualities to admire. But doing some things right does not excuse that which is done wrong. Lucas can love his mother but kill complete strangers... Roberts may work heartily unto the Lord at his jobs but he is hardly working at his marriage. God is not fooled nor swayed by our rationalizations. His standard of truth and goodness is set for us to follow always and in all situations... not just whenever we choose.

For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all. James 2:10 (KJV)
