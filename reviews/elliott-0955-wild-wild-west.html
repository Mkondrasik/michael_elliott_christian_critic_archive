Wild Wild West

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 2, 1999
Rating
2 stars

1 hr: 46 min

Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Trust

Author-verified transcription

One thing to remember about fireworks is that there is an ever present possibility of a dud. Nothing quite dampens a festive holiday mood like a rocket that doesn't flare or a sparkler that sputters. This Fourth of July's biggest dud may just have been found inside a theater. Wild Wild West, featuring the Heavyweight King of the Fourth, Will Smith, fails to generate even a spark, much less an explosion.

After starring in Independence Day and Men in Black, both of which opened during a fourth of July weekend, Will Smith became firecracker hot. News of him re-teaming with his Men in Black director, Barry Sonnenfeld, sent expectation levels for this project skyrocketing. But as they say in the rocket biz, "That which goes up, must eventually come crashing to earth." There must be a giant-sized crater over at Warner Brothers where the remains of any Wild Wild West expectations can be found.

Based on the popular TV show which aired on CCC from 1965-1969, the movie retains none of the charm of the small screen original, despite the presence of two of the most appealing actors working in films today.

Mr. Smith is James West, a shoot first, shoot second, shoot 'em again, then ask questions once they're dead" federal agent. Kevin Kline (A Midsummer Night's Dream) plays his "sidekick," US Marshal Artemus Gordon, a master of disguise and inventor extraordinaire. Together they team up in an attempt to save the United States from the evil intentions of Dr. Arliss Loveless (Kenneth Branagh, Henry V), a leg less, egomaniacal son of the South who plans to do what the Confederate Army could not... demand from President Ulysses S. Grant the unconditional surrender of these United States. Having kidnapped the world's greatest scientists, Loveless has succeeded in building the most destructive weapon the world has ever known. The only thing that might conceivably stop it is a shoe the size of Manhattan.

Salma Hayek's (The Faculty) cleavage makes an appearance, bringing the rest of the actress along for the ride (unnecessarily, it seems... she's given little to do and no real reason for being there.)

Kenneth Branagh, in a beard that looks like a 19th century monochromatic rendering of Darth Maul's makeup, chews more than his share of the Western scenery as the wheelchair bound Dr. Loveless, but he is more laughable than menacing.

Mr. Sonnenfeld wastes the recognizable talents of his accomplished cast, relying too heavily on anachronistic special effects to interest his audience. I, for one, would gladly have traded an eighty foot mechanical spider for some witty dialogue or an enjoyable interchange between interesting characters. Alas, it was not to be.

While the TV version enjoyed its share of gadgetry, its success was not dependent upon them. The movie which shares its name is such a pale imitation that it could have been subtitled, "How The West Was Wan." If Mr. Smith looked bored in the non-action sequences, it was nothing compared to how we felt watching them.

Of course, all of this is a disappointment... Especially to those of us who fondly remember the original series. Wild Wild West (the movie) was a heavily hyped project, whetting our appetites for an enjoyable skip down memory lane that instead turned into a marching bore.

No doubt, disappointment is something that we've all experienced. Man and the promises of man oftentimes fail to meet our expectations. All the more reason for us to be thankful we can put our trust in a God Who does not disappoint.

Scriptures say:

It is better to trust in the LORD than to put confidence in man. Psalms 118:8 [KJV]

God promises that He will do more than we could ever ask or think. The higher our expectations, the more He will do. And He never disappoints those who put their trust in Him. He may not be making movies, but one couldn't ask for or find a better director for one's life.
