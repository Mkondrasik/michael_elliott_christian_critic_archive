Evan Almighty

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 22, 2007
Rating
2½ stars

Original Author Copy

Author-verified transcription

In Evan Almighty, when newly elected congressman Evan Baxter (Steve Carrell) came up with the campaign slogan “Change the World,” he had no idea that anyone would take him literally. But God did. And when Evan won, God (Morgan Freeman) expected him to deliver.

When the not-so-subtle hints (references to Genesis 6:14 start appearing everywhere) don’t work, God bluntly tells Evan “I want you to build an ark.” It’s not exactly how Evan planned to spend his freshman year in congress.

After trying everything to avoid the inevitable, Evan begins his divine task, suffers through a physical transformation that has him looking the part of a modern-day Moses and endures same the ridicule that surely his predecessor faced thousands of years before him.

In this sequel to Bruce Almighty, screenwriter Steve Oedekerk and director Tom Shadyac have crafted a gentler, sweeter, and more family friendly PG comedy about an ordinary man called by God to do an extraordinary thing.

Steve Carrell sets the tone early by establishing Evan as a sweet and rather simple man already out of his depth. From there, the comedy builds as Evan gets deeper and deeper into uncharted waters until the only thing he can do is build the ark just to stay afloat in his own life. He plays the character with a resigned air that slowly builds to a faith born of conviction.

Morgan Freeman reprises his role of God with a light and confident touch. Lauren Graham plays Evan’s confused and sometimes supportive wife. John Goodman plays the heavy - a shady congressman who is trying to bull rush a piece of legislation into law. Wanda Sykes, John Michael Higgins and Molly Shannon all have minor roles that don’t make full use of their talents. There are scenes that don’t quite work and, of course, the believability factor is a bit on the low side. But Shadyac and company succeed in presenting a light and funny comedy for all ages that neither insults the intelligence nor offends the senses.

They do so with a level of sensitivity to faith that the movie industry often overlooks. When a character sneers at Evan, “Why would God choose you?” Evan smartly responds, “He chose all of us.” When God suddenly appears in the back seat of Evan’s car, Evan screams in fright. God humorously quotes himself, “Let it out son. It is the beginning of wisdom.” And there’s a nicely played scene between God and Evan’s wife regarding how prayers are answered that is fodder for some serious thought.

It is Evan himself that gives us the most important spiritual lesson. Even after he recognizes what God wants from him, he tries to ignore it because it isn’t convenient to his plans (a comment that God finds quite funny). What we all should know is that our plans, when they run contrary to God’s, aren’t worth carrying out.

O LORD, I know that the way of man is not in himself: it is not in man that walketh to direct his steps. Jeremiah 10:23 (KJV)

Our greatest successes come when we align our steps with the path that God has laid in front of us. Trying to blaze our own way simply leads us deeper into the maze of confusion that we find in this world. God never forces us to do things His way. He just knows that we’d have an easier time of it if we would stop resisting Him and accept his guidance and direction.
