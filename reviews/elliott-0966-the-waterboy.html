The Waterboy

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 6, 1998
Rating
1 star

1 hr: 30 min

Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Longsuits; Talents

Author-verified transcription

The Waterboy, a new movie by the team of Adam Sandler, Frank Coraci, and Tim Herlihy, proves one thing... their earlier collaboration (The Wedding Singer) was, as most of us suspected, a fluke. That movie was delightful. This movie is full of something, but it sure isn't delight.

Adam Sandler (Happy Gilmore) again plays a nebbish... Bobby Boucher (pronounced "boo-shay"), a stuttering Louisiana boob so attached to his Mama that he makes Norman Bates look almost normal. Using a lawn mower as his primary means of transportation, he commutes to a nearby college where he has a job as the official waterboy for the football team.

They, of course, hate him and taunt him mercilessly. After being fired from his job for being a distraction (it seems the team was spending more time playing jokes on him than playing football), he becomes the waterboy for the pitiful SCLSU Muddogs, a team that has lost its last forty games.

Hapless Coach Klein (Henry Winkler, TV's Happy Days) discovers in Bobby a hidden talent. The boy is a tackling dummy. That is to say, this dummy can tackle. Quick as a drop kick, Boucher is in uniform and the Muddogs begin a winning streak.

Problem is, Mama don't like this "foosball" game. She believes that it's from the "debil." Then again, she thinks girls and schooling are also from the "debil." After seeing a tattoo of Roy Orbison on his coach's rear end, Boucher gets the inspiration to defy Mama and play in the "debil's" game.

Really, the movie is that insipid. Adam Sandler reverts to his annoying Saturday Night Live form, drawing out one of his sketch characters to an excruciating hour and a half.

Oscar winner Kathy Bates (Primary Colors) proves that talent alone does not ensure quality. Perhaps she lost a bet to accept this role of Mama Boucher. Either that, or the debil made her do it.

Henry Winkler, Jerry Reed (Smokey and the Bandit), and Fairuza Balk (American History X) all turn in average performances. The most amusing character on screen is the assistant coach, Farmer Fran, played by Blake Clark (Alone in the Woods) who makes the Cajun accent completely unintelligible. But his is a one joke character and not deserving of much screen time.

Director Frank Coraci shows none of the control or mastery of the subject matter which he demonstrated in The Wedding Singer. Here, he allows his actors to play as broad as they choose, with less than satisfying results. The movie does have a few moments of simple fun and the football sequences, although unbelievable, are enjoyable enough to watch.

Bobby's ability to contribute to the success of the Muddogs due to his tackling skills could remind us that we all have certain long suits or talents that can be used for God's purposes.

For the body is not one member, but many. Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular. 1 Corinthians 12:14 & 27 [KJV]

In a human body, the foot cannot do the things that a hand can do. The eye cannot do the work of the finger. But as our bodies function in perfect unity: foot, hand, eye, finger...all working together, we can accomplish great feats. The human body, when all of its parts are healthy and operating normally, is unparalleled in its versatility, adaptability and usability.

Likewise, in the body of Christ where we are the individual parts, each of us have particular skills and abilities... long suits in which we excel. Alone, we may not be able to do much against the forces of this world. As we work together, in like-minded accord, there is nothing we cannot accomplish; no obstacle we cannot overcome.

But our long suits do not benefit anyone unless we utilize them. Unless we share them with others. The body of Christ needs every member to be strong and functioning. The Muddogs were losing every game with Bobby on the sidelines. It wasn't until he started participating that the team began to see victory.

Are you using your talents and abilities for the glory of God? Or do you insist on hiding your light under a bushel where it benefits no one?
