Author-verified transcription

Sandra Bullock and Melissa McCarthy bring the heat in The Heat, a profanity-laden cop comedy that is the cinematic equivalent of the proverbial bull in a china shop.

Bullock plays Ashburn, a buttoned up FBI agent who is extremely intuitive, highly intelligent, supremely confident, and therefore universally disliked among her peers. When a new drug lord appears on the scene in Boston, it is Ashburn who lands the assignment to track him and bring him to justice. She is paired with local cop Mullins (Melissa McCarthy), a foul-mouthed, overbearing force of nature feared by everyone who crosses her path: criminals, fellow officers, and her own family included.

The film pretty much consists of the relationship that forms between Ashburn and Mullins. It is not as much a personality “clash” as it is a personality head butt or kick to the groin as these polar opposites collide to form a team. Bullock and McCarthy are fully committed to their characters and, to their credit, manage to elicit genuine warmth despite the shock humor and crudity of the characters.

It is said that iron sharpens iron (Proverbs 27:17). The opposite is also true. We can be impacted or affected by those with whom we associate. The effect can either be positive or negative as the traits, attitudes, and mindsets of those close to us begin to infiltrate our own thinking.

In The Heat, the politically correct Ashburn is influenced by the inappropriately coarse loose cannon called Mullins, changing both her behavior and demeanor as the film progresses. The spiritual point to be made is that we should always be aware of those whom we allow to influence us and ask ourselves if it is a change to be desired?

“Do not be so deceived and misled! Evil companionships (communion, associations) corrupt and deprave good manners and morals and character.”1 Corinthians 15:33 (Amplified)

Because we live and work in this world, we will meet, associate with, and often form relationships with those who may not have the same spiritual knowledge or understanding. We must guard ourselves that their secular perceptions and viewpoints do not negatively affect the spiritual relationship with have with our God and Father who, through His loving guidance, has already given us instruction on how we are to conduct ourselves as ambassadors for Christ.
