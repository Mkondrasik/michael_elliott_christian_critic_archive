Author-verified transcription

Swartzenegger's done it. So has Van Damme. Why not Jet Li? His new film, The One, is a futuristic adventure set amidst parallel universes. It gives the martial arts star the unusual opportunity to play both the good and bad guy.

In a strange multiple-universe (abbreviated "multiverse,") reality, every person exists simultaneously on different planes with all "versions" of a person somehow sharing the same life energy. Travel between universes, though painful, is possible. It is also highly restricted, policed by Multiverse Agents such as Gabriel Yulaw (Jet Li, Romeo Must Die). In one such universe, a Yulaw discovers that should his counterpart die in a different universe, the life energy of the deceased is distributed among those versions remaining, making them stronger, faster, smarter, and in the case of this particular Yulaw, more lethal.

Yulaw starts visiting other universes solely for the purpose of killing his counterparts. His aim is to knock them all off, making himself "The One," and presumably the godlike recipient of all available Yulaw energy. After murdering himself 123 times in different universes, there now remains only two of him left in the entire multiverse: Yulaw, a superhuman killer; and Gabe, a hardworking cop who doesn't quite understand why he has also been getting more powerful. Gabe belongs to a universe which has not yet grasped the shared multiverse concept.

All of this is window dressing for a film which consists of little more than excuses for the indulgences of explosive experts, special effects artists, and martial arts choreographers.

Jet Li can kick and punch with the best of them and as long as he restricts himself to those activities, the film is a rush of martial arts adrenaline. It is when he begins acting that the film starts to wallow a bit.

Supporting Li in his dual role is Carla Gugino (Spy Kids) as the good Gabe's loving wife; Delroy Lindo (The Last Castle) as the veteran MV agent assigned to capture the bad Yulaw and banish him to the Hades penal colony universe; and Jason Statham (Snatch) as Lindo's headstrong and trigger-happy partner.

The One starts with a promising sci-fi premise that, in other hands, could potentially have lived up to its title. Instead, it might as well be called Another One, joining, as it does, a growing list of all-too-average cheeseball action films. Boasting a sky-high body count, testosterone level and ammunition budget, The One culminates in a predictable "dueling Jets" scene as Yulaw and Gabe (using completely different fighting styles) face each other thanks to the marvels of computer technology.

From Dracula to Highlander, the concept of gaining power by taking life from another has actually been around in movie form for decades. It is, of course, a perversion of the reality we see in Christ. Unlike these fictional accounts, the Christian believer is truly able to manifest the power of "the One." Not because life was taken, but because it was given.

There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus. Galatians 3:28 (KJV)

We do not attain "oneness" by eliminating everyone else. We attain it by joining others in Christ in a demonstration of like-minded unity. We need not take the life force from others to grow stronger, we are stronger when we band together, our hearts knit in love. What we come to learn is that true spiritual power, which is the only lasting power there is, cannot be taken. It cannot be bought or stolen. It is simply a gift from God. A gift which is freely available to all who believe.
