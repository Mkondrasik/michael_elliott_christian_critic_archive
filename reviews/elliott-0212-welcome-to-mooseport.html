Welcome to Mooseport

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 20, 2004
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Mark 12:301 John 4:18Mark 12:33

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Love; commitment; fear; relationships; wholehearted devotion

Author-verified transcription

Welcome to Mooseport is a pleasant if uninspired comedy about a former US president who retires to a small town and is asked to run for mayor. Little does he know that it will prove to be the toughest political campaign he's ever mounted.

The ex-president is Monroe Eagle Cole (Gene Hackman), one of the most popular presidents ever to have graced the office. His life after his two terms looks to be just as rosy with hefty appearance fees, book deals, and the commissioning of his presidential library all waiting in the wings. The one downside continues to be the constant irritation of having an ex-wife who is being aggressively vindictive regarding the division of their assets.

Having already lost his estate in Baltimore to her, he's looking to hold onto his summer place in Mooseport. He knows that if it is serving as the mayoral home, she won't be able to touch it and so he agrees to accept the city council's offer to serve as the town's mayor.

He thought he would be running unopposed but he makes the mistake of flirting with local vet Sally Mannis (Maura Tierney) who, for the past six years has been dating hardware store owner Handy Harrison (Ray Romano). When she accepts a date with the ex-president, Handy enters the mayoral race and, because he's a nice guy and popular resident, he manages to tie Cole in an early election poll.

The race immediately becomes something of a national joke and Cole's lucrative post-presidential offers begin to dwindle which forces him to campaign seriously lest he lose and become the biggest political laughingstock since Dan Quayle.

Director Donald Petrie and screenwriter Tom Schulman don't break any new ground here. They keep the film rather simple, playing to the strengths of their leads. Ray Romano plays a derivative of his TV character. As Handy, he portrays a decent, ethical guy whose major flaw appears to be a fear of commitment. Gene Hackman plays Cole as a man accustomed to getting his own way and is flustered when it doesn't happen. The women behind the men, Maura Tierney as Handy's patient girlfriend and Marcia Gay Harden as Cole's resourceful assistant, play the more astute, mature and intelligent characters.

It is Handy's hesitation to commit to his relationship with Sally that almost costs him his girl. His fear of commitment or rejection denies him the joy and pleasure that comes from giving one's whole self to another.

God designed us to love. When we do, it should not be halfhearted or reserved. When we fail to commit due to some irrational fear, we are actually failing to love. Scriptures state that perfect love casts out fear and he that fears is not made perfect in love.

And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment. Mark 12:30 (KJV)

We would not consider withholding any part of our heart, soul, mind, or strength from our God. Our fellowship with Him is a full-sharing, committed relationship. Our unions on earth can be as equally strong.
