Author-verified transcription

I’ll say this much for Little Nicky: It provides us with a taste of what some think hell might be like. Anyone wanting to experience eternal damnation need only try to sit through this cinematic bore of a movie. Seriously though, the film’s visual rendition of Hades is based more in fantasy than truth. In fact, Little Nicky has so many spiritual errors contained in its absurd premise, not least of which is the depiction of Satan’s domain, it isn’t worth counting them.

Adam Sandler (The Waterboy) stars as the title character, a nebbish troll of a subhuman with a crooked jaw, bad hair, and an affection for heavy metal music. If that weren’t enough, his father just happens to be ruler of the underworld. Harvey Keitel (U-571) plays the devil who has ruled for 10,000 years and is about to name which of his sons will next sit on his throne.

His surprising announcement enrages his two other sons, Adrian (Rhys Ifans, The Replacements) and Cassius (Tom “Tiny” Lister Jr., Next Friday), who decide to go to earth to wreak havoc and establish a literal hell on earth. Their actions upset the balance between good and evil and freeze the fiery gates of hell, preventing any more souls from entering. Unless Nicky can go to earth, and restore the balance by forcing his brothers back to hell where they belong, the devil will cease to exist and the gates to hell will remain forever closed. This, we are told, would be a bad thing. After all, it’s his dad.

Nicky is aided in his earthly quest by an effeminate roommate (Allen Covert, Big Daddy), two hard-rockin’ Satanists, and a talking bulldog named Mr. Beefy. He also falls in love with a sweet but slightly odd New Yorker named Valerie (Patricia Arquette, Stigmata.)

Perhaps recognizing this ill-conceived premise needed all the help it could get, Director Steven Brill (Heavyweights) has loaded the film with appearances by Saturday Night Live vets Kevin Nealon, Jon Lovitz, Dana Carvey, Ellen Cleghorne, Robert Smigel, Rob Schneider, Michael McKean, and David Spade. The late Chris Farley’s name is even used as a joke in the closing credits. All to no avail. Little Nicky is unfunny as hell.

In addition to the SNL cast members, a few other well known faces can be seen in cameo roles. These include Rodney Dangerfield, Ozzy Osbourne, Henry Winkler, the Harlem Globetrotters, Dan Marino, Quentin Tarantino, and Reese Witherspoon.

The filmmakers could have brought in every award winning actor this side of the River Styx, they still would be left with the fact that Sandler’s character is gratingly obnoxious, the humor is crude and clumsy, and the story is beyond ridiculous. Whether it is Hitler, dressed like a French maid, receiving his hellish punishment (which can only be described here as a pineapple goose) or a Valley girl angel describing Lucifer as a ‘hottie,’ the humor is forced and most likely offensive to those of strong spiritual beliefs.

The balance between good and evil is simply not up to Satan and his devil spirits to modulate. The spiritual evil that does exist is all bad. There is no balance as far as Satan is concerned. In contrast, the spiritual, godly force is all good. These two forces are at war. They aim not to strike a balance, but instead strive to prevail over the other.

Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness? 2 Corinthians 6:14 (KJV)

God and the devil are not two sides of the same coin. They are completely different coins and we are called to make a clear and decisive choice.

Much too much of what we visualize in our minds when we think of heaven and hell comes from the erroneous depiction found in the religious art of the Renaissance. In our day, the art which influences thought is largely found in the movies we see. Judging from the example of Little Nicky, deception, be it deliberate or not, continues. The Scriptures are the source of all truth and godly knowledge and as such must be our only guide in spiritual matters.
