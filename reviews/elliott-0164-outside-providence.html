Outside Providence

Author-verified transcription

Providence: 1) A city in Rhode Island; 2) Divine direction; 3) Prudent management. Even when taking into consideration all three possible definitions, the new cinematic offering from Miramax, Outside Providence, is aptly named. Providence has little to do with this film.

Set in Pawtucket, a small 'burb in Rhode Island, just outside of Providence, during the mid 1970's, the film follows the antics of Timothy "Dunph" Dunphy (Shawn Hatosy, The Faculty), nicknamed "Dildo" by his father. He and his underachieving friends do little else except get stoned or drunk and talk about sex while busting each others... well... no need to get graphic.

With illicit smoke, Dunph rear ends a parked police car which prompts a decision that could change the direction of Dunph's life. His blue collar father (Alec Baldwin, Mercury Rising) decides to send him away to "prep school" removing him from the influences of his small town and small-minded friends.

What Dunph discovers is that underneath the oxford shirts and blue blazers, these "preppies" are just as messed up as the friends he left behind in Rhode Island. With one exception... the beautiful and intelligent Jane Weston (Amy Smart, Varsity Blues) who inexplicably begins to date Dunph.

Outside Providence is based on the first and quasi autobiographical novel of Peter Farrelly (There's Something About Mary) who co-wrote the screenplay adaptation with his brother Bobby Farrelly and director Michael Corrente (American Buffalo).

The script has definite earmarks of being the first product of a burgeoning collegiate intellect, like it was something that needed to be written in order to clear the author's mind so that other works could be produced. Generally, such writings are best consigned to the bottom of a forgotten file drawer. This rambling, coming-of-age story has no focus or direction, is heavily laden with scenes of alcohol and drug abuse with apparently no point to them, fails to build our interest in any of the characters or their relationships, and already seems dated.

Outside Providence tries to utilize the same type of humor that was so successful in the Farrelly brothers' earlier work, There's Something About Mary, but here it falls flat, barely registering a chuckle. Dunph has a one-eyed, three legged dog that isn't allowed in the house, a crippled brother who performs his paper route duties in his wheelchair which is being towed, and a prep school roommate who becomes the victim of a cruel prank involving masturbation.

The only actor whose characterization rises above the material is Alec Baldwin's foul-mouthed, slovenly depiction of Dunph's working-class father. Not that it is particularly pleasant to watch, but at least there's something beneath the surface that gives the character a little depth. That cannot be said of the array of ciphers that share the screen.

Dunph's father is set up, rightly so, to be a pivotal figure in Dunph's life... albeit not always in a positive light. A single parent, (his wife died when Dunph was quite young), he was obviously ill-equipped to raise his two boys with anything resembling maternal care. As he puts it, "It's hard to be Ozzie when you ain't got a Harriet."

Still, handing out dope-slaps and embarrassing nicknames while generally setting a poor example for his boys to emulate is not made excusable by the lack of a woman's influence.

"And ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath; but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord." Ephesians 6:4 [KJV]

The impact a father has upon his children is immeasurable. Whether that impact will be a positive one or a negative one is entirely up to the individual. To ensure a positive impact, it is a wise father who chooses to stay inside the protective bounds of providence.
