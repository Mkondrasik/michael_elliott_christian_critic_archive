Undertow

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 22, 2004
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 47 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Envy; Competition; Brotherhood

Author-verified transcription

If you thought your relatives have problems, you've got nothing on the family at the center of the new drama Undertow by independent film director David Gordon Green (George Washington).

Father (and widower) John Munn (Dermot Mulroney) has his hands full as a dirt poor pig farmer living in rural Georgia with his two boys. Chris (Jamie Bell) is a teenager with a wild streak who is constantly getting in trouble with the law. Tim (Devon Alan) is his younger brother with an unexplained medical condition that leaves him weak and unable to eat much of anything except for the mud and paint that he can't stop himself from putting in his mouth.

When John's brother Deel (Josh Lucas) arrives home from prison, he is hesitantly welcomed into the Munn family homestead although it is obvious that tensions remain between the brothers. When those tensions increase to the violence that will inevitably come, the two young boys must flee for their lives by escaping into the Georgia woods and with little more than the raggedy clothes on their backs and a sock full of gold coins which is all Uncle Deel wanted in the first place.

Chris and Tim wander from place to place just trying to keep a step ahead of the homicidal Deel. Unfortunately, Chris can't go to the police because he knows, with his past record, they won't believe him.

It isn't hard to conjure up comparisons between this film and the Robert Mitchum thriller The Night of the Hunter. Both feature two children on their own who must show resourcefulness as they run through a rural countryside to escape a maniacal killer.

Described by some as being a "Southern Gothic" Undertow has scenes that are brilliantly executed but overall the film comes up a bit short. Initially compelling, it begins to break down a little more than halfway through the film and ends a bit too easily for all that has come before it.

Josh Lucas continues to make interesting choices and my guess is that, were it not for his uncanny resemblance to Matthew McConnaughey, he would be a bigger star than he is today. As it is, he is well worth watching.

Jamie Bell, who was so impressive in Billy Elliot, does fine work as the pensive teen who finds himself in a do or die situation with the responsibility of his weaker brother weighing on his shoulders.

Green films in a gritty, no frills kind of way and the result is a naturalistic style where the actors totally immerse themselves in their characters. In fact, many in the supporting cast are not actors at all but, as he has done in other films, townspeople from the area in which he is filming are tapped to play incidental roles.

The relationship between Deel and John is nothing new. It is as old as the record of Cain and Abel. God loved Abel and the pureness of his heart. Cain grew to resent and hate what he did not have.

Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous. 1 John 3:12 (KJV)

The consequences of Cain's sin were great and completely avoidable. Envy has no place between brothers - be they fleshly or spiritual. We should rejoice when our brothers succeed and are rewarded. To want their success as ours will lead us down a dark and unpleasant path.
