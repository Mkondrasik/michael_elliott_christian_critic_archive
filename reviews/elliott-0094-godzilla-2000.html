Godzilla 2000

Author-verified transcription

I like cheese. I like it in omelets; on hamburgers; when sprinkled lightly over a plate of pasta primavera. In rare cases, and in moderate doses, I even like it in movies. GODZILLA 2000, the 23rd entry in the Japanese series of monster movies starring the reptile of reptiles continues in its ever recognizable cheeseball mode and will surely delight Godzilla aficionados the world over. Me... I just sat back and enjoyed my cheese-flavored popcorn, smiling bemusedly at the PG rated monster antics which flashed on the screen.

The movie opens quickly with Godzilla (the rubber-suited Tsutomu Kitagawa) strolling through Japan, walking through bridges, power lines, and city streets, leaving a wake of destruction and panic stricken citizens in his path. The members of the Godzilla Prediction Network (GPN) attempt to get close enough to Godzilla for the reason of being able to flee for their lives whenever he turns their way.

The GPN are the good guys, wanting to study Godzilla for the benefit of mankind and the glory of Japan. Their goals are not shared by the members of Crisis Control Intelligence (CCI) who simply want to blast Godzilla into that big reptile house in the sky, using their unlimited supply of armor piercing missiles.

Enter Orga (translation: Giant Alien Millennian.) Lifted from the primordial ooze on the bottom of the ocean by the unbelievably stupid CCI, Orga comes to life in the shape of a giant flying rock, causing such scintillating dialogue as, “Hey, did you see a giant flying rock go by?”

Orga interrupts Godzilla from wreaking havoc upon Tokyo, which ticks the big guy off just a tad. Don’t you just know there’s going to be a showdown before the film comes to an end with Tokyo serving as the playing field?

Truth be told, this Godzilla is no better or worse than the many others which came before it. The style fits and compliments the series which bore it. If history is any gauge, the true enjoyment will come from watching this film when it finally makes it to the TV weekend midnight movie show airing in your city, usually hosted by a guy with a bad toupee and a cape. It is one of those guilty pleasures where you laugh more at the action on the screen than with it.

Particularly amusing is the over the top seriousness of CCI Chief Katagiri, played by Hiroshi Abe. Kudos also to the English actor who dubbed his voice in the deep and somber tones befitting this Japanese actor’s intense mannerisms.

For me, the only concession to the times in which we live was that some of the slangy expressions sprinkled through the script smacked of contemporary American indulgence. Describing the missiles, the commander proudly exclaimed that they would blast through Godzilla like “you-know-what through a goose.” Later, that same you-know-what was beatified with the expression, “Holy ‘you-know-what’.” The American slang, which was used liberally in the dubbed dialogue and is now cliched beyond redemption, seemed out of place and jarring... much like a 17-story fire-breathing lizard would be if one happened across it while strolling through downtown Tokyo.

The moral or message of Godzilla 2000 remains unchanged from the original movie. Godzilla was born in a post-nuclear world by the only country ever to experience a nuclear attack. The Japanese understand firsthand that within technology or science there exists the capability to destroy as well as benefit our world. As the CCI looks for new sources of power, they manage to awake Orga, thus unleashing a destructive force unto their world.

Our spiritual quest leads us to seek the ultimate “power” source.

God hath spoken once; twice have I heard this; that power belongeth unto God. Psalms 62:11 (KJV)

Whenever we turn from that truth and seek other sources of spiritual power, we unwittingly invite the destructive evil of a strong and vengeful enemy to stomp around our home and families.
