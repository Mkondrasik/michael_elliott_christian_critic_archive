Author-verified transcription

Looks like Hollywood has figured out how to resurrect a worn-out, done to death, franchise icon... simply go back to the beginning. It worked for Batman Begins in 2005 and now James Bond is back.. blonder and badder than ever.

With Casino Royale (don't even bother comparing it to the pseudo Bond film by the same name), the 007 franchise goes back to its roots. Gone are the sexual innuendoes, the perfunctory opening action sequence, and the many gadgets that exceed all known technology.

What is left is a new "00" agent named James Bond (Daniel Craig), a threat to the world, a high-stakes poker game, and a beautiful woman (three, actually... or four if you're as much of a Judi Dench fan as I am.) Craig, as Bond, is a cold-hearted killer (a requirement to all who carry the "00" moniker) but he's new to it and a bit of an enigma to the people to whom he supposedly reports. After a botched assignment that ends with a spectacular chase, demolition of a foreign embassy, and loss of a potential source, Bond takes it upon himself to track down the one lead he managed to obtain.

It eventually leads him to Le Chiffre (Mads Mikkelsen), an international financier who is helping terrorists with their monetary concerns by manipulating the stock market - using the very acts of terrorism he arranges to fund.

Director Martin Campbell and his team of screenwriters provide us with perhaps the most realistic Bond in the franchise's history. This Bond gets beat and bloodied. He makes mistakes and learns from them. He's got a hard edge but a soft spot in his heart which makes him vulnerable (to us and his enemies.) Craig is athletic enough to make us believe the character's physicality and studied enough to make us believe his intelligence. Though 007 purists may, at first, be a bit put off by the blondness of this Bond, their concerns will melt away after the first 30 minutes of this 2 ½ hour action adventure.

Eva Green is a Bond girl who is also his intellectual equal. A quick witted Treasury agent, she is the instrument by which Bond learns his greatest lesson.

Mads Mikkelsen is a different kind of Bond villain. Though ruthless in his own right, he is also under considerable stress as he owes money to people who don't take financial losses lightly. In other words, his motivation is quite simple - survival.

Bond's motivation is less clear although certainly some part of it may be due to an enormous ego. He is the best at what he does.. at least he believes that. Others who may even share that opinion are troubled that his ego may be the one Achilles' heel that he possesses.

They are right to be concerned. An ego, when not properly balanced with humility and self-awareness, can lead its owner down a path from which he may not return.

Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit? ¡here is more hope of a fool than of him. Proverbs 26:12 (KJV)

The point of this scripture is that a fool, who knows that he knows nothing, can learn. A man, who thinks he knows everything, rarely learns anything.
