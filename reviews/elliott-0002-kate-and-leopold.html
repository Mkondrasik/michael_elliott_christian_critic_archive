Kate and Leopold

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 25, 2001
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Peter 3:7 Proverbs 31:10 Proverbs 18:22

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Marriage; courtship and chivalry; honoring wives; relationships

Author-verified transcription

Chivalry is not dead.. it is alive and well and living in the 19th century. The trick is merely to bring it forward into the 21st. Thanks to the Hollywood crafted miracle of time travel, this trick becomes as familiar as pulling a rabbit out of a hat... but even an old trick, when done well, can achieve its desired effect. Kate and Leopold certainly does that. It is a predictable romantic comedy that manages to be light, airy and pleasantly attractive.

Leopold (Hugh Jackman, X-Men), third duke of Albany, is a 19th century bachelor unable (or unwilling) to find a marriageable woman who will meet both his desires and his own familial responsibilities.

Kate (Meg Ryan, Proof of Life), a 21st century businesswoman, is so busy scratching her way to the top of her profession that she views men merely as chauvinistic obstacles along her chosen career path.

If only these two crazy kids could meet... Wait! Along comes Kate's ex-boyfriend, Stuart (Liev Shreiber, Scream), who has conveniently discovered a time portal that allows him to travel back to 1876. Guess who follows him back through time?

Leopold is something of an anachronism in the 21st century... and a refreshing one at that. Courteous to a fault, well-spoken, sensitive to the needs of women, exhibiting both moral self-control and integrity, it doesn't take Leopold long to distance himself from the rest of mankind which has somehow managed to "devolve" into something less genuine and gentile. It helps that the male figures who are offered as comparisons include Kate's clueless boss J.J. (Bradley Whitford, The West Wing) her cute but has about half a clue.

We've seen Meg Ryan in these kind of romantic comedy roles before. It's nice to know that familiarity does not always breed contempt. She gives us nothing that we haven't seen in When Harry Met Sally, You've Got Mail, or Sleepless in Seattle, but she is appealing and still knows how to exude a warmth to which an audience responds.

What really makes the film work as well as it does is Hugh Jackman's charm and affable manner. He has appropriated the studied social mannerisms of an earlier age, showing us why they should always be considered as desirous qualities.

Director James Mangold (Girl, Interrupted) doesn't overreach, contenting himself with simply presenting an uncomplicated and sweet romance. It has been reported that he has made some editing changes in the film since it was last screened for critics. The reported changes eliminated what I considered to be major flaw. With that scene gone, Kate and Leopold should be a breezy, amusing, anachronistic romp.

Leopold's chivalry, especially his views on how a woman should be courted and treated, may seem old-fashioned but they are indeed timeless. Scriptures have long exhorted men to view their wives as being precious and vital to their lives. Proverbs even state that a virtuous woman is more valuable than rubies.

We are not about to argue with God's Word.

Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered. 1 Peter 3:7 (KJV)

If King James English doesn't do this particular verse justice as there is a oriental truth which doesn't quite translate to the modern mind. The weaker vessel should be equated to the most treasured item in the house. Such items were generally older and extremely fragile, but they were also the most valuable.

Any husband involved in a successful marriage will admit to the immeasurable contribution his wife brings to their life together. Kate and Leopold works as a romance because each of the titular characters recognizes what the other adds to the relationship.
