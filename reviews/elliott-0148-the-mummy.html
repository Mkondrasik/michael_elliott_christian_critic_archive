The Mummy

Author-verified transcription

In 1932, Boris Karloff wrapped himself in a pile of rotten bandages and managed to scare your parents and grandparents with his portrayal of The Mummy. Sixty-seven years later, writer/director Stephen Sommers (Deep Rising) has reawakened the creature. But this is not The Mummy that kept your father awake at nights.

With the advent of computer graphics, the modern creature is "juicier," more decomposed, and a whole lot uglier. But somehow, that just doesn't translate to scary. The Mummy movie of 1999 is more like a poor man's Indiana Jones. It's got a lot of action, lots of special effects, but very little that will involve the audience on a visceral level.

Part of the problem is with the script. Starting with the opening, gravely intoned narration, which describes how Imhotep, keeper of the dead, fell in love with the Pharoah's mistress and was sentenced to a gruesome and curse protected death, we understand that we are in for a high-tech but cheesy ride.

From a high-tech perspective, The Mummy does satisfy. The special effects are amazing: Sandstorms that appear out of nowhere and assume human characteristics; the depiction of the plagues of Egypt (part of the curse of Imhotep is that he will bring back those plagues); and of course, the mummy, a mere shell of a man who needs to borrow organs from those he meets in order to restore himself.

But special effects aside, we still have human actors involved. And they are a mixed bunch:

Brandon Fraser (Gods and Monsters) does an admirable job as Harrison Ford's stand-in. As the adventurer Rick O'Connell, Mr. Fraser has the tongue-in-cheek, cavalierly brave persona down pat. It's a pity that his supporting cast is not as strong.

Rachel Weisz (Chain Reaction) as Evelyn, an English librarian in an Egyptian library, who serves as love interest for both Rick and Imhotep, is, to put it simply, a simp. Her characterization has no depth and as an actress, she lacks the ability to overcome the "B" movie dialogue she has been given.

John Hannah (Sliding Doors) plays her brother Jonathan. He's a conniving, drunken loser who stumbles onto the secret of the lost city of Hamunaptra. His personality, which is all the screenwriters have given him to work with, gets under your skin within the first five minutes of his screen appearance.

Arnold Vosloo (Darkman II and III), once his computer animated structure "regenerates" enough to allow him to play the part of Imhotep, does present an imposing figure. I can only wonder if this would have been a more effective movie if Mr. Vosloo had been permitted to play The Mummy in the same manner as Mr. Karloff, without the benefit of computerized special effects. Perhaps our technological advances do not always equate to a better way of doing something.

Of course the story of The Mummy deals with the resurrection of the dead... from a devilish point of view. That this movie illustrates a lie should not be surprising to us. For every spiritual truth, the devil has made sure that there is a counterfeit that is designed to discredit that truth.

Scripturally, resurrection of the dead is a dominant theme. It is the resurrection and ascension of Jesus Christ which has made it available for us to receive holy spirit and eternal life. According to the Bible, it is a promise of God that all who died will be resurrected. Some (those who lived righteously or believed God to the end that they received holy spirit) will go on to eternal paradise. Others (those who rejected God and his Word) will be judged, found to be lacking, and will suffer a second and (this time) permanent death.

None of this entails rotting flesh, decomposed bodies or evil incarnate. That is the devil's fantasy. It is not our reality, nor is it the will of God. Movies such as The Mummy do not edify or strengthen our spiritual walk, but they do point out to us the illogical stupidity that stands in contrast to the truth of God.

It is fine to enjoy the ride, cheesy as it may be. We just have to make sure that we don't accept any of its lies or false doctrines. The only One Who has the power to resurrect the dead is God.
