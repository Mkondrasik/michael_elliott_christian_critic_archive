Author-verified transcription

Porn stars in high schools? An R-rated teenage sex comedy? Is it just me or do these concepts seem more than a little inappropriate? Be that as it may, The Girl Next Door fits both descriptions, providing titillation for high school students who will have to sneak in to see it. (Not a recommendation of mine.)

Matthew (Emile Hirsch) is a typical student. He's college bound with lofty aspirations but regrets that he always stayed rather conservative. To him, the cool crowd always seemed to have much more fun. He and his two best friends - Klitz (Paul Dano), a math geek and Eli (Chris Marquette), a video geek - don't seem to do much of anything remotely interesting.

Things look up for Matthew when a beautiful blonde named Danielle (Elisha Cuthbert) moves in next door. After an unusual first meeting where she forces him to strip on a public street, they become friends. When Eli, who is a porn aficionado, recognizes her from his vast video collection he informs Mathew of his amazing "luck" and gives him some terrible advice. When Mathew follows it, he alienates Danielle which sends her back to the unseemly industry she had been trying to leave. Guilt ridden, Matthew chases after her to persuade her to leave her past behind her and continue with her original plan to to change the course of her life.

He runs into some trouble when Danielle's porn producer resents Matthew's interference and begins to push back.

The script is an embarrassment in many ways: conceptually, constructionally, and thematically. Its head scratching pinnacle comes as Mathew, high on ecstasy, must deliver a speech on having moral fiber in order to receive a scholarship that will enable him to go to Georgetown University. His speech, which I assume is supposed to be poignant or meaningful, boils moral fiber down to this simplistic philosophy: "Whatever, dude." If you find the most important thing in your life, you do whatever you have to do, even if it isn't nice, to hold onto it. That's having moral fiber? What rubbish.

The lead actors aren't awful. Emile Hirsch has a nice fresh face and presents a likable character. Elisha Cuthbert is attractive. Paul Dano and Chris Marquette are appropriately geeky in a disturbing kind of way. Timothy Olyphant does some nice work as the porn producer that tries to keep Matthew from stealing his rising star.

Ultimately, however, we cannot escape the sheer stupidity of the concept itself. I cannot figure out who the filmmakers or studios were thinking were going to be the target audience for this infantile adult film but whoever they are, I'd be insulted if I were them.

Just to be able to talk about something edifying, let's look at Danielle's situation. She wants to escape her past but when she is reminded of it, the first thing she does is return to it. The only way to escape the past is to leave it in the past. Every single person should learn this truth because we all have once walked according to the courses of this evil world.

When we discover that there is a better way to live, we are encouraged to put off our "old man nature" and put on the new. To go back to our old sinful ways after being led out of that darkness and into the light is the most foolish and unprofitable thing imaginable.

But now, after that ye have known God, or rather are known of God, how turn ye again to the weak and beggarly elements, whereunto ye desire again to be in bondage? Galatians 4:9 (KJV)

We must not let anyone deceive us of our rightful position in Christ. Our pasts have been washed clean and nobody has the right to try and drive us back to a place where we no longer belong.
