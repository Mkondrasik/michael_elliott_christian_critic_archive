Antz

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 2, 1998
Rating
4 stars
Source

Author-verified transcription

Who'd have ever thought that Woody Allen's most effective screen role would be a neurotic worker ant in a computer animated feature film? Then again, perhaps the question should be: Why didn't anyone think of it sooner?

Antz is a delightfully funny and technically amazing film. It tells the story of Z-4195 (Woody Allen, Everybody Says I Love You), a worker ant and middle child (in a family of five million) who suffers from feelings of insignificance. And who wouldn't? Let's face it, what's one more ant among an entire colony?

Then, Princess Bala (Sharon Stone, The Mighty) literally dances into his life. The princess has been similarly disillusioned with her lot in life and decides to spend a night "slumming" with the workers. After an unusual turn on the dance floor, Z becomes smitten.

In order to see her again, Z poses as a soldier ant, changing places with his friend, Weaver (Sylvester Stallone, Copland). Through a series of events, Z and Bala discover a plot by General Mandible (Gene Hackman, Absolute Power) which will wipe out the worker ant population, leaving a stronger, soldier ant colony.

The script by Todd Alcott (Just Your Luck) and Chris and Paul Weitz (Clockstoppers) is witty and extremely humorous, although it leans towards more adult oriented themes which may not be appropriate for all ages.

The animation is top of the line. The vocal talent matches the drawn characters perfectly. In addition to the high profile personalities already mentioned, the cast includes: Christopher Walken (Suicide Kings) as Cutter, Mandible's second in command; Dan Aykroyd (Blues Brothers 2000) and Jane Curtin (Coneheads) as Chip and Muffy, a pair of helpful dragonflies; and Danny Glover (Lethal Weapon 4) as Barbados, a soldier ant who befriends Z.

We are obviously to sympathize with Z. While still a larva, he was labeled a worker ant. From that moment on, he was expected to conform to the worker ant definition. Being different was considered bad form... almost sacrilegious.

Many people expect the same of a born-again Christian. Once so labeled, we are expected to fit within a preconceived stereotype of what a born-again Christian should be. Usually, that picture is not a flattering one. Those who hold such stereotypes in their minds do not fully read the Scriptures.

Now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular. 1 Corinthians 12:27 [KJV]

We may be of the same body of Christ (just as the ants were of the same colony), but we are also members in particular. Each of us have contributions to make - long suits in which we excel to the benefit of the body of Christ. However, we are not, nor have we ever been robots or clones, dumbly mimicking the actions of others.

It is our individual skills and abilities that bring richness and diversity to the body of Christ. While we recognize that there are boundaries (the standards or commandments of God) within which we are to operate those skills and abilities, such boundaries are wide enough to allow plenty of room for individual growth and expression.

Z manages to hold onto those qualities which make him a unique ant while at the same time recognizing and appreciating the importance of working together as a colony (household). That is a spiritual lesson that we would all do well to learn.
