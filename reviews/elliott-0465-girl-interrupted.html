Author-verified transcription

In 1975, Jack Nicholson hit the screen in a big way as Randall Patrick MacMurphy, the larger than life sociopath in One Flew Over The Cuckoo's Nest. He was ably supported by a predominantly male ensemble cast. Now, a quarter of a century later, it has become the ladies' turn. Girl, Interrupted is based upon the autobiographical novel of Susanna Kaysen and takes a look at mental illness from the eyes of a seventeen year old who commits herself to a mental ward for an eighteen month long "rest." To tell the story, the filmmakers have managed to assemble quite a respectable collection of young and talented actresses.

Unlike Cuckoo's Nest, which clearly delineated the good guys from the bad, Girl, Interrupted blurs that line, focusing instead on the central character's confusion, questionable judgment, and eventual quest for mental soundness.

Winona Ryder (Celebrity) plays Susanna with a compassionate understanding. Diagnosed with a borderline personality disorder, manifesting itself through rebellious and self-destructive behavior, culminating in a halfhearted suicide attempt, Susanna is railroaded by her parents and doctors into accepting a one-way ticket to Claymoore, a local hospital.

There she meets the other residents with whom she forms close relationships: Georgina (Clea DuVall, The Astronaut's Wife), a pathological liar who, all things considered, would rather live in Oz; Daisy (Brittany Murphy, Drop Dead Gorgeous), a chicken-loving, laxative popping Daddy's girl, who is the first of the group to earn her release, though not in a conventional way; Polly (Elisabeth Moss, Anywhere But Here), a badly scarred burn victim whose heart has remained somehow unscathed; and a handful of others.

At the top of the list however is the indomitable Lisa (Angelina Jolie, The Bone Collector). MacMurphy, step aside... There's a new psycho in the ward. Brutally honest with everyone but herself, Lisa is a veritable whirlwind of raw emotion and Ms. Jolie plays it to the hilt, having Lisa wear her label of sociopath like a badge of honor. It is a showy performance, perhaps a bit too over the top, but still one which is sure to attract much attention.

Personally, I preferred the subtler touches found in the work of Ms. Ryder and her supporting cast. Vanessa Redgrave (Cradle Will Rock), Whoopi Goldberg (Deep End of the Ocean), and Jeffrey Tambor (Teaching Mrs. Tingle) give solid performances as the medical support staff at the institution whom Susanna initially distrusts and disrespects, only to find that she needs their guidance to find her way out of the disturbed mental state in which she is dwelling.

Director James Mangold (Copland) has taken Ms. Kaysen's novel, which immediately became a favorite among teenage girls, and used it to craft a movie which should be similarly popular.

Adolescence can be a strangely troubling time of change and transition. During such times it is not unusual to feel isolated and alone... somehow different from the rest of the world. There are scriptures which provide us with comfort from this natural but erroneous line of thinking.

There hath no temptation [or trial] taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it. 1 Corinthians 10:13 [KJV]

Knowing that God has not given us the "spirit of fear but of power and of love and of a sound mind," we should not accept anything less. There is no need to "get comfortable" with a feeling of depression. And there is no need to ever feel alone. We must resist our times of depression, building our minds ever stronger with the promises of God, believing that we are what His Word tells us we are, as we seek the company and fellowship of those who do similarly. It is our God-given right to claim mental wholeness.
