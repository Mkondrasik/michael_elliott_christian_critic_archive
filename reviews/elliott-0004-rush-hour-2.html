Rush Hour 2

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 3, 2001
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Corinthians 11:13-15 1 Thessalonians 5:21 1 John 4:1

Translation: NIV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Counterfeiting; deception; discernment; false apostles

Author-verified transcription

In 1998, New Line Cinema found they had a surprise hit in Rush Hour, a buddy cop comedy featuring Jackie Chan and Chris Tucker as cultural opposites who must work together to foil the bad guys. The film proved so successful with audiences that the obligatory sequel was spun... a sequel with a name as unoriginal as the story it tells: Rush Hour 2.

This time, it is LA detective Carter (Tucker) who is visiting Inspector Lee (Chan) in Hong Kong. Though Carter, who is on vacation, only wants to "mac out" and "mu shu" some ladies, Lee keeps himself busy with case after case that demands his attention. The one involving Ricky Tan (John Lone, M. Butterfly) and his Triad gang has him particularly agitated.

While looking into a bombing at the American embassy, they soon learn that a major counterfeiting operation (which will certainly destroy US economic stability) is forming and Ricky Tan is at the head of it all. After being dismissed by the official authorities, Lee and Carter decide to once again work outside the bounds of their own departments in order to bring the bad guys to justice.

Complicating matters is the presence of Isabella (Roselyn Sanchez, Held Up), a beautiful but corrupt customs agent in the employ of Tan who may or may not be working undercover for the Secret Service.

Director Brett Ratner helmed the original Rush Hour. This time, he's working from a script by Jeff Nathanson who penned the highly forgettable Speed 2: Cruise Control. If it weren't for the martial arts skill of Jackie Chan and the gregarious, often outrageous attitude of Chris Tucker, Rush Hour 2 might have joined Speed 2 at the bottom of the heap of painfully bad sequels. The stars work hard to make the film endurable. It would be too much to ask them to make it special.

Chan again displays his amazing ability to combine comedy with fight choreography. Here, he stages his fights on bamboo scaffolding, in a massage parlor, on a yacht, and in a Las Vegas casino. These sequences are plainly the most entertaining features of the film and as such will be found to be too short and infrequent.

Tucker has improved his fighting skills since the first RH, but it still isn't his long suit. He does excel on a Hong Kong karaoke stage as he gives the locals a little sampling of his Michael Jackson-like moves, but his "edge" appears to have been softened a bit which reduces his street smarts credibility.

The film gives off a bit of a musty air and the added presence of Ziyi Zhang (Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon) as a kung fu bomber does nothing to hide the stale mediocrity of the project. It appears to this reviewer that "Rush Hour" is indeed over. Any time the out-takes which run over the end credits prove to be more entertaining than the film from whence they came... it can be said that this franchise has run out of gas.

Counterfeiting is a crime which holds a special meaning to a biblical student. It is the process by which the false takes on the appearance of truth. The key to counterfeiting is to make the imitation look as much like the genuine as possible. It helps to include elements of the genuine into the fake as in Rush Hour 2 where US Treasury plates were being used to print nearly undetectable forgeries.

Our spiritual adversary knows and utilizes this trick very well. He will allow aspects of truth to be woven into his lies in order to make them believable. It is how so many well-meaning men and women who want to love and know God can become deceived and kept from their hearts' desire.

For such men are false apostles, deceitful workmen, masquerading as apostles of Christ. And no wonder, for Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light. It is not surprising, then, if his servants masquerade as servants of righteousness. Their end will be what their actions deserve. 2 Corinthians 11:13-15 (NIV)

We should never assume something is true. We are exhorted to prove all things. It is why God gave to us the standard of all truth... His Word.
