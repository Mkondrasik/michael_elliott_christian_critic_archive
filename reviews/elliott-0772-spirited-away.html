Confession time. I'm not a big fan of Japanese animation. I'm also not very appreciative of films whose messages contradict the Bible from which I derive my beliefs. These are two big strikes against Hayao Miyazaki's Spirited Away. The fact that despite this, I've given the film two and one half stars speaks volumes as to its artistic level. Did I like it? Not really. Can I recognize the artful craft of its maker? Yes... for it is undeniable.

Writer/director Hayao Miyazaki (Princess Mononoke) is something of a household name in Japan and interest in his films are increasing amidst American cinephiles. Spirited Away is arguably his most successful film, breaking Japanese box office records and winning the Golden Bear award at this year's Berlin Film Festival. It is the first animated film to have done so in fifty years.

The story is something akin to Alice in Wonderland. A whiny ten year old girl named Chihiro (Daveigh Chase, Lilo & Stitch) becomes trapped in the spirit world while visiting an abandoned amusement park. Separated from her parents, who were turned into swine after eating food left for the spirits, Chihiro must muster the courage to fend for herself in an unfamiliar and unfriendly place.

She ends up in a resort of sorts, a spa/bathhouse where spirits come to rest and rejuvenate. It is run by a cranky big-headed woman named Yubaba (Suzanne Pleshette, The Lion King II: Simba's Pride), who grudgingly gives Chihiro a job. In between the odd and menial tasks Chihiro must perform, she tries to figure out a way to save her parents and return to the human world before she loses her sense of identity.

The film will be released as a dubbed English language version via Walt Disney Studios but filmgoers should not make the assumption that the movie in any way has become "Disneyfied." This remains very much a Miyazaki production. There are dark elements present in his film which reflects a culture where spiritualism is an accepted part of their history.

Spirited Away is presented with the imaginative flair for which Miyazaki is well known. During her sojourn in the spirit world, Chihiro meets the multiple-armed Kamaji (David Ogden Stiers, Lilo & Stitch) and his simplistic soot creatures who keep the furnaces burning; an odorific stink god who has come to the bath looking to be purged; a majestic and benevolent dragonlike river god; and a wide range of other spirit beings, some of whom look to be extras from the Pokemon set.

The number and diversity evidenced in these spirit beings is reminiscent of biblical records. Within the scriptures, there are distinctions made between different kinds of spirits. In Romans 8:5, reference is made to a spirit of bondage. In Luke 13:11, a spirit of infirmity is mentioned. Other spirits include a familiar spirit (Leviticus 20:27), a spirit of jealousy (Numbers 5:14), a lying spirit (1 Kings 22:22), an unclean spirit (Matthew 12:43), and a spirit of divination (Acts 16:16).

These distinctions are included in the scriptures in order that we might be more fully aware of how the spiritual realm functions. God has also enabled us with the ability called discerning of spirits (1 Corinthians 12:10) so that we might not be ignorant of this category of life.

The spirits of Spirited Away are, for the most part, docile. They mean no harm towards humans and in fact, pay them almost no mind at all. Such is not the case with the spiritual realm that we study in the Bible. Which is why we must view the spiritual side of life with the discerning wisdom God has made available to us.

Dear friends, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world. 1 John 4:1 (NIV)

Because of the spirit of God which resides within us, we are now part of the spiritual realm even though we remain attached to this physical world. Let us walk wisely when dealing with spiritual matters and make our choices based upon the knowledge and wisdom of God.
