Surviving Christmas

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
October 22, 2004
Rating
1½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 27 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 55:2Acts 8:20Luke 8:14

Sermon / spiritual topic: Money; Materialism; Happiness

Author-verified transcription

Surviving Christmas was originally slated to open during last year's holiday season but the studio must have felt that their film would not live up to its title. So they yanked it and made the unusual decision to go for a non-holiday release. After viewing it we can see why. There are few survivors of this film... unless you count those members of the audience who fled for the exits.

Drew Latham (Ben Affleck) is a successful and wealthy executive whose holiday plans are dashed when his girlfriend (Jennifer Morrison) declared that she would rather spend Christmas with her family than jet off to Fiji with him. Emotionally unable to spend Christmas alone, he strikes upon a bizarre idea.

Arriving unexpectedly to his childhood home, he hires the people now living in it to pretend to be his family during the holiday weekend. Unfortunately, Drew's childlike attempts to build Christmas cheer within the dysfunctional Valco family are continually met with Grinch-like coldness.

Mike Mitchell (Deuce Bigalow: Male Gigalo) directs this off-season comedy with the same finesse it takes to decorate a tree by throwing clumps of tinsel on it. With the exception of a game but badly miscast Ben Affleck, we get the sense that the actors are aware that this is one Christmas present that needs to be returned.

James Gandolfini stews and glowers as the patriarch who, for $250,000, allows a potential lunatic to move into his house. He looks uncomfortable in the role and I can't say that I blame him. Catherine O'Hara plays his wife who rediscovers her sexuality in an embarrassing photo shoot. Other than that scene, she manages to keep a relatively low profile by providing minimal support.

Christina Applegate has the best success of the cast as she portrays the most normal member of the Valco family and the eventual love interest for Ben Affleck. The romance, of course doesn't make much sense and she is asked to ignore what would be her character's logical response in order to move the story forward but she manages to do it with a fair amount of grace.

Casting Ben Affleck was just a mistake. This role, which requires a goofy, childlike innocence, doesn't play to his strengths and he simply can't make the character work. Because his character is the focal point around which the movie revolves, there's little hope for it. The story itself fails and all that is left are a few comedic scenes that might be enjoyed on their own merit.

The movie, as ineffective as it is, does manage to illustrate a fallacy that exists in our culture. That fallacy is that money can cure all ills. It isn't true. Drew apparently had plenty of money which he used to try to resolve every difficulty or disappointment in his life. He gave his old girlfriend an expensive bracelet in hopes that she would stay with him. He paid people to pretend to love him. But all the money in the world could not buy what he most desired or what he most needed.

Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good, and let your soul delight itself in fatness. Isaiah 55:2 (KJV)

Money is a tool... and it can be a valuable one. But it is not the answer to every situation nor should it be the ultimate goal or focus for our lives. Money does not buy happiness. That's a truth that can be proven simply by paying for a ticket to this film.
