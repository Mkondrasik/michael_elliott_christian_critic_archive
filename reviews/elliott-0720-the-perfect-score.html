Author-verified transcription

Once a year, college-bound high school students nationwide are terrorized as they try to prepare for the S.A.T., the standardized test upon which hinges their future. In The Perfect Score, six very different students band together for a single goal: to steal the answers to the S.A.T. so they might score high enough to get the placement they desire.

Kyle (Chris Evans) has wanted to be an architect since he was seven years old. The school he has set his sights on is Cornell, the alma mater of some of the greatest architects in the country. His S.A.T. scores are about 400 points too low for Cornell to even consider him.

His best friend, Matty (Bryan Greenberg), has lower expectations. He plans on going to the University of Maryland where his girlfriend is already matriculating. But as his scores are even worse than Kyle's, he too has had his dreams crushed.

Then there's Anna (Erika Christensen), the prim and proper braniac who almost made high school valedictorian. She is all set to go to Brown because that's what her parents want for her. She froze during the first exam and is worried about doing the same on the retake.

Desmond is the high school basketball phenom who can have his choice of colleges, if his scores are high enough. He hasn't even bothered to take the S.A.T. yet because he knows they won't be. He's willing to go straight into the pros but his mama won't hear of it.

The rest of the gang is comprised of high school pothead Roy (Leonardo Nam) who seems to be involved only because he has nothing else to do and Francesca (Scarlett Johannson), a poor little rich girl whose daddy is too busy dating girls her age to pay her any attention. But since he owns the building that houses the test scores, the others need her to get them inside.

The film is less about the heist as it is about these six kids helping each other solve their own problems and hangups. It has got the same feel as The Breakfast Club with one huge difference. They needed John Hughes badly. Without him, The Perfect Score is merely a pale imitation. Even if grading on a curve, this one isn't going to pass.

It is one of those films where the kids, as messed up and confused as they are, have the insight and awareness to know that they are messed up and confused which makes them much smarter than adults. The only exception is Desmond's mom but she comes on rather late in the movie and is more of a plot device than anything else. For the most part, the message to teens seems to be, "you can work out your own problems and if you can't, get your friends to help you because your parents can't - they're part of your problem."

As far as the idea of stealing the test scores, the filmmakers end with a moral but unbelievable twist which is supposed to indicate that the students grew and matured as a result of their experiences together. It is pure hokum which might have had some meaning if we were emotionally invested in what happened to these characters. But we aren't.

Stealing or cheating one's way to success is always a bad idea. It is a shallow victory, void of meaning or significance.

Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth. 2 Timothy 2:15 (KJV)

We can't cheat or steal our way into God's favor. There's only one way to gain His approval. And in the long run, there's only one way to gain your own.
