The Aviator

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 25, 2004
Rating
3½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 Corinthians 15:58 Colossians 1:23 Hebrews 3:14

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Steadfastness; perseverance; vision; spiritual goals

Author-verified transcription

Eccentric millionaire? Lavish filmmaker? Charismatic ladies’ man? Daring aviator? Compulsive germaphobic? How does one even begin to describe the enigma that is Howard Hughes?

In The Aviator, director Martin Scorcese and his star, Leonardo DiCaprio, do a mammoth job of bringing this highly controversial individual to life.

After an odd and somewhat unsettling prologue where Howard the boy is being given a sponge bath and a hygiene lecture from his mother, we jump forward in time to find Howard the man directing what would become the most expensive film production of its time.

The decisions that Hughes made during the filming of Hell’s Angels gives us a strong indication of his view of life. He had a vision of what he wanted and no cost was too exorbitant for him to stop pursuing it. His unusual approach brought him disdain and ridicule from his peers who kept their eye firmly on profit margins but they ultimately had to admit that the results he garnered were something to be admired even if the means by which he achieved them were to be scorned.

Hughes got his money the old-fashioned way - he inherited it. His incredible wealth came from a family business which produced drill bits. It gave him the financial ability to pursue his life’s passions: film, Hollywood starlets, and (most especially) flying.

The film spans the couple of decades within which Hughes was a recognized aviation innovator. He broke the speed record; traveled around the world in four days; designed the largest plane known to man; and broke PanAm’s monopoly on international travel.

Hughes somehow found time to court the most desirable women of his day. Katherine Hepburn (brilliantly played by Cate Blanchette who deserves far more mention than this review allows) and Ava Gardner (Kate Beckinsale) were among those with whom he was associated.

Unfortunately, he also fell prey to mental disorder, growing more paranoid and mentally unstable as time passed. DiCaprio wisely allows the character’s instability to grow as the film progresses.

As good as he is, he is overshadowed whenever Cate Blanchett graces the screen. She is phenomenal as a young Kate Hepburn and will surely receive an Oscar nod for her performance.

Scorcese rarely lets the action lag and succeeds in depicting the look and feel of America in the 30s and 40s which is when much of the film takes place.

Hughes’ ability to set a vision for himself and relentlessly pursue it until it became a reality was the key to whatever successes he achieved. While his perfectionism may have contributed to his mental erosion, his steadfastness in the face of adversity and opposition is quite admirable. He showed an amazing degree of strength and toughness which allowed him to be to remain on course, refusing to be sidetracked or deterred from accomplishing his goal.

We should manifest the same mental toughness when it comes to the spiritual goals that we are to set for ourselves. No amount of persuasion or diversionary tactics should dissuade us from staying our course and realizing the desires of our hearts.

Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord. 1 Corinthians 15:58 (KJV)
