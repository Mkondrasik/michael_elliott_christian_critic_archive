Author-verified transcription

Box offices will turn green this weekend (in more ways than one) as The Hulk smashes its way into theaters nationwide. Ang Lee's (Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon) treatment of the Marvel comic book character in serious need of anger management is arguably the most anticipated movie of the summer. Though it proves to be something of a mixed bag, there's little doubt that it will generate ticket sales of Hulk-like proportions.

Eric Bana (Finding Nemo) stars as Bruce Banner, a man whose unbalanced father (Nick Nolte, The Good Thief) once foolishly experimented in genetic alteration. After using himself as a guinea pig, he inadvertently passed his mutated genes on to his son. Bruce, who grew up to be a genetic scientist himself, was unaware of his genetic abnormality until he was hit with what should have been a lethal dose of gamma radiation in a freak lab accident. Instead of killing him, it turned him into an off-again/on-again not-so-jolly green giant. Whenever Bruce gets emotionally agitated, he morphs into a 20 foot tall, jade-colored, muscle-bound monster capable of great destruction and incredible strength.

The US government is none too happy about having such a short-fused time bomb freely walking the streets without it being completely under their control. And so steps are taken to lock Bruce/the Hulk away for the rest of his natural life. Obviously, their plan has more than a few flaws in it.

Action fans may find themselves disappointed as the first hour or so of character introduction and exposition tends to drag on quite a bit. Director Ang Lee livens it up with some inventive scene transitions and a very effective score by Danny Elfman (Spider-man). But after a while it becomes all too evident that it is taking too long for the big guy to appear. Once he does, the film becomes the "CG-eye candy" that everyone is expecting.

While the Hulk is doing his smashing and leaping/flying thing, the film is entertaining enough but Lee seems to reaching for a profundity that neither the film nor its actors can support. Lee eventually reaches too far and the film ends as a muddled excess of sound and fury... signifying nothing.

Eric Bana appropriately plays Bruce as emotionally repressed and distant but this approach hardly makes for a charismatic central character. Jennifer Connolly (A Beautiful Mind) is much more appealing as his ex-girlfriend and coworker. She's the beauty that can calm the beast.

Nick Nolte is all over the emotional map, shamelessly overplaying his role as the mad scientist, while looking remarkably like his infamous DUI mug shot. Sam Elliott (We Were Soldiers) is more grounded, stereotypically so, as the tough as nails military commander. Josh Lucas (Sweet Home Alabama) is merely trapped inside a caricature of an evil government contractor looking to exploit the technology which made the Hulk.

Actually it was less technology and more human ego which was responsible for the Hulk existence. Banner's father was obsessed with improving God's design. He wanted "to give men the power to go beyond God's boundaries." Such ego will always produce negative results. It is the same self-imposed trap that tripped up God's arch enemy long before man arrived on the scene.
