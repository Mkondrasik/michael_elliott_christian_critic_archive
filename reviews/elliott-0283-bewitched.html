Bewitched

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 24, 2005
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
1 hr: 30 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Renewal of the mind; spiritual transformation; putting off the old man; identity

Author-verified transcription

The Hollywood trend of remaking classic TV shows continues. This time, someone thankfully came up with an innovative concept that made the old practice seem fresh. The talent behind Bewitched takes the ideas of the original 1960s series and makes them relevant to today's world by turning the film into a story about a production company's attempt to remake Bewitched as a new TV series.

They've got their anchor. Jack Wyatt (Will Ferrell), a rather pathetic movie star looking to give his career a kick in the pants, has agreed to take the role of Darren just as long as the show is retooled to make him the star. To make sure of this, he insists on casting an unknown as Samantha, his TV "witchy" wife.

Serendipitously enough, he finds Isabel Bigalow (Nicole Kidman) who is a real witch looking to live a normal suburban life. When Jack sees Isabel unconsciously twitching her nose (ala Elizabeth Montgomery), he insists she plays the part. She agrees... only because he is such a mess, she feels that he needs her. In her idealistic way, she feels complete when she is with him. This is what normal means to her.

But when she learns of his duplicity, she has a "normal" reaction. She isn't happy and, as Jack will learn, it isn't a smart thing to make a witch unhappy.

Credit Nora and Delia Ephron with the idea to capture the essence of the TV series without trying to remake it. Will Ferrell isn't playing Darren - he's playing an actor cast as Darren. The same is true for most of the rest of the cast.

It certainly helps to have a number of Oscar winners lending their talents. Nicole Kidman establishes herself as the star of the film with her frothy performance as Isabel, the witch who would be mortal. Her innocence, naiveté, and effervescent enthusiasm for human experience are delightful and set the tone for the entire production.

Michael Caine easily coasts through the film as Isabel's disapproving father. Shirley MacLaine could have done a bit more as Iris, the diva cast as Samantha's mother, Endora.

Will Ferrell continues to do his goofy shtick which many of his fans appreciate. For me, it's a bit needlessly over the top - as if he's trying too hard for the laugh.

While there are many laughs throughout the film, Nora Ephron has not been able to tie the entire production together seamlessly. There are obvious ebbs and flows and the film, as a result, feels uneven. Though only 90 minutes long, it seems to drag well past the 2 hour mark.

Although spells and witchcraft are most decidedly non-Christian, if we look past these elements we can see a spiritual truth emerge in Isabel's desire. She wants to be 'normal." She wants to cast off who she is (or rather who her world has defined her to be) in order to live the ideal life she has imagined for herself.

Is that so different from our desires? God has granted us the ability to change. We are not locked into the world's definition of who we are. We can leave our old man nature behind us and be the spiritual men and women that God has called us to be because he has given us the power to renew our minds and change our behavior.

That ye put off concerning the former conversation the old man, which is corrupt according to the deceitful lusts;' and be renewed in the spirit of you mind; and that ye put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness. Ephesians 4:22-24 (KJV)
