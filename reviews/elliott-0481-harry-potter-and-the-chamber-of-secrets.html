Author-verified transcription

Grab your wands and hold onto your owls! Harry Potter is back for another rollicking adventure tale set within the fantasy world of witches and wizardry. The Chamber of Secrets, the second in the series, is a bit darker, moves a bit slower, but is just as faithful to its source novel as the original movie.

As in the first film, the story begins in the "muggle," or non-wizard, world where Harry (Daniel Radcliffe) spends his summers. There, he is visited by a house-elf named Dobby who warns him not to return to Hogwarts School of Wizardry. It seems that there is a plot afoot that would bring harm to our young hero. Dobby's warnings seem prudent as certain events begin to occur which seem designed to keep Harry from Hogwarts.

After a harrowing rescue, thanks to friend Ron Weasley (Rupert Grint) and a flying car, Harry and the others resume their magical education at Hogwarts under the tutelage of their teachers, including braggart Gilderoy Lockhart (Kenneth Branagh), the new Dark Arts Professor. As might be expected for such an institution, unexplained events occur almost daily. Harry starts hearing voices and "mudbloods" (wizards born of muggle parents) are turning up petrified all over school.

It's up to Harry, Ron, and Hermione (Emma Watson) to not only discover who opened the deadly chamber of secrets but also to uncover what lies behind the attacks on the mudbloods before Hogwarts is closed for good.

The entire cast returns for the second installment of the series. The late Richard Harris makes his final screen appearance as Professor Dumbledore; Maggie Smith is Professor McGonagall; Alan Rickman is Professor Snape; and Robbie Coltrane is the lovable hairy giant Hagrid. Joining the cast are Kenneth Branagh as an insufferable glory-hound and Jason Isaacs as the devious Lucius Malfoy.

Director Chris Columbus, back at the helm for a second time, has a solid handle on this material. Visually, the film captures the look and feel of the distinctive settings described in J.K. Rowling's books.

And there can be no improving on the casting choices. From Radcliffe, Grint, and Watson (all of whom are perfect in their respective roles), to the inimitable Richard Harris and the amusingly shallow Kenneth Branagh, all actors portray their characters as if they were the only ones born to play them.

By now, most everyone is aware of the magical, occult-like elements contained in the Harry Potter series. Chamber of Secrets is no different. That, plus the inclusion of some intensely scary moments involving CGI-animated creatures may make the film unsuitable for the younger or more sensitive members of the family.

For those old enough to discern spiritual truth from error, there are some thematic elements in this film which could be used to teach certain truths that God would have us know and remember. The most obvious to me is the conflict which stems from the belief that some characters in the movie hold: mainly that wizardry should be practiced only by those of pure wizard blood, rather than by "mudbloods."

We can see that Rowling's fantasy imagination has its roots in the real world and involves issues which man has continually faced throughout history. The "final solution" of the Aryan nation was born from a smug belief that other races were somehow inferior to theirs. Going back in time even further, the early church members had to renew their minds to the newly revealed truth that God's grace extended beyond the twelve tribes of Israel to also include the Gentile nations.

Where there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond nor free: but Christ is all, and in all. Colossians 3:11 (KJV)

God does not judge man by the pureness of his blood but rather the pureness of his heart. We would do well to follow the exhortation of Philippians 2:3 and in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves."
