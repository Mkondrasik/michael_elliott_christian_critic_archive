Sermon / spiritual topic: Trust; Faithfulness; Discernment; Relying on God

Author-verified transcription

Thor’s been a busy god since he last helped save New York City in Marvel’s The Avengers. In Thor: The Dark World he returns to Asgard, where he promptly goes to work setting things right in the nine realms after they apparently fell into chaos and turmoil during his absence. A few swings of his hammer later, all is right with the worlds… or is it?

It turns out that there is a dark energy that predates even the Asgardians. Called The Aether, this energy is apparently indestructible, unstoppable and about to be discovered again after Thor’s grandfather helped to divert it from consuming all of life a few millennia ago.

The bad news for Thor (Chris Hemsworth) is that the person stumbling across this alien energy that defies physics, logic, and all natural law is none other than his love interest from the first film, Dr. Jane Foster (Natalie Portman). Once she becomes “infected” by this dark force, Thor has little choice but to bring her back to Asgard to meet Mom and Dad (Odin, played by Anthony Hopkins and Frigga, played by Rene Russo) in the hopes of ridding her of the evil energy that lies within.

In the meantime, The Aether has awakened Malekith (Christopher Eccleston), the leader of the Dark Elves who wish to use The Aether to return the universe to a perpetual state of darkness. Thor, in an attempt to defeat the darkness, has no choice but to turn to the one person containing the knowledge he needs: his evil brother Loki (Tom Hiddleston). But can he trust him?

Trust can be a leap of faith at times. It means to look beyond one’s self and one’s own capabilities to rely on someone else to do something beyond one’s control. Our level of trust often is based on our experiential history. It is easier to trust when someone has proved his level of trustworthiness. It is much harder to trust someone if they have a history of disappointing.

“Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint.” Proverbs 25:19 (KJV)

Misplaced trust is only evident when it is too late. It is only until we leave ourselves vulnerable to disappointment that we discover that we erred in judgment. A broken tooth will not enable us to chew effectively but we do not know that for sure until we first bite down. A foot out of joint will not support our full weight but we only discover this when we try to take our first step. And an unfaithful man will abandon us (or worse turn on us) in time of trouble. And many times, we discover this far too late to be of help in the situation we find ourselves.

Fortunately for us we need not place our trust in an unfaithful man… or any man for that matter. We are told to cast our gaze higher; to place our trust in the Almighty because He will never disappoint and He will always provide for us the best solution in every situation.

“Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.” Proverbs 3:5-6 (KJV)

God has established a track record of coming through in times of trouble. We can place our absolute trust in Him knowing that He will do what He says He will do. God is faithful and He does not disappoint. Of that we can have no doubt.
