The Out-of-Towners

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 2, 1999
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Romans 8:35-392 Corinthians 11:25-30Psalms 18:29

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Perseverance; adversity; commitment; overcoming obstacles; faithfulness

Author-verified transcription

In 1970, Neil Simon wrote a screenplay entitled The Out-of-Towners. Directed by Arthur Hiller, starring Jack Lemon and Sandy Dennis, this now classic comedy told the story of a husband and wife during a frenzied and obstacle-laden visit to New York City.

In 1999, Sam Weisman, Marc Lawrence, Steve Martin, and Goldie Hawn have teamed up to make a movie also called The Out-of-Towners, to tell the story of a husband and wife during a frenzied and obstacle-laden visit to New York City.

To compare the two films is inevitable yet almost does both a disservice, for despite the similarities, they are not the same. Where the former movie focused on and drew its comedy from the increasingly harried attempts by Jack Lemon to make an important job interview on time, the latter film only uses those events as a framework within which to explore a marriage relationship that has grown cold.

Henry Clark (Steve Martin, Father of the Bride) is an Ohio based advertising executive poised to attend the biggest interview of his life. Nancy (Goldie Hawn, The First Wives' Club), his wife of twenty-seven years, is suffering from "empty nest" syndrome as she watches the last of their children leave the house bound for college.

Events conspire to keep Henry from his job interview. Their plane is diverted to Boston, their luggage is lost by the airline, they miss the last train to NYC, they get mugged and robbed of all their money, they lose their hotel room, and the list just keeps getting worse... But it is exactly these obstacles and challenges that give Henry and Nancy the opportunity to see each other afresh for the first time in a quarter century, thus rekindling their romance.

Mr. Martin has ample opportunity to utilize his unique brand of physical comedy as well as his dry wit as the staid and somewhat stuffy ad executive. Inadvertently stumbling into a group sex-therapy session, Henry begs off from participating by stating, "We're from Ohio... We don't talk about sex."

Miss Hawn serves as a fine comic counterpoint as his wife who wants to "suck the marrow out of life." But it is John Cleese (A Fish Called Wanda) who, in his small supporting role of Mr. Mersault, the snooty manager of a luxury hotel (showing the shadings of his Fawlty Towers persona), nearly walks away with the picture.

It is disappointing that director Sam Weisman (Bye, Bye Love) and screenwriter Marc Lawrence (Forces of Nature) found it necessary to heavily sprinkle the film with sexual innuendoes and references. If this is what is needed to update a film in order to make it appealing to a '90's audience, it is a sad commentary on how we define ourselves as a people and what we look for in entertainment.

A far more positive message can be found in the framework of the film... The framework borrowed from the original screenplay by Neil Simon. From beginning to end, many obstacles were placed in the way of Henry and Nancy. Obstacles which threatened to stand in their way of achieving their desired goal.

As long as we live in this world, we will meet with obstacles that may tempt us to abandon our desired goal of living lives pleasing to God. To overcome those obstacles takes a commitment and perseverance to adhere to the written standard of the Word of God regardless of the outside influences that may beset us.

Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us. Romans 8:35 & 37 [KJV]

We have the ability to stand faithful throughout any adversity. Just as Henry and Nancy didn't forsake their goal of making it to that meeting on time, we should be as adamant never to weaken in our resolve to live righteously. As we do, God promises us the ability to overcome any obstacle.

For by thee I have run through a troop; and by my God have I leaped over a wall. Psalms 18:29 [KJV]
