After the Sunset

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 12, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Jeremiah 13:23 Psalms 55:21 Ephesians 4:28

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Habit; behavior patterns; change; discipline

Author-verified transcription

If you’re a high tech jewel thief whose exploits have made you a multi-millionaire which has allowed you to retire to an island paradise with your beautiful and sexy partner, would you risk all that in order to steal one more diamond from an impenetrable FBI protected location? If you’re a character in a Hollywood film, the answer is a no-brainer. Of course you would.

Actually, having no brain would be an asset while watching Brett Ratner’s newest comedy crime caper, After the Sunset. It’s a high-gloss, high-concept film that will fall completely apart the minute any thought is given to it. But Ratner, who cut his directorial teeth on the Rush Hour films, isn’t interested in appealing to the intelligentsia. Using a scruffy Pierce Brosnan and a scantily clad Salma Hayek, he’s made a film for audiences who like to watch pretty people in pretty settings following a pretty stupid plot.

Brosnan is Max Burdett, jewel thief extraordinaire, who has thus far managed to avoid capture and prosecution even though everyone in law enforcement seems to know that he’s guilty. His partner is Lola Cirillo (Salma Hayek) whose main assets are obvious... especially when she bends forward.

After their last heist, they retire to an offshore location to enjoy the rest of their lives in soon-to-be wedded bliss. Suddenly FBI agent Stan Lloyd (Woody Harrelson) appears on their doorstep because a cruise ship carrying an exhibit of precious gems including the elusive Napoleon diamond which Lloyd figures Burdett will try to purloin will be docking at this island resort for a weeklong stay.

Burdett, of course, pleads innocent, insisting that he’s retired and that he has no interest in the diamond. Lloyd doesn’t believe him. Lola doesn’t believe him. And we don’t believe him... Nor are we supposed to.

The humor of the film comes from the odd symbiotic relationship between Lloyd and Burdett. In a different universe, the two would be best friends. In fact, even in this universe they appear to be chummier than you’d expect a notorious thief and straight arrow Federal agent to be.

Don Cheadle stirs up the plot pot as the island’s main criminal element who tries to enlist Burdett to steal the diamond for him. Naomie Harris, as an island cop, provides a romantic interest for Woody.

The film starts with a fast paced and completely improbable jewel theft which sets the stage for what is to follow - a film that critics will lambaste but audiences will pay their hard earned money to see anyway. For all its faults, it has a mindless entertainment value that no critic can take away.

At the core of the film should be the question of Burdett’s criminal intentions. Is he trying to “go straight” or is it a ruse? The problem is that the answer to that question is immediately obvious. We can even find it in the Bible.

Can the Ethiopian change his skin, or the leopard his spots? then may ye also do good, that are accustomed to do evil. Jeremiah 13:23 (KJV)

The key word in that passage is “accustomed.” It is translated into English from the Aramaic word “limmud” meaning disciplined. Our patterns of behavior are ingrained into us through repeated and habitual practice. While it is possible to break those patterns and change our behavior it does not happen unless we discipline (or accustom) ourselves to a different way. That takes time and faithful practice. It is clear from the start that Burdett has not changed his spots.
