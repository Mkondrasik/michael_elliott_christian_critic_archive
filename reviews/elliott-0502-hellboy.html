Author-verified transcription

Chalk up another reason for comic book fans to go to the theaters. This time the movie is Hellboy, based on the unusual Dark Horse comic book character by Mike Mignola.

Nazi Germany, in order to find a miracle that would allow them to win their war, engages the occult master Rasputin (Karel Roden) to open a portal and bring forth a demon from hell that will lead the world to the apocalypse. Fortunately for the world, Professor Broom (John Hurt) is on hand and takes the small red devil and raises it as his own son, teaching him right from wrong.

Now grown to monstrous proportions, Hellboy (Ron Perlman) works for the Bureau of Paranormal Research and Defense as its number one defender against "things that go bump in the night." When Rasputin returns to restore Hellboy to the purpose for which he was born, things start bumping with alarming regularity. The biggest problem facing the BPRD is a hideous creature called Sammael, the Hound of Hell. Every time it is killed, two new ones are resurrected to take its place.

Believe it or not, Hellboy has more important things on his mind. He's in love with Liz Sherman (Selma Blair), who could pass in the "normal" world if it wasn't for her pyro-kinetic ability which she has trouble controlling. She's liable to burst into flames at times of high emotional stress. It's a good thing that Hellboy is fireproof.

What he isn't is normal. He's huge; he's got horns and a tail; and he's bright red. Unlike Liz, he just can't slip away to grab a cup of coffee at the neighborhood diner. It's a stumbling block in their relationship.

Hellboy is an interesting, complex character and Ron Perlman plays him to perfection. He's a kind of demonic working stiff with a wry sense of humor, a cocky but also self-effacing attitude, and enough emotional issues to keep a dozen psychiatrists occupied.

Doug Jones plays another freakish member of the BPRD, an amphibian named Abe Sapien who breathes underwater and has heightened sensory perceptions. Rupert Evans, in his film debut, does a nice job as the fresh-faced FBI trainee assigned to be Hellboy's liaison. He serves as the audience's surrogate and we are introduced to most of these supernatural events through his eyes.

There is a great deal of focus placed upon the occult and paranormal activity as well as an abundance of gruesome violence. A considerable amount of caution should be adopted when choosing if this is an appropriate film for you or your family. But surprisingly, Christian messages are communicated quite clearly during the course of the film. Some may be put off by the devilish influences that permeate the film - and this film is not for everyone's taste. Those who decide to view it might want to consider the major theme of the story: the right to choose.

Ultimately, the film is all about choice. Hellboy, though he was "created" for an evil or sinful purpose, was given the choice to serve good by a father who loved him. We don't have horns or a tail, but our story is similar. We were born dead in sins, subject to the god of this world and his evil ways. Our Father loved us enough to give us a choice as well as the ability to turn away from the evil and embrace the good.
