The Place Beyond the Pines

Sermon / spiritual topic: Fatherhood; Parenting; Role Models; Responsibility

Author-verified transcription

The Place Beyond the Pines is one-third of a great movie. Unfortunately its greatness is all contained in the first act. What follows gets progressively less interesting to watch.

The film opens with the character of Luke (Ryan Gossling), an expert motorcyclist who learns that his fling with a local waitress last time he passed through this town has resulted in a child. The subsequent blush of paternal responsibility leads him into some foolish and foolhardy decisions.

Act Two shifts over to follow the life of Avery, a rookie cop (Bradley Cooper), who struggles over the corruption he is discovering within his own police force while at the same time suppressing the guilt he feels over a shooting that has publicly labeled him as a hero.

In Act Three we skip forward fifteen years to view how the offspring of these men have turned out. Let’s just say that neither of the two men will be receiving “Father of the Year” awards any time soon.

Gosling’s smoldering presence makes the first third of the film as engrossing and entertaining an experience as we might want. But that quality simply isn’t consistently sustained with Bradley Cooper’s character and it is completely missing by the time the next generation appears for their time in the spotlight.

The Bible, of course, is rife with examples of father/son relationships. It is such an important bond that God chose it as the one He desired to have with us. In the film, we are presented with two men, both of them young fathers. Luke, who deeply wants to be a good dad, tries to go about it in the wrong way and loses his opportunity. Avery is so lost in his own ambitions that he doesn’t make the time to give his son what he most needs.

“If a son shall ask bread of any of you that is a father, will he give him a stone? Or if he ask a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent? Or if he shall ask an egg, will he offer him a scorpion? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?” Luke 11:11-13 (KJV)

Among the many things a father can give to his son, one of the most important is a positive and beneficial role model that the child can later emulate. Both of these characters fail to give this “good gift” to their respective sons. Unfortunately, in such cases, it is always the sons that seem to pay the price.
