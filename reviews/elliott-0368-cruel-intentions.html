Cruel Intentions

Author-verified transcription

What's next? The Muppet Babies do Lolita? As a high concept picture, Cruel Intentions is one of the worst ideas to have made it to the screen in recent memory. A teenage version of Dangerous Liaisons? What were the filmmakers thinking?

As with the 1988 film version which starred Glenn Close and John Malkovich, Cruel Intentions is based on Christopher Hampton's play, Les Liaisons Dangereuses which weaves a complex tale of seduction, deception, and treachery.

In this pubescent retelling of the story, Kathryn Merteuil-Valmont (Sarah Michelle Gellar, Simply Irresistible) is the popular class president of a highly exclusive private academy. What few people realize is that behind her pretended mask of propriety lies a heart as cold and devious as a serial killer's.

It is a heart that she shares only with her stepbrother, Sebastian (Ryan Phillippe, Playing By Heart.) These two take their pleasure in bringing forth misery in others. To them, people are merely conquests to be had or toys with which to play.

The latest challenge? The virginal Annette (Reese Witherspoon, Pleasantville), the daughter of the new headmaster of their academy, who has made her chastity a national issue by publishing a manifesto.

Sebastian and Kathryn make an obscene wager that is based on his ability to take Annette to bed. In the process, the web they weave ensnares a good number of their classmates. What they didn't expect was for Sebastian to fall in love with his intended victim.

The original play (and novel upon which the play was based) deals with highly complex psychological issues... with characters motivated by drives that Freud could have spent his career studying. To expect these young performers to capture the depth of emotion and fine nuances of the sick and perverted minds of these characters is asking far too much from them.

Perhaps if writer/director Roger Kumble (Provocateur) had adapted the tale to fit the age of his characters, he may have had something believable to share. But watching these young performers act out these sexual fantasies and perverse games was like seeing a 5 year old dress up in mommy's clothes and play make believe. We just get the feeling that all the characters are trying to act 10 or 15 years older than they are (with one obvious exception).

Neither Miss Gellar nor Mr. Phillippe have the range or experience needed to make their characters fully believable. Instead of being intrigued by their machinations, we tire of the smug, petulant arrogance of their youth.

Miss Witherspoon does not fare much better in this ill-concocted version. We simply don't see the underlying conflict and internal struggle that should be at the core of her character.

Selma Blair (Zoe, Duncan, Jack, and Jane) as Cecile, one of the pawns that figure into the scheming of Kathryn and Sebastian, has been directed to play her character as if eight were both her age and IQ percentage. Perhaps the thought was that, by comparison, the leads would appear more mature and sophisticated. Regardless, her portrayal was simply embarrassing to watch.

A parent's job is hard enough in this day and time. We certainly don't need films like these that blatantly cater to the youth of our society, sending messages to them that contradict any kind of sound, intelligent parental influence.

Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child; but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him. Proverbs 22:15 [KJV]

That rod of correction is the reproof and instruction of the Word of God. That's the parents' primary function - to teach their children how to live righteously. If we leave them on their own to figure life out or to "follow their heart", God tells us that we will be raising fools.

Knowing that teens are impressionable and seek role models whose behavior they will emulate, it is hard to say whose intentions were more cruel here... The characters in the film? Or those behind the scenes who are responsible for the making of it?
