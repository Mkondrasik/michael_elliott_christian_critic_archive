Facing the Giants

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
September 29, 2006
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Mark 10:27Jeremiah 32:17Job 42:2

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: God; faith; trusting God; possibilities; humility

Author-verified transcription

Regular readers of Movie Parables will recognize that I'm a big fan of sports oriented films. The main reason for this is that I find the formula of the sports genre fits exceedingly well with the philosophy of my site – finding spiritual lessons or analogies in the films of today.

Facing the Giants takes the essence of my reviews to the nth degree by giving us a sermon in the guise of a football story. It will be no surprise to those who have seen the film to hear that most of its cast and crew are members of the same church in Albany Georgia.

The film deals with an average football team, the Shiloh Eagles, and its sad sack of a coach, Grant Taylor (writer/director Kendrick) who, for the life of him, can't put together a winning season. He also can't seem to father a child and it seems that he may have trouble holding onto his job.

But as he recommits his life to God and dedicates the season, win or lose, to His glory, both he and his team enjoy a renewal of faith and the successes that follow.

As the film begins, it is painfully clear that this is not a Hollywood production. Earmarks of amateurism are evident in every frame. But the story has a strong message and, as the film progresses, that message is what hooks us and keeps us not only interested but engaged.

True, much of the film's elements have been borrowed from other sports films (the underdogs, the crippled kicker, the inspirational speeches, etc.), and its place kicker is named David and the championship game is against the Giants. Were it not for the earnestness of those involved, it would be easy to dismiss Facing the Giants as simply another well-intentioned cinematic project by Christian believers.

But while Kendrick and company may not have the professional chops to rival Spielberg and his associates, they clearly have something (or someone) in their corner. Despite the odds and despite the many shortcomings, when taken as a whole... the film works. It is entertaining, emotionally moving, and spiritually edifying. And I wish I could say that about every film I review.

The tagline of Facing the Giants and a verse of scripture we often hear quoted by its characters is: “With God, all things are possible.” I would think this was a verse that had special meaning to all those involved in the making of this little film.

The full verse reads as follows:

And Jesus looking upon them saith, With men it is impossible, but not with God: for with God all things are possible. Mark 10:27 (KJV)

It is a remarkable thing to discover how much more we can accomplish when we simply stop trying to do it all by ourselves. It is when we humble ourselves and turn to God for His strength, guidance, and deliverance that we discover how much farther we can go with Him.

We may have limitations but God knows them far better than we and still He encourages us to do our best with what we have and trust in Him to give us the results that we desire. It may take a miracle or two but, as the movie depicts, there is nothing that God can't do for those who love Him and put Him first.
