Author-verified transcription

Shades of Princess Diaries! The fairy tale of Cinderella once again gets dusted off and given a modern day makeover in The Prince & Me. This time, instead of inheriting a crown, our princess to be is given an opportunity to marry into one.

Julia Stiles stars as Paige Morgan, a premed student and farm girl at heart who has kept herself focused on her studies and plans to become a doctor so she can heal the underprivileged third world nations.

Into her school and life breezes Eddie (Luke Mably) who, unbeknownst to her is the Crown Prince and future king of Denmark. He's got an earned "bad boy" reputation which his country's tabloid newspapers helps to keep alive. After his latest indiscretion, he decides to duck out of public scrutiny by enrolling himself into the University of Wisconsin. Of course, he keeps his royal heritage a secret so he might get a taste of what it is like to be a commoner.

When Paige initially rebuffs his ill-mannered attempts at seduction, he becomes intrigued, then infatuated, and eventually head over heels in love with her. She, despite her better judgment, allows herself to respond to the attraction which is growing between them.

Then, of course the moment of the big revelation comes and Paige finds that she has a fairly big decision to make. Does she continue with her studies and plans in order to become the doctor she always wanted to be? Or does she stay with Eddie to become the future queen of Denmark?

The film works reasonably well for such a well trod formulaic story. Credit for that goes to some fairly decent chemistry taking place between the adorable Julia Stiles and the British actor Luke Mably. Their scenes together reflect a level of comfort and familiarity which adds credibility and draws the audience along for a ride that they've admittedly taken before.

Adding to the fun is the work of Ben Miller as the prince's beleaguered but unflappable assistant. Again, it is pretty much a prerequisite for films such as these but the actor is pleasant enough to watch that we don't mind the convention.

That isn't to say that every element of the film works. Some don't work at all. One long segment in particular that smacks of being "filler" is an extended lawnmower race between the prince and the best lawnmower racers Wisconsin has to offer. There's really no reason why the scene was included except to pad the film's running time for some reason.

Certainly, the major conflict in the film lies within Paige who is torn between two desires: the love she feels for Eddie; and the dream she has long carried for herself. It is a conflict that many of us have also felt. We know what we want until something else comes along that we also want and suddenly leaves us feeling that we have to make a choice.

Something which Paige must come to learn is that if we become too rigid in how we are to accomplish the goals we set for ourselves, we can lose out upon any additional blessings which God may wish to bestow upon us.

Now to Him who is able to do immeasurably more than all we ask or imagine, according to His power that is at work within us.. Ephesians 3:20 (NIV)

A God who is able to do more than we can ask or think, will often do just that. If we become inflexible as to how our prayers are "supposed" to be answered, we will miss out on the extra-abundance that God wants us to have.

The goals Paige set for herself were wonderful, admirable goals. But when Eddie came into her life, she became convinced that she had to choose between the two. Had she been more flexible with the "hows" of her plan, she would have realized that there was a way to have both.
