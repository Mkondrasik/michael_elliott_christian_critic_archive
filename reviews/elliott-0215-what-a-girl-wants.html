What a Girl Wants

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 4, 2003
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 2:12Ephesians 2:5Romans 10:9

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Fatherhood; salvation; reconciliation with God; belonging; spiritual family

Author-verified transcription

The answer to the titular question, What A Girl Wants, is answered in the first few minutes of the film. In the case of 17-year-old Daphne Reynolds (Amanda Bynes, Big Fat Liar), it is to have a relationship with her father whom she has never met.

She, of course knows who and where he is because she's heard the story all her life. Libby, her bohemian-like mother (Kelly Preston, A View From The Top), met Lord Henry Dashwood (Colin Firth, Bridget Jones Diary) while traveling in Morocco. They got married by a Bedouin chief but when they returned to his family manor in England, she was not welcomed with open arms. Seeing her as a liability to his political future, his advisors convinced Libby to return to America without him. She did, learned she was pregnant and chose not to tell Henry lest he reject her child the way she thinks he rejected her.

Seventeen years later, and sick of feeling a hole in her heart where a father's love should be, Daphne decides to travel to England and introduce herself to her father. Her timing is a bit off. Lord Dashwood is embroiled in a heated election and the sudden appearance of an American teen aged girl claiming to be his love child becomes the scandal that the British tabloids love to exploit.

In order to fit into Dashwood's stuffily proper life of service and responsibility, Daphne must suppress her bubbling American vivaciousness. It is something easier said than done. At the same time, the appearance of his young vibrant daughter reminds Henry of all he has sacrificed in order to represent his esteemed family name for the good of England.

The film starts off a bit sluggishly with Amanda Bynes trying a bit too hard to pump up the energy. That changes once Daphne reaches England where the clash of cultures becomes far more interesting to watch.

Also in the cast is Anna Chancellor (Four Weddings and a Funeral) as Dashwood's opportunistic fiance; Jonathan Pryce (Tomorrow Never Dies) as his slimy advisor; Eileen Atkins (The Hours) as the sympathetic Dashwood matriarch; and Oliver James (in his film debut) as a young English musician who falls for Daphne.

Directed by Dennie Gordon (The Adventures of Joe Dirt), it is a mildly entertaining, if not particularly innovative, film. One that is elevated by the fresh-faced appeal of Amanda Bynes (who should draw the target audience of young girls) and the solid presence of Colin Firth (who should draw their mothers).

Of course, we can all identify with Daphne because we all have experienced the emptiness caused by the separation of a father. More specifically, of our spiritual Father. When we are born into this world, we have no spiritual connection with God, our Creator. Many of us live a long time with a hole in our heart where a Father's love should be.

"That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world." Ephesians 2:12 (KJV)

Thankfully, because of the accomplishments and sacrifice of Jesus Christ, a way has been made to reconnect with our Father who longs for us to reach out to him. We need not feel alone or abandoned. We need only believe and accept His love as we follow His instructions which are outlined in Romans 10:9

"That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved (made whole)." Romans 10:9 (KJV)
