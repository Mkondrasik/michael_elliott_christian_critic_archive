Snitch

Author-verified transcription

What is a father to do? That is the central question behind Snitch, a film loosely based upon the true story of a teenager sentenced to 10 years in a federal penitentiary and the extreme steps his father takes to try to get him released.

Dwayne Johnson stars as John Matthews, owner of a construction business and father to Jason (Rafi Gavron), a college bound son who has been living with John’s ex-wife. When a “friend” of Jason’s uses him as a drop location to transport a parcel of drugs, the feds are waiting to take Jason into custody. Due to a new minimum penalty law for drug trafficking, Jason is looking at ten to thirty years behind bars. The only loophole is if he agrees to implicate someone else. If the feds get another arrest, Jason will get a reduced sentence.

The problem is Jason knows nothing about drugs or who is involved in them and he is unwilling to set one of his innocent friends up as a patsy in a drug sting. So it falls upon straight arrow John to put everything on the line and go “undercover” into the drug world to satisfy the federal prosecutors requirements to help his son.

Dwayne Johnson does a credible job as a desperate father out of his depth trying to save his son from a paying a steep price for a stupid mistake. Admittedly his pro wrestler body image works against him in the film. A flabbier, more average American male would have better served to highlight the absurdity of the DEA’s practice of using the general public to do their dangerous dirty work.

Jon Bernthal does some very nice work as an ex-con trying to leave his past behind him only to get sucked in against his will by John’s scheming. Susan Sarandon plays her federal prosecutor role with an ambitious and uncompromising air. Barry Pepper as an undercover DEA agent lends an air of authenticity to the sometimes over-sensationalized exaggerations of the story.

The film departs greatly from the real events upon which it was based. The true story was reported on a PBS Frontline documentary in 1999. The real John Matthews was unsuccessful in his attempts to infiltrate the drug world or implicate others. The real Jason Matthews pled guilty to the drug charges and served the ten-year minimum sentence. The documentary targeted the failed and hypocritical practice of mandatory sentencing. The film simply uses the practice as a plot point.

But in both the true version and the fictionalized version, the underlying message is the same. A father will move heaven and earth if it means saving or helping his son. Regardless of the wisdom of this particular father’s actions, his motivation is clear and should provide us with a high level of comfort.

“Or what man is there of you, whom if his son ask bread, will he give him a stone? Or if he ask a fish, will he give him a serpent? If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more shall your Father which is in heaven give good things to them that ask him?” Matthew 7:9-11 (KJV)

Our God, who is our heavenly Father, loves us with an immeasurable, unfathomable love. There is nothing that we can ever say or do to change that. It is in His very nature. And because it is, there is nothing He will not do to help us.
