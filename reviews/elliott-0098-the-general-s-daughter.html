The General's Daughter

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 18, 1999
Rating
3 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Isaiah 59:4Proverbs 21:3Ecclesiastes 8:11

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Justice; exposing evil; truth; responsibility

Author-verified transcription

John Travolta (A Civil Action) is an actor I never think of when mentally cataloguing my favorite film stars, yet when I think of his films, I’m hard pressed to find one that wasn’t well done.

He continues his streak with The General’s Daughter, a military mystery/thriller based on the novel by Nelson DeMille. Mr. Travolta plays Warrant Officer Paul Brenner, an undercover military cop who investigates criminal activity within the armed forces.

When Elizabeth Campbell (Leslie Stefanson, As Good As It Gets), the daughter of the base commander is raped and murdered, he is called on to peel away the subterfuge and layers of lies in order to reveal the truth. He is assisted by ex-lover and fellow investigator Sara Sunhill (Madeleine Stowe, Playing By Heart).

The suspects include James Woods (Vampires), as the girl’s commanding officer, James Cromwell (Babe), as the girl’s father, Timothy Hutton (Playing God), as the military provost on the base, Clarence Williams III (The Mod Squad), as the commander’s odd aide-de-camp, and just about every other male on the base as well as a few off base.

The abundance of suspects is one of the factors that allows the suspense in the film to build. Because of the way the mystery is unveiled, we are not sure who is guilty until the film gets ready to tell us.

The tension, which mounts quite quickly, is periodically released via scenes of humor or action. The humor predominately comes from the banter between Mr. Travolta and Miss Stowe. They play the history which the characters shared quite effectively, without directly addressing it.

Another aspect which worked beautifully for the film was the intellectual sparring between Brenner and Col. Moore, played by James Woods. Moore is the officer in charge of psychological operations. As the late Elizabeth told Brenner, “We [mess] with people’s minds.” There’s a lot of that going on in this film. Watching Moore and Brenner parry each other’s questions, using their words as probes to investigate what the other knows, is a definite highlight to the film and most welcome to this reviewer who has had his fill of insipid characters and inane dialogue. This is a smart film with intelligent characters which flow in and out of an intriguing story.

James Cromwell portrays the quintessential military man. His bearing, posture, and pattern of speech, are perfectly indicative of a lifetime of military service. He gives a very convincing performance.

Director Simon West (Con Air) has certainly crafted an intelligent thriller. Be advised that he also has included some graphic and disturbing images in his film. The rape of Elizabeth is especially brutal. But in the brutality, he makes a point which is spiritually valid. What should our response be to a crime that seemingly cannot be solved? Is it ever in our best interest to try and forget the crime took place?

Scriptures say:

Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil. Ecclesiastes 8:11 (KJV)

Brenner is relentless in his pursuit of the truth in order that justice may be done. Justice, it can be said, is more important than personal gain or personal sacrifice. As we get closer to the truth in this film, we find that others had an opportunity to respond to a despicable and criminal betrayal and perhaps, by doing so, change its tragic outcome. They stayed silent for what they perceived to be a “greater good.” In doing so, these military men betrayed not only Elizabeth, but also the principles that they vowed to uphold.

The scenes which depict the brutality may be disturbing and unsavory to watch, but the message comes through with great force. We must not ever, under any circumstances, for any reason, ever tolerate evil. We must expose it and seek justice. Without justice, there can be no peace.
