The Replacements

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 11, 2000
Rating
2½ stars

1 hr: 58 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Teamwork; Unity; Heart

Author-verified transcription

In 1987, an NFL players strike threatened to close down the entire football season. The team owners, in an attempt to keep the game (and their revenues) alive, decided to hire replacement players. As a longtime Washington Redskins fan, I found those replacement games to be even more exciting to watch than the regular games because of the “sandlot” quality of them. The replacements gave us the impression of having more in common with the average fan than the millionaire superstars who walked the picket line.

Screenwriter Vince McKewin (Fly Away Home) and director Howard Deutch (Grumpier Old Men) have taken the premise of this actual event and built a fictional story about a disparate group of has-beens and wannabees who come together for a second chance at glory. The Replacements captures that sense of the everyday man who reaches inside himself to become part of something special.

The Washington Sentinels haven’t been to the playoffs in over seven years. With four games left in the season, they must win three in order to gain a playoff position. When the league players strike, the team owner (Jack Warden, A Dog of Flanders) hires retired coach Jimmy McGinty (Gene Hackman, Enemy of the State) to build a team of replacements to last until the strike ends.

It is a mishmash crew he ends up with: a fast but butter-fingered wide receiver from a mini-mart; a sumo-wrestler turned offensive lineman; a cigarette smoking, beer-drinking Welshman called “The Leg” because he can kick a soccer ball the length of the field; a surly defensive back on “work release” from the Maryland institution where he was incarcerated; a crazed SWAT team member who doesn’t much care who he hits as long as he hits someone; and many others.

Leading this hodgepodge is retired quarterback Shane Falco (Keanu Reeves, The Matrix), best known for an abysmal performance during a Sugar Bowl. It is he who must somehow find a way to pull the replacements together to act and think as a team. And he has one week in which to do it.

The football sequences are quite funny, borrowing from other films like The Longest Yard and Unnecessary Roughness. John Madden and Pat Summerall, playing themselves, are given some of the better lines as they provide commentary on the shenanigans taking place on and off the field. A warning: the language is raw and there is a high level of sexual humor which comes mainly from the “replacement cheerleaders” hired from a nearby exotic dance club.

Mr. Reeves uses his unassuming and self-effacing nature to good effect as Falco. The contrast between his character, who cares about his teammates, and the character of the striking star quarterback Martel (Brett Cullen, The Killing Jar), who cares only about his bank balance, is clearly emphasized and is a good example of what truly drives success.

For a team of any kind to be successful, each individual must put the needs of their team above any personal goal or desire. Individually, they each make contributions but united, they must work together toward a common goal.

And the multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul: neither said any of them that ought of the things which he possessed was his own; but they had all things common. Acts 4:32 (KJV)

The believers of the first century Christian church were in one accord which is why they were so successful in building their church and moving the Word of God throughout the world. They weren’t motivated by personal glory or by money but rather it was their love for God that drove them forward.

In The Replacements, during half-time of a game being lost, McGinty was asked by a reporter what was needed for his team to get back into the game. His reply was one word: Heart. Not talent, not experience, not a bigger paycheck... Heart.

If we want to win in the spiritual contest of life, we must first strengthen our hearts with truth.
