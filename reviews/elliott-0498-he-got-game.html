Author-verified transcription

The latest "joint" belonging to director Spike Lee (Do The Right Thing) is He Got Game which takes us through a pivotal week of a young man's life. The young man, Jesus Shuttlesworth (Ray Allen of the NBA Milwaukee Bucks), is the nation's number one ranked high school basketball player. He is about to make the "most important decision he will ever make in his entire life." So say the myriad of college coaches who are recruiting him for their institutions.

This is heady stuff for a poor boy from the projects. To make matters worse, he's trying to make the decision on his own. His mother is dead; his father is in prison; and everyone else counseling him has their own self interests at heart.

With a week left to make this "most important decision of his life", his father Jake (Denzel Washington of Fallen) reenters the scene. He was released from prison by order of the governor to see if he could influence the young man to attend the governor's alma mater. If Jake succeeds, the rest of his prison sentence may very well be rescinded.

What the governor doesn't know is that the relationship between father and son is strained. Before Jake can hope to influence his son's decision, he has to first reestablish the bond that was long ago severed.

Mr. Washington gives what may be the most effective performance of his career. Gone are the pretty man Hollywood looks and the crisp educated dialect. Mr. Washington plays a man bowed but not broken by the system and the environment in which he lives. The redemption which the governor offers him lies deeper than the promise of personal freedom. Mr. Washington allows us to see Jake's internal struggle as he tries to connect with his estranged son and daughter.

Mr. Allen is not as adept on a sound stage as he is on a basketball court, but seeing as he is playing a character who is also stepping out of his element (a street player from the projects visiting top-ranked predominantly white colleges), his uneasiness works for him.

Mr. Lee's passion for the game of basketball is well documented. His courtside behavior at Madison Square Garden has even made national news. That passion can be seen on screen in many of the film's game scenes. A well-played game of basketball can be a thing of beauty and Mr. Lee captures the ballet-like moves of the offensive player, the hypnotic arc of the ball as it sails through the air into the basket, and the power of the defensive player denying his opponent from taking a shot. It is natural behavior that when you love something, as Mr. Lee obviously loves basketball, you want to portray it at its best.

For this reason we must conclude that, at some level, Mr. Lee must not care too much for women. The females of He Got Game, including Milla Jovovich (The Fifth Element) as a soft-hearted hooker, are cast in the most unflattering light imaginable. The sex scenes are hard, gritty, and bordering on pornographic. Many of the scenes are bluntly gratuitous. Whatever point Mr. Lee wishes to make, he could have certainly done it without asking his actresses to demean themselves on camera.

The depiction of the depths of corruption, avarice and greed surrounding college athletics is also quite troubling. Everyone who ever had anything to do with Jesus Shuttlesworth is now expecting a slice of the pie; a piece of the action. His Uncle Bubba, who took him in as a child.. his high school coach.. his two-timing girlfriend... everyone is expecting to cash in on Jesus. (Does that sound like any televangelist that you might know?) Once again, we see that truly "the love of money is the root of all evil." One of these days, mankind will get around to believing what the Word of God has been saying for centuries.

I'm not sure of the reason why Mr. Lee named the movie's central character after our Lord and Savior. It does, however, bring up an interesting point. Jesus is just a name. There are others who have been named Jesus -- some are even mentioned in the Bible. But there was only one Messiah. There is only one Jesus Christ, the only begotten son of God. Any other Jesus pales in comparison and importance.

The Jesus of this film kept hearing that he was making the most important decision of his life. That decision: Where was he going to play ball? As Christians, we see the absurdity of elevating that decision above all else. In the long run, it will be merely one more decision amongst a lifetime of decisions that Jesus Shuttlesworth will be called to make. The greatest and most important decision that man must make, we get to make every day: Am I going to live for God today? How we decide to answer that question will help shape all of eternity. The real Jesus always answered "Yes."
