There are some who believe that for every person in the world, there exists a corresponding "soul-mate." They believe that a person exists that is destined to be with them and only them. In the case of Fletcher McBracken (Brendan Fraser, George of the Jungle), a sweet-natured Texan street-performer who is the main character of Still Breathing, it is more than a belief... it is apparently a genetic reality.

It seems that the men in Fletcher's family dream about the women they love. That doesn't sound too far-fetched, does it? The weird thing is... they dream about her before ever actually meet her. Once the vision of their "true love" comes to them in the dream, all they have to do is find her and convince her to marry them.

Fletcher's true love, whose image he has pasted together in a Picasso-like montage using facial parts clipped out of various fashion magazines, turns out to be the beautiful Rosalyn Willoughby (Joanna Going, Inventing the Abbotts). But Rosalyn may not be the typical "dream come true." Jaded by life, she now supports herself by convincing men to invest their money in a fraudulent art scam she's concocted. To her, Fletcher is not her destiny, he's just another rich mark that needs fleecing. Trouble is, unbeknownst to her, he's not rich. At least, not financially.

Mr. Fraser plays Fletcher with a quirky gentleness and kindness that is immediately endearing. He allows the character to keep a sweet naiveté without sacrificing his intelligence. The simple life that Fletcher lives is one that is obviously of choice, not of necessity.

Miss Going is well cast as the LA girl hardened by life's many knocks. Her natural beauty is indeed the stuff dreams are made of.

Celeste Holm (Three Men and a Baby) makes a welcome appearance as Ida, the eccentrically grand matriarch of Fletcher's family.

First time writer/director James F. Robinson has put together an imperfect yet still charming tale of love conquering all... especially our own self-doubt.

All of us, at one time or another, have most probably considered ourselves unworthy of love. We may have deemed ourselves non-deserving of the care, affection and genuine love that someone may have been willing to bestow upon us.

Certainly that was the case with Rosalyn. She knew and recognized the person she had become and when Fletcher appeared with his vision of her as his fulfilled destiny, she could not immediately embrace the idea that she could be worthy of that kind of unconditional love.

How far a stretch is it for us to consider the love our heavenly Father has towards us? That He is both willing and able to not only forgive, but forget our past sins. That regardless of what we do now or in the future, He will not stop loving us.

But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. Romans 5:8 (NIV)

Such a love is hard to grasp. And it is sometimes difficult to accept. Fletcher was able to see past the image of the person that Rosalyn became and instead saw the heart of the person that she always was... deep inside. God sees us in much the same light. We just need to accept His love and believe that we can be the person that He says we can be.
