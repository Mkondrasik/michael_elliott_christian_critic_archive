Analyze That

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
December 6, 2002
Rating
1½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Sermon / spiritual topic: Change; transformation; repentance; renewing behavior; new life

Author-verified transcription

As Ben Sobol repeats periodically through Analyze That, "grieving is a process." That being the case, I'd suggest starting early. This sexually crude and largely unfunny sequel to Analyze This has given us plenty of reasons to mourn.

Sobol (Billy Crystal, Mr. Saturday Night) is the nervous psychotherapist who helped mob boss Paul Vitti (Robert DeNiro, City by the Sea) in the earlier film. He is once again called in as a consultant when Vitti shows signs of cracking while in the pressure filled prison environment of Sing Sing. It certainly isn't helping matters to learn that someone is trying to "whack" him. Even though Sobol's father has recently died, he agrees to get involved and Vitti is released to his watchful care.

Once out of prison, Vitti drops his insanity act and sets out to uncover his would be assassins. The only problem is that because he was released to Ben's care, Ben won't let him out of his sight. Let the "whacky" mob adventures begin.

In addition to DeNiro and Crystal, many of the original cast members are reprising their roles including Lisa Kudrow (Friends) as Ben's wife, Laura and Joe Viterelli (Shallow Hal) as Jelly, Vitti's right hand henchman.

Joining the cast are Cathy Moriarty-Gentile (Raging Bull) and Frank Gio (My Blue Heaven) as feuding mob bosses, neither of whom are happy to see Vitti out of prison.

The idea of an uptight nebbish therapist counseling an emotionally unstable Mafia don was good for some riotous laughter a few years back. But as Hollywood painfully proves time and time again, box office lightning rarely strikes twice in the same spot.

Though full of profanity, the original film was also filled with inspired hilarity. This time around, director Harold Ramis (Bedazzled) and cast kept the profanity and added sexual crudity but never quite found the humor. With a script offering few legitimate laughs, Billy Crystal and company settle for the Hollywood equivalent of borscht belt shtick. Perhaps there are some who will chuckle at the sight of Robert DeNiro prancing and singing the score of West Side Story or at Billy Crystal drooling out of a paralyzed lip but I can't count myself among them.

One of the running gags in the film is Sobol's attempts to find Vitti a "real" job... a job that doesn't require guns, bats, or piano wire. The problem is that Vitti brings his mob persona to every new job he gets. Whether working as a car salesman or as a maitre d' at an upscale restaurant, Paul Vitti's old life has a habit of following him.

It raises the question: is it really possible to change so dramatically? To go from being a cold blooded killer to a law-abiding citizen? God certainly thinks so. The evidence can be found in His Word.

Before the apostle Paul preached the good news of Christ to all of Asia Minor, he was known for relentlessly persecuting those that did. After he saw the light, his attitude took a full 180° turn.

The qualities that made him such a strong Pharisee did not depart from him. Many of those qualities is what made him such a strong disciple of the Lord. What changed was his motivation or heart. What caused him to change was his newly found awareness of the lordship of Jesus Christ.

I thank Christ Jesus our Lord, who has given me strength, that he considered me faithful, appointing me to his service. Even though I was once a blasphemer and a persecutor and a violent man, I was shown mercy because I acted in ignorance and unbelief. The grace of our Lord was poured out on me abundantly, along with the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus. 1 Timothy 1:12-14 (NIV)

Can people change? Sure. With the love of God, it happens all the time. People must do their part, by being willing to "put off the old man with his deeds" but it is God who enables us to change our ways.
