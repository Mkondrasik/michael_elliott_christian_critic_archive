Can't Hardly Wait

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 12, 1998
Rating
2 1/2 stars
Running time
1 hr: 40 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

2 Timothy 1:7Joshua 1:9Acts 18:9

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Fear; courage; action; parental guidance

Author-verified transcription

Graduation night and there's a party going on. It seems like the entire high school is on hand. Jocks, nerds, cheerleaders, geeks, the "in" crowd and those on the outside looking in. Now, on the eve of their future, everyone has gathered together in one big blowout bash to ask the two questions on everybody's mind: "Where's the beer?" and "Who wants sex?"

To be fair, thanks to an intelligent script and lively performances from its young cast, Can't Hardly Wait rises above the typical teen sex comedy.

Ethan Embry (Vegas Vacation) is Preston who has been carrying a statue of liberty sized torch (and a love letter) for prom queen Amanda (Jennifer Love Hewitt, I Know What You Did Last Summer) since freshman year. His problem is that she's been dating the football hero, Mike (Peter Facinelli, Dancer, Texas Pop. 81) throughout their entire high school life. Upon hearing that Mike has broken up with Amanda, Preston confides to his friend (and social outcast) Denise (Lauren Ambrose, In & Out) that he plans to go to the party to tell Amanda how he feels.

Also attending the party: Techno-nerd William (Charlie Korsmo, Hook) who has concocted a revenge plan against Mike in return for all the torment and abuse suffered at his hands; and Kenny (or Special K, as he calls himself) played by Seth Green (Austin Powers) who tries to be so cool and "in" that he fails to be either. Kenny's plan is to stay at the party until he finds someone to have sex with him.

The writing/directing team of Deborah Kaplan and Harry Elfont (A Very Brady Sequel) do a fine job of weaving these story lines together to come up with a cute and entertaining film.

Not that there aren't problems with the movie. Can't Hardly Wait falls into the same trap as many other teen-oriented films. Amazingly, there are no parental figures represented in this movie. These teen-age children are apparently on their own, making their own decisions, shaping their own futures without any kind of guidance or support other than that which they receive from their friends. This omission does a major disservice to the godly role a parent is to play in the life of his/her child. Sadly, more and more films, as they cater to the teenage market, choose to present the false and dangerous illusion that "teenagers know best."

Additionally, the depiction of free-flowing alcohol, the rampant obsession with sex, and the wanton acts of physical destruction are free of repercussions. While they are meant to evoke laughter (which they successfully do), no thought is given to the results of these actions or of the consequences they will bring. This is not only unrealistic, it is truly irresponsible.

On the positive side, the characters which we are meant to care most about are the exceptions to the rule. They don't drink. They are looking for love rather than sex. They prove themselves to be mature, responsible individuals which in and of itself lifts them a notch above those around them.

The audience can see from the beginning that it is simply a matter of time before Preston and Amanda get together. The only thing that is taking so long is the fear of rejection that keeps Preston from speaking his heart.

Fear is a crippling device of the devil. Throughout the scriptures we see that God had to continually remind His people to have no fear. "Fear not," "Be not afraid," "Be strong and have courage," are all exhortations from God that repeatedly appear in His Word. Fear must be defeated if we are to accomplish all that we desire.

Preston talks a lot about fate and signs of things meant to be. "Fate" is nothing more than a devilish smokescreen that keeps people in a passive state as they wait for their destiny to arrive. Many people will wait a lifetime for a ship that never comes in. Preston, unless he can learn to conquer his fear and take action, will be appearing in the sequel to Can't Hardly Wait titled Can Wait Forever, Because It's Never Going to Happen.
