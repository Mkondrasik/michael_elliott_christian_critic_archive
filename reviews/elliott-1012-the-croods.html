Author-verified transcription

If you have been waiting for The Flintstones to return to their animated glory, keep on waiting. While we wait we can enjoy their less advanced second cousins who have arrived on the screen in The Croods.

The Croods are a typical prehistoric family who have beaten the odds and survived the harsh and unforgiving world forming beneath their feet. Thanks for this must go to the patriarch of the clan, Grug (Nicolas Cage) who lives by one simple rule: “Don’t ever not be afraid.” Fear has kept him and his family alive all these years - mainly because he keeps them hidden inside a cave and far away from discovering anything new that might upset their precarious balance between life and death.

When teenaged daughter Eep (Emma Stone) slips out of the cave one night, she meets Guy (Ryan Reynolds), an outsider who is filled with new ideas and innovations – fire not being the least among them. When Guy warns of an impending danger, the Croods (including a reluctant Grug) decide to follow him to a land called Tomorrow where things, Guy promises, will be different.

The storytelling is a bit simplistic and the characters broadly drawn. While the artistic quality is first rate, the film itself never quite finds a steady stride. That said, it makes for passable entertainment for a rainy afternoon.

Grug makes a fundamental mistake when he promotes fear as a desired quality to be embraced. True, fear can keep us in check when we are about to perform a reckless or thoughtless action. But to map out a life plan based on the fears we carry will only serve to limit our lives, shrinking them to ungodly proportions.

Fear, by its very nature, is an inhibitor. It stops us from taking action that we need to take in order to grow or develop. It prevents us from making discoveries or choosing options that would benefit our lives. Fear will hold us back as surely as it would have held back the Croods, keeping them in caves and eventually causing their extinction.

It was Guy, representing the hope of tomorrow, who opened their eyes to the limitless potential of facing their fears and rising above them. It was he who enabled them to discover a brand new world.

“The fear of man brings a snare, but whoever leans on, trusts in, and puts his confidence in the Lord is safe and set on high.” Proverbs 29:25 (Amplified)
