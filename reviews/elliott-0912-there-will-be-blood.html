There Will Be Blood

2 hr: 38 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Greed; Love of Money; Choices

Author-verified transcription

Greed is NOT good (apologies to Wall Street's Gordon Gekko). But don't try telling that to There Will Be Blood's protagonist, Daniel Plainview (Daniel Day Lewis). He may just smile, nod and, once you turn your back, stab you in it.

There Will Be Blood is loosely based on Upton Sinclair's novel "Oil." Not having read it I can't speak to how loose the adaptation is. But the story is simple enough.

A prospector accidentally discovers oil and begins to make his fortune. His fortune takes a huge upturn with the success of a well in a small Texas town that is home to another opportunist, evangelist Eli Sunday (Paul Dano) who is looking to build his church. Their paths cross... repeatedly and ultimately tragically.

Plainview is accompanied by a young boy he introduces as his son, H.W. (Dillon Freasier). But he uses the boy more a means to humanize the avaricious oilman in him so the simple town folk will feel better about trusting him. They shouldn't. But that isn't something we immediately recognize.

Writer/Director Paul Thomas Anderson introduces Plainview to us slowly. (The first 20 minutes or more of the film are virtually void of dialogue). The film is primarily about the lead character's gradual descent into the monster his is to become. It is a lonely and empty life he lives because his only goal is to make money.

It is an observation that may have been easily missed if it were not for the late arriving inclusion of a mystery character named Henry (Kevin J. O'Connor), who claims to be Daniel's long lost brother. It is a brilliant addition to the story because it presents to us a missing element of Plainview's character... a longing for human relationships. His reaction to Henry's arrival and how that relationship plays out is central to understanding who and what Daniel Plainview is.

Paul Thomas Anderson isn't interested in making "popular" films. His earlier films (Boogie Nights, Magnolia, and Punch Drunk Love) were not mainstream commercial successes although his talent elevated them to a status worthy of notice. There Will Be Blood joins this list as a non-commercial film that will inevitably garner audience attention.

The film does have an ace card. Without Daniel Day Lewis' brilliant performance the film simply doesn't work. Normally, I can cite the names of all actors who appear in a major or supporting role in a film from memory. In this case, the persona of Daniel Day Lewis was so commanding, I had to look up his supporting cast in IMDb.com.

The one exception was Paul Dano, who played the small town evangelist. But that isn't necessarily a good thing. I remembered him because I felt he was too weak in a role that needed to be as powerfully felt as that of Plainview. That the film still garners a 2 ½ star review from me should reflect the strength of Daniel Day Lewis' performance.

But what the film communicates more than anything else is the danger that comes with the obsession of wealth. Daniel could have had a fine life. He was capable of having friends; building a family; working within a community towards the betterment of all men... but that wasn't the path he chose. He became embittered, suspicious, and distrustful of those who came into his life. Why? Because he didn't want to lose what he had gained. What he failed to ask himself was if what he had gained was worth losing all that he gave up.
