Author-verified transcription

The Spectacular Now tells a story familiar to everyone who has ever navigated the strange waters of a typical American high school. While most every student during his high school life manages to get himself (or herself) classified into some stereotypical clique or label, the reality is that such superficial divisions bear little meaning or give little insight into the soul of a person.

Sutter Keely (Miles Teller) is, on the surface, a hard-partying, quick-witted, congenial teen. Sutter presents to everyone the appearance of enjoying his life to the fullest. Aimee Finecky (Shailene Woodley) is content to just be one of the background faces, dwelling on the periphery of high school society, barely noticed and hardly remembered.

A chance meeting starts a friendship that turns into a spark and eventually a full-fledged romance. And we begin to see that these two characters cannot be so simply stereotyped. As individuals, their lives are complex and detailed. As their lives become intertwined, the complexities multiply exponentially.

This is an honest look at adolescence in today’s world and doesn’t sidestep the thorny issues of sex, alcohol use, and the consequences that come from poor decisions. The direction by James Ponsoldt is both sensitive and sure. The performances from the leads are deftly handled. But it is the script by Scott Neustadter and Michael H. Weber, based on the novel by Tim Tharp, that elevates the material from the standard teen soap operas we have been given over the years.

The title is a reference to Sutter’s philosophy of living in the moment and not worrying about the future but, as we come to learn, his philosophy is merely a cover for his fears. It is a cover that only works for a very short time. Eventually his past will catch up with him and his future will arrive only too soon, finding him ill prepared to handle it.

There is nothing wrong with focusing on the here and now. It is a very exciting and refreshing way to live life – but that doesn’t mean we are to ignore the past. Nor are we to blind ourselves to the future. Learning from the past; Living in the present; Looking towards the future. That is the walk or lifestyle of the wise.

”Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself yet to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: Forgetting what is behind [past] and straining toward what is ahead [future], I press on [present] toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus.” Philippians 3:13-14 (NIV)

There’s a trick to living in the spectacular now and that is not to try and dwell in it because the “now” is here and gone in the blink of an eye. The now is ever changing and we must change with it. By trying to live in a now moment too long, we will quickly find ourselves mired in the past. It is by living in each moment with our eyes and actions focused on the future that we may fully experience the richness and effulgence of the life God has designed for us.
