Author-verified transcription

It still remains the greatest story ever told. It is therefore all the more surprising to realize it has been ten years since The Passion of The Christ was released to an enthusiastic Christian audience who crushed box office records on their way to see their faith represented on screen. Son of God hopes to recapture that success with a compassionate, respectful depiction of the life of the one man who quite literally changed the world and became the most important figure in all of human history.

Produced by Mark Burnett and Roma Downey (who performs double duty by playing Mary, mother of Jesus,) Son of God is comprised of scenes originally aired as part of the History Channel’s mini-series The Bible along with never before seen footage to tell the story of the birth, earthly ministry, death, and resurrection of the Christ.

Such a subject will produce both awed appreciation as well as veiled (or often unveiled) criticism. It is impossible for the filmmakers, despite their valiant efforts to be all-inclusive, to appease every group or sect that exists, most who have different interpretations or understanding of the scriptures. I too found what I believe to be doctrinal errors in the film that contradict my current understanding of biblical truths. But such errors should not deter us from the many positive and spiritually edifying benefits that come from a cinematic viewing of the life of the Lord Jesus Christ. As the good book instructs, “Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.” 1 Thessalonians 5:21 (KJV) It is an exhortation to which I heartily subscribe.

And this production is very good. Portuguese actor Diogo Mordago stars as the title character with every ounce of the grace, love and compassion we would expect to find in the Lord. This is a very human and sympathetic Jesus, subject to the emotions, pains, joys and disappointments common to all men.

Other standouts in the cast are Adrian Schiller as the high priest Caiaphas who is unable to see past his own religiosity to recognize the presence of the Lord; Roma Downey as Mary who must suffer the torment of watching her son being crucified before her eyes; Darwin Shaw as Peter who fluctuates between being Jesus’s most devoted follower to one who denies knowing him; and Greg Hicks, as the politically minded Pilate, forced by events and circumstances to condemn the most innocent man who ever lived to a most painful and undeserved death.

The film is surprisingly fluid, flowing smoothly despite its episodic structure. The scriptures state that no book could ever contain all that Jesus did (John 21:25) and, similarly, there is no way for any film to contain all records relating to Jesus that we know from the scriptures. But many of the high points are there: feeding the multitudes; healing a lame man; raising Lazarus from the dead; walking on water; confronting the Pharisees; overturning the table of the money changers; and much more are all respectfully and dramatically depicted during its two and a quarter hour running time.

The central question is asked by Jesus himself when he says to his apostles: “Who do you say I am?” It is a question each of us must eventually answer for ourselves. It is the most important, life-altering question a man or woman will ever face. How we respond dictates the very path or course of our life.

Here are some of the answers that would be found acceptable and good in the eyes of God:

He is the way, the truth, and the life. (John 14:6)

He is the Alpha and the Omega. (Revelation 22:13)

He is the savior of the world. (1 John 4:14)

He is the redeemer of man. (Galatians 4:4-5)

He is the propitiation for our sins. (1 John 4:10)

He is the mediator between God and man. (1Timothy 2:5)

He is the Word made flesh. (John 1:14)

He is the only begotten Son of God. (John 3:16)
