The Wedding Crashers

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
July 15, 2005
Rating
2½ stars

1 hr: 59 mins
Source
Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Love

Author-verified transcription

John and Jeremy are used to being the life of the party... any party... anywhere. The only trouble is, it’s all an act. In The Wedding Crashers, these two plan their sexual lives around the marriage vows of other people.

Their job as divorce mediators has left Jack (Owen Wilson) and Jeremy (Vince Vaughn) more than a little jaded when it comes to the sanctity of the institution. What they do recognize is that weddings offer an easy opportunity to meet and seduce women who have been swept up in the romanticism of the event.

They are willing to say and do anything in order to reach their objective - bedding as many beauties as time and their libido will allow. They are incorrigible, unconscionable, and seemingly unstoppable... until they crash the wrong party.

Inserting themselves into the posh reception of the daughter of a high ranking government official (Christopher Walken), our modern day lotharios begin to break their own inviolate rules.

Jack shows signs of growing a heart when he meets Claire (Rachel McAdams) who happens to be engaged to a super-competitive jerk. Vince allows himself to be cornered by Claire’s crazed and sexually hyperactive sister Gloria (Isla Fisher). When the reception leads to a weekend invitation their friendship and their reputations are threatened. All because their lies begin to catch up with them.

This adult comedy contains a considerable amount of nudity, sexual situations, and coarse language. It also contains two strong performances by a pair of accomplished comedic actors who surprisingly compliment each other’s style and cadence.

Owen Wilson’s laid back, molasses thick speech patterns contrast nicely with Vince Vaughn’s rapid fire staccato. And yet despite the readily apparent differences in their cultural backgrounds, we have no trouble believing them to be the best of friends.

Rachel McAdams, as Jack’s dream girl, handles a relatively unchallenging role with unmemorable ease and comfort. Isla Fisher, as Jeremy’s nightmare, is absolutely hysterical - a woman that some, at first glance, might even consider to be borderline psychotic.

Screenwriters Steve Faber and Bob Fisher have done their research well, touching upon areas all too familiar to anyone who has ever attended a wedding or wedding reception. For that matter, anyone who has ever dared enter into the dating arena will find recognizable signposts, humorously exaggerated to be sure but nonetheless familiar.

What the film does not do is provide a positive example of a loving couple who has successfully charted the marriage waters. A definition of true love is provided during the course of the film. According to The Wedding Crashers, true love is the soul’s recognition of its counterpoint in another.

It sounds romantic. But love - true love - is based on more than romance. It is based on more than an emotional discovery. Love is a decision we make. It is a commitment we choose to accept. It is not so much that we find love as it is that we choose to love.

“And this is love, that we walk after commandments. This is the commandment, That, as ye have heard from the beginning, ye should walk in it.” 2 John 1:6 (KJV)

Love is not an emotion; it is the reason behind the emotion. It is not a discovery. It is a choice. John, tiring of the emptiness that followed his deceptions, finally made the choice to love. This choice, as difficult as it proved to be, was far more rewarding than any meaningless sexual conquest.
