Space Cowboys

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
August 4, 2000
Rating
2½ stars
Running time
2 hr: 10 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Proverbs 16:31Job 12:12Deuteronomy 4:9

Sermon / spiritual topic: Aging; Wisdom; Respect

Author-verified transcription

Regardless of age, boys will always have their fantasies. For the post WWII crowd, becoming an astronaut and going into space is a dream that many red-blooded American males may have carried at one time or another. In Space Cowboys, four Air Force pilots who make up the space exploration project known as Daedalus find out that they have to hold onto their dream just a little longer. Deemed too reckless and independent by the political powers that be, they've been pulled from the project and replaced... by a monkey.

Of course that was forty years ago and those boys have since moved on with their lives. But the dream never dies. When a wayward Russian satellite begins to fall out of orbit, it is found to have a guidance system so antiquated that there is only one aeronautical engineer able (read: old enough) to fix the problem. And this time, no chimp will stand in his way.

Frank Corvin (Clint Eastwood, True Crime), the original designer of that guidance system and the team leader of the long defunct Project Daedalus agrees to fix the problem only if his original crew flies with him. So, Hawk (Tommy Lee Jones, Double Jeopardy), now a daredevil crop duster, Jerry (Donald Sutherland, Instinct), now a roller coaster engineer, and Tank (James Garner, Twilight), now a Baptist minister, reconnect with Frank after 40 years to rekindle their never forgotten hope of blasting into space.

Sending these four geriatrics on a space shuttle was not the idea of NASA executive Bob Gerson (James Cromwell, The Green Mile), who was the commander who pulled the plug on these Air Force pilots forty years ago. And he is not above trying to scuttle their hopes once again.

The movie follows the intense and grueling physical training of this over-the-hill gang (the media labels them "The Ripe Stuff") with the expected comedic results. But once our gray-haired heroes leave the earth, they find their mission to be a lot more complicated than was first thought and the movie shifts to action mode.

Clint Eastwood is not only one of the stars of the picture, he also co-produced and directed it. While the pacing is a bit slow and the film loses some of its focus in the final reels, the special effects are impressive and the story, though derivative (read: Armageddon), is well-told.

The film could have benefited from a trim here or there. The romantic sub-plot between the aging Hawk and the much younger Mission Control Director (Marcia Gay Harden, Meet Joe Black) didn't particularly play well and I felt that the animosity between Gerson and Team Daedalus was over-emphasized to a ridiculous level.

Still, it is fun to watch this very accomplished and experienced cast at work. Mr. Eastwood's trademark scowl and squint still work for him. Mr. Jones' laconic drawl and easy-going delivery are also on display throughout the film. In a supporting role, William Devane (Payback) is excellent as a NASA flight director who finds the entire mission to be on the suspicious side.

All in all, the old boys still have it. And that should come to no surprise. Grey hair is not a sign of shame or disgrace. It is more like a badge of honor, deserving of respect.

Gray hair is a crown of splendor; it is attained by a righteous life. Proverbs 16:31 (NIV)

There is much to be learned from a life long lived. Younger generations would do well to give ear to their elders, asking questions and demanding answers. God has commanded parents to not only teach their children, but also teach their children's children. Within that generational span there is even more knowledge attained that can be of use to the generations following.

The young astronauts learned to respect their older counterparts during course of the mission. How much more they would have learned if they gave them the respect they deserved because of all they accomplished in the 60+ years before the mission?
