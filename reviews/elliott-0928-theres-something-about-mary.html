There's Something About Mary

Author-verified transcription

There's Something About Mary... We just aren't sure what that "something" is.

Directors Peter and Bobby Farrelly have taken a commonly-held high school fantasy and turned it into a movie. Granted, it takes a few warped turns here and there, but what else should we expect from the team that brought us Dumb and Dumber and Kingpin?

Ted Stroemann (Ben Stiller, Zero Effect) is a high school nerd who, through a quirk of fate, gets an opportunity to go to the prom with Mary, the prettiest girl in school, played by Cameron Diaz (My Best Friend's Wedding). His dream date turns into a nightmare when Ted is rushed to the hospital after having an embarrassingly serious zipper accident in Mary's bathroom. (We'll pause briefly to allow our gentlemen readers to finish cringing.)

Thirteen years later, Ted can't stop thinking about Mary. He decides to hire a sleaze ball of a detective (Matt Dillon, Wild Things) to find her. Miracle of miracles, the sleaze ball does! And in so doing, falls in love with Mary himself. There's nothing new there... Mary is apparently able to attract men with no effort and even less awareness.

Miss Diaz is beautiful enough to be the object of a million fantasies. But as she did in My Best Friend's Wedding, she also infuses her character with a genuine sweetness. The combination is killer.

Ben Stiller is appropriately geeky as the high school Ted. He certainly has the look down pat... greasy hair, braces, and all. His "grown-up" Ted is not as interesting. Mr. Stiller plays it pretty much straight, which gives his supporting cast the freedom to play extremely broad... and they make the most of that opportunity.

That supporting cast includes a very funny Matt Dillon as Pat Healy, the large-toothed private eye; W. Earl Brown (Kiss The Girls) as Warren, Mary's mentally challenged and ear-sensitive brother; Chris Elliott (Cabin Boy) as Dom, Ted's hive covered friend; and Lin Shaye (Living Out Loud) as the leathery-faced Magda.

The brothers Farrelly seem to enjoy pushing the envelope of convention to see how much audiences will accept. There's Something About Mary includes the already mentioned "zipper incident," as well as a masturbation scene with an unusual consequence, numerous jokes made at the expense of the physically or mentally handicapped, and a dog which suffers tremendous abuse, including a scene where Healy tries to revive the obviously dead animal by electric shock. All of the above are played for laughs. And for the most part, the audience responded enthusiastically. Sadly, to answer the Farrellys' implied question: it appears that today's audiences will accept just about anything.

Somewhere in the midst of all this puerile humor, there's a movie that is trying to be told. A sweet little story about a somewhat average guy who falls in love with a recognizably above average girl.

And there is no doubt that Mary is definitely above average. It is abundantly clear. Put her in a room with one hundred other women, Mary is the one that would stand out. Some people have a certain quality that sets them apart from the crowd. Mary has that special something. But then again, so do we! The difference is that our special something is real and definable.

That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as lights in the world. Philippians 2:15 [KJV]

It is the living Word of God made manifest in our speech and actions that makes our light shine. As we faithfully stand upon the truth that we know, and boldly hold forth that truth at every opportunity, we make an impact. The world takes notice of us. They see that we are different... that there truly is "something about us." We know that the "something" is the manifested power of Christ in us, the hope of glory.
