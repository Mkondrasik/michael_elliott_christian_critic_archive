You Got Served

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
January 30, 2004
Rating
2 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Friendship; loyalty; adversity; reconciliation; support

Author-verified transcription

You Got Served is to break dancing as 8 Mile was to rap. In the film, groups of talented street dancers face off in high energy dance "battles" for pride and cash prizes. It's too bad that a lame and uninteresting story had to come along for the ride.

Elgin and David (L & D) are best friends who see dancing as their ticket to prosperity. With a crew comprised of other neighborhood friends, they regularly compete at Mr. Rad's warehouse against other crews from other neighborhoods. So far, L & D's has been the crew to beat, usually winning every competition they enter.

When a new crew from Orange County challenges them, L & D find themselves standing outside the winner's circle for the first time in a long while. To make matters worse, L is uncomfortable with the attention D has been giving his sister, Liyah. He puts his foot down and forbids them to date, forcing D to decide what is more important - the girl or his friendship and dance partnership. He opts for the girl.

When L runs into serious financial trouble, he has to risk everything on an MTV-sponsored dance competition called The Big Bounce. The winner will receive $50,000 and a spot in L'il Kim's next music video. But without D, L's new crew may not have what it takes to get past the Orange County dancers.

The dancers are fantastic. To use the vernacular, they got skills. But the excess baggage that the film carries (the romantic love story, a mobster looking for payback, a drive-by shooting) cheapens the film and lowers its appeal dramatically.

Director Chris Stokes, who manages the hip hop group IMX, gets a bit too cute with the camera and chops up the action instead of keeping the lens trained on the dancers who really are amazing.

Marques Houston, who plays Elgin, was a member of Immature and is currently in IMX. Houston's half-brother Omarion plays David and is in B2K, another group managed by Stokes. Omarion's cousin, J-Boog, is also in B2K and plays Rico, a crew member for L&D. Wade Robson, who plays the leader of the Orange County crew is a well known choreographer for *Nsync and Britney Spears.

The dances are full of energy, athleticism, and innovation. If the movie was comprised entirely of these dance sequences, it would be well worth the price of admission. But with such a poorly conceived and poorly executed story line, the value of the film sinks considerably.

Even though the story is on the lame side, the moral it tells is valid. Friends... close friends... need to be there for each other to lend support, encouragement, advice, and strength especially in times of trouble.

A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity. Proverbs 17:17 (KJV)

No argument or disagreement should ever stand in the way of a close friendship. If we cannot reconcile our differences with our friends, how will we ever reconcile with those less friendly?
