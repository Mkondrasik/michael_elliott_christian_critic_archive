The Great Beauty

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
November 15, 2013
Rating
2 stars
Running time
2 hr: 22 min
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Ephesians 3:13Colossians 1:10-111 Peter 4:3-4

Sermon / spiritual topic: Purpose; Meaning; Worldliness; Fruitful Living

Author-verified transcription

The Great Beauty (La Grande Bellezza), the 2013 Oscar winner for best foreign film, tells the story of a man whose one success led to a comfortable if meaningless life as a well recognized raconteur of the Italian high life once brutally immortalized by Federico Fellini in La Dolce Vita. Not surprisingly, the “life” that is on display is no more appealing now as it was then.

Jep Gambordella (Toni Servillio) wrote the critically acclaimed “The Human Apparatus” as a young man and has been resting on that single laurel ever since. Now, on his 65th birthday, he is finding that his circle of friends, his popularity, even his very existence all adds up to less than the sum of its parts. He won’t find much of an argument. The hedonism, pretentiousness, and debauchery shown on the screen evoke a sad and empty feeling. Unfortunately, it consumes much of the running time of this film.

Jep struggles to maintain his indifferent, superior air among the idle upper class, but we can see the signs of self-doubt trying to emerge. He learns of the passing of his once great love; he tries to kindle a relationship with an aging stripper; he seeks spiritual answers from a Roman Cardinal uninterested in providing answers; and through it all he uses his intellect and wit to keep a courteous distance from fully engaging in life.

Although the film has been critically acclaimed and touted as a worthy comparison to the films of Fellini, I found it to be slow moving and ultimately disappointing. There is a small emotional payoff at the end of the film but the getting there is excruciating.

Toni Servillio is well cast and cuts a fine well-tailored figure as a soul-wearied man about town. The nuances of his expression and vocalisms are exquisitely managed. There are no other real standouts in the cast mainly due to the shallowness of the characters they play but near the end of the film we are introduced to a character who does have a bit of substance to her and it is through her that Jep comes to a realization about who he is and what he has become.

God, as the Creator of life, had a purpose in mind when he formed his creation. We can sometimes forget this in the living of it. But seeing the meaningless existence of the characters in The Great Beauty played out for us over nearly two and a half hours, we can recognize that what they are doing and how they are living is surely not what God had in mind for us.

“Let us walk honestly, as in the day; not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying.” Ephesians 3:13 (KJV)

For what is the ultimate profit in such a life? What true benefits are gained from it? For forty years Jep lived a lascivious lifestyle and what does he have to show for it other than a sense of ennui and unhappiness? God has a much better plan in mind for us.

“That ye might walk worthy of the Lord unto all pleasing, being fruitful in every good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God; strengthened with all might, according to his glorious power, unto all patience and long-suffering with joyfulness;” Colossians 1:10-11 (KJV)

There is great beauty to be found in this world. Jep was looking for it in all the wrong places. To find great beauty, one first has to look within.
