Frozen

Author-verified transcription

There’s a new princess in town… two of them in fact. In Frozen, Disney adds to its formidable (and highly marketable) princess collection the characters of Princess Anna and Princess Elsa. And it would come as no surprise to me to learn that millions of little girls have switched their allegiance to make these wintery princesses their personal Disney favorites. I’m pretty sure their moms already have.

While Hans Christian Anderson’s The Snow Queen is credited as the source material, there is little of his story embedded in the film. Frozen focuses on the relationship between two sisters who become separated at a young age. The oldest, Elsa, is afflicted with an icy power she cannot control and so she fears she is a danger to all around her. She agrees to be isolated for the good of all.

Anna knows none of this. She only knows that her big sister whom she loves suddenly will have nothing to do with her. And so they grow up in the same castle… but they never come into contact with each other. That is, until the day of Elsa’s coronation when the castle doors must be opened so the realm may meet and greet their new queen. On that day, Anna’s enthusiasm for human contact overreaches and Elsa’s power is revealed to all. She flees her home and her regal responsibilities, leaving a frozen wasteland in her wake. Anna has no choice but to follow her sister and get her to restore the land to its idyllic state.

In reading this summary, it may seem to be a bit dark and dreary. The truth is that the film is so much more than the story it tells. Frozen is light, airy, and fresh with a feel-good aura that emanates from every frame. The emotional bond between the sisters is honest and pure and will be felt by even the iciest heart. And if that is not enough, the character of Olaf, a snowman with a perpetually sunny disposition, will plaster a smile on even the most ardent Disney-hater.

The animation is as good as anything Pixar has done. The vocal talent is first rate and cast perfectly for their characters. To cap it all off, the songs by the husband/wife team of Robert Lopez and Kristen Anderson-Lopez, are among the best ever featured in a Disney film. Frozen is a mega-hit and one that will most likely restore some of the luster to the Disney name.

Elsa’s fear has kept her locked up and isolated. She is afraid of human interaction; afraid of what she may inadvertently do. Her fear has blocked her from living a full and enjoyable life. And that is what fear does. It stops us in our tracks. It prevents us from achieving goals and attaining the desires of our hearts. It is an enemy.

But there is one thing that can defeat fear. There is one thing more powerful. It is the one thing that can save us when fear has overtaken our heart.

“There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love.” 1 John 4:18 (KJV)

Love can be presented as a magical thing, just as it is in this film. But love is not at all magical. Love is a God thing. And that makes it even more powerful.
