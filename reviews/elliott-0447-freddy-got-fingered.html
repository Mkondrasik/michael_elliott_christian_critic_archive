Author-verified transcription

If the title of this particular film isn't enough to keep people away from the theater, perhaps this review will help provide a stronger deterrence. One can only hope. The only reason I didn't walk out of Freddy Got Fingered is due to a personal code of ethics that I hold which prevents me from reviewing a film that I don't see in its entirety. Perhaps it is time to rethink that policy.

Freddy Got Fingered is, in my estimation, not only a complete waste of time, but a waste of money, celluloid and talent. Devoid of intelligence or wit, it relies solely upon the far-fetched, no boundaries comedic styling of Tom Green (Road Trip), who is credited as the star, the director, and the co-writer. No plot summary is really needed. The characters in the film defy logical description. The humor is of the most puerile and nonsensical nature.

Green plays a character called Gord Brody, a 28 year old loser who lives in his parents' basement and is on a career path which would have him making cheese sandwiches for the rest of his life. That is, unless he can sell his bizarre "doodles" to a Hollywood animation studio. His father (Rip Torn, The Larry Sanders Show) is pushing him towards the cheese.

That's pretty much it. For the next ninety minutes, we are subjected to an onslaught of outrageously staged bits of questionable "humor." Not content with pushing the envelope of taste, Green decides to simply take a bulldozer to the whole post office.

Whether slinging a newborn over his head by its umbilical cord or manually pleasuring both a horse and an elephant, Green demonstrates that there is relatively little that he will not do in search of a reaction. In the case of this reviewer, the reaction was one of disgust and disdain. I was not touched nor tickled by the relationship between Gord and the paraplegic amateur rocket scientist Betty (Marisa Coughlin, Gossip) whose idea of foreplay involves having her lifeless legs beaten with a bamboo stick.

Nor did I laugh when Gord skinned a buck and paraded around in its bloody pelt like a loon. And somehow the repeated physical abuse of the next door neighbor's young son, who was more often bloodied than not, struck me as decisively unfunny.

I have long been on record as saying that any film can be used to illustrate a scriptural truth. Even a film as distastefully irreverent as Freddy Got Fingered has to borrow from life for its exaggerated observations of extreme human behavior. Even though the target audience of this film would most likely not be interested to hear what the Bible says, there is a spiritual parallel to be seen.

In the film, Gord is desirous to receive approval from his domineering and caustic father. Instead, he continually receives verbal insults and attacks producing extreme negative reactions. Certainly, the relationship between a father and son is one to which God has given much attention. There are things that a father is responsible for and things which he is cautioned against.

Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged. Colossians 3:21 (KJV)

Instead of continually belittling Gord, his father should have strengthened him, supported him in his endeavors, and helped him to find his way. But let's face it, such loving and nurturing behavior would not be welcome in a film such as this.

Just in case anyone is wondering... The Freddy of the title is Gord's younger brother played by Eddie Kaye Thomas (American Pie). A conscientious, straight-arrow bank employee who dismisses his older brother's sophomoric antics, Freddy is taken from the family home because Gord, in order to get back at his dad, makes up a story about inappropriate touching between his father and brother. It is, of course, a lie. Freddy was not actually "fingered." Sadly, the audience of this film will be abused in so many more ways that it doesn't really matter.
