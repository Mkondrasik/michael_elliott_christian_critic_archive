Bulworth

Michael Elliott
Original publication date
May 22, 1998
Rating
2 stars

1 hr: 48 min

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Race; equality; one blood; truth; hypocrisy

Author-verified transcription

Jay Billington Bulworth knows how the political game is played. He should... He's been playing it for as long as he can remember. Now entering into the final stretch of his Senate reelection campaign, he finds he can no longer handle the hypocrisy which engulfs his life.

Bulworth opens with the Senator sitting in an empty room, reviewing his 30-second ads ("We stand on the threshold of a new millenium") while crying uncontrollably. Later in the film, Bulworth offers this piece of hindsight, "Never make life and death decisions when you're in the middle of a suicidal depression." His decision? To take out an inflated life insurance policy and then hire someone to kill him.

But still, his campaign marches on. Only now, knowing that death awaits, the standard speeches don't seem as important to him. For the first time in his political career, Bulworth starts speaking truth. And much to his surprise, it feels good! And much to the greater surprise of his handlers, people begin to respond to this new and unusual message. Now, if it only weren't for that contract on his life...

Warren Beatty (Love Affair), directed, co-wrote, and plays the title character in this scathing political satire which pulls no punches. He is equally hard on Democrats and Republicans alike who have become almost indistinguishable from each other because both groups have their hands thrust deeply into the pockets of big business and will dance to whatever tune is being played.

Senator Bulworth is now dancing to a different drummer... and the rhythm is coming directly from the streets. Introduced to the urban hip hop culture of the 'hood' by a trio of black volunteers, Bulworth begins delivering his message as an aging white rapper. Here the movie may have overstepped itself as Mr. Beatty will not be winning any Grammies for his musical abilities. One bad rap attempt would have been amusing. As an ongoing plot device it became tedious.

Halle Berry (Why Do Fools Fall In Love) is cast as a tough, street-wise "girl from the 'hood" to whom Bulworth becomes attached. Miss Berry is fine in her role as the 26 year-old Nina but we never believe the attraction that is supposed to exist between her and the 60 year-old Bulworth.

Oliver Platt (Dangerous Beauty) has some amusing moments as Dennis Murphy, Bulworth's chief-of-staff, who can't begin to fathom what his candidate is doing, yet tries to put a proper spin on it anyway. Don Cheadle (Out of Sight) is also effective as L.D., a drug dealer who uses children to carry out his illegal activities.

The script is written with a heavy dose of profane street language, and Bulworth, during his various rapping diatribes, gets around to offending just about everyone.

During an appearance on the stodgy TV program "American Politics", Bulworth, dressed in his borrowed rap clothes of shorts, baggy shirt, shades and a ski cap, calls for the "deconstruction of race". When asked for an explanation, he further details the plan simply as everybody should "sleep with" everybody else until we're all the same color.

There is truth within this rather ludicrous suggestion, of course. Race should be a non-issue between men. Skin color is not a distinction made by God... nor should it ever be the source of division between His people.

...Seeing He giveth to all life, and breath, and all things; and hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation; that they should seek the Lord... Acts 17:25-27 [KJV]

There may always be cultural differences, ethnic differences, and educational differences between men but the one thing we should remember is that God made us all of one blood and the "life of all flesh is the blood thereof." [Leviticus 17:14].

God looks on the heart, not the skin. If we desire to live a life that is pleasing to him, this is a mindset that we would do well to emulate. Not a hypocritical facade of tolerance, but a heart response to tear down the racial barriers that divide us.
