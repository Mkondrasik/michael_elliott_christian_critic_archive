Author-verified transcription

Well defined characters; family friendly story; advanced computer animation? It looks like Dreamworks has finally honed in on Pixar's secret formula with their newest release, Over the Hedge. It is a delightful mix of slapstick and wit which gives both parent and child something to enjoy.

Based upon a popular comic strip, the star of the picture is a resourceful raccoon named RJ (Bruce Willis) who has made the mistake of pilfering the junk food supply of a hibernating bear (Nick Nolte). If he doesn't replace it, he'll be on the bear's menu when the bear wakes up - one week hence.

For help, RJ turns to the odd family of woodland animals who are living in the middle of a newly constructed subdivision. Being foragers, they're accustomed to finding food - but will they help an outsider like RJ? That decision may be up to the group's leader, Verne (Garry Shandling), a turtle who makes most decisions based upon how much his tail tingles... and his tail has been tingling ever since RJ appeared.

The others welcome RJ into the fold quickly. They include: Hammy (Steve Carrell), a hyperactive squirrel; Ozzie (William Shatner) and Heather (Avril Lavigne), an over-emoting possum and his daughter; Lou and Penny (Eugene Levy and Catherine O'Hara), a pair of parental porcupines; and Stella (Wanda Sykes), a skunk who knows how to clear a room.

The vocal talents are especially well cast. Willis is at his smart-alecky best, a loner who lives by his wits but learns the benefits of belonging to a family. Steve Carrell steals the show as Hammy, an adrenalin-filled squirrel who wears his emotions on his fur. William Shatner pokes fun at his much-maligned over-acting style by playing possum with a death scene worthy of Sarah Bernhardt.

The animation is first rate, enabling us to enjoy the story and emotionally connect with the characters. For children, the characters are cute and funny; the action broad and comical. For adults, the satirical look at suburban life, where everything seems to revolve around food is unmistakable. Over the Hedge may not have reached the heights of a Toy Story or a Finding Nemo, but it is head and shoulders above anything else a non-Pixar studio has produced.

During the course of the story, a struggle for leadership ensues. Verne, a cautious and practical leader finds his authority challenged by the bold and brash actions of RJ. As more and more of the "family" find themselves drawn to the outgoing and adventurous raccoon, Verne begins to question if it isn't time to step down.

That he even asks the question tells us that he has the right mindset for a leader. His concern was to do what would be best for the group... even if it meant giving up or sharing his position of authority. RJ's mind was not on what he could do for the group but rather on what the group could do for him.

For they that are such serve not our Lord Jesus Christ, but their own belly; and by good words and fair speeches deceive the hearts of the simple. Romans 16:18 (KJV)

By blindly following the self-serving RJ, the woodland creatures put themselves at risk. When choosing to follow a leader, it is always best to look down the path he's walking to make sure it's a place you desire to go.
