Gun Shy

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
February 4, 2000
Rating
2 stars

Original Author Copy

Sermon / spiritual topic: Fear; trust; believing; peace

Author-verified transcription

Fear immobilizes. Build up enough fear and both the body and mind will start to shut down. Such is the state in which Charlie (Liam Neeson, The Haunting) finds himself in Buena Vista Pictures’ Gun Shy.

The problem here is that Charlie is an undercover cop in the middle of a sensitive and long-running sting operation involving a Colombian drug cartel and a Mafia money laundering scheme. It’s not as if he could take a vacation and find someone to cover his shift.

So Charlie does the next best thing. He joins a group therapy session. Soon, amidst the tales of male emasculation and job insecurities, Charlie is sharing his fears about having pistols waved in his face and seeing men killed in front of him - much to the amazement of the average businessmen who are part of his group. All he wants, he repeatedly states, is to retire and get for himself “the ocean view.”

In between the killers and his therapy, Charlie meets Judy Tripp (Sandra Bullock, 28 Days), a kindly nurse who demonstrates her perky bedside manner while giving Charlie an enema. Can Charlie survive this last assignment? Will he find lasting peace and love with Judy? Could there be an informant in the FBI who is planning to double-cross Charlie? Why is it we don’t really care about these questions?

It’s the latter question which is the hardest to answer. The acting, for the most part is more than acceptable. Mr. Neeson projects a character who has the assurance of a seasoned professional but also allows us to see the mounting insecurities of a man on the edge. And he does so in a comical fashion.

Oliver Platt (Bicentennial Man) is Fulvio Nesstra, the Mafia link to the money laundering that the Colombian cartel needs. His exaggerated machismo is only a cover for his own insecurities and desire to escape the family business and is a source of much of the dark humor of the film.

Ms. Bullock, who also produced the film, is hardly in it... although from promotional trailers it is easy to be led to believe otherwise. Her scenes are rather brief and her character is shallowly written. But she plays off of Liam Neeson well and is always good for a spark or two of that indescribable personal electricity.

Where the film loses course is in the writing. Continuity of time is not held to a strict linear structure and it is unclear whenever the film departs from time’s normal progression. Also, the plot and character development are so loosely constructed that major holes of logic are permitted.

There are a few scenes which are enjoyable, particularly the scenes between Mr. Neeson and his therapy group or any of the scenes with Mr. Platt. But as a whole, the film fails to connect.

This isn’t to say that it can’t have a salient point or two to teach us about fear. Midway through the film, Judy reproofs Charlie by saying, “You aren’t alive if you are consumed by fear.”

There’s a certain amount of truth to that. Not literally of course, for to feel anything, including fear, means that you are a living organism. But the richness and fullness of life is cut off to one who allows fear to dictate his actions and thoughts.

The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe. Proverbs 29:25 (KJV)

The King James version of the Bible often records that we should “Fear God.” This contradicts the very nature of our God Who is always good. I, for a long time, had difficulty reconciling the phrase “Fear God.” I eventually came to realize that, contextually, the phrase means that we should hold God’s promises above all, trusting and respecting them in the face of anything else. Instead of “Fear God,” a clearer translation would be “Respect God” or “Revere God.”

Whenever we “fear” something, we give that something undue respect or prominence in our thinking. Fear is nothing more than a form of believing -- believing for the worst outcome or believing negatively. By believing the promises of God without doubt, we eliminate the negative worldly fear from our thinking. Our thoughts will be positive, not negative, and we can live in the lasting peace that comes from trusting in the goodness of God.
