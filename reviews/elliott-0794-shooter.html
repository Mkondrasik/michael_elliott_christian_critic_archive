Assassination plots; political double-crossing; loner vs. the man... these are the elements for a blockbuster action movie. Shooter doesn't disappoint in the action category... it just doesn't satisfy that well in the movie category.

When the government abandoned military sniper Bob Lee Swagger (Mark Wahlberg) behind enemy lines during a sensitive top secret mission, he didn't take too kindly to the decision. After he returned to the States, those responsible for that decision mysteriously "disappeared." You would have thought that would be a lesson not to mess with this guy.

But evil minds aren't always quick on the uptake. Swagger is set up by more governmental strategists to take the fall for a presidential assassination but he manages to survive the plan to make him a guilty corpse. All that is left for him is to discover who framed him and take care of business.

Against him is every law enforcement agency in the world. On his side are a young widow (Kate Mara) and a rookie FBI agent (Michael Pena). With those kind of odds (and in this kind of movie) you just have to know that those rooting for the government are going to be sorely disappointed.

There are a whole lot of action/explosions going on with a very high body count that multiplies as the film progresses. Unfortunately the story skips over some of the exposition and the rest is lost to a mumbling lead actor so it isn't always clear what's going on.

Mark Wahlberg, who was so brilliant in The Departed, doesn't have the juice to shed much light on this project. He swallows so much of his dialogue that my suggestion to anyone watching this on DVD would be to turn on the captions feature so you have a fighting chance to follow the story.

His costars (Michael Pena and Kate Mara) are adequate if a bit one dimensional. His adversaries - Danny Glover as a duplicitous conspirator, Ned Beatty as an unscrupulous senator, and Elias Koteas as a sociopathic killer - offer little more than these thumbnail descriptions reveal.

Director Antoine Fuqua keeps the action pounding away and the explosions keep getting bigger and more spectacular but at the end of the day there's got to be something more substantial upon which to hang all the cinematic tricks and trappings. That's where the script by Jonathin Lemkin finally proves inadequate.

Swagger admits that his character flaw - the button his manipulators were able to push so effectively - was one of blind patriotism. They appealed to his sense of duty and honor and he responded without question.

Being good does not have to equate to being stupid or gullible. We are under no obligation to trust everyone and everything on faith alone. Even the early Christian church leaders exhorted those who they were trying to attract:

Prove all things; hold fast that which is good. 1 Thessalonians 5:21 (KJV)

We have been given the greatest gift in this world: the ability to discern between good and evil; between right and wrong. We have the greatest source of truth known to man because we have the written Word of God. By using our powers of discernment and proving all things through the litmus test of God's Word, we can make sound decisions for our lives and walk through this world with great wisdom and strength.
