Frailty

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
April 12, 2002
Rating
4 stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

1 John 4:12 Chronicles 18:20-211 John 1:5

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Spiritual discernment; deception; testing spirits; the nature of God

Author-verified transcription

William Shakespeare spoke too soon. Frailty, thy name is no longer woman! Thy name has been given to a creepy, gothic horror tale rife with religious undercurrents. And make no mistake, creepy is the operative word in that sentence.

On the surface, all appears normal at the Meiks' masculine household as a widowed mechanic (Bill Paxton, U-571) does his best to raise his two young sons. Using a loving hand and religious instruction, Dad forges a strong bond with his boys.

That bond is tested one night as Dad awakens them to share the news that an angel had visited him with a shocking pronouncement: the Meiks have been selected by God to do battle against the demons who populate the earth.

While the youngest boy, Adam (Jeremy Sumpter in his film debut), compares their new demon slayer roles to that of his favorite comic book super heroes, his older brother Fenton (Matthew O'Leary, Domestic Disturbance) is more than a little incredulous and suspicious of his father's mental condition. His incredulity turns to horror as Dad, under instruction from his "angel," begins bringing the demons home for the execution of God's ultimate justice. Of course, to us, these demons look and act just like everyday people.

Twelve-year-old Fenton is torn between his love for a father whom he now knows has gone mad, his responsibility towards his nine-year-old brother who seems tempted to follow in his father's footsteps, and his awareness of the evil being performed in his own house. Something has got to give and in this psychological thriller, there's no telling what it might be.

Actor/director Bill Paxton demonstrates an impressive aptitude for the setting of mood in his feature film directorial debut. He does not subject us to graphic, blood-splattering violence, but neither does he spare us from the sense of horror as those violent acts take place off screen.

The film is told in a series of flashbacks, narrated by Matthew McConaughey (The Wedding Planner) who plays the grown up Meiks boy. Hearing his macabre tale is FBI agent Wesley Doyle (Powers Boothe, Men of Honor).

Paxton draws from his actors a level of conviction that is downright frightening. That the actors underplay their roles so well and so convincingly makes the unbelievable scenario being played out before us chillingly real.

Where the film goes astray is not in its artistic composition but in the way it interprets the nature of God. To accept the premise of the story, one must entertain the possibility that God would actually condone the butchering of people and give his followers instructions to commit murder.

Such an interpretation is completely contradictory to the message of Christ whose life and accomplishments made salvation available to all. Jesus dealt with many a devil spirit when he walked this earth, but never did so at the destruction of the human who was possessed.

Just as there is a spiritual truth and messengers who bear that truth, there is also spiritual deception which has its own messengers. God cautions us not to blindly accept every spiritual message we receive but to discern the good from the evil.

Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world. 1 John 4:1 (KJV)

We can know beyond any doubt that whoever it was that was communicating with Dad, it was not He who is all love and all light and in whom there is no darkness at all. The "frailty" of the title must refer to the weakness of mind that would lead people to lay such horror as is depicted in this film at the feet of our God. It simply does not belong there.
