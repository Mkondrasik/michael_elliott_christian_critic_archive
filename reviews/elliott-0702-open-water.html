Author-verified transcription

In Open Water, a dream vacation turns into a shark-infested nightmare when two young lovers are abandoned by a charter boat while in the middle of the ocean.

Daniel (Daniel Travis) and Susan (Blanchard Ryan) are young professionals whose busy lives leave little time for play. When a small window opens up in their calendars, they hastily arrange a little R and R - at a tropical resort where they plan to lazily lounge on the beach and perhaps do some scuba diving.

They aren't alone. Twenty other people share the boat that takes them out to sea to the reef they plan to explore. Due to a distraction and some shoddy head-counting, only eighteen are taken back to shore. When Daniel and Susan resurface, the boat is nowhere to be seen.

The rest of the film consists of them floating in the middle of the ocean, examining the limited options available to them and passing through a range of emotional highs and lows.

This low-budget (under $200,000), digitally-shot movie most closely resembles The Blair Witch Project in its style and structure. The central characters foolishly place themselves in harm's way and throughout the rest of the film we simply wait for harm to come to them.

There's not much to the story itself nor, if truth be told, to the characters. Blanchard Ryan is the stronger of the two actors and builds a believable emotional progression for Susan, letting the seriousness of their situation slowly dawn upon her. Daniel Travis is less successful in ramping up to his emotional outbursts and so they seem forced and somewhat artificial.

Director Chris Kentis recognizes the built-in tension to the situation and supports it by keeping the camera close to the surface of the water. In this way, we see what the characters are seeing - which isn't much. A flash of a shark's fin fills us with dread while an outline of a ship on the horizon provides the slightest glimmer of hope. Most of the time, the characters face nothing but open water for miles around and the knowledge that, in this situation, humans are at the bottom of the food chain. The tension is uncomfortably real.

Even though Open Water runs a mere 79 minutes, it does feel as though it has been stretched too thin. Although the filmmakers try to sustain the mood throughout they do not provide the needed emotional ebb and flow to keep the audience hooked to the characters' plight. There are long stretches where the film, much like its characters, simply drifts aimlessly.

During their many hours of ordeal, Daniel and Susan eventually get around to playing the blame game. She blames him for allowing them to become separated from the group. She has a point. They were in a hostile and unfamiliar environment. It would have been prudent for them to remain with the group.

Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: 1 Peter 5:8 (KJV)

When stalking a herd, a lion will look for a weaker animal - one who has dropped off the pace and is lagging behind the rest of the animals. Isolated and defenseless, that animal becomes his prey.

Our spiritual adversary is compared to a lion because he acts in a similar manner. He preys on the weak and defenseless which is why God has called us to band together with others of like minds. Together we are stronger and more able to repel the spiritual attacks of our enemy. Even better, we often avoid the attacks from occurring because of the safety that comes with being part of a strong group.
