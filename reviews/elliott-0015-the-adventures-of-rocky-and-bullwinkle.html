The Adventures of Rocky and Bullwinkle

Byline
Michael Elliott
Original publication date
June 30, 2000
Rating
2½ stars
Source
Original Author Copy

Scripture references

Matthew 18:4 1 John 4:4 John 20:29

Translation: KJV
Sermon / spiritual topic: Childlike belief; humility; conviction; faith

Author-verified transcription

Time has not been kind to Frostbite Falls, Minnesota, home to the most famous animated moose and squirrel team in TV history. When The Bullwinkle Show was canceled, the town slowly began to fall into disrepair and its residents into despair.

Such is the premise which opens THE ADVENTURES OF ROCKY AND BULLWINKLE, a mixed live action/animated comedy tribute to the popular characters of Jay Ward.

While Rocky (voice by June Foray, who originally voiced the character over 35 years ago) has forgotten how to fly, Bullwinkle (voice by Keith Scott, George of the Jungle) is remarkably unchanged by the passage of time, remaining as he was then, a clueless innocent. The pair of ex-heroes live off of shrinking residual checks in a state of perpetual reruns.

Meanwhile in Pottsylvania, the fall of the Iron Curtain has driven the evil Fearless Leader, Boris Badinoff, and Natasha Fatale underground. They emerge in a strange land called Hollywood, where anything is possible - including crossing over into the real world.

Once there, Fearless Leader (Robert Deniro, Analyze This) devises a plan to program such insipid television shows that anyone watching them will turn into a drooling, mindless zombie. As Bullwinkle points out, "Who will know the difference?"

The FBI call upon our favorite moose and squirrel to save the world and thwart the plans of the Fearless Leader. But first they, along with Special Agent Karen Sympathy (Piper Perabo, Coyote Ugly) must avoid the traps being set for them by Boris (Jason Alexander, Seinfeld) and Natasha (Rene Russo, The Thomas Crown Affair).

All of the elements of the original TV show are attempted in this feature film... From the deep and velvet tones of the narrator (also voiced by Keith Scott), to the cheesy but memorable music, and of course, the simply awful puns and wordplay which made the original cartoon a guilty pleasure for adults as well as entertaining and enjoyable for children.

It is the latter element which is copied with only sporadic success. The one missing ingredient is the input and guiding hand of producer Jay Ward who died in 1989 and who brought an intelligence and wit to the original Bullwinkle Show. While children of all ages should still enjoy the shenanigans of the characters, the subtle humor geared towards the adults is not as effective.

There are moments however where glimpses of cleverness peek through. Mr. Deniro's Fearless Leader sparkles with an evil glee, and even launches into an amusing Taxi Driver riff of dialogue. His hair-brained scheme for world domination has an appropriate, though not-so-subtly, sarcastic edge to it. And a few cameo appearances by some well-known faces can bring a smile to the face.

The CGI technology enabling live actors to share the screen with 3-D animated characters continues to improve and develop. In THE ADVENTURES OF ROCKY AND BULLWINKLE, the two worlds interact almost seamlessly.

Stemming from Agent Sympathy's suppression of her "inner child" which yearns to break out of the "adult shell" she feels required to don, the overall moral of the story appears to be that "what you believe as a child can still be true when you grow up." A nice sentiment, but a bit misleading as it doesn't explain what truth is.

Perhaps a better analogy would be that the way a child believes, with the pureness of heart and total conviction of mind, is still available to achieve as an adult even in the face of a cynical and disbelieving world.

Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, the same is greatest in the kingdom of heaven. Matthew 18:4 (KJV)

The world can have a way of wearing down the conviction of even the most ardent of believers. But we can rise above the world by staying our minds on the one truth the world cannot destroy for it is backed up by the One Who is greater than the world.
