Author-verified transcription

Vile is not a word I generally pull out of my thesaurus when composing a review, but in all honesty, there really is no more appropriate word to describe Terry Gilliam's latest film, Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas.

Based on the autobiographical book by Hunter S. Thompson, Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas depicts a drug-immersed trip to Las Vegas which Mr. Thompson and his lawyer "Dr. Gonzo" underwent in the late 1960s.

Never having read Mr. Thompson's book, I have to say that this movie has done nothing to build even an iota of desire within me to go and begin reading it now. The excesses which the movie depicts are nauseating. The depths to which man can sink are unfathomable to the spiritual mind.

Johnny Depp (Donnie Brasco) plays Raoul Duke (a.k.a. Hunter S. Thompson) as if he were a comic strip character (which of course he is - the character of Duke in the Doonesbury strip is obviously based upon the life of Mr. Thompson.) Perhaps it is an impossible task to find a center of reality amidst the drug induced abnormalities that comprise Mr. Thompson's psyche. But because Mr. Depp was unable to make his character real to us, watching his adventures into the psychedelic realm while surrounded by the gaudiness of Vegas was neither interesting nor enjoyable.

Benicio Del Toro (Excess Baggage) is simply gross as Dr. Gonzo. Whether wielding a knife in some unexplained psychotic episode or vomiting out of the side of a car, his offensive antics are difficult to watch.

Terry Gilliam (Twelve Monkeys) is obviously talented as a director, but his script selections (The Fisher King, Brazil) have always struck me as being bizarre and unappealing, catering to the underbelly of our society.

Revelation 18:23 (KJV) tells us that:

"by thy (the devil's) sorceries were all nations deceived."

The word sorceries is translated from the Greek word pharmakeia from which we get the English word, "pharmacy" or "pharmaceutical." It obviously indicates the presence of drugs.

Mind altering drugs are a device of the devil used to deceive people. There is a spiritual force behind them. The mind is the spiritual battlefield. It is where the conflict rages. Repeated imbibing, ingesting, or otherwise partaking of a substance which makes one relinquish control of one's mind is spiritual suicide. Devil spirits will be able to come in freely, without resistance, and make themselves at home in such a mind.

The squalor in which these two characters lived during their drug-crazed visit was a telltale sign of devil spirit possession. People with a sound mind would not tolerate living amidst the utter filth and sordid conditions that many devil spirits prefer.

There is nothing amusing or entertaining about watching a two hour film about drug (devil) possessed people. Like being the only sober person at a fraternity kegger, one sees clearly the ugliness behind the drunken or drugged behavior. The drug (devil spirit) takes over the mind and therefore controls the behavior. As Mr. Depp's character says in the film after yet another confrontation with the out-of-control Dr. Gonzo, "You can turn your back on a person... but never turn your back on a drug."

My advice: Turn your back on this movie and keep on walking.
