It's enough to give idolatry a bad name. The American Idol finalists go the Beach Blanket Bingo route in From Justin to Kelly, hoping to ride the wave of popularity before America's short attention span banishes them to "whatever-happened-to" territory. If there is justice, this movie will only speed that process.

Kelly Clarkson (the winner of American Idol) plays a virginal Texas waitress/pop singer who gets pressured by her friends into going on a Spring Break vacation for a week of PG-rated debauchery.

Justin Guarini (the runner up) plays the party guru of Pennsylvania who is responsible for livening up the Spring Break scene with whipped cream bikini contests and other such nonsense. Oh yeah... he sings too.

Of course, the two "idols" fall in love at first glance and spend the rest of the movie in a series of misunderstandings and miscommunications which threaten to keep them apart.

Like the Annette Funicello/Frankie Avalon beach movies of the 60s which serve as this film's inspiration, the plot (what little there is) is inanely simplistic. It is there only to give us something to watch in between songs, which are hard enough to watch as it is.

Each of the central characters can be summed up with stereotypical terms: Kelly, the nice girl; Alexa (Katherine Bailess), the party girl; Kaya (Anika Noni

Rose), the smart girl; Justin, the nice guy; Brandon (Greg Siff), the harmless stud; and Eddie (Brian Dietzen), the nerd. The descriptions give the actors far more credit than is deserved. Their costumes tell us more about their characters than their acting abilities.

One of the things that the film lacks is an edge... a brutally honest edge. Where oh where was Simon Cowell? Couldn't he have played the Eric von Zipper part?

From Justin to Kelly is a musical, if we want to use that term in the loosest way possible. It just isn't a good one. The original score by Michael Wandmacher (Max Keeble's Big Move) is pop pabulum of the worst variety. The voices of Justin and Kelly are the one strength of the movie but the songs they sing are simultaneously bland and annoying. Even when they borrow from known standards such as KC and the Sunshine Band's hit, "That's the Way (uh huh, uh huh) I Like It," the result is flat and uninspired.

Also, the 60's throwback to the way sex is handled is somewhat creepy. Bikinis are smaller, dance moves far more suggestive, everybody talks about "getting some" but, of course, no one does. The fact that nothing is overtly shown on screen is rather meaningless. This is a sexually oriented film presented in an asexual manner. Weird.

Truth be told, the concept (an updated beach movie) was not a bad one. Had the producers operated from artistic motives rather than commercial ones, the film might have had potential. As it is, rushing this atrocity through production while its stars are still somewhat famous is nothing but transparent exploitation which is so blatant that even the often gullible American public can't help but recognize it.

We can't say that God doesn't give us ample warning. Idols will always disappoint us. Their images and exalted positions have been built upon lies and fantasies. They are temporary diversions, attracting our attention and even sometimes our worship. But they give nothing of value back. An idol can come in all kinds of forms. It might be a golden calf, a brass ring, a sports hero, or a couple of young kids with big voices. The results are always the same. T

his is why little children, keep yourselves from idols. 1 John 5:21 (KJV)

Great advice. I encourage you to take it.
