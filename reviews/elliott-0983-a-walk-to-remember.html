A Walk To Remember

Author-verified transcription

Who would have thought that A Walk To Remember would turn out to be the first pleasant movie surprise of 2002? Sure, it will have its fair share of detractors who will deride it for what they’ll claim to be its sappy, sentimental content but judging from the number of sniffles coming from all corners of the theater where it was screening, it seems like this teary inspirational teen romance is able to touch a wide number of hearts.

In the small southern town of Beaufort, Landon Carter (Shane West, Once and Again) is a big man on his high school campus. He’s cool; he’s popular. He’s also aimless and unmotivated. Consequently, he has absolutely no future of any consequence in sight. When a foolish prank endangers a fellow student, Landon’s punishment is far worse than suspension. Helping the janitor after school, tutoring special needs students, and working on the school play are not activities for members of the “cool crowd.” They’re for the geeks and freaks... people like Jamie Sullivan (Mandy Moore, The Princess Diaries).

Jamie, the daughter of the town’s Baptist minister (Peter Coyote, The Basket) is a bit of a plain Jane whose lack of fashion sense and overt Christian beliefs make her an easy target for sarcastic remarks thrown by Landon and his friends. Suddenly thrust into “her world,” Landon is able to see that Jamie is living the life that she chooses, undeterred by the cruel remarks aimed her way. It gets Landon’s attention. That attention grows into admiration. Admiration blossoms into love. And love, as we know, changes people.

Admittedly, there’s nothing all that original about this story, based on the novel by Nicholas Sparks (Message in a Bottle). It even comes with an emotional sucker punch which all but guarantees the hankie industry that its next couple of weeks will be prosperous ones.

Because of the sincerity of both the message and the film’s leading actors, we’d be too hasty to simply dismiss A Walk To Remember as being a large screen soap opera. True, the film wears its heart on its sleeve but at least it is a healthy heart that beats soundly and resonates with love.

Shane West is central to the success of A Walk to Remember. As Landon, it is his emotional journey that we track. He has enough of the James Dean kind of broodiness that gives him the edge he needs early on in the film, but never totally loses the sense of decency that makes his “transformation” believable.

Mandy Moore sheds her pop star image to drab down for the part of Jamie and she is no less beautiful for doing so. After so many laughable or ho-hum feature film performances from other pop singers, it’s refreshing to finally find one that is simple and honest and reaches us on an emotional level.

Noted choreographer Adam Shankman, who made his directorial debut last year with The Wedding Planner, achieves far greater success with this his sophomore effort. It isn’t flawless, but its flaws are forgivable in light of its strong message regarding the redemptive power of love.

All of us were, at one time, like Landon. Perhaps not with his degree of “coolness,” but we were just as lost, with a future that was just as bleak... spiritually speaking. It was God’s love that rescued us, pulling us back from the brink and effecting a radical change within us. God’s love transformed us and filled the void that had always existed in our lives. The power of love... true spiritual love... cannot be overstated.

Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres. Love never fails. 1 Corinthians 13:4-8a (NIV)

A walk to remember will always be a walk of love.
