Author-verified transcription

With the new and amazing effects that today's cinematic technology can produce, it was only a matter of time before a human version of the popular animated detective cum Swiss army knife was brought to the big screen. True to its title character, Disney's Inspector Gadget pulls out every gizmo imaginable to try and entertain the kiddies.

John Brown (Matthew Broderick, Election) is a typical boy scout turned security guard who dreams of being a hero cop... a prospect that does not loom particularly large on his horizon. At least not until certain events transpire that place him in the midst of a robbery/murder. While pursuing the bad guys, John's car is blown up by the evil Sanford Scolex (Rupert Everett, An Ideal Husband). Of course, John happened to be in it at the time.

The beautiful Dr. Brenda Bradford (Joely Fisher, TV's Ellen), whose father was the victim of the aforementioned murder, chooses the barely living, body cast-encased John as the prototype for her new experiment... the first functional, bionic crime-fighting police officer. And thus, Inspector Gadget was born, described as a combination of Columbo and Nintendo.

His name is derived from the multiplicity of gizmos and gadgets built into his circuitry. From finger lighters and hat helicopters to a telescopic neck and a car that puts Knight Rider's Kitt to shame, Inspector Gadget has a tool for every purpose and a purpose for every tool. Now, if he only had time to read his operator's manual...

But crime waits for no man (or machine). Sanford Scolex AKA Claw, is out to obtain the secret to Dr. Bradford's robotics success. It will be up to Gadget to foil his plans and save the day. "Wowser!"

Matthew Broderick was an inspired choice to play the often clueless but always honest Inspector Gadget. And the filmmakers even give him the opportunity to play opposite himself by writing in an evil twin robot, which he plays with unabashed glee.

Joely Fisher is Gadget's romantic interest, although at this PG level, this amounts to little more than a few goo-goo eyes and fluttering lashes.

Rupert Everett is a bit of a disappointment as the evil Claw. As brilliant as he was in An Ideal Husband and My Best Friend's Wedding, he flat-out misses the mark here. Perhaps he should study Mike Myers' prototypical work as Dr. Evil in the Austin Powers series before attempting a sequel.

Director David Kellogg (Cool As Ice) is mostly known for his TV commercials and perhaps therein lies the major problem for the film. Inspector Gadget, though it contains short snippets of amusing clips, doesn't manage to sustain itself throughout its short 88 minute running time.

The script alternates between silly and sappy. Judging from the restlessness of the younger members of the audience, there wasn't quite enough to the film to keep them riveted (and thereby squirm-free). However, just before prepubescent bedlam threatened to break loose, an action sequence captured their attention and peace was restored.

They particularly enjoyed the various gadgetry built into the main character. To employ one, the title character would say, "Go, go, gadget"... and then name the device he needed. The name of that device would depend on the situation at hand. If he needed a flame-thrower, he wouldn't call for a parachute.

God exhorts us to use His resources in much the same way. As we identify the various needs in our lives, we are to claim the promise or exercise the manifestation that will help us address or meet each need.

Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. Ephesians 6:11 (KJV)

The armor (or resources) of God may not be as showy as Inspector Gadget's toys, but they are just as effective [even more so] in combating our spiritual enemies.
