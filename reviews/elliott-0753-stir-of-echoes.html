Author-verified transcription

It would appear that the lad in The Sixth Sense is not the only one seeing dead people this summer. In Stir of Echoes, this particular ability runs in Tom Witzky's family and from the looks of things, Tom is none too happy about it.

Tom (Kevin Bacon, Wild Things) is a hardworking, blue-collar employee, living in a working class Chicago neighborhood with his lovely wife Maggie (Kathryn Erbe, Dream With The Fishes) and adorable young son Jake (Zachary David Cope, in his film debut). On the surface, there is nothing especially noteworthy about them. Oh sure... Jake may occasionally have long conversations with invisible beings and maybe he knows things no 6 year old could possibly know... But besides that, they are a typical American family living a typically American life.

That is, until Tom is given a posthypnotic suggestion by his sister-in-law (Illeana Douglas, Grace Of My Heart) who, thinking that Tom must broaden his mental attitudes, left his mind an open receiver for any preternatural stimuli floating about the cosmos.

Suddenly Tom starts getting flashes of gruesome and violent images which he cannot explain or understand. As an audience, we commiserate with his plight because writer/director David Koepp keeps us in the same state of confusion as Tom by giving us no frame of reference for the images we see.

In order to hang onto his sanity, Tom must figure out why these visions are being shown to him. As he follows the clues only he (and the audience) can see, the visions begin to make more sense even as his behavior becomes more erratic... senseless to everyone around him. Everyone that is, except his son.

David Koepp (The Trigger Effect) has crafted a suspenseful mystery that is more disturbing than scary. A rape sequence which appears near the end of the mystery is clearly troubling even though, as in The Sixth Sense, the basis is biblically unfounded. Mr. Koepp also sprinkles the movie with a number of appropriately placed humorous moments in order to break the mounting tension.

Kevin Bacon's character is in the dark, figuring out what he is seeing even as we are. He plays Tom's growing awareness of a nonphysical plane of existence with just the right mixture of skepticism, awe, concern and frustration.

Kathryn Erbe turns in a strong performance as Maggie, the only one in the Witzky household who isn't seeing things. She admirably portrays a young wife and mother struggling to understand and cope with events affecting her family that are well beyond her comprehension.

Young Zachary David Cope is cute as a button. Mr. Koepp has wisely not demanded more of him than his tender age is capable of providing. Master Cope does manage to get across a level of calm acceptance that contrasts nicely with his screen father's frenzied discoveries. After all, Jake has been seeing these dead people clearly for years. His father suppressed his "gift" until it was awakened by an ill-conceived party challenge which resulted in the doors of his mind being unlocked and opened wide via hypnotism.

The mind is where the spiritual battles are fought. We are continually exhorted by God, in the Scriptures, to control our thoughts, gird the loins of our minds, and hide His Word in our minds and hearts. Anything that requires us to relinquish control of our minds, whether it be chemically induced or merely an act of will, is something that should be considered very carefully as it would make us spiritually vulnerable.

Therefore, prepare your minds for action; be self-controlled; set your hope fully on the grace to be given you when Jesus Christ is revealed. 1 Peter 1:13 (NIV)

The only way to truly prepare our minds for spiritual action is to feed it with the Truth of our spiritual Father. As we discipline our minds to think the Word of God, regardless of the situation with which we are presented, we are ready for anything. Discipline requires self-control... something hypnotism demands that we abandon.
