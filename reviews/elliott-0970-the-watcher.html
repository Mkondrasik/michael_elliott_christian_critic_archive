Author-verified transcription

The thing about a cat and mouse game is that only the cat thinks of it as a game. The mouse is just trying to survive. In The Watcher, serial killer David Allen Griffen (Keanu Reeves, The Replacements) is the cat who has followed his favorite mouse, FBI agent Joel Campbell (James Spader, Supernova) from LA To Chicago.

After failing to capture Griffen, who was allegedly responsible for the murders of eleven young women, Campbell suffers a mental collapse and takes a disability leave, retreating to Chicago to heal his broken spirit. For Griffen, killing isn't the same without Campbell there to chase him so he relocates as well and forces Campbell to participate in his gruesome game by sending him a photo of his next victim and giving him the day to find her before he acts.

Campbell, still haunted by the memories of the past and suffering from severe migraines, throws himself back into the case, eliciting help from the FBI, the Chicago police department, and his therapist Polly (Marisa Tomei, The Slums of Beverly Hills). But this cat and mouse game is one at which Griffen excels. Always staying one step ahead of those trying to stop him, Griffen extends his killing spree, causing Campbell to relive the guilt and feeling of helplessness which caused his initial breakdown.

Mr. Spader plays the flawed hero well. His character clearly does not want to be a part of this psuedo-symbiotic relationship but realizes that he has little choice. Mr. Spader is quite effective in conveying the inner conflict as well as the deteriorating physical effects which the said conflict brings.

Mr. Reeves also does surprisingly well in the role of the serial killer. Spookily charming, and chillingly unemotional, Griffen is less concerned with the killings than he is with the relationship he's forging with Campbell. There's a detachment to his character that adds to the suspense and tension of the film.

The performance of Chris Ellis (October Sky) as Chicago detective Hollis should provide a shot in the arm for police machismo everywhere as he humorously manages to carry on a cell phone conversation while engaged in a high speed pursuit.

Director Joe Charbanic brings his music video experience to this, his first feature film attempt. He uses a great deal of slow motion, stop action, and rapid cuts which results in a very stylized film. This works on some levels and distracts on others. It helps communicate the disorientation being felt by Campbell but its repeated use also distances the audience, preventing them from being drawn into the action.

In an aspect of the film which may be a sad commentary on our overly busy and self-centered society, the police begin distributing the photos of Griffen's next victims via the Chicago media and yet no one, out of the entire city, is able to make a proper identification in time to save the lives of these young women. The impression left is that we just don't take the time to notice those around us.

Griffen chooses his victims carefully and as the title suggests, he watches them before he acts. He studies their routines and their schedules. He knows where they live, where they work, where they eat. He knows what their strengths are and he knows what their weaknesses are. In that respect, he is like the personal spiritual adversary against whom we battle.

Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: 1 Peter 5:8 (KJV)

Our adversary doesn't attack and devour everyone whose path crosses his. He selects his prey, studying for weaknesses, looking for a break in the hedge of protection which God fashions for His children and which is kept strong by our believing. This is why God continually exhorts us to be strong, to study His Word, to believe His promises, to keep ourselves within His spiritual protection. He knows that both an understanding of His Word and the faithfulness to live it is crucially important if we are to prevail over our adversary who is continuously watching us looking for an opening to attack.

We may be mice in this spiritual cat and mouse game, but we can be wise and invincible mice when we choose to heed the Word of God.
