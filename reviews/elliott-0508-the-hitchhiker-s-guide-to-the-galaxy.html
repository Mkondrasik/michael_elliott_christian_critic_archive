Douglas Adams' offbeat sci-fi comic adventure of Arthur Dent and his towel has successfully negotiated its way through multiple reincarnations: Radio show, a trilogy of novels; television special; interactive computer game and now at long last... a full length feature film.

Director Garth Jennings, making his feature debut, and screenwriter Karey Kirkpatrick, working from a screenplay originally drafted by Adams, have tried to keep the unique voice of the author alive throughout the film and they sometimes succeed. Unfortunately, in this hit and miss production, sometimes they do not.

Dent (Martin Freeman) awakens one morning to find out that his house is about to be razed to make way for a new bypass. It turns out to be the least of his problems. His friend Ford (Mos Def), arrives to inform him that 1- he's an alien and 2- Earth is about to be demolished to make way for an interstellar freeway.

No sooner than you can say "Don't Panic," Dent and Ford hitch a ride on a passing space craft and begin to cruise the universe in search of the ultimate question. (The ultimate answer had been discovered some years ago but makes no sense without the proper question to put in front of it.)

Many of Adams' characters appear in the film. Joining Ford and Dent are Trillian (Zooey Deschanel) a young Earth woman who Dent once almost picked up at a party; Zaphod Beeblebrox (Sam Rockwell), the two-headed, three-armed, half-witted Galactic President; and Marvin the depressed robot (voice by Alan Rickman).

What the film lacks is Adams' near giddiness when it comes to language. His books were delightful reads because of the way words or phrases were unexpectedly twisted and used. The film offers a bit of the same but relies too heavily on the visual elements which are nothing special. The humor of The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy could always be found in Adams' unique perspective which is best conveyed by adopting his literary speech patterns and cadences.

Adams' originality is well represented as various familiar objects we remember from his books make their appearances throughout the film. The babel fish is a living language translator. The Vogons are a large, ugly species known for their bureaucratic ways and horrendous poetry. The Infinite Improbability Drive is a handy device to have if you don't care where you're going but need to leave in a hurry. And, of course, the Guide is a must have for any intergalactic traveler.

The Guide is a convenient and easy-to-use electronic reference book that contains everything you may need to know about anything found in the universe. It is one of Adams' cleverest inventions but is nothing new to those of us who study the true guide to the galaxy.

According as his divine power hath given unto us all things that pertain unto life and godliness, through the knowledge of him that hath called us to glory and virtue: 2 Peter 1:3 (KJV)

God gave us all things pertaining unto life and godliness because he committed His will into a written form. The Bible is to be our guide through this life. Instead of "Don't Panic" embossed on the cover, God's phrase, "Fear Not," is etched on every page.
