Author-verified transcription

Entrapment reflects two of the most popular trends occurring in Hollywood today. The first is the romantic pairing of a geriatric gentleman with a young and beautiful partner (ie. Bulworth). The second is the growing penchant to turn thieves or criminals into goodhearted and likable protagonists behind whom the audience is to rally (ie. Out of Sight).

Those general observations being made, Entrapment also happens to be a glossy-looking, stunt-filled, technologically clever movie about top-of-the-line professionals plying their craft, illegal as that may be.

68 year old Sean Connery (Playing By Heart) is Robert "Mac" MacDougal, a thief who has the reputation of taking an impossible job and making it "doable." He is paired with 29 year old Catherine Zeta-Jones (The Mask of Zorro), as Gin Baker, an insurance agent who goes undercover in an attempt to entrap Mac.

The bait she uses is far from normal... an ancient Chinese mask worth millions, followed by a computer heist worth billions. We're not talking about a local convenience store stickup here. We're also not talking about a normal insurance agent. Could it be that an "exciting" life of crime and all that money is beginning to tempt her to join forces with Mac?

Director Jon Amiel (Copycat) has put together an entertaining film which contains enough surprising twists and turns to keep things lively. Using exotic locations and high tech gadgetry like laser beam grids, infrared goggles, and retinal scans, he has, for the most part, been able to distract us from the laughable implausibility of the script.

Mr. Connery continues to exude the charm that he brings to all (or most) of his film portrayals.

We are willing to overlook the fact that he is too old for the role he is playing in exchange for the pleasure of seeing him again on screen.

The extraordinarily beautiful Miss Zeta-Jones does little more than demonstrate her agility and pliability here. While her performance is not eminently memorable (although most men will probably recall her "inch worming" under the laser-beam security system), her high profile work in this movie and in The Mask of Zorro, should assure us that we will be seeing her again in future projects. (In fact, she will next appear opposite Liam Neeson in The Haunting, due to be released in July.)

The supporting cast includes Ving Rhames (Out of Sight) as Mac's "partner" who is somehow able to exert some measure of control over him, Will Patton (Armageddon) as Cruz, a manager of the insurance company who begins to distrust Gin's motives, and Maury Chaykin (Love and Death on Long Island) who continues his stream of offbeat characterizations as the Buddha-like, diaper-wearing black marketer, Conrad Greene.

While it is disappointing that so many filmmakers find it necessary to depict lawbreakers in a positive way, there is a spiritual lesson to be found in the actions of these characters.

In order to succeed in their plan to steal the Chinese mask from its seemingly impenetrable location, Gin and Mac train for days, perfecting their timing, honing their skills, planning for every foreseeable contingency so as to walk away victorious.

If a criminal is willing and able to be that disciplined in order to achieve the evil goals they desire, shouldn't we apply ourselves with the same amount of drive and discipline to achieve our godly goals? If we wish to counter the impact made upon our culture by those who reject or ignore God and His Word, we must be relentless in our pursuit of the truth and its application in our lives.

Brethren, be not children in understanding: Howbeit in malice be children, but in understanding be men. 1 Corinthians 14:20 /KJV]

No athlete ever gets better at his sport without practicing. No student ever gets more knowledgeable in his subject without studying. The same logic applies to spiritual understanding. To become stronger spiritually requires a commitment and discipline to exercise one's spiritual senses. And, as Mac would say "It's doable."
