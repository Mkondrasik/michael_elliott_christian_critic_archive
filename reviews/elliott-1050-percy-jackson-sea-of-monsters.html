Author-verified transcription

Demi-gods aren’t what they used to be. Percy Jackson (Logan Lerman), the half-blood son of Poseidon, returns to the screen in Percy Jackson: Sea of Monsters. One would think that after saving the world in the first film, the teen-aged hero would be allowed a bit of a swagger. But Percy fears that he may be a one-quest wonder so there’s no surprise when the next world saving event comes along, he doesn’t get the nod.

After learning of an ancient prophecy that may involve him, he takes the initiative to begin his own quest and praise Zeus that he does for once again Percy is the reluctant hero that is needed to defeat an enemy who threatens to destroy his very existence along with all others like him.

Accompanying him in this hero’s journey is his good friend and goat-boy Grover (Brandon T. Jackson), gal pal Annabeth (Alexandra Daddario), and newly discovered half-brother Tyson (Douglas Smith) who happens to be a Cyclops. A step ahead is his chief rival in his demigod training camp, Clarisse (Levin Rambin) and his arch-enemy and fellow demigod Luke (Jake Abel). They are all on the search for the mythical Golden Fleece which has magical healing properties that are needed to save the camp and eventually the rest of the Olympus controlled world.

Further plot description is not needed as little of it exists. Once the premise is set, the film settles into its high definition, 3D, action scenes which puts the main characters in and out of danger

Throughout this quest, Percy questions whether he is capable of fulfilling his destiny and successfully accomplishing the life-threatening mission that awaits him. It is a natural reaction to feel ill-equipped and somehow unable to meet the expectations of others and achieve the goals set before us. The thing to remember at those times is that our success does not rely upon our own abilities. It is God that directs our paths and it is God that will cause us to be victorious as we follow and obey His guidance.

“I have strength for all things in Christ who empowers me [I am ready for anything and equal to anything through Him Who infuses inner strength into me; I am self-sufficient in Christ's sufficiency].” Philippians 4:13 (Amplified)

God did not choose us because of our natural abilities or super-human strength. He chose us because He knew we would believe in Him and trust Him to provide everything we would need to be successful in the work that He would have us do.
