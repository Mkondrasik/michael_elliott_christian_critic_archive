Author-verified transcription

Greg Iles' novel 24 Hours is the kind of "easy read" one might stumble across at an airport bookstand and choose to buy for the long flight that may lay ahead. The film, Trapped, based upon that book, might be best viewed under those circumstances... on board a long flight with nothing else to do. If the headset is free of charge, even better.

The story is of a kidnapping. The perfect crime, the kidnapper tells us, because everything is controlled. As long as nobody panics, nobody gets hurt. It would seem that naiveté is not restricted to the pure at heart.

The kidnapper (Kevin Bacon, Hollow Man) stays with the mommy (Charlize Theron, Sweet November) while accomplice #1 (Courtney Love, Beat) hangs out with the daddy (Stuart Townsend, Queen of the Damned). Accomplice #2 (Pruitt Taylor Vince, Simone) has the dubious task of staying with the young "kidnapee" (Dakota Fanning, I Am Sam) whom he must kill if his two partners do not check in with him periodically.

Within 24 hours, the transaction will be made - child and parents will be reunited while parents and two hundred fifty thousand of their hard earned dollars will be separated. That's the plan, anyway. But things do not always go according to plan. And this time, complications (aka plot twists) ensue. A few too many to be credible and a few too obvious to count as surprising developments.

Director Luis Mandoki (Angel Eyes) tries to distract us from the gaping holes in the plot by using hand held camera techniques. He succeeded to a degree. His choice of shaky camera techniques was indeed distracting... but only in a negative sense.

The pity of this is that the cast is uniformly decent. Kevin Bacon is able to portray either hero or villain with equal success and he uses that ability to great effect to play an icily cold, confident bad guy. Charlize Theron is effective in her role as an upscale damsel in distress whose child is being threatened. Pruitt Taylor Vince is also believable as a dimwitted lout who is given charge of the child.

The main problem seems to be the fact that though the producers have brought this talent together, they failed to provide them a worthy script to perform. Iles has kept his screenplay pretty close to the action of the book. But what might have been acceptable on the printed page in Iles' book does not translate well to the screen. The gaps of logic are far more noticible, the characters' motivations less believable, and the climatic ending was too far over the top.

I'm guessing that the film's title refers to the victims' feeling of helplessness in the face of the kidnapper's "perfect crime." It is a feeling which our spiritual enemy also wants us to know. We should remember that the only people who are truly "trapped" are those who have no hope of rescue. We need not count ourselves among that number.

Blessed be the LORD, who hath not given us as a prey to their teeth. Our soul is escaped as a bird out of the snare of the fowlers: the snare is broken, and we are escaped. Our help is in the name of the LORD, who made heaven and earth. Psalms 124:6-8 (KJV)

Our God is a god of deliverance. As we are beset by opposition, be it physical, financial, emotional, or spiritual, He will provide a way for us to escape. In the midst of adversity, we should remember His promises and keep ourselves at peace, believing that He is faithful to keep those promises.
