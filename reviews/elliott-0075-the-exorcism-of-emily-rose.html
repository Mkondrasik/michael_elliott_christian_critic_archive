The Exorcism of Emily Rose

Author-verified transcription

Proving the existence of Satan in a court of law? That has to be a hard sell. And yet that is exactly what defense attorney Erin Bruner (Laura Linney) must do in The Exorcism of Emily Rose.

Less a horror film than it is a courtroom drama, EER delivers its scares through the possibility that the events which it depicts might very well be true. We’re told at the beginning that the film was inspired by actual events. Whether or not you believe what the film suggests is another matter altogether.

The man on trial is Father Moore (Tom Wilkenson), a Catholic priest who was called to the aid of 19 year old Emily Rose (Jennifer Carpenter). Diagnosed by doctors as having psychotic epilepsy, Emily has not been responding to their treatment. Moore concludes and her parents agree that demonic forces have taken control of the young girl’s body. He receives permission to perform an exorcism.

When the ritual fails, Moore finds himself charged with negligent homicide. He refuses the state’s plea agreement because, much to the embarrassment of the church, Moore is determined to take the stand so he can tell Emily’s story.

In an interesting twist, Moore is being prosecuted by a churchgoer (Campbell Scott). His defense attorney is an ambitious woman (Laura Linney) and a professed agnostic. In other words, she doesn’t know what she believes and has spent little time to think about it.

Emily’s story is told in flashback fashion as witness after witness appears to reveal the details of the chilling events which took the life of this young woman. Jennifer Carpenter puts herself through an emotional wringer as she portrays the titular character stuck in what some may literally call a “living hell.”

Laura Linney is quite believable as the no-nonsense lawyer climbing up the corporate ladder who suddenly discovers herself coming to a spiritual/supernatural rung. It is her honest performance that gives the film its weight.

Fans of the horror genre may be disappointed with the lack of fright scenes. There are few “jump out of your seat” moments although the ones that do come are quite effective. Likewise, fans of courtroom thrillers may be disappointed over the lack of dramatic tension or plot twists that are so familiar in that genre.

But with powerful performances and a compelling subject matter, The Exorcism of Emily Rose presents legitimate questions for us to answer. Is demonic possession possible? Does Satan exist? If the answers to those questions are yes, then where is God and why does he allow this evil to prevail? I can easily see this film leading to some interesting discussions regarding the intersection of the spiritual and the natural realms.

Of course, many are predisposed not to believe in the spiritual realities or the spiritual battle that is part of our daily existence. No amount of persuasion will convince them otherwise. It isn’t that they are stubborn. It’s just that they are unable to see.

But the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him: neither can he know then, because they are spiritually discerned. 1 Corinthians 2:14 (KJV)

It takes spirit to know spirit. Demonic possession is a reality that is well documented in the only source that counts - the standard for truth as it relates to spiritual matters: the Bible.
