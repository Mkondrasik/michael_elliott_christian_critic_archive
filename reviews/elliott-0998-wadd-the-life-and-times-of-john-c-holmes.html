Author-verified transcription

It takes a certain amount of "chutzpah" for a filmmaker to send someone called The Christian Critic a documentary film on the undisputed "king of porn," John C. Holmes. And yet, there it was, in a plain brown wrapper in my PO Box.

After the bomb squad left (one can never be too careful), I gave the film a look see. The film, which was described to me as an "R-rated film about an X-rated industry," seems to want to paint a picture of an average country boy, endowed with an above average piece of male equipment, who only wanted people to love him.

It is very difficult to separate the technical aspects used from the subject matter that was chosen. Suffice it to say, I cannot recommend this film for viewing. Obviously prurient, it sheds little insight on its subject other than relate certain factual events that transpired of which some may be unaware.

One of the favored directorial techniques was to edit the personal interviews in such a way that those professing their opinions contradicted each other. If one thought Mr. Holmes had "a heart of gold," there was someone else who thought him to be self-absorbed and heartless. Hearing such differing opinions juxtaposed so they came on top of each other may have been amusing at first, but it soon became evident that we were not getting any closer to understanding the enigma of a man who made the decisions he did.

And poor decisions they were too. Following his entrance into the adult film world, Mr. Holmes ruins his marriage, gets involved with drugs, crime, and murder. There's obvious lying, a hint of prostitution, evidence of homosexuality, sexual involvement with minors, all of which continued until his contracting AIDS which led to his death at age 43.

Scriptures say that we can know a man by the "fruit" that his life bears. It is clear that Mr. Holmes' life was full of bushel after bushel of rotten fruit.
