Author-verified transcription

That Dirk Pitt is arguably the cheesiest name ever given to a hero hasn't stopped author Clive Cussler from using it as the core of a popular literary franchise. In Sahara, Dirk Pitt is searching for a mysterious ironclad Confederate warship that he believes is buried somewhere under the African desert sands.

Implausible? Yes. But even more incredible is that after the story sets up this hard to believe premise, it abandons it for something even more outlandish.

After Dirk (Matthew McConaughey) and his faithful sidekick Al (Steve Zahn) convince their benefactor and partner ex-Admiral Sandecker (William H. Macy) to lend them the use of his boat, they set out in search of the missing Civil War vessel. Along the way they meet Dr. Eva Rojas (Penelope Cruz) of the World Health Organization (W.H.O.) who is in a quest of her own.

A mysterious plague has been spreading throughout African villages and Rojas is trying to find the source before it grows too large to be contained. Dirk and Al decide to abandon their search when an African warlord attacks the good doctor before she discovers his dark secret. They switch gears (and hats) to become daring rescuers and world-savers.

Director Breck Eisner, making his feature film debut, doesn't seem to trust the material enough to keep a consistent tone throughout. The most successful segments of the film come from the playful interaction between McConaughey and Zahn though it took nearly 45 minutes before their relationship is fully established during the film's first real action sequence. It doesn't help that the somberness of Cruz's character and the seriousness of an unlikely worldwide environmental threat place a damper on all the high budget fun trying to break free.

Still, if you like your adventures to be of the mindless variety, you may find something to appreciate in Sahara. It's just that finding it may be a bit like coming across an oasis while wandering about in an arid wasteland. You're so thankful that it's there that you almost forget that you're still in a wasteland... until its time to move on.

Dirk and Al prove their resourcefulness in numerous ways as they manage to extract themselves from one impossible situation after another. Whether it is constructing a windsurfer out of a crashed airplane or single-handedly beating back an entire African army, Dirk and Al rise to the challenge -- often being surprised at the positive outcome of whatever hair-brained scheme they attempt.

Though they are impressive at how they extricate themselves from tight spots, their skills pale in comparison to God who is the master of escape and deliverance. He parted the Red Sea to allow Moses and the children of Israel to escape Egypt. He kept Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego from burning in the fiery furnace. He broke Paul out of prison with an earthquake which opened the jail cell. He saved Daniel from the mouths of lions and David from the sword of a giant.

But the most remarkable escape from certain death he ever managed to engineer was to save all men and woman from a lifetime of bondage, pain and suffering via the sacrifice of one perfect man -- his son, Jesus Christ.

And if any man hear my words, and believe not, I judge him not: for I came not to judge the world, but to save the world. John 12:47 (KJV)

As we believe unto salvation we slip out of the devil's noose to live, not just for another day, but for all of eternity.
