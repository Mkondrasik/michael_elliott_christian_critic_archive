Author-verified transcription

"Re-dial 'M' For Murder" could have been the title for this remake of the 1954 classic Hitchcock thriller. Michael Douglas (The Game), and Gwyneth Paltrow (Emma) team up in A Perfect Murder, taking on the screen roles first portrayed by Ray Milland and Grace Kelly.

For the benefit of those born after 1980, A Perfect Murder, like Dial M For Murder, is based on the play by Frederick Knott. Mr. Douglas plays Steven Taylor, a wealthy financier whose wealth is rapidly leaving him as a result of bad (read that illegal) financial decisions. It's a good thing that his young, beautiful, albeit unfaithful, wife, Emily, played by Miss Paltrow, is wealthy in her own right. The down side is that she may be leaving him as well. She has fallen in love with David Shaw (Viggo Mortensen of G.I. Jane), a struggling artist, who may not be everything he appears to be.

Taking the "til death do us part" vow much too seriously, Steven, faced with the possible loss of his wife's money, puts into action what he thinks will be the perfect murder. He soon finds out that there is no such thing.

As was true with the play upon which this movie was based, A Perfect Murder is not a typical whodunit thriller. The audience, from the very inception of the plan, knows that Steven will be the "who" responsible for the "it" being "dun". The excitement comes from watching the plan unravel with the twists and turns that come about as the characters adjust to the increasingly complex set of circumstances with which they must deal as a result of the lies which they continue to tell.

And tell them they do. Mr. Douglas is very adept in his duplicitous role. Just when we think that his character is about to be caught, he's able to deflect any suspicion from him. Even though we, as an audience know he's lying, he's still very convincing. To his credit as an actor, although he plays a liar, a cheat and a murderer, he's not altogether without charm.

Miss Paltrow is, as they say of the character she plays, a sublime experience. Portraying a "victim" can be a thankless and unrewarding task. She more than rises to the challenge, able to show a solid core of strength to what could have been a weak characterization. There are few actresses that are able to manifest the qualities and the class of a Grace Kelly. Miss Paltrow, thankfully, is one.

Mr. Mortensen is nothing more than serviceable as David. While he does have the pretty face necessary for the role, he wasn't completely convincing when asked to portray the more intricate shadings of the character.

Director Andrew Davis (The Fugitive) keeps the tension high and the melodrama higher as he pushes all the right buttons and pulls all the right strings that keep us, the audience, on the edge of our seats. True, there may be some implausible moments in the film. At one point, when Miss Paltrow left her bath to answer a phone that had to have rung at least a dozen times, my wife nudged me and said, "Right... like these millionaires can't afford a phone in their master bathroom?"

But still, we tend to overlook these minor points in lieu of watching a nicely written, properly directed, and well acted movie such as A Perfect Murder.

Spiritually speaking, this movie could be used as a primer for devil spirits. Lying, murder, adultery, envy, greed. it's all there. It is interesting to note that money does play a predominant part in the telling of this story. We are led to believe that the adultery does not bother Steven anywhere near as much as the thought of losing his wife's money. It's the money that drives Steven to lie and kill. The scriptures say that the "love of money is the root of all evil."

Notice that the scriptures say that it's the love of money and not money itself that is the root of all evil. Perhaps if Steven loved his wife more than he loved her money, she wouldn't have given in to the temptation of a younger lover. If only Steven respected his wife and loved her as much as the scriptures direct husbands to love their wives, this movie could have been titled, A Perfect Marriage.
