Scary Movie

Author-verified transcription

During a thunderstorm which accompanied the press screening of the new comedy/horror spoof, Scary Movie, the theater in which we were seated lost power twice. Naturally, no power means no film screening. In this particular case, that would prove to be merely wishful thinking. The scary part actually came when the power was finally restored and we had to sit through the rest of this excruciating excuse for a motion picture.

As a spoof of the horror genre, SCARY MOVIE fails miserably. The film makes obvious references to the Scream series, What I Did Last Summer, The Sixth Sense, and even The Matrix but instead of offering an intelligent satire, it exploits these types of films in order to employ a brand of sexual and scatological humor that out-Farrellys the brothers that made Me, Myself and Irene. As a comedy, Scary Movie doesn't just border on obscenity, it bathes in it.

There is really no plot to describe because the film doesn't pretend to follow a logical narrative. It comes across as a series of sketches tied together by an inept serial killer who is inexplicably pursuing a not-so-young group of teenagers.

The film boasts no less than six credited screenwriters, once again giving proof to the adage that more is not necessarily better. The dialogue is simply awful, and the characters are transparent stereotypes whose sole aim, presumably, is to offend whichever classification of human being they so shallowly represent.

Director Keenen Ivory Wayans (In Living Color) clumsily directs each scene, telegraphing any "jokes" long before they are played. As a result, we have a poorly structured and poorly filmed movie whose humor is based on shocking the audience with an forced audacity, pushing the limits of taste and propriety well beyond any reasonable definition of decency.

Scary Movie is only scary when we consider what it takes for any film to reach the point of a wide theatrical release. That people in the film industry believe this to be the kind of film and kind of humor which will have enough of a mainstream appeal to earn a profit and thus justify a reported twenty million dollar budget, is more than scary. It is an indictment of the culture that we, as a people, have helped to build.

Anyone who has been a regular reader of my reviews will know that I use secular films to make spiritual points. For that reason, I do not condemn films that do not adhere to a strict biblical doctrine, but rather use them as an illustration to what biblical doctrine is. That said, to the best of my recollection, this film is the first I've seen since I've started reviewing that is so void of content or substance that I feel justified in using the following scripture:

But shun profane and vain babblings: for they will increase unto more ungodliness. And their word will eat as doth a canker: 2 Timothy 2:16-17 (KJV)

Some things are so unedifying that they should be avoided. Everyone must make up their own minds, of course. For me, would that I had those 90 minutes back that I spent watching this film so as to put them to better use.
