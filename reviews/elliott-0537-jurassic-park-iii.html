If you're looking for signs of intelligence in Jurassic Park III, stick with the dinosaurs. You won't find it in the human characters. I mean, how much intelligence does it take for even fictional characters to realize that going to an island populated by massive carnivorous beasts is not a good idea? Hello?? Can you say man-eater?

Sam Neill (The Dish), who skipped the second outing of Jurassic Park, is back again as Dr. Alan Grant. Although he has vowed never again to return to the site of his narrow escape from the reptilian jaws of death, he is easily tricked by Paul Kirby (William H. Macy, Magnolia) and his wife Amanda (Tea Leoni, The Family Man) into leading a foolhardy "tour" of Isla Sorna, the second InGen site where manufactured prehistoric creatures roam freely, feasting on trees, grasses, each other and, of course, anyone stupid enough to set foot upon their island.

The Kirbys, along with a number of bit players (hereafter called "dino food"), are joined by Grant and his protégé Billy (Alessandro Nivola, Timecode) in what turns out to be an ill-conceived rescue mission. The Kirby's 14-year-old son (Trevor Morgan, The Patriot) has become trapped on Isla Sorna following a freak parasailing accident. I know... but as unlikely as all this may be, it's as good an excuse as any to get them all on the island. Once there, the focus immediately shifts to their attempts to get off the island (something that makes a great deal more sense). It's then that the film kicks into high gear.

Sam Neill has successfully morphed his character of Dr. Grant into a sardonic version of Indiana Jones - complete with hat and accessories. William H. Macy adds a sympathetic quality to the film as a regular dad who is thrust into a role of reluctant hero.

Director Joe Johnston (Jumanji) takes the reins from Stephen Spielberg, who directed the first two JP films, and injects his own brand of "theme park ride" scares that the genre demands. That said, there is still more than enough screaming and stomping to go around and quite honestly, the dinosaurs have never looked better.

Surprisingly, T-Rex is no longer the baddest dude-a-saurus on the island. That title has been passed on to a creature called Spinosaurus who squares off against T-Rex early on in the film. Also making their first (and long overdue) appearance in the JP series are the Pteranodons (flying dinosaurs) and they are spectacular. With the inclusion of these two creatures, the search and rescue party will encounter prehistoric attacks from land, sea, and air... pretty much ensuring that where ever they may go, they'll be wishing that they were someplace else.

Dinosaurs are amazing creatures whose existence cannot be disputed. Many Christians admittedly have some trouble reconciling the scientific data which comes from studying fossils with the biblical data which comes from studying the Word. The two are not (nor can they be) contradictory. The most rational and logical key to explaining the time differences which appear to exist is found not in a verse, but between two verses:

And in the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was without form (tohuw), and void. Genesis 1:1-2a (KJV)

We know that God did not initially create the world to be without form and void:

For thus saith the LORD that created the heavens; God himself that formed the earth and made it; he hath established it, he created it not in vain (tohuw). Isaiah 45:18a (KJV)

The most rational explanation I've heard to reconcile these two apparent contradictions is that the verb "was" in Genesis 1:2 is better translated as "became". God created the earth suitable for life. It became without form and void. There must be an indeterminate amount of time that exists between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2. Time enough for dinosaurs to have ruled the earth before it became without form and void, thus causing their extinction and ushering in the time when humans became the dominant life force.
