Author-verified transcription

Zoo animals let loose in the wild? It is a premise rife with possibilities. Unfortunately, in Dreamworks' newest animated film, Madagascar, those possibilities are pretty much left unrealized.

Marty (Chris Rock) is a ten year old zebra growing bored and discontented with his life behind bars at New York's Central Park Zoo. Even though his friends, Alex the Lion (Ben Stiller), Gloria the Hippo (Jada Pinkett Smith), and Melman the Giraffe (David Schwimmer) try to cheer him up, he won't be consoled.

Only when the zoo's wise guy penguins stage a breakout so they can head towards Antarctica does Marty perk up. Going to "the wild" is just the thing he needs to free himself from his doldrums. When he decides to follow the penguins' lead and take the subway to places unknown, his friends, who are otherwise quite content with their pampered zoo life, set off in hot pursuit so they might bring him to his "horse senses."

When this menagerie is discovered off zoo property, it is decided that their animal instincts may be too strong for public exhibition. Before they can say "Madagascar," these zoo pals find themselves having to fend for themselves in the wilds of "the wild."

The "fish-out-of-water" scenario is a timeless classic used in many a successful comedy. The computer animated artwork is sharp and clean. The characters are distinct and the humor is somewhat clever. So what's the problem?

It boils down to the cast. On the whole, there's too little energy driving these bland vocal performances. Watching how badly this opportunity is squandered underscores just how vital the matching of voices to characters can be. When it works (i.e. Finding Nemo) it is movie magic. When it doesn't, everything else is negatively affected.

Ben Stiller was simply the wrong choice for the egocentric lion. Chris Rock's humor is edgy and sarcastic - qualities that Marty the Zebra doesn't have. Jada Pinkett Smith and David Schwimmer are instantly forgettable.

The penguins are the best part of the film and Sacha Baron Cohen (aka Ali G) does a nice job as the whacked out lemur king of Madagascar.

The film explores an unusually deep subject, albeit in a superficial way. Marty and company appear to have found paradise until Alex's carnivorous ways become more and more dominant. Although they are his friends, he is soon seeing Marty and the others as entrée items.

We can't really blame Alex - he's a lion. It is in his nature to be a predator. We humans don't have that excuse. God has given us the ability to change our "natural man" ways.

You were taught, with regard to your former way of life, to put off your old self, which is being corrupted by its deceitful desires; to be made new in the attitude of your minds; and to put on the new self, created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness. Ephesians 4:22-24 (KJV)

Forget about opposable thumbs. This is the one thing that sets us far above the animal kingdom - our ability to change our ways and rise above our natural instincts.
