Author-verified transcription

In The Beginning, NBC's Bible-based miniseries which will run on Sunday and Monday evening (November 12th - 13th) starts, oddly enough, not at the beginning but rather twelve chapters into the book of Genesis, as Abram leads his household to the land God had shown him. But the series doesn't linger there long. Depicting 430 years in a matter of 4 hours doesn't allow much room for in depth introspection.

As a result, much of In The Beginning has the feel of a children's Bible book, one which relates these great historical records merely as stories without teaching the spiritual significance behind them. Even so, it is interesting to see these records being acted out and hopefully they might provide a door of utterance for someone who has studied the Bible enough to share some deeper spiritual insight with those who may be watching.

Following the biblically recorded historical events from one generation to the next, In The Beginning touches upon highlights from the lives of those Old Testament believers whose records can be found in Genesis and Exodus.

Though the miniseries picks up the story well after Noah and the flood, Abram (Martin Landau, Crimes and Misdemeanors) provides a bit of a flashback by inspirationally telling his followers a campfire story... the story of creation. After those brief expository remarks, the film progresses posthaste as each story must be quickly told before we can move onto the next one.

The records which are illustrated include Abraham and Sarah having a baby even though they were well past age (a fact somewhat diminished by the casting of the still beautiful Jacqueline Bisset as the eighty year old Sarah); Abraham's willingness to sacrifice that son as what he believed would be a burnt offering to God; Esau and Jacob (Frederick Weller, The Beach Boys: An American Family) each vying for their father's blessing; Jacob's love for Rachel and his seven years of labor only to be deceived by her father; Jacob's wrestling with an angel of God; Joseph (Eddie Cibrian, But I'm a Cheerleader) being sold into slavery by his brothers but rising to a position of esteem and power in Pharaoh's court. The miniseries finally comes to an end with Moses leading his people out of Egyptian captivity and into the wilderness where the promise of God along with His commandments await them.

A disappointment of In the Beginning has to do with the amount of material they try to cover without having the time in which to do them justice. Part one covers the span from Abram's journey to Joseph being sold into slavery. Part two covers Joseph's experiences in Egypt and leads into the birth, life, and final days of Moses. For the most part, the filmmakers stayed pretty close to the facts as recorded in the Bible. But sometimes facts alone simply aren't enough to illustrate or define truth.

While watching In The Beginning, a biblical record that is not depicted in the mini-series kept coming to my mind. In Acts 8, Philip meets an Ethiopian studying a scroll containing the book of Isaiah. He asks the Ethiopian, "Do you understand what you are reading?" The Ethiopian honestly replies, "How can I except some man should guide me?" I can see this incident replayed in our day and time.

A simple narrative of the biblical records such as we find in this mini-series can serve a godly purpose, but they won't do the job alone. As the Ethiopian knew, teachers are needed to help put these records into spiritual perspective; to show how and why these events occurred and where God's hand was in each occurrence. Without such spiritual guidance, these tremendous records of God's power and deliverance can be mistaken as fictional stories of little or no importance. That would be a sin because there is nothing that could be farther from the truth.
