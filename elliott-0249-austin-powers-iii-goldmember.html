Austin Powers III: Goldmember

Author-verified transcription

Austin Powers III: Goldmember goes to the well of sexual innuendo for the third time and once again finds little in the way of refreshing entertainment. In fact, this multimillion dollar franchise has become somewhat tiresome... rather like an aging uncle who insists upon telling the same stories every time he visits.

Mike Myers stars as multiple characters, not only reprising his roles as Austin Powers, Dr. Evil, and Fat Bastard but also by adding a new persona: Goldmember, a strange, skin-damaged Dutch man whose "twig and berries" were gilded in what must have been a rather painful smelting accident.

The plot, in keeping with the tradition of Austin Powers films, is both nonsensical and inconsequential. Dr. Evil plans on flooding the world by using a tractor beam to cause a golden asteroid to melt the polar ice caps. It is a simple enough plan, though without real purpose, made more complicated by forcing a trip through time back to 1975 to pick up Goldmember, secret agent Foxxy Cleopatra (Beyoncé Knowles of Destiny's Child), and Austin's dad (Michael Caine, Last Orders), all who seem to be partying together at New York's Studio 54 during the height of the disco era.

After that bit of unnecessary distraction, we return to 2002 where a little sibling rivalry between Dr. Evil's son, Scott (Seth Green), and Evil's clone, Mini-Me (Verne Troyer), is causing tension in the villain's lair. All is not tea and crumpets in the Powers' camp either as the heroic father and son also have unresolved issues.

Perhaps the most telling piece of evidence that this franchise has "jumped the shark" is the blatant overuse of celebrity cameos that pepper the film. When no fresh ideas are available, why not distract the audience with a familiar face in an unexpected setting? It's an old trick that was run into the ground back in the 1970s with the Batman TV series.

Mike Myers does show his versatility in presenting no less than four distinctly different characters interacting with one another through the magic of cinema. Prosthetics aside, the fact that he can lose himself in each character to the point that we are never consciously aware that it is the same actor doing shtick is something of an achievement. It is just that his achievement is diminished by the sheer inanity of the project itself. Would that he would find a film that is actually worthy of his chameleon-like abilities.

Beyoncé Knowles captures the strong "sista" attitude of a blaxploitation heroine. Michael Caine is his usual dapper self as Nigel Powers. Fred Savage (The Wonder Years) must keep a straight face as an undercover "mole" with a facial abnormality. Of course, most of the regular Austin Powers characters are back for another ride on this silly little money train.

Keep in mind that I wasn't a great fan of the previous two Austin Powers' films. Goldmember closely copies its predecessors so my reaction is quite predictable. There are obviously a great many people who hold a different opinion when it comes to this kind of humor.

Of course, within the outrageous, over the top comedy are some pretty down to earth relationship issues. The father/son relationship plays an important part for both Austin Powers as well as Dr. Evil. While both of them felt neglected by their dads, they responded in completely different ways. Powers turned to overachieving in hopes to impress his father; Evil turned instead to... well... evil.

Because their fathers neglected them in the way they were raised, they were left to themselves to develop their own behavioral patterns. A father should be instrumental in not only setting an example for their child to follow, but in encouraging, correcting, and reproving that child as needed.

As ye know how we exhorted and comforted and charged every one of you, as a father doth his children That ye would walk worthy of God, who hath called you unto his kingdom and glory. 1 Thessalonians 2:11-12 (KJV)
